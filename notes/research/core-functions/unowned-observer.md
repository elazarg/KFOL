# One common observer carrier and its dependence completion

Mathematical research, without new Lean claims. This places all original
observer faces in one carrier and gives a sharp theorem about adding roles.
It also shows why a canonical completion from semantic dependence is not
enough to solve joint realizability.

Let I be a finite owner set of size K>=3. Terms here use variables and
owned choices from I only; there are no constants or role switches.

## The common carrier

Let B_I consist of the K atoms r in I and two further values N,P. Its
flat union has P absorbing, N absorbing all atoms, and the union of two
different atoms equal to N. Define choice r to return r if either operand
is atom r, and otherwise return their flat union.

For an additional unowned role omega, the map from nonempty winner sets on
I union {omega} sending omega-containing menus to P, old singleton menus
{r} to atom r, and all remaining menus to N is a homomorphism for every
I-owned choice. Thus B_I is an actual observer quotient, with K+2 states.

For each k in I the subset B_I\{atom k} is a subalgebra. Choice k acts
there as flat union, because no input can be its absorbing atom. This face
is exactly the original k-observer algebra: old menus containing k map to
P, rival singletons to their atoms, and ambiguous k-omitting menus to N.

Consequently a candidate F on the original winner carrier is core exactly
when its observer operations factor through their quotients and are the
restrictions of one B_I term. Necessity follows by interpreting the same
word. Conversely a word with those face restrictions has the same original
k-membership bit as F for every k, so it realizes F. This is an exact common-
carrier reformulation; the term-extension quantifier is still a witness,
not the requested intrinsic characterization.

## The only essential congruence collapses N and P

For S subseteq I, let theta_S have the one block {N,P} union S and leave
every other atom separate. The congruences of B_I are exactly these 2^K
relations together with equality. In particular theta_empty is its unique
least nontrivial congruence, so B_I is subdirectly irreducible.

Each theta_S is compatible with every choice: changing an operand inside
its large block either meets an outside absorbing owner atom, giving that
same atom, or keeps the result inside the block. To prove completeness,
any nontrivial identification forces N=P. If r=N, compare choice_r(P,r)
with choice_r(P,N) to obtain r=P as well. If r=P, choose distinct old
atoms r,s,t and compare choice_s(r,t)=N with choice_s(P,t)=P. If r=s
for two distinct atoms, choose a third owner t and compare choice_t(r,r)=r
with choice_t(r,s)=N, reducing to the first case. The case N=P is immediate.
Once N and P are identified, two atoms cannot form a separate nontrivial
block: the same argument puts them in the N,P block. This proves the list.

Write D_I for the profile quotient B_I/(N=P). All proper quotients of B_I
factor through D_I. Its maximal proper subalgebras are precisely the K
omitted-atom faces and the P-free subalgebra, which is isomorphic to D_I.
Indeed a subalgebra omitting an atom lies in the corresponding face. If
it contains every atom, a foreign-owned choice of two distinct atoms forces
N; if it also contained P it would be the whole algebra.

Every face is a quotient of A_I, as above, and D_I is its singleton-status
quotient. Conversely, the membership-observer maps embed A_I into the
product of its K faces. Thus the variety generated by the proper subalgebras
and proper quotients of B_I is exactly the variety generated by A_I.
The identity below separates B_I itself from that variety. No splitting
or unique-coatom assertion about the full subvariety lattice is needed.

## Sharp identity stability through K variables

**Theorem.** If two core terms using at most K distinct variables agree on
the winner algebra A_I, they agree over every larger role set J containing
I, provided no new-role-owned gates are introduced. The bound K is sharp.

First their B_I operations agree on every omitted-atom face, by their
original equality. They also have the same old-owner singleton profiles.
On a P-free input, the B_I output cannot be P; its singleton profiles
determine whether it is an atom r, and otherwise it is N. On a P-containing
input with at most K coordinates, at most K-1 old atoms occur, so the input
lies in some omitted-atom face. This proves equality on B_I.

For any old winner k in J, collapse k-containing menus to P, old rival
singletons to their atoms, and everything else to N, including singletons
of unowned new roles. This is a homomorphism to the old k-observer face.
That face is already a surjective quotient of A_I, so original equality
fixes every old membership bit on J. For any new winner omega, the analogous
homomorphism goes to B_I and its membership bit is fixed by the equality
just proved. All winner bits therefore agree. The argument also allows
infinite J; only the input arity and old owner set need be finite.

For sharpness use K+1 variables x and y_r (r in I). On A_I the following
two expressions are equal:

    x union UNION_r y_r,
    (UNION_r y_r) union UNION_r (x choice_r y_r).           (1)

Every winner k in x survives in its own k-owned summand, every y_r is
preserved by the first union, and conservativity gives the converse
inclusion. Union is an abbreviation for its ordinary core implementation.
In B_I, put x=P and y_r=atom r. The first expression is P; the second is
N, since every owned summand absorbs to its atom. Thus equality fails after
adding one unowned role, at the first possible input arity K+1.

There is also stability at every arity if the two terms have a common
unused old owner. Let U be the union of their used gate owners, proper in I,
and choose omega in I\U. The coarse omega-observer is a surjective
homomorphism A_I -> B_U. Its N value is represented by two owned atoms
when |U|>=2; when |U|<=1, another unused old singleton supplies N. Equality
therefore descends to B_U. Every unowned winner on any larger carrier is
observed through B_U, and every owned winner through an omitted-atom face
of B_U, so equality extends. Consequently role-set-specific identities need
both at least K+1 variables and joint use of every old owner. Identity (1)
attains both bounds.

The B_I algebra also captures the complete identity theory stable under
adding unowned roles. Equality on B_I implies equality on every A_J by the
same observer argument; the converse follows because B_I is a quotient of
A_(I union {omega}). Thus it suffices to use this single (K+2)-element
carrier to test identities across all ambient extensions of the fixed
owner signature.

## The profile quotient has exact locality K

The term operations of D_I are exactly the admissible singleton-profile
tuples: a value is atom r when its r-profile is true at the r-atomic input
positions, and is the collapsed ambiguity otherwise. Profile synthesis on
A_I and passage to its quotient give the converse.

The least uniform number of valuation rows needed to recognize these term
operations is exactly K. For the upper bound, K-point interpolation transfers
the two-row singleton-profile factorization and profile monotonicity, the
one-row top values, and the K-row rainbow common-source condition. The
resulting profiles are admissible, so synthesis gives a term with exactly
the given operation on D_I.

For sharpness take one input variable for each owner and prescribe

    phi_r(E) iff I\{r} subseteq E.

Define f to return r if this holds for its r-atomic input positions, and
collapsed ambiguity otherwise. At most one owner qualifies: two qualifying
atomic sets would share I\{r,s}, which is nonempty because K>=3, whereas
different atomic sets are disjoint. These are precisely f's profiles.
They are not admissible, since the K winning coalitions I\{r} have empty
intersection.

Any K-1 valuation rows have a common core interpolant. Choose an owner q
that is never their atomic output. Strengthen only its profile to unanimity
E=I. The modified profiles are admissible: every rainbow intersection
contains q. They preserve all the sampled values, since they alter no
positive q-bit in those rows and leave the other profiles unchanged.
Synthesis supplies the interpolant. Thus f is (K-1)-point interpolable
but not a term operation. In particular D_I is finitely related, with
exact local interpolation threshold K.

There is a matching near-unanimity statement. On K+1 variables the profiles

    phi_r(E) iff |E|>=K

are admissible: K winning coalitions omit at most K variables altogether,
so have a common variable. Synthesis gives a (K+1)-ary term on D_I.
With all but one input equal to an atom r it returns r; with all but one
equal to ambiguity, no atom appears K times and it returns ambiguity.
Thus this is a near-unanimity term. No term of arity l<=K can have that
property: choose l distinct owners and give them the l winning coalitions
V\{i}, with top coalitions for every remaining owner. Their intersection
is empty, contradicting admissibility. Hence K+1 is the least
near-unanimity arity. On B_I, in contrast, {N,P} is a two-element union
subalgebra, so no near-unanimity term is possible.

## The extra N/P distinction restores unbounded locality

The same is false for B_I, despite its having only one more element. An
elementary padding construction makes this precise without introducing
gates owned by a newly added observer.

Choose two old owners b!=c. Let t use only their choices, with Boolean
c-profile psi. Starting with t, repeatedly apply choice_c with the sibling
t choice_b x_j, for every input variable j. Call the result E_t. If
t evaluates to {b}, every sibling is {b}, so E_t={b}. If t evaluates to
{c}, the outer c-fold absorbs. Otherwise no sibling can be {c}: it is
{b} when x_j={b}, and t union x_j in the other cases. The outer fold
therefore unions, giving the full input union. Hence E_t is exactly the
special greatest profile completion with profiles psi at c, its Boolean
dual at b, and full-support unanimity at every other owner.

This construction uses b,c only and works on every ambient winner carrier.
Finite unions of these completions use a third old owner for derived union.
Therefore all deletion patches and the extra collapsed cover in the
[Gray-crown proof](exponential-crowns.md) can be chosen in the fixed
I-owned signature, even after adding an unowned omega. Taking the coarse
omega-observer gives their actual B_I terms, for every d>=2.

On B_I the resulting crown operation is flat input union, except that a
selected region with c-atoms on L_x, b-atoms outside U_x, and a free value
different from b,c at U_x\L_x returns N. Exact atoms and that free-value
condition are reflected by the quotient, so the patch formulas descend
on every input. They prove 2^d-point interpolation on 2d variables.
The same matching/opposite b,c,P rows violate cyclic observer persistence;
lifting P to {omega} proves that no I-owned term interpolates them. Thus
the (2^d+1)-point failure is retained as well. Pair-separating lower
transversals also make every proper identification minor core, exactly as
in [the prime-obstruction proof](prime-obstructions.md).

Consequently B_I is not finitely related. Splitting the single ambiguity
element of the exactly K-local profile algebra into N and P is already
enough to require unbounded global coherence. Compression to this common
carrier does not reduce the full problem to a finite relation basis.

## A canonical dependence completion

Let F be an actual core operation on A_I with finitely many inputs. Given
v in B_I^V, define a cofactor domain Q(v): coordinates with atom r are fixed
to {r}, and coordinates with N or P range over the entire old carrier A_I.
Define D_F(v) by

* atom r if F is constantly {r} on Q(v);
* P if some P-marked coordinate is essential for F restricted to Q(v);
* N otherwise.

Essentiality here concerns the full winner-set-valued cofactor, not just
one membership bit. A constant atom cofactor has no essential coordinate,
so the first two alternatives do not conflict.

**Theorem.** D_F is monotone in the flat-union order, has the old singleton
profiles of F, agrees with every omitted-atom observer face of F, and lies
below every term-induced B_I extension of F.

For the atom claim, F is constantly {r} on Q(v) exactly when its r-profile
is true at the fixed r-input positions. One direction evaluates all free
inputs at N_r=I\{r}; the other uses monotonicity of that profile after
arbitrary assignments to the free coordinates. Thus these are exactly the
atom outputs of every representing word.

For the P lower bound, let G be a term extension and suppose G(v) is not P.
Choose arbitrary old menus for the N positions and put a menu containing
fresh omega at every P position. Their observed input refines v: an old
singleton at an N position is below N, and an old ambiguous menu observes
as N. Monotonicity of G makes omega absent from the actual output.
Omission independence now permits arbitrary simultaneous changes of all
omega-containing input menus, leaving the output unchanged. In particular
F is independent of every P-position on Q(v). The contrapositive proves
the lower bound. Finite arity identifies joint independence with absence
of an essential P-coordinate.

If v is in the face missing atom k and its prescribed observer output is
P, set all P-inputs to {k} and all N-inputs to N_k. The original output
contains k. Changing every P-input to N_k removes k from all inputs, hence
from the output. Along a one-coordinate-at-a-time change, some P-coordinate
is essential on Q(v). Therefore D_F(v)=P. Together with the atom claim
and the lower bound, this proves agreement on all faces.

For monotonicity, increasing an input in the flat-union order only enlarges
Q(v) and can add P-marks; an essential marked coordinate remains essential
and marked. A cofactor that becomes a constant atom on a larger domain was
already that same constant on the smaller domain. These observations prove
the required order preservation.

In arity at most K, the face and profile uniqueness argument shows that
D_F is the unique term-induced extension. The next example fails already
at K+1 inputs, so this arity bound is sharp.

## Dependence does not detect every compulsory fresh winner

Choose distinct a,b,c in I and the K+1 variables X and Z_r, r in I.
Let R start with Z_a choice_c Z_b, then append every remaining Z_r
using choice_a. Set

    T=(X choice_a Z_c) choice_b R.                        (2)

For K=3, this is the four-variable word

    (X choice_0 Z_2) choice_1
        ((Z_0 choice_2 Z_1) choice_0 Z_2).

The variable Z_c is shared; this is not a primitive read-once word.
Let F be T's operation on A_I. Fix Z_r={r}. The initial R value is
{a,b}; each appended singleton has owner different from a, so R returns I.
The left subtree X choice_a {c} can never be {b}: its absorbing case
is {a}, and otherwise its output contains c. Thus the root always returns
I, for every old menu X. This cofactor is constant.

At v=(P,(r)_(r in I)) in B_I, D_F(v)=N. The actual term T returns P.
More strongly, every old-carrier realizer of F returns P there. This is
forced by just three positive singleton-profile coalitions, independently
of the choice of realizer. Write V={X} union {Z_r:r in I}, and put

    A_a={X,Z_a,Z_b},
    A_b={X,Z_c},
    A_c={X,Z_a,Z_c} union {Z_r:r notin {a,b,c}},
    A_r=V for all other old owners r.

They are winning coalitions for T's respective profiles. For A_a the left
a-profile holds through X, and the right one through Z_a,Z_b. For A_b
the root absorbs through the left b-profile at X,Z_c. For A_c the left
c-profile holds at X,Z_c, and the right one at Z_a,Z_c and every appended
Z_r other than Z_c. All top coalitions are winning by idempotence.
Every realizer of F has the same old singleton profiles and hence retains
these positive bits on the extended carrier I union {omega}.

Set X={omega}, Z_r={r}, and add the top coalition A_omega=V. The full
coalition intersection is {X}, since A_a intersection A_b={X} and X
belongs to A_c. No rival singleton blocks this source: Z_a is excluded by
A_b, Z_b by A_c, Z_c by A_a, and every other Z_r by A_a intersection A_b.
The rainbow positive-profile lower bound therefore forces omega in every
realizer's output at this row. Applying the omega-observer gives P at v.
This argument does not need an assumption about a realizer's support.

Consequently D_F is not term-induced, despite its correct faces, singleton
profiles, monotonicity and complete old cofactor-essentiality data. The
first possible missing-face arity K+1 is already enough for this failure.

The unresolved issue is the integrability of a completion: simultaneous
semantic dependence and correct observer faces do not suffice to produce
one common core term. This common carrier and its canonical lower bound
make the missing condition more explicit; they do not supply it.
