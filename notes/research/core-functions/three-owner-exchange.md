# Rainbow budgets and feedback elimination for owner closures

Mathematical research, without Lean claims. A closed colored-cover law
constrains the canonical owner closures of every core operation. It
subsumes hereditary singleton-profile admissibility and cross-value
dependence composition. Its exact budget lemmas use projections for
rainbow queries and a closure-complement fold for same-owner queries.

A four-variable obstruction passes pair-cut, reciprocal shared-witness
compatibility, hereditary profiles and exact functional dependence,
but violates this law. Its proper neutral faces and identification
minors are core. Unlike the earlier
[two-rule obstruction](separable-closure-counterexample.md), its pure
ports cannot come from any core operation, including a nonseparable one.

Fix finite owners I with |I|>=3 and finite nonempty variables V.
Canonical closures are the [cofactor dependence closures](cofactor-dependence-closures.md);
unused inputs can be removed so they are loopless. Write Phi_r^T
for a word's owner-r singleton profile.

## Exact budget tests and the general elimination law

The proof uses an exact feasibility lemma for profiles and differently
colored omission budgets.

**Rainbow-budget projection lemma.** Fix an observing owner k and a
finite nonempty variable set V. Let R be a set of positive-profile
owners, with one coalition A_p for each p in R. Let S be a set of
query owners disjoint from R and not containing k. For each s in S
choose disjoint sets X_s,P_s. Define q_s by putting I on X_s, the
s singleton on P_s, and N_k=I\{k} everywhere else. Put

    C=INTERSECTION_(p in R) A_p,

with C=V when R is empty. There is a core word T satisfying

    Phi_p^T(A_p)=true for every p in R,
    k notin T(q_s) for every s in S                  (RB)

if and only if

    C\UNION_(s in S) X_s != empty.                  (1)

Moreover, whenever (RB) is feasible, a projection already witnesses it.
If x belongs to (1), projection x has all the required profiles and
omits k at every query, since x is outside every X_s.

For necessity, each budget forces a positive singleton profile:

    k notin T(q_s) implies Phi_s^T(V\X_s)=true.     (RB-profile)

Indeed decrease every input outside X_s to {s}, keeping X_s at I.
This decreases the original input menus: outside P_s union X_s
was N_k, which contains s since s!=k. Input inclusion monotonicity
keeps k absent from the output. The new inputs are only {s} and I,
and every core operation preserves this two-element set. Its output
must therefore be {s}, since I contains k. This is exactly the
profile in (RB-profile).

Now use the given positive profiles at A_p, these forced profiles at
V\X_s, and the top coalition V for every remaining owner. Their
rainbow common-source condition gives

    INTERSECTION_p A_p intersect INTERSECTION_s (V\X_s)
        = C\UNION_s X_s != empty.

This proves necessity, including empty R or S, directly from
monotonicity and the singleton-profile theorem.

There is a complementary closed test when arbitrarily many budgets all
have the **same** owner s!=k, with X_i intersect P_i empty. Keep the
positive-profile owners R disjoint from s, and set C=INTERSECTION_(p in R) A_p as above. Let d be the
closure generated by the displayed implications P_i => X_i, meaning

    P_i subseteq D implies X_i subseteq D

for every d-closed set D. Then the profiles and all these budgets are
jointly feasible exactly when

    B=V\d(V\C) is nonempty.                         (one-color)

The s-fold of all variables in B is a witness. To see necessity, prune
every non-s-owned gate of a purported word to a child retaining all
positive profiles. At a root owned by p in R choose its p-positive
child; all other positive profiles pass to both. At other non-s roots
either child works. Such gates act as union at every budget row, so
pruning only decreases their outputs. Retain s-roots, whose monotonicity
preserves this output inequality and whose positive foreign profiles
require both children. The resulting word is an s-fold on a nonempty
support B' subseteq C and still satisfies all budgets.

An s-fold on B' omits the observing winner at query i exactly when

    B' intersects X_i implies B' intersects P_i.

Thus V\B' is d-closed and contains V\C. It contains d(V\C), so
B' subseteq B and B is nonempty. Conversely this largest B has the
same implication property by closure of its complement, proving the
claimed witness. If the required budgets are all captures of one
given owner closure c_s, simply take d=c_s. A proper subfamily instead
uses its generated Horn closure, not the entire supplied c_s.

More generally, the same pruning keeps only gates whose owners occur
among the queries and leaves in C. With several repeated query colors,
the remaining word can still have arbitrary alternation; neither of
the two closed end cases above settles that mixed case.

Now fix an observing owner r and a finite query index set J. Give
each i an owner s_i!=r and variable sets X_i,P_i. Require

    X_i intersect P_i = empty;
    X_i subseteq c_(s_i)(P_i).                       (2)

For each i require that A intersect P_i can be covered by a selection
of displayed blocks X_j whose owners s_j are pairwise distinct and
all different from s_i. The selection may be empty. Equivalently,
there is J_i subseteq J such that

    s_j!=s_i for j in J_i;
    j -> s_j is injective on J_i;
    A intersect P_i subseteq UNION_(j in J_i) X_j.  (3)

Assume also

    UNION_i (P_i union X_i) subseteq c_r(A).         (4)

Then the canonical closures of every core operation satisfy

    c_r(A)=c_r(A\UNION_i X_i).                       (FE)

Everything in this implication is given closure data. Condition (3)
is a direct colored cover test among the displayed coalitions; it
supplies no elimination order, occurrence graph, or unknown realizing
object. Several queries may have the same owner. Each individual
cover uses at most one query of each other owner.

Take the neutral residual on

    U=A union UNION_i (P_i union X_i).

Assumption (4) makes its r-profile positive at A. At row q_i, put I
on X_i, the s_i atom on P_i, and N_r elsewhere. The capture condition
in (2) and simultaneous cofactor independence imply that q_i omits r.
Replacing X_i by N_r leaves the same output, and then all inputs omit r.

Induct on a word satisfying that positive profile and all the row budgets.
At an r-root every budget passes to both children, and one child is
A-positive. At a foreign root u both children are A-positive. Every
budget q_j with s_j!=u passes directly, because that query has no atomic
u input. For an index i with s_i=u, neither child can have its u-profile
positive at P_i. Otherwise use its two positive profiles r:A and u:P_i,
together with the already-passed budgets indexed by J_i. Those query
owners are distinct and different from r,u. Condition (3) covers the
intersection of the two positive coalitions, contradicting the
rainbow-budget projection lemma. Thus no u-profile blocker exists at
any u-colored query, and all those budgets pass too. This also covers
owners absent from the index family. Induction makes the r-profile
positive at A\UNION_i X_i: at an r-root one positive child suffices,
and at every foreign root both are positive. A leaf satisfying all
budgets must lie in that set.

Consequently U subseteq c_r(A\UNION_i X_i). In particular A is
contained there, so closure idempotence and monotonicity give (FE).
If U or J is empty the identity is immediate.

With one query per owner, condition (3) simplifies to

    A intersect P_s subseteq UNION_(t!=s) X_t
        for every query owner s.                   (5)

If A\UNION_i X_i is empty while A is nonempty, looplessness makes
(2)-(4) impossible for a genuine core system.

The two-owner block case requires each intersection to be covered by
the other block. It gives the following three-owner absorption law.
For distinct owners r,s,t, distinct heads x,y,z, and a context L
excluding those heads,

    y in c_r(L union {x,z}),
    x in c_s({y,z}), z in c_t({x,y})
        imply {x,y,z} subseteq c_r(L).               (C3)

Choose A=L union {x,z}, X_s={x}, P_s={y,z}, X_t={z}, P_t={x,y}.
The premise puts both queries' sets inside c_r(A), while their cover
conditions are A intersect P_s={z} and A intersect P_t={x}.
Thus (FE) gives c_r(A)=c_r(L), and c_r(A) already contains all
three heads. This proves (C3).

This is a closed necessary elimination law, with arbitrary block sizes
and arbitrarily many queries at a fixed owner count. It is not complete
for joint pure-port realization, as the eight-variable example below
shows even at three owners.

### Hereditary profiles and elementary composition follow

For loopless owner closures, (FE) already implies hereditary
common-source admissibility. Let U be nonempty and suppose coalitions
E_s subseteq U span U at their respective owners. If their common
intersection were empty, fix an observing owner r and use

    A=E_r;
    P_s=E_s, X_s=U\E_s for every s!=r.

Each X_s is captured from P_s. Their premises and captured blocks
lie in U subseteq c_r(E_r). Moreover

    A intersect P_s subseteq UNION_(t!=r,s) X_t,

because a point violating this cover would lie in every E_t. Thus
(FE) gives c_r(E_r)=c_r(empty)=empty, contradicting that it contains
U. The spanning profiles on U are monotone and normalized by the
closure laws and looplessness. Their common-source condition therefore
makes them an admissible singleton-profile tuple. This holds for every
nonempty U, without separately imposing hereditary versions of (FE).

The elementary cross-value composition law follows as well. In fact
the stronger simultaneous form holds:

    P subseteq c_r(E)\E implies c_s(P) subseteq c_r(E)
        for every owner s.                         (6)

For s=r this is closure idempotence. For s!=r take any
j in c_s(P) outside E union P, put A=E union {j}, and use the single
query X={j}, premise P, owner s. Its required cover is empty, since
A intersect P=empty. Also P union {j} subseteq c_r(A). Thus (FE)
gives c_r(E union {j})=c_r(E), proving j in c_r(E). Points already
in E union P satisfy the conclusion directly. Taking P={i} recovers
the elementary composition law from cofactor dependence.

These deductions make hereditary profiles and elementary composition
redundant among the necessary pure-port laws. Nevertheless loopless
closures plus strong (FE) do not characterize joint **pure-port**
realizability: the eight-variable example below satisfies them and
has no common realizing word.

Separately, even pure-port realizability does not make the separable
reconstruction core. The
[complete-head circuit family](complete-head-circuits.md) with three
private nonempty contexts and an additional observing owner has
noncore separable reconstruction but exactly the pure closures of its
explicit core minorant. It therefore satisfies every necessary law
on joint pure ports, including (FE), as well as pair-cut and reciprocal
intersection. Full guard compatibility retains information beyond
this pure-port subproblem.

### Independent bases and conditional foreign closures

Call B r-independent when b notin c_r(B\{b}) for every b in B.
For such B put U=c_r(B), D=U\B, and, for each s!=r, define on B

    d_s(E)=c_s(E union D) intersect B.                 (IB)

These are closures even without (FE) or foreign closedness of U.
Extensivity and monotonicity are immediate. If G=c_s(E union D), then
D union d_s(E) subseteq G, so d_s(d_s(E)) subseteq G intersect B=d_s(E);
extensivity gives the reverse inclusion. In particular this argument
applies when checking a proposed closure tuple. One must not assume that
U is foreign-closed: the actual core word

    a union (i choice_r a) union (j choice_s a)

has c_r({a})={a,i} while c_s({a,i})={a,i,j}.

**Independent-base form of (FE).** A loopless closure tuple satisfies
strong (FE) exactly when, for every r-independent B, the conditional
closures (IB) admit no configuration with the following properties:

* queries i have owners s_i!=r and sets X_i,Q_i subseteq B;
* X_i subseteq d_(s_i)(Q_i), X_i intersect Q_i=empty;
* for every i, Q_i is covered by X_j from a selection of queries having
  distinct owners, all different from s_i;
* UNION_i X_i is nonempty.

For necessity, lift each premise to P_i=Q_i union D. All queried sets
lie in c_r(B), the rainbow covers are unchanged, and (FE) would give
c_r(B)=c_r(B\UNION_i X_i). This contradicts independence.
Conversely, if (FE) fails at A, choose an inclusion-minimal B subseteq A
with c_r(B)=c_r(A). It is r-independent. Set

    Y_i=X_i intersect B, Q_i=P_i intersect B.

The original premises lie in c_r(A)=B union D, so
Y_i subseteq c_(s_i)(Q_i union D) intersect B. Disjointness and the
rainbow covers restrict to B. Some Y_i is nonempty: otherwise
B subseteq A\UNION_i X_i would contradict the failure of (FE).
Thus these restricted blocks form the forbidden configuration.

**Necessary conditional profile compatibility.** If (FE) holds, every
d_s is loopless and their spanning profiles are common-source admissible
on every nonempty retained L subseteq B. To see this, suppose coalitions
A_s subseteq L satisfy L subseteq d_s(A_s), but have empty common
intersection. Use one query per foreign owner, with

    X_s=L\A_s, Q_s=A_s.

Each A_s is covered by the other X_t, since their common intersection
is empty. Their head union is L, contradicting the independent-base
form. Taking L={x}, A_s=empty for one owner and A_t=L for the others
also excludes x in d_s(empty). Monotonicity and normalization of the
retained spanning profiles then follow from the closure laws.

Conversely this hereditary profile condition excludes configurations
with at most one query per owner. Put L=UNION_s X_s and A_s=L\X_s,
using A_s=L for absent query owners. Since Q_s subseteq L\X_s and
X_s subseteq d_s(Q_s), every A_s spans L, while their intersection is
empty. Whether the condition also excludes all configurations with
repeated query owners is settled below for two foreign owners. The
implication fails with three foreign owners, by the
[eight-variable construction](feedback-three-colors.md). Thus its
two-color scope is essential.

### Two-color compression

Let d_1,d_2 be arbitrary closures on a finite set B, allowing loops.
The following conditions are equivalent:

1. For every nonempty L subseteq B and coalitions A_1,A_2 subseteq L,
   if L subseteq d_1(A_1) and L subseteq d_2(A_2), then
   A_1 intersect A_2 is nonempty.
2. Disjoint C,E subseteq B with C subseteq d_1(E) and
   E subseteq d_2(C) must both be empty.
3. There is no nonempty-head feedback configuration for these two
   closures: queries have X_i subseteq d_(s_i)(Q_i),
   X_i intersect Q_i=empty, and each Q_i is covered by at most one
   opposite-owner head, with UNION_i X_i nonempty.

An empty premise may use the empty cover. In particular a loop
x in d_s(empty) gives a forbidden configuration with the single query
X={x}, Q=empty. It also contradicts condition 1 on L={x}, using an
empty coalition for s and L for the other owner. Thus each of the
three conditions excludes loops. For condition 2 this follows by
taking one of C,E empty. Once loops are excluded, conditions 1 and 2
are equivalent by taking L=C union E, or the union of two disjoint
spanning coalitions.

A failure of condition 1 also gives a two-query feedback configuration
directly. From disjoint spanning coalitions A_1,A_2 on L take

    X_s=L\A_s, Q_s=A_s, s in {1,2}.

Their captured blocks cover each other's premises and their union is L.
This includes empty coalitions and proves that condition 3 implies
condition 1.

For the remaining direction assume condition 1 and suppose a feedback
configuration exists. Looplessness makes the premise of every nonempty
head nonempty. Choose an opposite-owner head covering that premise.
It is nonempty too, so following these choices in the finite query
set eventually gives an alternating directed cycle. Replace each
cycle head by the preceding premise, a nonempty subset of that head.
Its own capture and head-premise disjointness remain true, and its
premise is now exactly the next head. Reindex this cycle by
i=0,...,m-1, cyclically, so that

    A_i subseteq d_1(B_i), B_i subseteq d_2(A_(i+1));
    A_i intersect B_i = B_i intersect A_(i+1) = empty.  (AC)

All these sets are nonempty. Define two ordered activation seeds:

    P_1=UNION_(i=0,...,m-1)
          (B_i \ UNION_(h<i) A_h);
    P_2=UNION_(j=0,...,m-1)
          (A_((j+1) mod m) \ UNION_(h<j) B_h).       (AS)

The d_1 rules activate in order 0,...,m-1 from P_1: each B_i is
contained in the seed together with the previously generated A_h.
Likewise the d_2 rules activate in that order from P_2. Hence

    UNION_i A_i subseteq d_1(P_1),
    UNION_i B_i subseteq d_2(P_2).

Since P_1 is contained in the B union and P_2 in the A union, this gives

    P_2 subseteq d_1(P_1), P_1 subseteq d_2(P_2).    (MC)

The seeds are disjoint. If x belonged to both, choose i,j witnessing
its membership in B_i and A_((j+1) mod m), with the prefix exclusions
in (AS). The second prefix exclusion implies i>=j. If j<m-1, the
first implies i<=j+1. Then i=j contradicts B_j intersect A_(j+1)=empty,
while i=j+1 contradicts B_(j+1) intersect A_(j+1)=empty. If j=m-1,
the first exclusion forces i=0, contrary to i>=m-1 when m>=2.
For m=1 the disjointness is just A_0 intersect B_0=empty.

Both seeds are nonempty: P_1 contains B_0 and P_2 contains
A_(1 mod m). Thus (MC) supplies disjoint spanning coalitions on
the nonempty set P_1 union P_2, contradicting condition 1. This
completes the two-color compression theorem at every cycle length.

### An exact three-owner conditional test for strong feedback

Suppose the original owner set has exactly three elements. A loopless
tuple c satisfies full strong (FE) if and only if the following
condition holds. For each observing owner r and every r-independent B,
put D=c_r(B)\B and call the other owners s,t. Then

    C,E subseteq B, C intersect E=empty,
    C subseteq c_s(E union D),
    E subseteq c_t(C union D)
        imply C=E=empty.                            (K3)

Indeed the independent-base form characterizes (FE) by absence of
feedback for the conditional closures d_s,d_t on B. Two-color
compression makes this equivalent to condition 2 above, and (IB)
translates it exactly to (K3). One of C,E is allowed to be empty;
these cases exclude conditional foreign loops and cannot be dropped.

Equivalently, at exactly three owners, strong (FE) says that the
tuple of conditional foreign spanning profiles is common-source
admissible on every nonempty retained subset of every independent
base. No repeated-query condition remains beyond that hereditary test.

### Strong feedback is insufficient for joint pure ports

Take just the three owners A,B,C and their eight-variable closures
d_A,d_B,d_C from [the three-color feedback example](feedback-three-colors.md).
They are loopless. The conditional independent-base analysis there
shows hereditary common-source compatibility for the two foreign
closures at every observer and independent base. The exact three-owner
test above therefore proves that this tuple satisfies full strong (FE).

It nevertheless has no common core word. If a word T realized these
pure ports, interpret the same syntax after adjoining a fresh owner rho.
Every old-owner trace is unchanged: its Q_r coordinate uses exactly
the same owned multiplication and foreign joins. Looplessness implies
that T uses all eight variables. Since T has no rho-owned node, its
rho trace is the join of all eight inputs, whose pure closure is the
identity. Thus T would realize the extended tuple

    c_A=d_A, c_B=d_B, c_C=d_C, c_rho=id.

But the six queries in the cited construction have head union V and
rainbow covers using A,B,C. With observing owner rho and base V,
strong (FE) would give c_rho(V)=c_rho(empty), or V=empty.
This contradiction rules out T. The argument uses stability of pure
traces under an unused-owner extension, not stability of the original
winner-valued operation or of its separable reconstruction.

Consequently a complete pure-port criterion must impose compatibility
with this fresh identity coordinate as well as the feedback laws at
the supplied owners. Strong (FE) at the original owner set alone is
insufficient, including when that set has exactly three elements.

Even compatibility with every unused identity owner is insufficient.
The [nine-variable profile obstruction](feedback-profile-obstruction.md)
passes all these feedback tests for every fixed owner count K>=3.
Its six captures nevertheless force a singleton profile missing from
the supplied closure data. This is a profile-persistence obstruction,
not a malformed cofactor semantics: the reconstruction has exact
dependence closures and hereditary omission independence.

## A counterexample to the previous closure laws

Specialize to owners r=0,s=1,t=2 and variables V={x,y,z,W}. Define

    c_0(E)=E union ({y} if {x,z,W} subseteq E else empty);
    c_1(E)=E union ({x} if {y,z} subseteq E else empty);
    c_2(E)=E union ({z} if {x,y} subseteq E else empty);
    c_u(E)=E for every other owner u.                (7)

These are loopless closures. Their source-separable reconstruction is

    F(w)=W
         union (x unless y=z={1})
         union (y unless x=z=W={0})
         union (z unless x=y={2}).                   (8)

Here a variable on the right denotes its whole input menu. An omitted
term contributes the empty set. The unconditional W makes F nonempty.
The three trigger patterns are mutually incompatible, so at most one
head is suppressed at a valuation.

The earlier tests all hold:

* **Pair-cut (P).** If one rule fires, using its added head to fire a
  differently colored rule would require another premise shared by both
  rules to have two different owners. The disjoint premise sets in (P)
  cannot do this. Hence no new second head escapes the existing closure
  union.
* **Reciprocal intersection (CI), equivalently (C2) given (P).** The
  0/1 reciprocal pair shares z, the 0/2 pair shares x, and the 1/2 pair
  shares y, after removing the two heads.
* **Hereditary common-source (H).** In any retained set containing W,
  every spanning coalition contains W, since no closure captures it.
  Without W, only the 1/2 pair remains. On {x,y,z} its spanning
  coalitions both contain y; on smaller nonempty sets all restricted
  closures are identities.
* **Exact functional dependence.** Fixing x=z=W={0} makes F constantly
  {0}, independently of y. Fixing y=z={1} makes F={1} union W,
  independently of x. Fixing x=y={2} makes F={2} union W,
  independently of z. All remaining one-color essentials have their
  pure-source probes as witnesses. Thus (7) are the actual cofactor
  dependence closures of F. This also supplies omission independence,
  full old-value outcast and their neutral restrictions by the arguments
  in [cofactor dependence](cofactor-dependence-closures.md).

Its exact singleton profiles are xzW at owner 0, yzW at owner 1, xyW at
owner 2, and unanimity at every other owner. In particular they are
admissible, with common source W. On all-ambiguous inputs F is the input
union, so every variable is essential.

These closures violate (C3): its premises hold with L={W}, while
c_0({W})={W}. Thus they cannot be jointly realized even as pure ports
of a nonseparable core term; in particular F is noncore.

## All proper input reductions are core

Deleting x,y or z neutrally leaves input union. Deleting W leaves
the shared-context two-rule operation

    y union (x star_1 (y star_2 z))
      union (z star_2 (y star_1 x)).                  (9)

All smaller nonempty neutral faces are therefore core as well. The empty
face is zero.

There are six pair-identification minors. Writing U for the identified
input, the following primitive-choice/derived-union words realize them:

| identification | word |
|---|---|
| x=y=U | W union U union (z star_2 U) |
| y=z=U | W union U union (x star_1 U) |
| x=z=U | W union U union (y star_0 (U star_1 W)) |
| W=x=U | U union (y star_0 (U star_2 z)) union (z star_2 (U star_0 y)) |
| W=z=U | U union (x star_1 (U star_0 y)) union (y star_0 (U star_1 x)) |
| W=y=U | U union (x star_1 (U star_2 z)) union (z star_2 (U star_1 x)) |

The last three use the two-rule common-context construction. Every
proper identification minor factors through one of these six and is
therefore core. Idempotence, essential support and the canonical factor
recovery argument from [block decomposition](block-decomposition.md)
then show that F has no proper disjoint idempotent decomposition: its
outer diagonal minor and its inner neutral factors would all be core,
making F core as well.

The obstruction therefore survives all proper neutral and identification
reductions. It does not say that such reductions or the new law (C3)
form a complete criterion. It refutes sufficiency of the preceding
pairwise laws even after all those reductions have been checked.
