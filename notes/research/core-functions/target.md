# Characterization target and the nonfinite-relatedness theorem

The original target is a short structural characterization of jointly
realizable canonical source guards, comparable to the monotonicity and
common-source conditions for singleton profiles. Row deletion is exact
recursive recognition; it does not replace recursion by closed conditions.

The concrete finite-relatedness question is now settled negatively.
Fix any finite role set K≥3. For every m≥0, there is an operation

    F : A_K^(m+3) → A_K

such that every m valuations have a common core-term interpolant, yet F is
not a core term operation. K stays fixed as m grows. The proof treats all
ambient role sets with three distinguished owners and proves each patch on
every valuation, including ambiguous values and additional owners.

The complete statements are `Paper.core_not_finitely_related` and
`Paper.core_no_finite_relation_basis`. The latter says explicitly that, for
any finite family of core-invariant relations, some nonterm operation
preserves every member of the family. `Core/RelationalInterpolation`
identifies m-point interpolation with preservation of every m-ary invariant
relation; `Core/PatchInterpolation` proves its antitonicity in m.

## The cyclic construction

Take n=m+3 inputs with cyclic successor i↦i+1, and distinct owners a,b,c.
Normally F returns the union of all inputs. On the region with input i
exactly {c}, input i+1 neither {b} nor {c}, and all other inputs {b}, it
returns {b,c}. The n exceptional regions are disjoint.

Deleting any one exceptional region gives a core operation. The proof cuts
the cycle into a path and separates Boolean intervals by prefix disjunctions,
nonadjacent-pair conjunctions and cardinality thresholds. Greatest singleton
profile completions turn these Boolean separators into actual core terms,
whose finite union is exactly the deletion patch on every input.
Each valuation disagrees with at most one deletion patch. Any m valuations
therefore share a patch, since n>m.

The full cycle violates cyclic persistence. At its forward rows, with c at i,
a at i+1 and b elsewhere, F omits a; at the opposite rows it includes a.
Every core term that includes a at an opposite row must include a at some
forward row. In the observer-a quotient, core terms expand into finite unions
of lattice polynomials on b<a<c. Each polynomial induces a monotone Boolean
function. Vanishing derivatives on all forward edges force vanishing on the
opposite edges by finite cyclic propagation.

Definitions are in `Definitions/CoreCrown`; the proof runs through
`CrownBoolean`, `CrownPatch`, `CrownBasePatch`, `CrownRotation`, `CrownPatches`,
`CyclicPersistence` and `CrownObstruction`. `CrownFullSyntax` also proves the
counterexample idempotent, so idempotent extraction rules out full-language
realizations. Thus the expansions by constants and role switches are not
finitely related either. `FiniteRelationObstruction` takes
the maximum arity of an arbitrary finite relation family, including empty
families and nullary relations. All proof closures use only propext,
Classical.choice and Quot.sound. The sole finite observer-table check uses
kernel `decide`, with a general induction supplying the all-term theorem.

Nonfinite relatedness excludes characterization by a fixed finite family of
invariant relations. It does not exclude every possible short structural law
with unbounded quantification.

## Closed obstructions and the remaining target

The report now proves three further structural results. One scalar observer
on atomic inputs is realizable exactly when its full interval is positive and
no positive interval is reversed by the preorder generated by inclusions and
zero-interval reversals (`Report.core_atomic_observer`). The three-owner view
is the classical four-element bichain with observer union added; even its pure
Boolean-projection fiber recovers the entire union-expanded clone one arity
lower (`Report.bichain_projection_fiber` and its uniqueness clause).

Separate views do not amalgamate automatically. Three explicit core
polynomials specify every observer's complete view of a genuine nonempty
operation, with unanimity singleton profiles, but no core term matches it even
on three atomic rows (`Report.core_observer_amalgamation`). Thus scalar
criteria plus admissible singleton profiles cannot give the full answer.

The invariant relations themselves have a closed presentation. Indexed
colored clutters satisfying boundary incidences and a no-trap cut axiom give
exactly all core-invariant relations (`Report.core_closed_hypergraph_basis`).
Preservation yields a universal criterion for full operations and a condition
on the positive/negative bits of canonical guard tables
(`Report.core_hypergraph_operations`, `Report.core_hypergraph_guards`). Its
statement contains no recursion or supplied elimination order. This is a
complete infinite obstruction basis; it still quantifies over auxiliary
clutter presentations. The original goal of a short intrinsic condition
*directly on jointly realizable guards* remains active. The closed basis
should not be described as having met that stronger simplicity target.

[Further failed approaches](global-characterization.md) record why atomic
polarization and simple coherence schemas do not remove this remaining gap.

There are now closed positive classifications beyond read-once trees.
[Additive owner closures](additive-closure-characterization.md), where a
capture always has a single predecessor, have an exact two-law colored
preorder criterion. Component spines construct every realizable guard
family in this class, with source-linear minorants and greatest free
completion attained by polynomial-size primitive words. For raw monomial
guards, first check agreement of
the pure-owner factors across observers, then the two colored arrow laws.
This settles that case, not nonadditive closures or guard disjunctions.
[Loopless closures classify two-owner CNFs](closure-core-operations.md),
and their [globally minimal operations are precisely matroid CNFs](minimal-cnf-matroids.md).
[Canonical colored closures](separable-closures.md) also exist for every
core operation, but their [separable product hull need not be core](separable-hull-failure.md),
even when those closures came from one actual core word.

For free independent-status data, [one pattern has a complete clause theory](free-pattern-interpolation.md),
while [two patterns already require further compatibility](free-pattern-amalgamation.md).
The ternary quota over three old owners has a complete thirteen-element
free-extension classification. These results locate concrete parts of the
compatibility problem; neither the closure reduction nor that fixed fibre
is the requested all-arity characterization of ordinary prime guards.

The [unit-quota poison theorem](quota-poison-graphs.md) now gives a closed
mixed-pattern criterion at unbounded active-owner count, but only for
its specified query projection. [Mixed closure outcast](mixed-closure-outcast.md)
now holds at every partial coloring for source-separable core functions:
for each full coloring, the union of the color-restricted owner closures
is itself idempotent. A two-erasure lemma handles saturated fibers without
identifying a chosen word's fresh selections with the canonical free set.
The resulting [canonical common-carrier extension](canonical-separable-extension.md)
matches every old observer face, preserves the ternary outcast invariant,
and bounds all term extensions, without a proof of its own term attainment.
The [four-variable counterexample](separable-closure-counterexample.md)
shows these laws are insufficient for synthetic closure data, even with
hereditary profile admissibility, nonempty neutral residuals, global
omission and core identification minors. Its canonical full types all
pass the known individual tests; a neutral facet is still noncore.
This requires any further criterion to respect neutral restrictions;
global tests cannot substitute for their hereditary versions. The wrapper
also has a known disjoint factorization, so it supplies no obstruction
to a criterion explicitly reduced to prime factors and all their faces.
The [mixed-color global minimum](mixed-cnf-minimum.md) shows why maximal
admissible profiles and disjoint matroid decompositions cannot describe
all minima. The complete compatibility of arbitrary source guards remains
the objective.

The [canonical column-sound clutter](canonical-sound-barrier.md) fails the
no-trap law at every arity at least two, already on one isolated-source
probe per input. Its greatest admissible subfamily exists, but identifying
that subfamily with a safety kernel would only repackage recognition.

## Exact exponential locality threshold

`Report.core_exponential_locality` strengthens the quantitative obstruction.
For every finite role set K>=3 and d>=2, a function on 2d paired inputs is
2^d-point core-interpolable, is not (2^d+1)-point interpolable, is idempotent,
and has no realization even with winner constants and internal role switches.
The generic interval and crown-frame proofs hold on full winner-valued inputs,
including additional roles. The Gray enumeration is constructed and proved in
Lean; no Hamiltonicity theorem is assumed.

The matching patches alone give 2^d-1 locality. The sharp statement adds a
common collapsed cover to obtain 2^d+1 core patches, with at most one possible
mismatch at each valuation. A concrete set of matching atomic rows and one
opposite row proves the upper failure. See [exponential-crowns.md](exponential-crowns.md).
This excludes polynomial-in-arity locality bounds; it does not provide the
remaining direct structural criterion on jointly realizable guards.

The same explicit family has a further mathematical strengthening:
[every proper identification minor is core, every proper neutral residual
is input union, and the operation is prime under disjoint idempotent
composition](prime-obstructions.md). Thus canonical block decomposition
cannot leave only bounded-arity exceptional cases. Its smaller core
operations already agree on every overlap, yet admit no common global
core realization. This strengthening is a written research proof; it does
not add a Lean claim to the existing locality theorem.

## Profile relations and one bound

The owner-colored Boolean profile algebra has a complete restricted Horn
presentation: forced bits, same-owner implications and rainbow conflicts.
One output bound together with rainbow positive profile requirements admits
a closed input criterion and projection/two-leaf witnesses. However, a
four-variable example shows that even one bound creates new conflicts between
owners: an admissible full profile tuple can have a safe witness for each
owner separately and no common safe witness. See [profile-bounds.md](profile-bounds.md).
These results do not settle the remaining direct guard characterization.

## Complete bounded profiles and arbitrary row obstructions

`Report.core_bounded_profiles` gives a short closed criterion for all exact
singleton-profile tables together with one upper winner bound. The right
side has only top bits, a direct same-owner separation test and a direct
full-owner rainbow test. For an unrestricted bound it is exactly the old
admissibility criterion. This does not characterize simultaneous full-value
source guards; see [profile-bounds.md](profile-bounds.md).

`Report.core_omission_independence` shows that all inputs containing an
omitted winner may be changed arbitrarily without changing any output.
`Report.core_negative_budget_obstructions` uses this to construct atomic
staircases with n constraints, no joint core witness, and a projection witness
for every proper subfamily. Thus even upper bounds alone have no fixed row
Helly number. See [negative-budgets.md](negative-budgets.md).

The canonical-prime and extremal-polarization shortcuts are also refuted:
[free-polarization](free-polarization/free-polarization-extrema.md) preserves
small standalone certificates. The full direct characterization remains active.

## Neutral faces and their joint compatibility

`Report.core_neutral_elimination` proves that deleting an input through
winnerwise neutral sections leaves zero or a genuine lower-arity core
operation. The full projection-profile fiber is exactly the lower-arity core
lifted by reset, plus projection, with a unique nonzero parameter.
`Report.core_crown_neutral_sections` shows that every proper neutral face of
the crown obstruction is nevertheless just input union. Thus even checking
all proper faces does not characterize the clone.

The stronger atomic/neutral-face gluing rule is also refuted in research:
one four-variable operation has a common core atomic realizer and compatible
core neutral faces, yet its observer trace is the nonterm essential extension
of AB OR AC OR BD. The example also satisfies the profile, provenance and
omission laws. Its written proof and finite checks are separate from the
kernel-checked elimination theorem. Details and the constructive compiler
are in [ghost-elimination.md](ghost-elimination.md); the
[standalone counterexample](face-gluing/README.md) supplies explicit witnesses
and a checker without a clone-enumeration assumption.
