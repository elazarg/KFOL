# Review of the rewritten manuscript (K-FOG: Role-Owned Choice and Dependent Interaction)

Reviewed: working tree of 2026-10-28, 13:06 (main.tex and its root-level inputs; 18 pages).
Build is clean: one underfull hbox and no undefined references. `make check` passes,
and the archive manifest verifies.

## Verdict

The rewrite fixes the earlier correctness problems by removing nearly all the
earlier claims. What remains is essentially correct. It is not a contribution, though,
and it is no longer the paper you described:

- **It has no logic.** There are no connectives, no negation, no consequence relation,
  no proof rules and no cut. The K-player quantifier appears only as a binder in a
  session-type-like script language. "Truth" is replaced by "objectives observe
  executions" (interpretations.tex:47-64). That means the paper avoids the
  semantics question rather than answering it.
- **The paper itself proves that the core adds nothing.** Prop. definability
  (composition.tex:274-288) shows that every construction is a conservative
  definitional extension of the data calculus D. Every theorem in §§2-4 is either an
  unfolding of a definition or a direct consequence of D's metatheory.
- **The core is prior art, and the closest instance is uncited.** Toninho and Yoshida,
  *Certifying data in multiparty session types* (JLAMP 90, 2017; LNCS 9600, 2016),
  already project value-dependent global types to local dependent types for each
  endpoint. They also handle the hard case: participants with partial views, using
  singleton types and bundling of unknown data. This paper's public core broadcasts
  every value to every role, so it is the easy special case of their problem. It
  also lacks their recursion and branching.
- **The disclosure layer has no semantics connecting audiences to what players
  observe.** Its three results are a tautology, a congruence of function
  application, and Aumann's 1976 characterisation of common knowledge.
- **None of the candidate-novel material from notes/assessment.md is present.** That
  material was the free median algebra FM(K), realization, the equivariance no-go,
  and the rational-play semantics. Worse, the carrier choice in (4) rules out the
  rational-play (SPE) direction (see M5).

## Major issues

**M1. There is no statement of contribution, and every theorem is routine.**
The intro (intro.tex:24-32) lists Abramsky, Xi–Wu, Toninho–Caires–Pfenning and
Wu–Xi, then says "we use these constructions as the basis for one explicit account".
It never says what is new. The theorems break down as follows:

- Lemma substitution (core.tex:201-217) is a syntactic identity, because L_i is a
  meta-level function.
- Thm interaction, Cor preservation and Thm termination (interaction.tex:61-130):
  Run_G(p) is a D-term (Prop definability), so these follow from typing in D and
  from D's strong normalization and confluence.
- Thm equivariance (interaction.tex:162-176) is a renaming of indices.
- Thm associativity (composition.tex:236-259) is Σ-associativity plus currying, up to
  extensional equality.
- Thm adequacy (interpretations.tex:27-39) is the standard reduction from
  strategies to plans of action, i.e. the reduced normal form.
- Prop classical correspondence (interpretations.tex:80-93) is Hintikka's evaluation
  game.
- Prop permutation ≠ complement (interpretations.tex:99-106) is a counting argument.

A referee will read the "Theorem" labels as padding.

**M2. The core is the broadcast special case of known dependent multiparty session types.**
Besides Toninho–Yoshida, the trace type (9) is exactly Escardó–Oliva's `Path`
(*Higher-order games with dependent types*, Def. 2.3: `Path [] = 1`,
`Path (X :: Xf) = (x:X) × Path(Xf x)`). Their paper is cited (foundations.tex:154-159)
only as "complementary". The two-role case of (4) is Hancock–Setzer's interaction
structures, where the command side is Σ and the response side is Π
(*Interactive programs in dependent type theory*, CSL 2000). That is also uncited.
Compared with this prior art, the only new ingredient in the public core is the
owner annotation together with broadcast, and broadcast is the simplification.

**M3. The disclosure semantics is not defined, and audiences never enter a theorem.**
- (20)-(21) define store transitions. But there is no grammar for disclosure scripts,
  and no definition of histories, executions or choice occurrences m. Example (23)
  uses a script syntax that appears nowhere else.
- The observation maps o_m are *declared* separately by "the specification"
  (disclosure.tex:35-38). They are required to *include* authorized cells, but nothing
  forces them to *exclude* unauthorized ones.
- As a result, the audience field S_c plays no role in any result. The central
  sealed-bid claim ("his binding policy has no direct input for a", disclosure.tex:63-64)
  is asserted, not proved.

Specific results:

- **Prop binding (disclosure.tex:70-85).** Its last sentence quantifies over "executions
  that preserve its original binding", which makes it a tautology. Also, (21) removes
  c from Ω, so the same execution cannot open c twice. The property that actually
  deserves the name *binding* is not stated: two executions that agree up to Bind(c)
  open c to the same value, whatever happens between bind and publication. It holds
  here and is one line to prove. The *hiding* property needs o_m to be derived from ρ:
  the policies of non-audience roles before Pub(c) factor through the history with c's
  value erased. It is the one result that would make this section say something.
- **Prop uniformity (disclosure.tex:96-103)** says that o(h) = o(h') implies
  f(o(h)) = f(o(h')).
- **Thm common knowledge (disclosure.tex:151-164)** is Aumann's characterisation: an
  event measurable with respect to every partition is constant on the cells of their
  meet. It is also an *iff*, not merely the "sufficient condition" claimed in
  abstract.tex:16. If p = p(h) is common knowledge at every h, then in particular each
  i knows it, which is (25). Cite Aumann 1976, *Agreeing to disagree*.

**M4. Boundary sequencing quietly introduces simultaneous moves.**
- At End⟨R_i⟩ each role's return is computed from the trace alone, and the boundary
  (17) publishes all returns at once. So `End⟨R_i⟩ ▷ H` is a simultaneous-move stage.
- No script can express this. Re-encoding the returns as owned choices would let later
  roles see earlier returns.
- This is the only imperfect-information construct in the "public" core, and the text
  doesn't mention it (composition.tex:185-188 says the opposite in spirit).
- The composite is also not a script, so §4's "composition" is a new conversation
  language, not cut. That is fine, but say it.

**M5. Σ-plans fix the reduced strategic form, which excludes subgame perfection.**
- The Σ in (4) discards a role's behaviour after its own counterfactual moves
  (core.tex:185-193 notes this).
- Subgame-perfect equilibrium, and anything else that tests sequential rationality,
  needs behaviour at every history. That is Escardó–Oliva's
  `Strategy (X :: Xf) = X × ((x:X) → Strategy (Xf x))`, not Σ.
- So the claim that "solution concepts are observations of that object"
  (abstract.tex:20-21, interpretations.tex:61-64) is true for Nash equilibrium but
  false for SPE when the object is a profile of endpoint terms.
- If rational-play semantics remains a goal, the endpoint carrier has to change, or the
  paper has to say that it cannot.

## Minor issues

- **m1. The owner-sequence restriction is mostly cosmetic** (core.tex:130-135).
  D has universes (core.tex:103). Interleaving K binders per level, with each move type
  set to `if owner(h)=i then A(h) else Unit`, encodes any bounded-depth tree with
  history-dependent owners, up to isomorphism of endpoint types. The real limitation
  is bounded depth (no recursion). State that instead.
- **m2. Assumptions on D are unclear.** (10) needs strong Σ (or large elimination on
  Tr), and core.tex:92-98 only says "dependent sums". Since the paper cites Herbelin's
  strong-Σ degeneracy, it should say which kind it means.
- **m3. The reduction relation for Thm termination is not fully defined.** Rule (14)
  needs canonical heads. The non-canonical variant (interaction.tex:90-93) is prose,
  and the "interleaving" clause of the theorem quantifies over a combined relation
  that the paper never defines. Via definability, the theorem only needs SN and
  confluence of the expanded D-term.
- **m4. πp and the direction of π_* are never defined** (interaction.tex:159-166).
  The check uses (πp)_j = p_{π⁻¹(j)}.
- **m5. Example (23) clashes notation.** Roles A, B are also data types A, B, and a
  value `:= a` written inside a script conflates the script with a transition label.
- **m6. foundations.tex:118-123 asserts an exact match with Abramsky's L_A without
  proof.** Either prove it (fixed broadcast prefix, induction) or weaken it.
- **m7. The finite check tests two Python interpreters against each other.** It cannot
  test D-level claims. It's harmless, but development.tex:105-109 overstates what it
  shows.
- **m8. Missing citations.**
  - Toninho–Yoshida 2017 (M2).
  - Hancock–Setzer 2000 (M2).
  - Aumann 1976 (M3).
  - Kuhn 1953 or Rubinstein 1991 for plans versus strategies.
  - Hintikka, for the evaluation game.
  - Canetti–Fischlin 2001: (20)-(21) is the ideal commitment functionality F_COM.
- **m9. Citations verified.**
  - XiWu2023 (arXiv:2309.01317), WuXi2018 (arXiv:1808.00077) and the
    EscardoOliva2023 DOI all resolve correctly.
  - The VegasCore claims match revision 0a534aa: `Vegas/Source/Basic.lean` has the
    `Open` index of commitments, `PublicationResult` failure results, and
    `ret` requiring no outstanding commitment.
  - The VegasCore entry is a self-citation credited to "VegasCore contributors".
    Decide whether that is deliberate.

## What would make this a paper

1. **The hiding/binding theorem for commit–reveal in a dependent setting.** Derive o_m
   from ρ and S_c, then prove that non-audience policies factor through erasure of c
   until Pub(c). Allow other roles' *types* to depend on c only through an abstract
   package, as disclosure.tex:49-53 already proposes. A dependent type theory in which a
   variable is bound before it is visible, and visibility later changes without
   rebinding, is the one plausibly new type-theoretic object in this draft. It is the
   problem Toninho–Yoshida solve with singleton types for third parties, but with the
   dimension of time added.
2. **Or return to the K-ary semantics.** Put back the results that are new (FM(K),
   realization, no-go) and the rational-play semantics. Use full-strategy carriers
   (M5) so that SPE is an observation.
3. **Either way**, demote the definitional results to lemmas, add an explicit "new
   versus known" paragraph, and cite Toninho–Yoshida prominently.
