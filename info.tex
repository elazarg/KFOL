
\section{IF-style binders for roles}
\label{subsec:if-binders}
We refine the player binder to carry an \emph{independence set}, in the style of
Independence-Friendly (IF) logic~\cite{HintikkaSandu1989}. The primitive
syntax is
\[
  \Phi \Coloneqq
  \langle \fo_0,\dots,\fo_{K-1} \rangle
  \;\mid\;
  P_i x \setminus V.\,\Phi
\]
where $i \in \{0,\dots,K-1\}$ is the role making the move, $x$ is the chosen
object, and $V$ is a finite set of \emph{previously bound variables from
which this choice must be independent}. Informally, \emph{``player $i$
chooses a value for $x$, and $i$'s choice may not depend on the values
already bound to the variables in $V$.''}

\paragraph{Why variable-level, not role-level.}
An earlier version of this section attached the independence set to
\emph{roles} instead ($P_i x / J$, $J \subseteq \{0,\dots,K-1\}$: ``the
future moves of roles in $J$ must not depend on this choice''), matching the
natural reading of a broadcast being withheld from certain participants.
That grammar is strictly less expressive than what the rest of the paper
needs and, in places, already assumed: the DQBF-hardness reduction of
\Cref{prop:nexptime-if} requires each existential binder $x_j$ --- all owned
by the \emph{same} role --- to exclude an arbitrary, binder-specific subset
$H_j$ of the previously bound universal variables. Role-level exclusion
cannot express this once two binders share an owner, because the number of
distinct exclusion sets available is bounded by the number of role subsets,
independent of how many variables that role has bound; a $K=2$ construction
has only two role-level exclusion sets available ($\emptyset$ or the other
role) no matter how many existential variables $P_0$ binds. The tensor
combinator $\otimes$ of \Cref{subsubsec:seq-vs-tensor} has the same
requirement and already used variable-level exclusion sets without this
section licensing it. We correct that mismatch here by making variable-level
exclusion the primitive and deriving role-level exclusion from it.

\paragraph{Role-level exclusion, as a derived notation.}
For a formula $\Psi = P_{k_1}z_1\setminus V_1.\,\dots.\,P_{k_n}z_n\setminus
V_n.\,\langle\vec\fo\rangle$, a variable $x$, and a set of roles $J$, define
\[
  \mathrm{hide}(\Psi,x,J) :=
  P_{k_1}z_1\setminus V_1'.\,\dots.\,P_{k_n}z_n\setminus V_n'.\,\langle\vec\fo\rangle,
  \qquad
  V_\ell' := \begin{cases} V_\ell\cup\{x\} & k_\ell\in J\\ V_\ell & k_\ell\notin J.\end{cases}
\]
Then, for $J \subseteq \{0,\dots,K-1\}\setminus\{i\}$,
\[
  P_i x / J.\,\Phi \;:=\; P_i x\setminus\emptyset.\,\mathrm{hide}(\Phi,x,J)
\]
recovers the role-level reading exactly: $x$ is added to the slash-set of
\emph{every} later binder owned by a role in $J$, so no future move of any
role in $J$ can depend on $x$. (The same style of rewriting, run in the
opposite direction --- removing a variable from later slash-sets rather than
adding one --- is $\mathrm{vis\text{-}add}$ in
\Cref{subsubsec:clock-megaphone}.) This is the notation used by the MPST
translation of \Cref{sec:mpst-connection}, whose well-formedness condition
($V\subseteq\mathcal P\setminus\{i\}$, i.e.\ every binder's exclusion set is
already role-uniform) should be read as restricting that translation to
formulas built from this derived form, not as a property of \KFOL\ formulas
in general.

\paragraph{Semantics.}
The semantics of this binder is identical to $P_i x.\,\Phi$,
but it adds a \emph{constraint on valid strategies}.
A strategy profile $\profile$ is \emph{valid} for $\Phi$ if,
for every binder $P_i x \setminus V.\,\Phi'$, the strategy
function $\profile_i$ is independent of $V$.

Formally, for any two assignments $s_1, s_2$ over the free
variables of $P_i x \setminus V.\,\Phi'$,
\[
\bigl(\forall v \notin V,\ s_1(v) = s_2(v)\bigr)
\implies
\profile_i(P_i x \setminus V.\,\Phi', s_1) = \profile_i(P_i x \setminus V.\,\Phi', s_2).
\]
(Note: The implication states that if two assignments agree on all
variables \emph{except} possibly for those in $V$, the strategy must
produce the same choice. This formally captures independence from $V$.)

The definitions of evaluation $\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket$ and forceability $F_i$
(\Cref{def:k-semantics} and \Cref{def:k-force})
are unchanged, but the quantification $\exists \profile_i$
is now restricted to \emph{valid} strategies.

\paragraph{This is a syntactic, not a semantic, guarantee.}
Validity constrains the \emph{strategy function's argument list}: $x$'s
chosen value cannot be computed as a function of $V$. It does not follow
that the \emph{outcome} --- whether $\phi_i$ ends up true --- is independent
of $V$; $\phi_i$ can still relate $x$ and $V$ through the terminal formula
itself, in exactly the way a classical IF-logic formula's truth conditions
can involve two syntactically independent variables through a shared atomic
subformula~\cite{sep-logic-if}. This is not a defect: it is what the
\Cref{prop:nexptime-if} DQBF reduction exploits (the matrix $f$ entangles
$x_j$ and the universal variables it cannot see), and it is the expected,
IF-logic-standard reading. It does mean $\setminus V$ alone gives no
epistemic guarantee that role $i$'s later behavior, or the outcome, is
uncorrelated with $V$ in any stronger sense --- only that $i$'s choice
function cannot name $V$ as an input. A genuinely stronger, more
epistemic notion of independence, closer to a guarantee about what is
knowable rather than only about a function's argument list, would need to be
built from something like \Cref{subsec:prop-annot-binders}'s identifiability
machinery ($\mathrm{Id}_u$), which tracks what is \emph{derivable} from
public commitments rather than only what a binder is syntactically permitted
to name. We have not built this stronger notion or compared its forceability
theory to the one in this paper.

\subsection{Syntax Sugar: Where-Clauses}
\label{subsec:where}
For convenience we allow binders to carry an explicit local condition, in the
style of refined MPST. A binder
\[
  P_i x : \psi.\,\Phi
\]
is required to use only variables that were bound earlier in the prefix and are
visible to player $i$ (i.e.\ not hidden from $i$ by a slash-set). Its meaning is
defined by desugaring to the core calculus as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item $\psi$ is conjoined to player $i$'s terminal component, expressing that
        $i$ is responsible for establishing it;
  \item for every other player $j$, every subformula of $j$'s terminal component
        that depends on $x$ is wrapped under $\psi \rightarrow (\cdot)$,
        expressing that $j$ may rely on $i$ having established $\psi$.
\end{enumerate}
Intuitively, a where-clause records at the point of the move which property the
owner promises to make true, and makes this promise available as an assumption
to later players.
