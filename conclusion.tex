\section{Conclusion}

A role-owned choice has one witness and several endpoint readings.
Established dependent sum/product constructions explain its execution:
the owner supplies a value and every participant continues at the same
instance. Full strategies retain the counterfactual continuations that
an executable plan may omit.

This distinction gives a concrete rational-play analysis. For finite
one-winner trees, the possible SPE winners form an algebra with explicit
owned choice operations. Its two-role generated algebra is Boolean.
A three-role distributivity counterexample proves that the larger algebra
has no faithful coordinatewise Boolean meet/join representation.

Stable binding adds a temporal distinction. A value is fixed in one phase
and later becomes usable in additional local contexts. The disclosure
calculus derives those contexts and views from audiences. Its two-execution
theorem proves hiding through prefixes with no direct or dependent release,
including private intermediate data with dependent types.

These constructions define the semantic interfaces a larger proof theory
must preserve: ownership, shared dependent witnesses, full alternative
behavior, and permitted observations. An original unified logic or type
theory still requires its connection rules and a comparison theorem beyond
the established constructions used here. The present draft supplies
precise finite claims and counterexamples on which that work can build.
