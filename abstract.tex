\begin{abstract}
A role-owned choice has one witness and several local readings: its owner
supplies a value, the other endpoints receive it, and all continue at the
same dependent instance. We develop a working foundation for a calculus
that preserves this multi-role interaction, including its counterfactual
behavior and changes of visibility.

The public execution layer uses established dependent session and game
constructions. We distinguish full strategies from reduced executable
plans and show exactly what their reduction preserves. Strategic analysis
uses full strategies: neither Nash equilibrium nor subgame perfection is
a property of the actual execution alone. For finite one-winner trees,
backward induction yields a concrete algebra of sets of possible SPE
winners. At two roles its generated values are Boolean; at three or more,
a distributivity counterexample rules out faithful representation as
coordinatewise Boolean meet/join viewpoints.

For delayed disclosure we define protocols, audience-indexed local contexts,
histories, and views computed from those contexts. Binding fixes an
immutable dependent value; publication changes visibility without rebinding.
We prove preservation of phase typing and two-execution hiding for prefixes
with no release of the protected value or its dependencies. This condition
accounts for indirect disclosure through other cells.

The construction identifies precise semantic and typing interfaces for a
dependent multiparty proof calculus. The public metatheory is background,
while the rational algebra and staged visibility discipline give concrete
targets for further proof theory and comparison with prior work.
\end{abstract}
