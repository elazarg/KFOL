\section{From scripted interaction to dependent multiparty cut}
\label{sec:development}

The public calculus establishes complete interaction and a particular
boundary composition. General components need a more flexible interface:
they may consume some endpoints, offer others, and communicate before any
component has returned. This section specifies the next calculus and the
results that would connect it to the present core.

\subsection{Open endpoint interfaces}

A prospective judgment has the form
\[
 \Gamma_R;\Delta\vdash p\mathrel{\triangleright}c:G@R,
 \tag{27}
\]
where $R\subseteq\Roles$ is the controlled role set, $\Gamma_R$ is the
permitted pure context, and $\Delta$ records the other interaction resources
used by $p$. The use discipline for those resources is part of the calculus.
Ordinary data need not be linear because an endpoint capability is linear.

A connection rule must supply a coherent collection of endpoints for a
channel. For a binder owned by $r$, exactly one endpoint in that collection
must supply its witness; all relevant receiving endpoints must instantiate
the same continuation. A partition of role sets supplies the ownership
condition. The rule must additionally account for dependent contexts and
channel dependencies.

Likewise, a component type of the schematic form
\[
 (c:G@R)\multimap(d:H@S)
 \tag{28}
\]
would describe an implementation using one endpoint to provide another.
Application would be connection at the input boundary. This is an interactive
interface transformer, distinct from a material implication between terminal
goals. The rules for (28), including identity and its allowable wiring, remain
to be defined for the extended language.

\subsection{The proof obligations}

The extension should establish the following results for its stated syntax.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Dependent substitution and subject reduction.} Substituting a
      component at an interface preserves every affected endpoint type,
      including types indexed by previously communicated values.
\item \textbf{Identity and composition.} Forwarding implements the specified
      identity interface, and connection is associative at the declared
      observational equality. These statements must retain any intermediate
      information observable through the boundary.
\item \textbf{Coherence and progress.} A closed coherent configuration either
      returns or can reduce. Acyclic wiring or proof-theoretic restrictions
      must rule out channel cycles that would leave all endpoints waiting.
\item \textbf{Normalization and equality.} Establish termination for an
      appropriate finite fragment and confluence, or a specified weaker
      equivalence, for its reductions. Cut elimination alone is not a proof
      of either strong normalization or unique normal forms for arbitrary
      reduction relations.
\item \textbf{Role and observation transport.} Role permutations transport
      derivations and reduction. Information-aware cut must entail (24),
      transport the phase contexts underlying (22), and preserve the sealed
      prefix judgment of \cref{sec:hiding} when no release is authorized.
\item \textbf{Semantic adequacy.} Relate open components to full strategies
      as well as complete execution, with observations and interface contexts
      stated explicitly. A quotient preserving execution alone cannot support
      subgame-perfect analysis.
\end{enumerate}

These are extension targets. The public-script results prove their restricted
counterparts for (2)--(19); the observation laws give semantic conditions for
the information-aware variants. Establishing the targets for (27)--(28)
requires new definitions and proofs.

\subsection{Three boundaries worth preserving}

\paragraph{Grouping roles and sharing information.}
An endpoint can control several roles, but control and observation are separate
permissions. Replacing several independent participants with one process that
shares all their private memories can change the interaction. A role-grouping
operation must specify that change or preserve separate local views. A
many-to-one renaming is therefore not automatically a symmetry like (15).

\paragraph{Independent composition and public scheduling.}
Independent components have no access to one another's newly generated data
except through a declared interface. Reordering their events is sound only
under that information discipline. If a scheduler's order, timing, or pending
messages are observable, those observations belong in the model. Equality of
final stores alone is not equality of the information available during play.

\paragraph{Stable data and control effects.}
Types in the present core depend on stable values. A control extension must
state which effectful expressions may occur in types and which conversions
remain valid. Its two-role fragment must already satisfy those conditions;
introducing additional roles does not remove a counterexample expressible in
two roles. Publication, being a change of availability of an immutable value,
does not itself require unrestricted continuation capture.

\subsection{Mechanization}

The public fragment has a small dependency order for formalization:
script formation and substitution; local plan and full strategy
interpretations; trace and return families; complete execution; role
transport; and public boundary composition.
Its proofs can be parameterized over an ordinary dependent model before
committing to an implementation of the data calculus. The information layer
adds the phase contexts, operational histories, derived views, and the
two-store invariant in the hiding proof. The rational analysis adds full
subgame-perfect profiles before quotienting to winner sets.

The accompanying finite models compare two explicit execution procedures,
exercise finite policy reduction and role transport, and test dependent
action domains and finite knowledge partitions. Additional checks enumerate
small full SPE profiles, distinguish equilibria from executions and plans,
and execute the phase and sealing rules on finite stores.
These checks exercise only the Python models: they do not check the syntax,
conversion, or normalization metatheory of $\D$. The statements in this
paper have mathematical proofs and are not reported as proof-assistant-checked.
