# Minimal choice calculi and strategic realization

Research proposal — 28 October 2026

The objective is a small calculus of role-owned choice with a precise account of substitution, sharing, and observation. Its semantics should retain exactly the information that an admissible continuation can use to distinguish two terms. The first mathematical target is to characterize when equilibrium outcomes compose through a finite algebra, and what prevents that composition when decisions must be shared across histories.

The starting language has only winner constants and binary choices indexed by their owner. Existing game semantics and backward induction supply its interpretation. The research contribution sought is an algebraic characterization, a minimality theorem, or an obstruction theorem about this interpretation and its admissible substitutions. A dependent type theory is a later consumer of these results.

## Motivation

Replacing the existential and universal quantifiers by a family of owner-indexed choices removes the privileged verifier–falsifier pair from the syntax. The underlying construction is already present in [Abramsky’s multiplayer game semantics](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/samson.abramsky/admproc.pdf). The question here concerns which small algebra and structural rules capture rational play when there are three or more roles.

The useful connection to The Hidden Geometry of Chance is its common-realization question: individually admissible descriptions need not come from one legal global object. For a choice calculus, the descriptions may be continuation strategies, and the global object must use one admissible strategy for each player across actual and counterfactual histories.

This perspective exposes the substantive assumption behind backward induction. Strategies in separate, fully observed child trees can be assembled freely. Identifying decisions across histories can destroy that freedom. The proposed research studies the algebraic consequences of that change while keeping the language small.

## The starting calculus

Fix a finite set I of K roles. Begin with finite terms

```text
G ::= ⟨i⟩ | G ⊕ᵣ G              i,r ∈ I
```

The leaf ⟨i⟩ awards the unique win to i. At G ⊕ᵣ H, role r chooses a child. Histories are public, and every player has a full pure strategy specifying its decision at every history where it moves, including histories that its own strategy would avoid. Winning has utility 1 and every other outcome utility 0.

Let B(G) be the set of winners attained by pure subgame-perfect equilibrium profiles. In this finite perfect-information setting, backward induction gives

```text
B(⟨i⟩) = {i}
B(G ⊕ᵣ H) = B(G) ⋆ᵣ B(H)

S ⋆ᵣ T = {r}       if S = {r} or T = {r}
          S ∪ T     otherwise.
```

The operands are nonempty subsets of I. Each operation ⋆ᵣ is associative, commutative, and idempotent. Relabeling the roles transports the operations. With two roles, the values generated by winner constants are the two singletons, and the operations recover Boolean disjunction and conjunction. With at least three roles, every nonempty subset is generated. These are starting facts from the current finite analysis in [rational.tex](../rational.tex).

The set B(G) records possible winners across equilibria. It is neither a probability distribution nor a declaration that some player can force the outcome. In particular, membership i ∈ B(G) has an existential equilibrium witness, including the relevant off-path strategy choices.

History-dependent trees and full strategies have established dependent-type formulations in [Escardó and Oliva](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07735). Their construction supplies background for the operational model; the present target is compression and algebraic structure.

## Three concrete mathematical observations

### Branch strategies need a common realization

Consider a finite game in which Alice chooses a bit b and Bob then chooses a guess g. Bob wins exactly when g = b.

If Bob observes b, his optimal continuation is to guess b at each history. These two prescriptions assemble into one legal contingent strategy. Every pure subgame-perfect equilibrium gives Bob the win.

If Bob does not observe b, his two decision nodes belong to one information set. A pure strategy must prescribe the same guess at both. No pure Nash equilibrium exists: at a matching pair Alice can profitably switch her bit; at a mismatching pair Bob can profitably switch his guess. There is no proper continuation subgame separating those two Bob nodes, so pure subgame perfection does not restore existence.

The underlying owner-labeled tree and terminal winners are unchanged. The admissible strategy space has changed. Applying the public-tree recurrence after forgetting this information constraint incorrectly returns {Bob}.

This is an elementary obstruction example, not a novelty claim. It proves that owner labels and terminal winner sets alone do not support the same recurrence under arbitrary identifications of information sets.

### Definable union uses repeated descriptions

For three pairwise distinct roles r, s, t, the algebra satisfies

```text
J(x,y) = (x ⋆ᵣ y) ⋆ₜ (x ⋆ₛ y) = x ∪ y.
```

If neither inner operation collapses to its owner singleton, both return x ∪ y. If exactly one collapses, its singleton already belongs to the other result, and the outer owner is different. If both collapse, the inputs are the two distinct owner singletons and the outer operation returns their union. Thus all cases give x ∪ y.

This realizes an owner-independent operation using owner-indexed choices. Its syntax contains two occurrences of each argument. Ordinary substitution expands each occurrence into a separate subtree with its own history-dependent strategy entries.

Substitution of one shared strategy decision has a different meaning. Sharing a previously chosen value has another meaning again. The union identity establishes the first interpretation; determining its behavior under the other interpretations is a research problem.

### Convex observations can erase independence

For finite nonempty action sets A and D, define

```text
Indep = {p ⊗ q : p ∈ Δ(A), q ∈ Δ(D)}
Joint = Δ(A × D).
```

Every point mass on A × D is a product law. Therefore

```text
conv(Indep) = Joint.
```

Every linear cost consequently has the same minimum and maximum over these two sets. For binary actions, however,

```text
½δ₍₀,₀₎ + ½δ₍₁,₁₎
```

does not factor into its marginals. Equal optimized linear costs do not characterize independent realizability in this example.

This limits what occupation geometry can establish for a strategic calculus. A quotient by convex hull can preserve those costs while admitting a shared random source that the original system did not possess. Strategic observations must also account for admissible deviations and the information available when choices are made.

## The proposed research questions

### Exact composition under interface constraints

Use finite staged games as the first comparison model. A decision has an owner, a finite action set, and an information state determined by its permitted observations. Information sets have consistent action sets and respect perfect recall. A full pure strategy is a function from a player’s information states to legal actions.

An interface specifies which decisions, observations, or already selected values are shared between components. Initially, study a single binary decision exposed at an interface. This keeps every compatibility obstruction finite and explicit.

Restriction sends a global strategy to its component strategies. Where components share a decision, the restrictions must agree. Conversely, compatible partial strategy functions can be united when their domains cover the decisions and their action types agree. This elementary gluing result is the feasibility baseline.

Equilibrium requires a separate argument. Agreement of component strategies does not imply that they resist a unilateral deviation changing several decisions belonging to the same player. Nor can a component cut across an information set automatically be treated as a proper subgame.

**Target A.** For a specified class of finite interfaces, characterize when replacing components by their winner sets B preserves equilibrium winners under every permitted composition and every assignment of unique winners to terminal outcomes.

The tree interface with separate public histories is the positive baseline. The hidden-bit game supplies a negative example. The desired result is an explicit criterion between these cases, with a counterexample construction when the criterion fails. “All compatible local equilibria glue” is a question to resolve under stated hypotheses, not an assumed principle.

### Structural rules for repeated choices

Separate three operations in the semantic experiments:

| Operation | What is reused | Required account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Substitution of a description | A term expanded at each occurrence | Separate strategy entries at distinct histories |
| Sharing a realized value | One earlier choice and its result | One binding, with specified observations |
| Sharing a strategy decision | One policy entry used at several nodes | Agreement across those nodes and whole-player deviations |

Fresh independent randomness becomes a fourth operation only in the probabilistic extension.

**Target B.** Determine which equations and definability results of the owned-choice algebra survive each interpretation of repetition. Begin with idempotence, the three-owner union term, and substitution into contexts containing two occurrences of a variable.

The useful output is either a structural rule with a semantic preservation theorem or a finite witness showing why that rule fails. A type discipline should record exactly the distinctions needed by those theorems. Known distinctions between values and computations provide a starting point; their owned-choice algebra is the object to characterize.

### The smallest adequate semantic object

Fix an explicit grammar T of legal tests before choosing a semantic quotient. Begin with owned-choice contexts and winner observations. Add one interface experiment at a time, such as sharing a binary decision or hiding one observation. A test specifies the binding and visibility of every shared resource.

Define

```text
G ≈ₜ H  iff  Obs(C[G]) = Obs(C[H]) for every C ∈ T.
```

For the public tree fragment, Obs is B. In the pure imperfect-information comparisons, the observation may be empty because an equilibrium need not exist. The solution concept used by each test class must be specified.

This is ordinary contextual equivalence. Defining it is not the contribution. The challenge is to represent it by a small, explicit algebra and derive useful equations.

**Target C.** Construct a semantic representation α and prove both directions of

```text
α(G) = α(H)  iff  G ≈ₜ H.
```

For the original fragment, B already supplies an exact compositional observation. For each proposed interface operation, either extend that representation minimally or prove a lower bound on the distinctions every adequate representation must retain. If a finite representation exists, seek normal forms or a sound and complete equational calculus.

Retaining complete strategies and all deviation behavior is a reference semantics against which to prove correctness. The research target is a smaller representation with the same observations. A proof that a proposed compression cannot support a particular operation would also be a substantive outcome.

## Probability and the open game benchmark

The first theorem package concerns pure strategies and finite trees. A probabilistic extension must specify independent player randomization, correlation between a single player’s contingent decisions, and any public or private shared random source. These are different structures.

[Ghani, Hedges, Winschel, and Zahn](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.04641) provide the established pure open-game comparison: components are interpreted relative to contexts, with equilibrium and off-equilibrium best-response behavior. This proposal does not use that paper as a correctness result for mixed-profile composition.

For mixed profiles, the comparison starts with the historical development in GameTheory. In particular, the source at [revision a738c90e](https://github.com/elazarg/GameTheory/tree/a738c90ef4c2b192aa9ed9f2af1c574bf2036447/GameTheory/Languages/OpenGame) contains the following concrete declarations for its encoded paper-style probabilistic semantics:

- `probabilistic_leftIdentity_requires_convexity`: left identity can enlarge the selected set by convexification.
- `convexIso_not_left_seq_congruence`: equivalence after convex closure need not be a congruence for sequential composition.
- `probabilistic_seq_bracketings_incomparable`: the two canonical bracketings admit incomparable equilibrium sets in an explicit example.
- `saturated_seq_not_associative`: closing the selected sets after composition does not restore associativity in the exhibited construction.

These historical proof artifacts are regression cases to recover with their exact definitions and dependencies. They do not certify a different mixed-profile construction merely because it has similar terminology. The sound mixed-profile implementation identified in GameTheory’s history must be recovered and its precise interface stated before it serves as a positive benchmark.

An additional local lesson comes from [GameTheory’s comparison audit](../../GameTheory/docs/ExperimentLog.md): its sequential interface retained the context idea while deriving optimality from deviation semantics, rather than accepting a component’s equilibrium predicate as an unconstrained field. The proposed calculus should likewise derive its chosen equilibrium concept from execution, information, and admissible deviations.

**Target D.** If probability is introduced, prove strategy-law preservation and transport of whole-player deviations before claiming equilibrium preservation. Prove unit, associativity, and congruence laws for the actual composition and actual observational equivalence. Convexification is a semantic operation requiring justification.

This extension is conditional on the pure analysis identifying a result that needs probability.

## What transfers from common realization and causal transport

Three mechanisms from the GoC inventory guide the investigation.

**Realization.** Replace an assertion that local semantic values are compatible by a construction of one global admissible strategy profile. Preserve the same opponents’ strategies when comparing a profile with a unilateral deviation.

**Obstruction.** When realization fails, produce a finite certificate. In the first examples this can be two incompatible prescriptions at one information set, a joint law that fails to factor, or a player deviation omitted by a local optimality check. General circulation–potential duality becomes relevant only if a later recursive model supplies a concrete transition structure.

**Observation.** Preserve distinctions that legal future tests can detect. Equal final values or equal optimized expected costs may lose information about when a value became available or which counterfactual choices share a policy.

The broad local-to-global theme has a precise predecessor in [Abramsky and Brandenburger’s global-section treatment of contextuality](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.0264). Translating a strategy model into that language would be a useful representation step; novelty would require a strategic characterization beyond the translation itself.

The causal-transport connection is methodological at this stage. In finite discrete time, [Backhoff-Veraguas, Bartl, Beiglböck, and Eder](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00368) identify adapted topologies and characterize the weak adapted topology through continuity of optimal-stopping values. This supports choosing legal sequential tests before deciding which distinctions to quotient away. Their theorem is not a strategic-equilibrium theorem.

## Relation to existing algebra and logic

The closest comparisons address different parts of the proposal:

| Source | What it supplies | Comparison required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Abramsky | Owner-indexed connectives and quantifiers, role symmetry, partial-information games | Which part of the proposed syntax and strategy model is already represented |
| [Olde Loohuis and Venema](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/1012623/156995_334017.pdf) | Multiplayer algebras and complete calculi based on players having winning strategies | Whether a proposed quotient has collapsed to their guarantee semantics |
| Escardó and Oliva | Dependent game trees and full optimal strategies | Which adequacy and backward-induction results can be reused |
| [Bolt, Hedges, and Zahn](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.06810) | Limitations of computing all SPE plays using the studied nondeterministic selection-function product | Whether a proposed composition falls within that obstruction |
| Pure open games | Context-dependent strategic composition | Whether the proposed smaller representation preserves the needed deviations |
| GameTheory historical mixed development | Explicit probabilistic composition obstructions and a source for the mixed-profile benchmark | Exact hypotheses, strategy-law semantics, and recoverable proofs |

A finite algebra of equilibrium winners and an algebra of players’ guarantees answer different questions. Both are useful comparisons. An asserted new logic must state its observations and show where its equations differ from existing ones.

## Work sequence and deliverables

### Establish the finite reference semantics

Define full strategies, execution, unilateral replacement of a player’s whole strategy, and pure subgame perfection. Prove that B is calculated by ⋆ᵣ and produce equilibrium witnesses for each returned winner. Keep this adequacy theorem separate from the extensional algebraic identities.

Deliverable: a small Lean development connecting the existing finite algebra to the game semantics, with explicit assumptions and no change of solution concept between the two sides.

### Examine one shared binary decision

Specify its owner, information partition, action type, and permitted component composition. Prove strategy gluing for compatible assignments. Search for equilibrium-preserving composition criteria, retaining failed candidates and their smallest counterexamples. Include the observed-bit and hidden-bit games, and a player owning decisions in more than one component.

Deliverable: an exact theorem for a stated interface class, or an obstruction theorem delimiting winner-set composition.

### Extract the algebraic result

Use the validated interface class to classify structural rules and contextual equivalence. Seek a canonical representation, an equational axiomatization, or a semantic lower bound. Check the union construction under the selected account of repetition.

Deliverable: a result that explains which algebraic laws hold and why, together with a precise comparison to the closest existing semantics.

### Derive the first typing rules

Introduce only the distinctions forced by the preceding theorems: reusable descriptions, shared values, or shared decisions. Give each structural rule an operational interpretation and prove preservation of the selected observations. Begin with simple types; introduce dependent types when a concrete dependency-preservation theorem needs them.

Deliverable: a small typed calculus with a substitution theorem tied to strategic realization. A probability extension proceeds through Target D if required.

## Criteria for a successful contribution

A useful first paper should deliver at least one of the following:

- An explicit characterization of when the finite equilibrium algebra remains compositional under a meaningful class of interfaces.
- A minimal semantic representation, with distinguishing contexts proving that its retained information is necessary.
- A sound and complete equational calculus or a structural classification for a precisely stated owned-choice fragment.
- A nondefinability theorem explaining which sharing or substitution operation a proposed small semantics cannot support.

The known syntax, backward induction, strategy gluing, and contextual-equivalence definition supply the foundations. The contribution must lie in the resulting characterization, algebra, or obstruction. If the surviving object is equivalent to an existing calculus, the result should identify that equivalence explicitly and reuse its theory.

The immediate priority is Targets A–C over the finite pure fragment. They turn the common-realization insight into a bounded mathematical problem while preserving the original ambition: understand how a family of equally primitive owner-indexed choices can support a coherent logic and, eventually, a useful type theory.
