\begin{abstract}
For any number of roles at least three, every idempotent operation
definable using owned choices, winner constants, and internal role
switches is already definable by choices and variables alone. This
intrinsic core has a syntactic recognition algorithm. Idempotency needs
only $\lceil\log_2(K+1)\rceil$ fixed test values for $K\geq4$, optimally,
with three needed at $K=3$. A common-leaf separation theorem identifies
the deterministic atomic fragment at every arity with the known clone
$S_2$: essentially unary or at most two-valued functions. Thus an
idempotent term preserving unique outcomes on all atomic inputs must
project one input there. For ambiguity we identify an affine factorpower
quotient and classify the singleton fibers of two-permutation actions,
where marginal row bounds fail. Relative reset unifies the constructions
and gives a right-normal-band quotient on homogeneous observations. The
common-leaf theorem, including internal switches, and the reset laws are
kernel-checked in Lean. The remaining structural theorems have written proofs
and independent finite checks; the full clone and a finite equational
basis remain open. Equation validity is coNP-complete even in the
intrinsic core, without constants or internal switches.
\end{abstract}

\section{A small algebra and its questions}

Fix a finite role set $I$ of size $K\geq3$. Its values are nonempty
winner sets. In finite public games with one winner at each leaf, each
role values its own win at one and every other outcome at zero. The
possible winners over full pure subgame-perfect equilibria compose by
\[
 S\ow r T=\begin{cases}\{r\},&S=\{r\}\text{ or }T=\{r\},\\
 S\cup T,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]
The intended signature also has singleton constants and internal role
switches $S\mapsto\pi S$ for every $\pi\in\Sym(I)$. These switches
rename owners and winners inside a component. Terms have the grammar
\[
 t::=\langle i\rangle\mid x_j\mid t\oplus_r t\mid\pi(t).
\]
The full-strategy interpretation and exact equilibrium recurrence are
proved in \texttt{Basic.lean}. Hidden choices and sequential equilibrium
are outside this public fragment.

Three distinct owners define neutral union:
\[
 J(x,y)=(x\ow r y)\ow t(x\ow s y)=x\cup y.
\]
Consequently every nonempty constant, including $I$, is ground definable.
We use these derived operations freely. Repeated variable occurrences
denote independently solved copies, as required by the term algebra.

The preceding note \texttt{symmetric.tex} proves two uniform facts used
here. On each fixed singleton-or-ambiguous status cell, every term is a
union of a fixed constant set and permutation images of its ambiguous
inputs. Every failed
equation therefore has a countervaluation in which each input has at
most two winners. Every singleton and every pair is necessary in a fixed
test domain. Those mathematical proofs, including the separating terms,
are not yet complete Lean developments.

The three-role unary classification in that note has a quotient by
partial permutations. For more roles, a coordinate can depend on two or
more input membership bits: $x\cup(1\ 2)(x)$ at $K=4$ is an elementary
example. The right general object retains these disjunctive source sets,
and also the constant coordinates.

\section{Idempotency identifies an intrinsic core}\label{sec:core}

A positive-arity term operation is \emph{idempotent} when
$t(S,\ldots,S)=S$ for every nonempty $S$. This semantic condition
identifies a smaller syntax. For a variable occurrence, compose all
role permutations on its path from the root, in the order in which
they act on its value.

\begin{theorem}[Exact idempotent elimination]\label{thm:core}
A term is idempotent if and only if it has no winner leaves and every
variable occurrence has identity accumulated permutation. Consequently
the idempotent part of the full term clone is exactly the clone generated
by the $K$ binary owned choices, without constants or internal switches.
Recognition and extraction of such a term take polynomial time in the
explicit input description.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On the all-ambiguous cell, track a constant set $C$ and the
variable-occurrence permutations. If any winner leaf occurs, $C$ is
nonempty: it starts nonempty at that leaf, is transported by switches,
survives unions, and a collapse at an owned node replaces it by a
nonempty owner singleton. A node with no constant contribution cannot
collapse on this cell, since every live child is ambiguous. Thus a term
containing a winner leaf has some fixed nonempty $C$ in every ambiguous
diagonal output. But $\bigcap_{|S|\geq2}S=\varnothing$ for $K\geq3$,
so the term cannot be idempotent.

Without winner leaves, every intermediate value on this cell is
ambiguous. Every choice is union. The diagonal value is therefore the
union of $\pi S$ over all variable occurrences. Idempotency forces each
$\pi S\subseteq S$. If $\pi(i)\ne i$, a pair containing $i$ and omitting
$\pi(i)$ contradicts that inclusion. Every accumulated permutation is
identity.

Transport switches through choices using
$\pi(u\star_r v)=\pi u\star_{\pi(r)}\pi v$ and compose them.
All leaf permutations disappear. The remaining constant-free owned term
is idempotent because its primitive choices are. A traversal accumulating
permutations both checks the criterion and transports the owners,
without expanding the tree.
\end{proof}

The no-switching calculus thus contains \emph{every} idempotent definable
operation. Internal switching still enlarges the complete clone and
changes its congruences, as the preceding notes show.

\begin{corollary}[Semilattice trace]\label{cor:trace}
On all-ambiguous inputs, every idempotent term is union over a fixed
nonempty subset of its arguments. These restrictions form exactly the
semilattice term clone, and restriction is a surjective clone
homomorphism. There is no near-unanimity term of any finite arity, and
no Mal'cev term. Every variable occurring in a core term is semantically
essential, so every valid core identity has the same variables on both
sides.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
In the constant-free core all intermediate ambiguous values are
ambiguous; the result is union of the occurring variables. Distinct
supports are separated by a pair and $I$. Composition unions supports,
and every nonempty support is realized by derived union. A nonempty
join cannot satisfy near-unanimity: put the larger of those two values
at a selected argument and the smaller at all others. The Mal'cev
identities would require support both in the first argument alone and
in the third alone. Operations satisfying either collection of
identities are already idempotent. The same two-value test detects the
presence of each argument, proving essentiality. Regularity of identities
is the standard two-element semilattice observation: interpret every
primitive choice as join on $\{P,I\}$, for a proper ambiguous pair $P$.
An identity must then have the same variable support on both sides.
This is the usual regular-identity setting of semilattice sums
\cite{Plonka1967}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Intrinsic player views]\label{cor:views}
Every idempotent operation in the full role-switching language acts
compositionally on each of the $K+1$-state player views
\[
 q_k(S)=
 \begin{cases}
 P,&k\in S,\\
 i,&S=\{i\},\ i\ne k,\\
 N,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
These give a subdirect representation by $K$ factors. The family is
irredundant: none of its player views can be omitted.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Each $q_k$ is a congruence quotient for every owned choice, proved in
\texttt{Perspectives.lean}. Theorem~\ref{thm:core} extends this to all
idempotent operations, even when their original definitions contain
internal switches. All membership bits are recovered from the $P$
states, so the family separates values. Each view is onto. Omitting
$q_k$ identifies $I\setminus\{k\}$ and $I$, which every other player
views as $P$.
\end{proof}

The decomposition that fails for unrestricted internal role switching
therefore holds intrinsically for all idempotent definable operations.
The representation is subdirect, not the full Cartesian product.

\subsection{An optimal logarithmic test set}

A fixed domain $D\subseteq\Pow(I)$ \emph{certifies idempotency} when,
for terms of every arity, agreement $t(S,\ldots,S)=S$ for $S\in D$
implies idempotency. This tests a specific diagonal law, rather than
all term equations.

\begin{theorem}[Exact ambiguous certificates]\label{thm:idem-domain}
For a domain consisting of ambiguous values, certification holds exactly
when
\[
 \bigcap D=\varnothing,\qquad
 (\mathbf1_{i\in S})_{S\in D}\text{ are distinct for all }i\in I.
\]
The minimum size of a certifying domain, even allowing singleton test
values, is three for $K=3$, and $\lceil\log_2(K+1)\rceil$ for $K\geq4$.
Both minima are attained using ambiguous values alone.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $k\in\bigcap D$, the nonidentity term $x\cup\{k\}$ fixes every test.
If two roles have the same incidence vector, their transposition fixes
every test. These necessary conditions apply also to singleton tests.

Conversely suppose every test is ambiguous and the two conditions hold.
An agreeing term's constant contribution lies in $\bigcap D$, so is
empty. Hence it has no winner leaves. Each occurrence permutation
satisfies $\pi S\subseteq S$ on each test, and finiteness makes these
equalities. A permutation fixing each test can only exchange roles with
identical incidence vectors. It is identity. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:core}.

With $m$ tests the conditions require $K$ distinct binary words of
length $m$, excluding the all-one word. Thus $K\leq2^m-1$. For $K\geq4$
set $m=\lceil\log_2(K+1)\rceil$. Start the role signatures with the $m$
distinct two-bit words supported on a cycle on $m$ bits. Each bit occurs
twice, making every test ambiguous. Add distinct words other than the
all-one word until there are $K$ signatures.

For $K=3$, two tests satisfying the necessary conditions have signatures
$00,01,10$, hence are two distinct singletons. The reduct's ambiguity
adder from \texttt{report.tex} fixes every singleton but adds $c$ to
ambiguous inputs, so those tests cannot certify idempotency.
Three pairs certify it.
\end{proof}

At $K=7$, three ambiguous diagonal tests suffice, compared with $28$
values for testing every full-signature equation. The improvement is
specific to idempotency.

\section{Separation and deterministic atomic expressivity}\label{sec:sep}

Fix input environments $v_a$ and pairwise disjoint target regions
$W_a\subseteq I$. A source occurrence is a winner leaf, or a variable
leaf together with its accumulated permutation.

\begin{theorem}[One common source]\label{thm:source}
If $t(v_a)\subseteq W_a$ for every $a$, one source occurrence of $t$
already has its value inside $W_a$ in every environment simultaneously.
If there are at least two target regions, it is a variable occurrence.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At an owned node with owner $r$, at most one target contains $r$.
In every other environment the output cannot collapse to $\{r\}$;
it is union, so both children lie in the target. In the possible
exceptional environment, either both children lie in the target or a
child is the absorbing owner singleton and lies in it. Choose that child:
it works in all environments. Descend to a leaf. At a switch pull the
targets back along the permutation, preserving disjointness. A winner
leaf has the same nonempty value in all environments and cannot fit
two disjoint targets.
\end{proof}

Distinct singleton outputs at any number of tests are thus jointly
explained by one occurrence. It cannot change between tests.
\texttt{Separation.lean} proves this for arbitrary index types and target
predicates, with internal permutations explicit in the syntax.

Call a term \emph{atom-preserving} if it returns a singleton on every
singleton-input valuation. It induces a function $f:I^n\to I$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact deterministic atomic clone]\label{thm:S2}
At every positive arity, the functions induced by atom-preserving terms
are exactly the essentially unary or at most two-valued functions.
This is the known clone $S_2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\iota_3\subseteq I^3$ be the relation of triples with a repeated
entry. Three input environments having this property in every
coordinate cannot have three distinct singleton outputs:
Theorem~\ref{thm:source} would give one permutation sending an input
triple with repetition to three distinct roles. Every induced function
therefore preserves $\iota_3$.

Use the established presentation $\operatorname{Pol}(\iota_3)=S_2$,
recorded by Szendrei\cite[Proposition~2.2]{Szendrei2012} in the
S\l upecki--Burle classification. It applies for every finite $K\geq3$.
This is a use of that classification, not a new classification of $S_2$.

Conversely every unary map $h:I\to I$ has an atomic realization.
The term $x\star_a(a\ b)(x)$ merges atomic $b$ into $a$, fixing the
others. Merge each $h$-fiber into a representative, then permute the
representatives to the prescribed images.

For distinct output roles $a,b$, tests of input labels are unary maps
into $\{a,b\}$. On these atomic values, $\star_a$ is Boolean OR and
$\star_b$ Boolean AND, with truth $a$. Finite DNF of label tests
realizes every $I^n\to\{a,b\}$. Constants cover the one-valued case.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Idempotent atomic determinacy]
An idempotent, atom-preserving term projects one input on all atomic
valuations. A genuinely multiargument rule using at least three output
roles must return ambiguity somewhere on atomic inputs.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Idempotency makes its atomic function surjective. As $K\geq3$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:S2} makes it essentially unary, and the diagonal law
makes that unary map identity.
\end{proof}

The exact number of induced $n$-ary atomic functions is
\[
 \binom K2(2^{K^n}-2)+K+
 n\left(K^K-\binom K2(2^K-2)-K\right).
\]
The first part counts functions with at most two values. The remaining
essentially unary functions have a unique essential argument.
For $K=3,n=2$ the count is $1545$. This classifies deterministic atomic
behavior, leaving mixed and ambiguous valuations available for richer
operations.

\section{The known relation monoid and its affine extension}

Regard a relation $R\subseteq I\times I$ as sending source roles to
output roles, and write $R[S]$ for its direct image. Let $\FP(\Sym(I))$
be the monoid of nonempty unions of permutation graphs, with relational
composition. This is the \emph{factorpower} of the natural symmetric-group
action, introduced by Ganyushkin and Mazorchuk and surveyed by
Mazorchuk\cite{GanyushkinMazorchuk1995,Mazorchuk2009}. In the usual
definition, nonempty subsets $P,Q\subseteq\Sym(I)$ are identified when
$\{\pi(i):\pi\in P\}=\{\pi(i):\pi\in Q\}$ for every $i$.
Thus the relation monoid appearing here is exactly a known object.

Form its affine extension
\[
 \mathcal E_I=\mathcal P(I)\rtimes\FP(\Sym(I)),\qquad
 (C,R)(D,Q)=(C\cup R[D],R\circ Q).
\]
The action on $\mathcal P(I)$ is by direct image; it preserves empty set
and unions, so this multiplication is associative. Its identity is
$(\varnothing,\mathrm{id})$. The affine action is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine}
 F_{C,R}(S)=C\cup R[S].
\end{equation}
Every such map sends ambiguous sets to ambiguous sets because $R$
contains a permutation. Restrict these maps to
$\mathcal A_I=\{S\subseteq I:|S|\geq2\}$ and identify equal restrictions.
Call the resulting transformation monoid $\mathcal Q_I$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact ambiguity quotient]\label{thm:quotient}
Let $\mathcal M_I$ be the monoid of unary term functions fixing $I$.
Restriction to $\mathcal A_I$ is a surjective monoid homomorphism
$\mathcal M_I\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal Q_I$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A unary term is either globally constant or has live provenance
$C\cup\bigcup_{\pi\in P}\pi S$ on ambiguous inputs, with $P$ nonempty.
Here is a simultaneous induction establishing that dichotomy. The
variable is live and a winner is constant. Switching transports constants
and permutations. At an owned node, a live child is always ambiguous on
ambiguous inputs. Thus collapse there occurs exactly when a globally
constant child is the owner singleton, making the whole node globally
constant. Otherwise union the live provenances and the constant children.
A node with only constant children is again globally constant.

Every live expression fixes $I$. Hence a top-preserving unary term is
live or is the constant $I$, and has a restriction of the displayed form.
Conversely, for any $(C,R)$ choose a nonempty finite set of permutations
whose union is $R$. The term joining their images of $x$ and the
singleton constants in $C$ realizes \eqref{eq:affine} on all inputs and
fixes $I$. Restriction respects composition since all these maps preserve
$\mathcal A_I$.
\end{proof}

The constant part cannot be suppressed. For example,
$S\mapsto S\cup\{a\}$ on ambiguous inputs cannot equal $R[S]$ for a
union of permutations $R$. Every other output coordinate $b\ne a$ has
the singleton source $b$. As shown below, that source is uniquely
determined by the restricted action. Thus every constituent permutation
must fix all $b\ne a$, hence also $a$. Their union is the identity and
cannot make $a$ constantly present. The relevant object is an affine
extension and a quotient of a known monoid, not just the factorpower.

\section{Canonical rows, matching, and synthesis}

For an output role $k$ let
$R^{-1}(k)=\{i:(i,k)\in R\}$. Membership of $k$ in
\eqref{eq:affine} is either constant or the predicate
$S\cap R^{-1}(k)\ne\varnothing$. Every such source set is nonempty.
On ambiguous inputs this predicate is constantly true exactly when its
source set has size at least $K-1$.

\begin{lemma}[Unique row representation]\label{lem:rows}
Every action in $\mathcal Q_I$ has a unique row for each output role,
of one of the following forms:
\[
 *\quad\text{(always present)},\qquad
 A_k\subseteq I,\quad 1\leq|A_k|\leq K-2
 \quad\text{(present iff }S\cap A_k\ne\varnothing\text{)}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The preceding threshold gives existence. A nonconstant row has a pair
disjoint from its source set, distinguishing it from $*$. If two small
source sets $A,B$ differ, choose $i\in A\setminus B$, swapping their
names if necessary. Since $|B|\leq K-2$, choose a different
$j\notin B$. The pair $\{i,j\}$ meets $A$ and misses $B$, distinguishing
the rows.
\end{proof}

Not every syntactically possible collection of rows is realizable. Build
a bipartite graph $H$ with source and output copies of $I$. A nonconstant
output row $k$ allows exactly the edges from $A_k$; a constant row allows
edges from every source. Edges into nonconstant rows are
\emph{prescribed}; edges into constant rows are only allowed.

\begin{theorem}[Matching criterion]\label{thm:matching}
A collection of canonical rows belongs to $\mathcal Q_I$ exactly when
every prescribed edge belongs to a perfect matching of $H$. If all rows
are constant, it always belongs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For necessity, choose a representative $(C,R)$. A nonconstant row
cannot belong to $C$, and its preimage under $R$ is exactly its canonical
source set by Lemma~\ref{lem:rows}. Every prescribed edge lies in some
permutation constituent of $R$. That permutation is a perfect matching
of $H$.

Conversely, choose a perfect matching for each prescribed edge. Their
union $R$ is a union of permutations. At a nonconstant row its preimage
is contained in the allowed source set and includes every prescribed
source, so it is exactly $A_k$. Set $C$ equal to the set of constant
output rows. Then $(C,R)$ realizes the given rows. If no edges are
prescribed, take $R$ to be the identity and $C=I$.
\end{proof}

For comparison, the analogous criterion with \emph{all} relation edges
prescribed is the standard factorpower realizability criterion
(Mazorchuk\cite[Section~2.1]{Mazorchuk2009}). Here the constant rows leave
their matching edges free, and the ambiguity threshold removes larger
nonconstant source sets. An ordinary Hall existence test alone is
insufficient. At $K=4$, rows
\[
 (\{1\},\{1,2\},*,*)
\]
allow a perfect matching, but the prescribed edge from source $1$ to
output $2$ belongs to none: output $1$ already requires source $1$.
The collection is therefore not realizable.

\begin{corollary}[Polynomial recognition and synthesis]
Given $K$ canonical rows, membership in $\mathcal Q_I$ is decidable in
polynomial time. Every member has a realization by at most $K^2$
permutation images, together with its constant roles, and by an ordinary
term of size polynomial in $K$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For each of the at most $K(K-2)$ prescribed edges, force that edge and
find a perfect matching in the remaining graph. Standard augmenting
paths suffice. Use those witness permutations in the synthesis from
Theorem~\ref{thm:quotient}. Join the pieces in a balanced tree. Expanding
the fixed derived-union term duplicates each argument twice; balanced
joining of $m$ pieces has size $O(m^2)$ rather than the exponential
expansion of a skew join. Singleton constant pieces and explicit
permutation descriptions have polynomial size too.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Canonical composition]\label{thm:composition}
For outer rows $A$ and inner rows $B$, the composite row at $k$ is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $*$ if $A_k=*$ or some $i\in A_k$ has $B_i=*$;
\item otherwise $\bigcup_{i\in A_k}B_i$, changed to $*$ if that union
has at least $K-1$ elements.
\end{enumerate}
This computes composition in time polynomial in $K$ on descriptions of
$O(K^2)$ bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Substitute the inner membership disjunctions into the outer membership
disjunction. A constant true disjunct makes the result true; otherwise
the sources union. Apply the ambiguity threshold. Closure follows also
from the affine multiplication in $\mathcal E_I$.
\end{proof}

For three roles every nonconstant row is a singleton. The matching
criterion says that their sources must be distinct. Thus these rows
are exactly a partial bijection, with $*$ on the missing output roles,
recovering the $34$-element symmetric inverse monoid. Four roles admit
two-source rows, and the matching criterion is a genuine further
constraint. Exhausting the criterion and, independently, all affine
permutation-union representatives gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|rr}
K&|\FP(\Sym(I))|&|\mathcal Q_I|\\\hline
3&49&34\\
4&7443&1979
\end{array}
\]
These are finite computations, with complete enumerations reproduced by
\texttt{check\_general.py}. They count the ambiguity quotient, not the
full unary term monoid. Its singleton fibers for general $K$ are still
unclassified.

\section{Relative reset and exact two-permutation fibers}

\subsection{A uniform reset operation}

Given $A,B$, start with $A\cup B$ and, once for each owner $r$, replace
the current value $U$ by $U\star_r B$. Write the resulting derived
binary operation as $\Delta(A,B)$.

\begin{lemma}[Relative reset]\label{lem:relative}
\[
 \Delta(A,B)=
 \begin{cases}B,&|B|=1,\\A\cup B,&|B|\geq2.\end{cases}
\]
Its value is independent of the enumeration of owners. It is
idempotent and equivariant.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every intermediate value contains $B$. If $B$ is ambiguous, both
operands are ambiguous, and each step preserves $A\cup B$. If
$B=\{r\}$, its own step forces that singleton; every later step leaves
it fixed. Idempotency and equivariance follow from the displayed law.
The atomic and ambiguous cases are proved in \texttt{Separation.lean}.
\end{proof}

This single construction replaces the earlier separate atomic reset
devices. It also gives a precise, familiar observational quotient.
For an idempotent atom-preserving term, record its support $J$ on
ambiguous inputs and its projected argument $j$ on atomic inputs.
The extracted core cannot depend on an absent argument, so $j\in J$.

\begin{proposition}[Homogeneous observation quotient]\label{prop:pointed}
These pairs are exactly $(J,j)$ with $\varnothing\ne J$ and $j\in J$.
They compose by
\[
 (J,j)\bigl((L_i,\ell_i)_i\bigr)
   =\left(\bigcup_{i\in J}L_i,\ell_j\right).
\]
They form the term clone of right normal bands. Relative reset maps to
its binary multiplication.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Atomic and all-ambiguous input regions are invariant under these
operations, so their observations respect substitution. Every pair is
realized by $\Delta(\bigcup_{i\in J}x_i,x_j)$, and distinct pairs are
separated by atomic valuations or by a pair and $I$ on ambiguous inputs.
The binary operation on pairs is $(J,j)(L,\ell)=(J\cup L,\ell)$:
word terms remember their variable set and their last variable.
Associativity, $xx=x$, and $xyz=yxz$ present exactly these word
invariants, the standard right-normal-band
calculus\cite[Section~8]{RomanowskaSmith1989}.
\end{proof}

This is a quotient of operations observed on two invariant regions,
not a quotient identifying winner-set values. Mixed valuations retain
more information. For distinct $a,b,c$,
\[
 \Delta(\Delta(\{c\},\{a\}),\{a,b\})=\{a,b\},\qquad
 \Delta(\{c\},\Delta(\{a\},\{a,b\}))=\{a,b,c\}.
\]
Thus $\Delta$ is not associative in the full algebra. The right-normal-band
presentation is complete for the stated observation quotient only.
The companion note \texttt{reset.tex} gives the complete mixed-input
semantics of the reset-only fragment: antimatroids with a unique initial
element, polynomial prerequisite normal forms, and a three-element
generator identified with Waldhauser's $G_3^{\rm op}$\cite{Waldhauser2006}.

\subsection{Marginal atomic bounds do not suffice}

For $K=4$ let $\sigma=(1\ 2\ 3\ 4)$ and fix the ambiguity action
$F(S)=S\cup\sigma S$. Each atomic row must be a nonempty subset of
$\{i,\sigma(i)\}$, with no mandatory singleton-source image. These
obvious marginal bounds allow the atomic function
\[
 h(1)=1,\quad h(2)=2,\quad h(3)=3,\quad h(4)=1.
\]
Nevertheless no term has these rows and ambiguity action $F$.
We prove a uniform classification explaining the obstruction.

\begin{lemma}[Localization without constant coordinates]\label{lem:local}
Suppose a unary term has ambiguity action $R[S]$, with no constant
output coordinate. It contains no winner leaves. Push switches to its
variable leaves.
Every remaining leaf permutation lies in the perfect matchings of the
canonical support graph.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On ambiguous inputs a live term has constant-plus-permutation provenance.
Any constant contribution would make an output coordinate constant,
so it is empty. As in Theorem~\ref{thm:core}, any winner leaf would force
a nonempty constant contribution, so there are none. Each leaf
permutation contributes its graph to $R$. For nonconstant rows the
canonical source sets are uniquely determined, so its graph lies in
the allowed graph. Conversely every such permutation is a perfect
matching and an allowed generator.
\end{proof}

For $R=\mathrm{id}\cup\sigma$ and $K\geq4$, every source row has size
one or two, hence is nonconstant. Its perfect matchings independently
choose identity or $\sigma$ on each nontrivial cycle: choosing a shifted
edge forces the next shifted edge around that cycle.

For a cycle $C$, describe an atomic row at $i\in C$ by
$0$ for $\{i\}$, $1$ for $\{\sigma(i)\}$, and $?$ for their pair.
A fixed point has just its one mandatory singleton.

\begin{theorem}[Exact two-permutation fibers]\label{thm:cycles}
Fix $K\geq4$ and the ambiguity action $S\mapsto S\cup\sigma S$.
The choices on different cycles of $\sigma$ are independent.
On a cycle of length $m$ the permitted atomic tables are exactly:
\begin{itemize}
\item for $m=1$, its fixed singleton;
\item for $m=2$, every word in $\{0,1,?\}^2$;
\item for $m\geq3$, every word in $\{0,?\}^m$ or $\{1,?\}^m$,
and the $m$ words with $0$ at $r$, $1$ at $\sigma^{-1}(r)$,
and $?$ elsewhere.
\end{itemize}
Consequently the fiber size is
\[
 \prod_{C\text{ a cycle of }\sigma}g(|C|),\qquad
 g(1)=1,\quad g(2)=9,\quad g(m)=2^{m+1}+m-1\ (m\geq3).
\]
An arbitrary action $p[S]\cup q[S]$ is covered by normalizing the output
with $p^{-1}$ and taking $\sigma=p^{-1}q$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For necessity, the atomic upper bound gives the displayed row options.
Suppose a long cycle has row $0$ at $i$ and row $1$ at $j$. If
$i\ne\sigma(j)$, these are distinct singleton winners. The common-source
theorem requires one allowed leaf permutation producing both. But an
allowed matching on this cycle is either identity everywhere or shifted
everywhere. Neither does so. Thus $i=\sigma(j)$. If both kinds of
singleton occur there can be only one of each, giving precisely the
listed exceptional words. For a fixed point nonemptiness forces its
singleton; for a two-cycle this argument imposes no further restriction.

For synthesis on one cycle, start with the union of its two permutation
generators, giving all $?$ rows. To force $0$ at selected positions,
successively apply $U\mapsto U\star_i x$. This affects only the chosen
row and retains earlier $0$ rows. To force $1$ instead, use
$U\mapsto U\star_{\sigma(i)}\sigma(x)$. The exceptional word at $r$ is
$x\star_r\sigma(x)$. These constructions also realize all nine words
on a two-cycle.

The allowed leaf permutations already form the Cartesian product of
the two orientations on each cycle. All owned operations and union are
idempotent. Closure of a Cartesian product under idempotent
coordinatewise operations is the product of the local closures.
For two coordinates, suppose $t$ realizes $a$ from generators
$a_1,\ldots,a_n$ and $u$ realizes $b$ from $b_1,\ldots,b_m$.
Choose $z_{ij}=(a_i,b_j)$ from the full Cartesian product of generators.
Then
\[
 t\bigl(u(z_{11},\ldots,z_{1m}),\ldots,
         u(z_{n1},\ldots,z_{nm})\bigr)=(a,b):
\]
each inner application preserves $a_i$ by idempotency, and the outer
application preserves $b$ by idempotency. Induct on the number of
coordinates. Thus these local atomic tables
can be realized independently, by a term $t$ whose ambiguity support
lies in $\mathrm{id}\cup\sigma$.

Finally apply $\Delta(E,t)$ where $E(x)=x\cup\sigma(x)$.
On ambiguous inputs $t(S)\subseteq E(S)$ and $t(S)$ is ambiguous, so
reset retains exactly $E(S)$. On an atomic input the prescribed row
$t(\{i\})$ is either a singleton or the full allowed pair. In the first
case reset selects it; in the second $E(\{i\})$ already equals it.
This restores full ambiguity support without changing any atomic row.
The two uniform-orientation families overlap once; the $m$ exceptional
words are separate. This proves the count and independence.
\end{proof}

The example above violates this coherence condition at inputs $2$ and
$4$. A four-cycle has $35$ permitted tables, not the $3^4=81$ allowed
by its marginal bounds. Longer cycles give $68$ and $133$ at lengths
five and six. If every atomic row is a singleton, a long cycle must
choose one orientation everywhere; a two-cycle may also merge its two
roles.

\begin{corollary}[All four-role fibers without constant coordinates]
At $K=4$, every ambiguity action without constant coordinates is a union
of two permutations. There are $282$ such actions and exactly $6378$
unary term functions above them.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every canonical row has at most two sources. A singleton row forces its
matched source to occur nowhere else, since every edge extends to a
matching. Remove such pairs. In the remaining support graph all output
degrees are two, while every input degree is at least two: an input of
degree one would force its output to have degree one. Counting edges
makes all input degrees two as well. Components are even cycles, each
the union of two matchings. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:cycles}.

For normalized cycle types $1^4,\,2\,1^2,\,2^2,\,3\,1,\,4$, the numbers
of distinct support graphs are respectively $24,72,18,96,72$. These
follow by choosing the first permutation and the cycle permutation, then
dividing by the two orientation choices in each nontrivial component.
They sum to $282$. The fiber sizes are respectively $1,9,81,18,35$,
giving $6378$ in total. The enumeration in \texttt{check\_fibers.py}
independently checks every four-role support.
\end{proof}

\section{Reversible contexts at every number of roles}

\begin{lemma}
Every term function is monotone for inclusion on nonempty winner sets.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Constants, variables, and permutation images are monotone. Suppose
$S\subseteq S'$ and $T\subseteq T'$, all nonempty. If an upper operand
equals $\{r\}$, its corresponding lower operand is also $\{r\}$, and
both owned outputs equal $\{r\}$. Otherwise the upper output is
$S'\cup T'$. The lower output is either $S\cup T$, which is contained
in it, or $\{r\}$ supplied by one lower operand, which is contained in
the corresponding upper operand. Thus owned choice is monotone.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[All unary units]
For every $K\geq3$, the group of units of the full unary term monoid is
exactly $\Sym(I)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A unit and its term inverse are monotone, so they form an order
automorphism of the poset $\Pow(I)$. Such an automorphism permutes its
minimal elements, the singletons. Every set is determined by the
singletons below it, so that permutation determines the whole map.
Conversely every role permutation and its inverse are terms.
\end{proof}

This generalizes the three-role unit calculation without requiring a
higher-role classification of singleton fibers. It is a direct use of
the familiar order-automorphism argument for the Boolean subset poset.

\section{Equational validity is coNP-complete}

The decision problem takes two ordinary tree terms, with any number of
variables, and asks whether they agree at every nonempty-set valuation.
Fixing $K$ fixes the algebra and its signature. In the uniform version,
role names have binary encodings and permutations are explicit lists of
their images. A succinct encoding of terms by shared recursive
descriptions is not assumed.

\begin{theorem}[Equational complexity]\label{thm:complexity}
For every fixed $K\geq3$, equational validity is coNP-complete, already
in the signature with only variables and owned choices, without winner
constants or role switches. The uniform problem with $K$
as part of the input is coNP-complete as well.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $K$, a failed valuation is a polynomial certificate since the
algebra is finite and term evaluation is linear up to fixed constants.
For variable $K$, use the pair countervaluation theorem. A certificate
lists at most two role labels per input variable. Evaluate terms using
sparse sets: an owned node either collapses or unions its inputs, while
a permutation preserves cardinality. Each intermediate set has size at
most twice the number of variable leaves below it plus the number of
winner leaves. This is polynomial in tree-term size, independently of
the exponential size of the carrier and even when $K$ is binary encoded.
Evaluation and comparison are therefore polynomial. Validity is in coNP.

For hardness fix distinct roles $a,b$ and put $P(S)=[S=\{a\}]$.
On arbitrary nonempty inputs,
\[
 P(S\ow a T)=P(S)\lor P(T),\qquad
 P(S\ow b T)=P(S)\land P(T).
\]
The first equality is the absorbing-owner test. For the second, a
collapse to $\{b\}$ cannot pass $P$; otherwise a union is $\{a\}$ exactly
when both nonempty operands are $\{a\}$. Compile a constant-free monotone
Boolean formula $F$ to $\widehat F$ by replacing disjunction and
conjunction with these choices and leaving variables unchanged.
Then $P(\widehat F(v))=F(P\circ v)$ for every valuation, by induction.

The standard dual-rail reduction gives hardness directly. Given a CNF
formula, handle an empty clause by the fixed valid equation $x=x$,
and a CNF with no clauses by the fixed invalid equation $x=y$.
For the remaining $\varphi(z_1,\ldots,z_n)$, replace positive occurrences
of $z_i$ by $p_i$ and negative occurrences by $n_i$, producing a monotone
formula $\Phi(p,n)$. Put
\[
 A=\left(\bigwedge_i(p_i\lor n_i)\right)\land\Phi(p,n),
 \qquad B=\bigvee_i(p_i\land n_i).
\]
The equation $A\lor B=B$ fails exactly when $A=1$ and $B=0$.
Those conditions choose exactly one rail for every variable and satisfy
$\varphi$. Conversely any satisfying assignment provides such rails.
To reflect this Boolean equation as equality of winner sets, let $W$
be the union of all rail variables, expressed using a balanced fold of
$J$. Every constant-free term $u$ over those variables satisfies
$u(v)\subseteq W(v)$. Hence the constant-free gate
\[
 G_W(u)=u\ow a W
 =\begin{cases}
 \{a\},&u=\{a\}\text{ or }W=\{a\},\\
 W,&\text{otherwise}
 \end{cases}
\]
gives the identity
\[
 G_W(\widehat A\ow a\widehat B)=G_W(\widehat B).
\]
If $\varphi$ is unsatisfiable, the Boolean predicates agree on every
valuation. When $W=\{a\}$ both sides are $\{a\}$; otherwise the gate
maps equal predicates to the same winner set. If $\varphi$ is satisfiable,
assign each true rail $\{a\}$ and each false rail $\{b\}$.
Then $W=\{a,b\}$, $A=1$, and $B=0$, so the displayed identity fails.
These formulas use no Boolean constants in $A,B$.

Thus CNF unsatisfiability reduces to constant-free equation validity.
It is coNP-complete by the standard SAT theorem \cite{Cook1971}.
The balanced expansion of $W$ has $O(n^2)$ size: each $J$ uses its two
arguments twice. Formula sizes and their owned-choice translations are
polynomial, with no constants or switches. Hardness at $K=3$ also gives
uniform hardness.
\end{proof}

The gate uses the common input union as its false value, so it does not
require a named winner or $I$. The complexity method is standard; this
constant-free form follows the argument in
\texttt{notes/review-reset-2026-10-30.md}. Unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$,
the entire idempotent core has no polynomial-time canonicalization with
polynomial-time comparison. This does not preclude a finite equational
basis or an effective proof calculus.

\section{Evidence, attribution, and the next mathematical gap}

\texttt{Separation.lean} introduces syntax containing internal role
permutations, records the accumulated permutation at each source, and
proves the common-source theorem for arbitrary families of disjoint
target predicates. Its atomic corollary supplies a common permuted
variable for any injective family of singleton outputs. It also proves
the atomic and ambiguous relative-reset laws. The idempotent elimination,
optimal idempotency domains, semilattice trace, $S_2$ identification,
right-normal-band quotient, and general fiber classification remain
mathematical proofs. In particular the use of
$\operatorname{Pol}(\iota_3)=S_2$ is an explicit application of an external
theorem, not a fact currently imported into Lean.

\texttt{BooleanCore.lean} proves the gate, the two Boolean operations,
and the embedding of arbitrary monotone Boolean formula equations. It
uses ordinary syntax from the preceding modules and proves both
directions of validity preservation for all nonempty input valuations.
It also proves the singleton-predicate interpretation on arbitrary
nonempty inputs, constant-free monotone compilation, and the relative
gate using a containing input union. The SAT reduction and
complexity-class statements remain mathematical
proofs above. Lean 4.34.1 checks the module with only \texttt{Std}; the
axiom audit reports only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and
\texttt{Quot.sound}, with no admitted statements or new axioms.

\texttt{ambiguity.py} implements the matching criterion, witness
permutations, balanced syntax synthesis, and canonical composition.
\texttt{check\_general.py} compares two independent complete
enumerations at $K=3,4$: admissible canonical rows versus affine images
of the factorpower, itself enumerated by union closure of all
permutations. It checks semantic uniqueness, every composition at $K=3$,
sampled compositions at $K=4$, and actual term synthesis. It also checks
the Boolean gates through $K=6$ and the dual-rail equivalence on all
$4369$ CNFs with up to three clauses on two variables, including empty
clauses. It additionally compiles actual constant-free equation pairs for
$28$ CNFs, including a contradiction, and tests each on all $7^4$ mixed
valuations. These support the written proofs, rather than replacing them.

\texttt{core.py} implements the exact idempotent criterion and extracts
the equivalent constant-free owned term. \texttt{check\_core.py} compares
this with full diagonal evaluation on $1200$ terms at $K=3,\ldots,6$,
including transported and cancelling internal switches. It checks every
ambiguous domain at $K=3,4$, every domain against all $751$ unary functions
at $K=3$, and optimal constructions through $K=64$.

\texttt{fibers.py} constructs actual terms for the two-permutation
fibers and for all deterministic atomic operations in $S_2$.
\texttt{check\_fibers.py} independently exhausts closure of allowed
permutation leaves, compares it with the cycle formulas, and evaluates
the synthesized terms on every winner set. Cases include every cycle
type at $K=4$, cycles of lengths five and six, and two disjoint
three-cycles. It checks every four-role support without constant
coordinates. Separately it tests all $19683$ binary functions on three
roles against preservation of $\iota_3$, obtaining precisely the $1545$
functions in $S_2$, and synthesizes each one. These are independent
finite checks at the specified sizes, not generic proof certificates.

The factorpower, $S_2$ and its relation presentation, right normal bands,
binary separating codes, semidirect products, matching algorithms,
Boolean hardness reduction, and subset-poset automorphism argument are
reused established mathematics. The candidate contribution lies in
their exact relationship to rational owned choice, together with the
common-source and idempotent-elimination theorems and the exact cycle
fibers. Identifying the known objects does not establish priority for
these connections.

There is still no finite equational basis or proof calculus. The
all-arity pair theorem controls input tests, but not which tables on
those tests are realizable. The ambiguity quotient controls one part of
unary composition. Its two-permutation fibers are now known, while
general fibers with constant coordinates or larger source sets remain
open. The intrinsic idempotent core gives a smaller target for a uniform
presentation, with no constants or internal-switch constructors needed.
Its equation validity is already coNP-complete; reset's polynomial-time
normalization cannot extend to the whole core unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.
The right-normal-band quotient presents only homogeneous observations;
mixed inputs are an explicit remaining obstruction. The residual
bound needed by the conditional Willard route in \texttt{symmetric.tex}
also remains open. Reset itself has a published finite basis, imported
in \texttt{reset.tex} from Freese et al.\ \cite[Theorem~3.7]{FreeseEtAl2002};
it is no longer a finite-basis research target.

There is also a signature boundary for smaller fragments. Reset plus
one fixed $\star_a$, without switches, is a separate complexity question.
If all role permutations are available, it expresses every $\star_b$:
choose $\pi(a)=b$ and use
$\pi(\pi^{-1}x\star_a\pi^{-1}y)$. A family closed under all role
renamings and containing one owned choice therefore contains the whole
hard core. A tractable role-symmetric extension of reset must use weaker
owner-sensitive operations or restrict admissible syntax. No dependent
type-theoretic claim follows yet.
