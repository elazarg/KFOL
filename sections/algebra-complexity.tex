\section{The complexity of equations}\label{sec:complexity}

Equations between reset terms can be decided in polynomial time. What can we add and stay polynomial? Among the natural candidates, very little (\cref{tab:complexity}).

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Signature & Equation validity & \\
\midrule
any one of $\star_r$, $\cup$, $\del$ & polynomial & \cref{thm:raw-pairs}\\
any two of them, including $\star_a,\star_b$ & coNP-complete & \cref{thm:raw-pairs}\\
$\del$ and one $H_a$ & polynomial & \cref{owner:thm:one-table}\\
$H_a$ and $H_b$ & coNP-complete & \cref{thm:H-hardness}\\
$\mathbf C$, $\mathbf A$, or $\mathbf A^{\rm sym}$ & coNP-complete & \cref{gen:thm:complexity}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The complexity of equation validity for terms without shared subexpressions, at fixed $K$. Here $H_a$ is the guarded choice~\eqref{reset:eq:guarded}.}
\label{tab:complexity}
\end{table}

\begin{theorem}[Pair dichotomy]\label{thm:raw-pairs}
Fix $K$. For a signature chosen from $\{\star_r:r\in I\}\cup\{\cup,\del\}$, equation validity is polynomial if the signature has at most one operation, and coNP-complete otherwise.
\end{theorem}

\begin{idea}
A single semilattice operation only sees variable supports, and reset is \cref{reset:thm:three}. For hardness, one device runs through the reductions, which start from CNF unsatisfiability; here it is for reset with union. Fix an input $t$, and read a literal input as false when it equals $t$. The encoding is
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Boolean condition & winner-set condition\\
\midrule
literal $\ell$ false & $\ell=t$, for an atom $t$\\
clause $C$ true & $g_C=t\cup\bigcup_{\ell\in C}\ell$ is ambiguous\\
every clause true & $c$ keeps its winner in $A=(\cdots(c\del g_{C_1})\cdots)\del g_{C_m}$\\
both literals of $u$ true & $c$ keeps its winner in $(c\del(u\cup t))\del(\bar u\cup t)$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The equation is $A\del R=R$, where $R$ contains every other input together with the test of the last row for each Boolean variable. Unless both sides collapse to an atomic input, every winner other than that of $c$ already lies in $R$. So the sides differ exactly when $c$ keeps its winner in $A$ and in no test of $R$: every clause is true and no variable has both literals true, which is a satisfying assignment. An ambiguous $t$ would make every test keep $c$, so a counterexample has $t$ atomic.

Two owners use the same shape, reading a literal as false when it equals $\{a\}$. Clauses become $\star_b$-folds, which equal $\{a\}$ exactly when every literal does, and the chains become $\star_a$-folds, which keep a winner of $c$ exactly when no operand equals $\{a\}$. A literal equal to $\{b\}$ makes both sides $\{b\}$.
\end{idea}

\begin{corollary}[Equational complexity]\label{gen:thm:complexity}
Equation validity in $\mathbf C$, $\mathbf A$, and $\mathbf A^{\rm sym}$ is coNP-complete, at every fixed $K$ and with $K$ as part of the input.
\end{corollary}

Short certificates come from \cref{sym:thm:pairs}. So, unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$, the core has no polynomial-time canonicalization with polynomial-time comparison.

The tree representation matters. With shared subexpressions, equation validity is coNP-complete even for
reset alone, already when the variables range over the chain
$Z\subset U\subset T$ of \cref{reset:thm:three} (\cref{reset:thm:circuits}). Sharing lets successive clauses reuse one
continuation, so a circuit of linear size can compute an operation
that every reset term needs exponentially many leaves to express
(\cref{reset:prop:large-circuit-table}).

Preserving atoms does not help: $\{H_a,H_b\}$ is already coNP-complete (\cref{thm:H-hardness}). When $z$ is ambiguous, $H_s(z,y,u)$ keeps a winner of $u$ lying outside $z$ and $y$ exactly when $y\ne\{s\}$, and the atoms $\{a\}$ and $\{b\}$ are mutually exclusive values of $y$, which is all a Boolean variable needs.
