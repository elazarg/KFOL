\paragraph{Simultaneous bounds with one permitted atomic absorber.}
Arbitrarily many non-atomic bounds have a closed joint test when the
allowed atomic absorptions have one common owner. The bounds themselves
need not be nested or have the same allowed set.

Fix finite $V$, an owner $r$, and bounds
$t(w^h)\subseteq U_h$, indexed by $h$. Put
\[
 P_h=\{j:w^h_j\subseteq U_h\},\qquad
 E_s^h=\{j:w^h_j=\{s\}\}.
\]
Assume
\begin{equation}\label{core:eq:single-absorber-budget}
 E_s^h=\varnothing\quad\text{whenever }s\in U_h\setminus\{r\}.
\end{equation}
An allowed singleton produced by any core term at any bounded row
can therefore only be $\{r\}$.
Let $d$ be the Horn closure on $V$ generated by
\[
 E_r^h\longrightarrow V\setminus P_h\quad(r\in U_h),
 \qquad
 \varnothing\longrightarrow V\setminus P_h\quad(r\notin U_h),
\]
and set $G(D)=V\setminus d(V\setminus D)$. The closure may have loops.

\begin{theorem}[Profiles under simultaneous budgets]
\label{core:thm:simultaneous-profile-budgets}
Let $K\geq3$ and assume \eqref{core:eq:single-absorber-budget}.
For one requested positive coalition $A_s$ at each owner $s\in J$,
there is a core term respecting every bound and satisfying every
request if and only if
\[
 G\biggl(\bigcap_{s\in J\setminus\{r\}}A_s\biggr)
 \cap\begin{cases}A_r,&r\in J,\\V,&r\notin J\end{cases}
 \ne\varnothing.
\]
For a pair $\Phi_q(A)=1$, $\Phi_q(B)=0$, simultaneous feasibility is
equivalent to
\[
 \begin{cases}
  G(V\setminus B)\cap A\ne\varnothing,&q=r,\\
  G(A)\setminus B\ne\varnothing,&q\ne r.
 \end{cases}
\]
Consequently a prescribed full table $(\phi_q)_q$ has a core realizer
respecting all the bounds exactly when:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\phi_q(V)=1$ for every owner;
\item every pair with $\phi_q(A)=1$, $\phi_q(B)=0$ passes the displayed
  pair test;
\item every selection of winning coalitions $A_q$ for all owners satisfies
  \[
   G\biggl(\bigcap_{q\ne r}A_q\biggr)\cap A_r\ne\varnothing.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a nonempty support $S\subseteq V$, the $\star_r$-fold on $S$
respects row $h$ exactly when
\[
 S\subseteq P_h
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 r\in U_h\text{ and }S\cap E_r^h\ne\varnothing.
\]
Indeed an $r$-atomic input absorbs the fold; otherwise its value is the
union of the selected inputs. Complementing $S$ gives exactly the Horn
rules above. Thus $G(D)$ is the greatest safe fold support contained
in $D$.

For the two query forms it suffices to consider such folds. Start from
any simultaneous witness, retain every $r$-owned gate, and prune each
foreign-owned gate to one child. The $r$-profile can only increase:
foreign gates combine its bits by conjunction. At any row where an
original subword respects the bound, a foreign-owned root cannot
absorb: that would produce an allowed foreign singleton, contradicting
\eqref{core:eq:single-absorber-budget}. Both children are therefore
bounded there. At an $r$-root, an existing $r$-absorbing child stays
absorbing because its $r$-profile increases; otherwise both children
are bounded. Induction applies to each child's bounded rows and
preserves every original budget simultaneously.

The pruning choices also retain the requested profile information.
For a positive rainbow, at a deleted gate choose a child meeting its
owner's request; all other owners' requests already hold in both
children. At a retained gate distribute the requests according to
its profile disjunction/conjunction. For a positive/negative pair at
owner $q$, a deleted $q$-gate has a positive child and both children
negative on $B$; any other deleted gate has a negative child and both
positive on $A$. Retained gates again pass the appropriate requirements
to their children. These inductions leave only $r$-gates, hence a fold.

Such a fold has owner-$r$ profile $[S\cap A\ne\varnothing]$ and every
foreign profile $[S\subseteq A]$. Its support conditions give exactly
the two tests, since $G(D)$ is the greatest safe support in $D$.

Finally, the complete profile tables of all simultaneously bounded
terms form a relation closed under every mixed owner gate: combining
two values contained in $U_h$ stays in $U_h$. Apply
\cref{core:thm:profile-relations}. The pair tests account for all
same-owner implications, and the rainbow tests for all rainbow
conflicts. Top bits account for forced bits and extend partial
rainbows to all owners. The resulting table therefore belongs to
the bounded relation. This assembly can use all owners; the fold
normalization is required only for the individual queries.
\end{proof}

The closure incorporates genuine interactions between rows. For distinct
$a,b,c$, take $r=a$, both allowed sets $U_1=U_2=\{a,b\}$, and
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &x&y&z\\\hline
 w^1&\{c\}&\{a\}&\{a,b\}\\
 w^2&\{a,b\}&\{c\}&\{a\}.
\end{array}
\]
The requests $\Phi_a(\{x\})=1$ and $\Phi_b(\{x,y\})=1$ are feasible
under either bound separately: $x\star_a y$ works for the first and
$x$ for the second. Jointly, the Horn rules are
$\{y\}\to\{x\}$ and $\{z\}\to\{y\}$. A support contained in
$\{x,y\}$ has $z$ in its complement; closure forces first $y$ and
then $x$ into the complement. Thus $G(\{x,y\})=\varnothing$, and
the joint rainbow test fails. The theorem uses one closure of all
budgets, rather than a conjunction of separate one-row tests.
