\section{What one player can see}\label{sec:perspectives}

Suppose we care only about whether a player $k$ can win. How much of a value matters? Working in $\A$, let $S\equiv_k T$ when no context can tell them apart: $k\in f(S)\Leftrightarrow k\in f(T)$ for every unary polynomial $f$. This is the contextual, or Leibniz, congruence of the
logical matrix designating the sets containing $k$ \cite{BlokPigozzi1989,Font1993}.

\begin{proposition}[Player views]\label{view:thm:view}
$S\equiv_k T$ exactly when $S,T$ agree on whether they contain $k$ and on whether they equal $\{i\}$, for each $i\ne k$. So there are exactly $K+1$ classes:
$\pv$, the sets containing $k$; $a_i$, the atom $\{i\}$, for each $i\ne k$; and $\nv$, the ambiguous sets omitting $k$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{idea}
For $k\ne r$, the role $k$ belongs to $S\ow r T$ exactly when it belongs to an input and neither input is $\{r\}$. So this information is preserved by every operation. None of it can be dropped: the context $x\ow i\{k\}$ excludes $k$ exactly when $x=\{i\}$, so each other player's
atom is a trap that $k$ must recognize.
\end{idea}

Membership determines a set, so the $K$ views together are faithful: $\A$ is a subdirect product of the quotients $C_k=\A/{\equiv_k}$ (\cref{view:cor:product}). In $C_k$ each $\star_r$ is the join of a small order. For $r\ne k$, the atoms $a_i$ with $i\ne r$ are incomparable and lie below $\nv$, then comes $\pv$, and the absorbing atom $a_r$ is on top. For $r=k$, all the atoms lie below $\nv$, then comes $\pv$.

Views differ from guarantees. Forcing separates $\{k\}$ from $\{k,j\}$, whereas $k$'s view identifies them. Conversely, observing only unique winners collapses all ambiguous sets (\cref{game:prop:obs}) and leaves the coordinate operations of Olde Loohuis and Venema's guarantee algebra
\cite[Definition~3.20]{OldeLoohuisVenema2010}, restricted to its empty and singleton values. Possible winners retain what guarantees discard.

Internal role switching changes this picture. If $S,T$ differ at $i$, a permutation carrying $i$ to $k$ shows $k$ the difference, so the one-player quotient becomes equality, and the congruences of $\A$ (\cref{view:thm:congruences}) collapse to a chain of ambiguity thresholds
(\cref{view:thm:sym}).
