\section{Verification details}\label{app:verification}

The paper's results are kernel-checked in Lean~4.35.0 with mathlib. The root file \texttt{Paper.lean} restates, in the namespace \texttt{KFOL.Paper}, every theorem of the main text, the required clauses of report results cited by the main text, and the further facts the main text asserts: $132$ statements, listed by result in \cref{tab:status}. Each is followed by a check that its proof uses no axioms beyond \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}, and nothing is admitted. No theorem from the literature enters as a hypothesis. Where a written proof cites one, as for the reset basis and the minimum one-owner generator \cite{FreeseEtAl2002}, owned choice with union \cite{HardingRomanowska2017}, and the atomic clone \cite{Szendrei2012}, the Lean proof is direct.

The definitions are in \path{KFOL/Definitions}, apart from the proofs, and \path{notes/paper-definition-surface.md} lists every definition that each statement depends on. Winner sets are nonempty sets of roles. Subgame-perfect equilibrium is defined by full profiles and whole-player deviations in every subtree, independently of backward induction, and it agrees with Nash equilibrium of the GameTheory library in every subtree. Sealed rounds use assessments: sequential rationality at every information set, and consistency as a limit of completely mixed profiles. Complexity is measured on the deterministic multitape Turing machines of ComplexityLib, and coNP-completeness has its textbook meaning, with polynomial-time many-one reductions. Inputs are binary words encoding tree terms, with binary variable names, explicit permutations, and, when it is part of the input, $K$ in binary; the decoders reject malformed words. The algebraic modules import neither GameTheory nor ComplexityLib.

The production artifact is the single development in \path{KFOL/}. The earlier \texttt{Std}-only sources were retired and remain available at revision \texttt{7014dbb}; they are not current proof evidence. Report-only claims not represented below have written proofs and finite checks (\cref{tab:report}). The programs in \path{checks/} recompute small cases independently of Lean (\cref{app:finite-checks}). \texttt{make check paper} builds the production proofs, runs the audits and checks, and builds both manuscripts.

\subsection{Finite checks}\label{app:finite-checks}

The programs in \path{checks/} are independent of the Lean development. Except where a count is itself the result, they test implementations at the stated sizes, and for results in \texttt{Paper.lean} they serve as regression tests. They
enumerate small full profiles and deviations, synthesize the $131$
reduct and $751$ full three-role unary functions, and compare congruences,
tests, matching criteria, and fibers with independent constructions.
They check the Boolean reduction on $4369$ small CNFs and $28$ actual
constant-free equation pairs on all $7^4$ mixed valuations. Reset checks
compare antimatroid enumeration with term closure through arity four,
all $140^2$ products, and $20^4$ candidate root-fixed five-input tables,
and compare conservative models with forest enumeration. Separate
bit-parallel checks evaluate the native fragment equations on every
winner-set valuation: $11$ one-variable and $604$ two-variable CNFs at
$K=3$, with $823543$ valuations per two-variable reset/union equation
and $2401$ per two-variable $H$ equation. These include empty clauses,
contradictions, and tautological clauses. At $K=4$ the one-variable
reset/union equations are checked on all $759375$ valuations. The
complete-graph identities are checked on all $16807$ valuations at
$K=3$ and $11390625$ at $K=4$, with explicit next-role counterexamples.
Scalar evaluation independently checks witnesses and samples from the
bit-parallel tables. Separate
one-owner checks compare $403$ three-input terms on every $7^3$
valuation at $K=3$, and sample $K=4,5$, with explicit singleton-or-pair
separators. Two-owner checks compare both owners' guard semantics for
$303$ terms on every $7^3$ valuation and test $4000$ separators,
including an equation on which only the outsider disagrees. The
one-owner synthesis check compares every canonical candidate with full
four-state term closure through arity three ($1,4,27$ functions), and
all $8000$ root-fixed four-input tables with independent exhaustive
path search ($201$ accepted). It also synthesizes $750$ sampled terms
at arities $5,6,8$, checks their canonical tables and linear size bound,
and compares native winner-set evaluations. Another $250$ checks
compare the clone formula with native term substitution. The
four-state projection is also checked on all $33509$ triples of values
at $K=3,4,5$, together with the conservative concrete model and all
$512$ smaller candidate expansions. The basis-obstruction check verifies
all four proper-subalgebra embeddings with the native winner-set evaluator,
the separating valuation, and root expansion on every four-input
valuation at $K=3,4$. The conservative-presentation check evaluates all
seventeen equations on $\mathbf F$, compares them with passing-relation
coherence on all $569$ forest-based four-element candidates ($281$ pass),
and samples the guard-threshold assertions. It also checks the
nonconservative countermodel and the irreducible quotient using native
winner-set operations. The unrestricted-basis check evaluates all thirty-six
equations on $10420$ native valuations of $\mathbf F$. It checks
the two-source certificates in both directions for all $20412$ reset
and $H_a$ products of the free three-generated algebra, and checks
normalization and certificates for $240$ sampled identities at
arities $4,6,8$.
The pair-dichotomy checks cover all
$604$ two-variable CNFs at $K=3$: $16807$ valuations per equation
for two owned choices and $823543$ for reset with owned choice.
At $K=4$ all $11$ one-variable CNFs are checked on $3375$ and
$759375$ valuations respectively. Separate
models execute dependent phase and sealing rules. These computations
check their finite implementations, not the conversion or normalization
metatheory of the dependent data calculus.
Private-connection checks enumerate all $32$ full profiles of a finite
dependent-vector protocol, all seven cuts and $28$ three-fragment splits.
They check the complete decision domains and unilateral deviations,
stable indices and pending cells, hidden views, and composition of the
sealing scan. Restricting a suffix to the actual prefix store is tested
as a rejected loss of off-path strategy data.
Sealed-round checks compare complete rational Nash support rectangles
with direct unilateral deviations on a grid for $256$ two-by-two
continuation tables. They check $2209$ diagonal games, rational synthesis
through denominator $100$, $24$ reciprocal games and $576$ threshold
contexts, as well as role transport, commitment exchange, and the
nonconvex distribution-set witness.
For the open-game obstruction of \cref{se:sec:open-game-obstruction}, an exact-rational checker reproduces the unit, incomparable-bracketing, and convex-repair witnesses. \path{scripts/check_external_open_game.py} rebuilds the pinned external Lean artifact from an isolated checkout under its original Lean~4.32.2, checks its source hashes, and audits the axioms of its five theorems; it is not part of \texttt{make check}.

Selective-reset checks compare $123$ native two-owner terms at each
of $K=3,4,5$, exhaustively on the $7^3$ three-role valuations and by
sampling larger carriers. They compare the original two-owned-choice
expression with selective reset on every triple of winner sets at those
sizes. Every nonempty proper blocking policy receives guard-semantics
and explicit-separator checks.

Mixed-policy checks compare $70$ three-input terms, hence $4900$ ordered
pairs, in each of eleven configurations. Nested chains including both
endpoint policies are checked at $K=3,4,5$ on all winner-set valuations,
separately for every observer. Four crossing configurations at $K=5$
are checked against full equality and a complete observer. Four
covering-policy configurations at $K=4,5$ receive the same checks.
The joint-test normalization is also checked on $100$ depth-four
three-input terms at $K=3$, on every valuation and observer. Every failed
guard comparison gets an explicit least-guard separator, checked with
the scalar set evaluator. Native two-policy hardness reductions are
checked on all $604$ two-variable CNFs for a disjoint pair at $K=3$,
and for an observer-relative overlapping pair at $K=3,4$; the respective
valuation counts are $2401$, $2401$, and $50625$ per equation. The
five-role easy-algebra/hard-observer example is checked on all $11$
one-variable CNFs and $29791$ valuations each. The three pairwise-overlapping
policies on three roles are checked on the same $604$ two-variable CNFs.
The calibrated full-equation reduction is checked on all $604$
two-variable CNFs at $K=3$, with $117649$ valuations per equation,
and all $11$ one-variable CNFs at $K=4$, with $11390625$ valuations
per equation. A fifth-role countervaluation checks the resulting
separation of equational theories.

Two-player checks compute the term functions by exact closure at arities
$1$ and $2$, with counts $6$, $84$ and, with the middle constant, $11$, $197$.
They compare the normal-form criterion with the three-element order on
$40000$ random pairs, and the sealed-round class with the two serializations on
all $21243$ class matrices up to $3\times3$ and with exact values on $120099$
games with two rows.

The arbitrary-family checks consider every subfamily of the six
nonempty proper policies on three roles, forty sampled four-role
families, a four-role family requiring three minimal passing patterns
in its hardness construction, and four larger examples. In the tested cases with an
intersection-closed pattern family and a repeated pattern, they compare
the guard algorithm with complete three-input winner-set tables and
check explicit separators. In the hard configurations they evaluate
the reductions expanded into the original policy signature. All eleven
one-variable CNFs are checked exhaustively for the three-role cases;
four-role cases check an empty conjunction, an empty clause, and a
contradiction exhaustively; the selected four-role family checks all
eleven one-variable CNFs. The five-role example with every pair easy
and the triple hard checks all eleven one-variable CNFs on all $29791$
valuations. The five-role case with all patterns distinct uses scalar
samples and explicit SAT witnesses. General union-of-tests normalization is also
checked against native evaluation, including hard families.
The reset-circuit checks evaluate all eleven one-variable and all
$604$ two-variable CNFs on every three-role valuation, respectively
$2401$ and $117649$ per circuit equation, and the one-variable cases
on all $50625$ four-role valuations. They independently check every
valuation on the fixed three-element reset chain and the exact linear
gate count.

The core-basis check verifies the seven dissemilattice laws on
$163484$ native winner-set triples at $K=3,4,5$, and performs
$3726$ operation comparisons for the three-state embeddings, over
every ordered triple of distinct roles. It also checks the failed
reverse distributive law.

The core-obstruction check makes $200616$ quotient-map comparisons
and checks all $446$ generator sets of size at most $K$ in the
countermodels at $K=3,4,5,6$. It verifies their owner-exhaustion failures
and all $2401$ native valuations of that identity at $K=3$.
The binary classification is checked on every native valuation of
all $31176$ products in the free two-generated algebras at $K=3,4,5$,
of sizes $20,37,70$, respectively.

For the core results that remain written, \path{checks/check_core_unary_polynomials.py} enumerates the entire $131$-function unary polynomial closure at $K=3$ and compares it with the normal forms, and at $K=4$ it verifies all $6575$ functions by explicit synthesis, with their distinctness and stabilization. Conservative models are checked on chains of sizes two through six. The atomic quotient is checked at $K=3,\ldots,6$ and near-unanimity at $K=3,\ldots,8$. \path{checks/check_core_atomic_basis.py} makes $675733$ equation evaluations for the atomic basis, the lifting basis, and its normalization consequences, using every observer and $D_K$ at $K=3,\ldots,6$ and fully expanded core terms at $K=3$. The $236$ lattice and absorber templates leave exactly the $18$ predicted two-element quotients, and $D_K$ passes the lifts and fails exhaustion. All principal ideals of $247$ models verify the closure maps, meets, kernel equality, and normalized operations on $32343$ input pairs.
