\section{Verification details}\label{app:verification}

The formal development uses Lean 4.35.0. The legacy modules in \texttt{lean/} use only \texttt{Std}; the independent algebra slice in \texttt{KFOL/} uses mathlib v4.35.0 and exposes complete migrated statements in root \texttt{Paper.lean}. Neither development has admitted proofs or added axioms; audits report only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound} where needed. The algebra audit also checks compiled transitive imports, excluding GameTheory and complexity libraries. \Cref{tab:status} records the evidence for each main result: ``Lean'' names kernel-checked theorems, ``written'' means a mathematical proof in this paper, and ``external'' means a theorem imported from the literature, which is not a Lean axiom. Finite checks test their implementations at stated sizes. Run \texttt{make check paper} to reproduce them.

The shared equational infrastructure in \path{KFOL/Equational} reuses mathlib's terms, homomorphisms, substructures and quotients. It proves soundness and completeness for arbitrary finitary signatures, HSP preservation, and identity transfer from subalgebras generated by bounded variable supports. Checked full/core codecs preserve syntax, evaluation, support and substitution. Regression cases include unused variables, nullary identities, empty products, and empty zero-generator core closures. These are foundations for the basis proofs; the reset-specific development below proves six-law completeness. The forty-law presentation and uniform nonfinite-basis construction remain separate obligations.

\subsection{Written proofs and finite checks}

Complete congruence classifications,
the external $S_2$ step, unary fibers,
polynomial machine bounds for table algorithms, forest membership in the reset
variety, and full complexity reduction have written proofs here. Finite
checks do not make them generic Lean theorems. Reset basis completeness
is now kernel-checked by the path argument in \path{KFOL/Reset/Basis}.
The candidate-table criterion, compact realization size and executable
recognizer are checked in \path{KFOL/Reset/Realizability}; its path selection
proves existence rather than providing an executable syntax synthesizer.
Dependent script metatheory and hiding also have written proofs.
For sealed rounds, the SE correspondence, consistency and public-boundary
pasting, semialgebraic closure, scalar game realization and contextual
full abstraction have written proofs in \cref{app:proofs-sealed}.
\texttt{SealedRounds.lean} proves the stage and arithmetic certificates;
it does not define a general SE assessment or connect the full round
syntax to the threshold probes internally.
For the smaller hard fragments, \texttt{Fragments.lean} checks the
semantic certificates under the stated baseline-containment hypotheses,
including both absorption and fresh-marker separation. Assembly of
the syntactic reductions, their polynomial tree sizes, and the final
complexity statements are written proofs in \cref{app:proofs-hard}.
\texttt{RawFragments.lean} additionally checks the semantic fold,
absorption, and guard-transfer certificates for the reductions of the pair dichotomy;
it does not formalize the CNF syntax assembly or polynomial reductions.
For the tractable fragment, \texttt{OwnerFragments.lean} proves the
generic guard criterion and executable checker correctness, including
\path{Term.single_table_equation_iff}, \path{Term.equationB_correct},
and, in \texttt{OwnerModels.lean}, \path{Term.four_element_complete}.
The minimum-generator proof uses Freese et al.'s three-element
subalgebra theorem externally; its remaining ternary obstruction is
kernel-checked as \path{three_state_obstruction}. Polynomial size bounds and the
observer-relative complexity dichotomy are written deductions in
\cref{app:proofs-owner}.
The coloring proof has a generic Lean pigeonhole/guard certificate;
the complete identity family and its role-set embeddings are proved
mathematically there.

The arbitrary-family checks consider every subfamily of the six
nonempty proper policies on three roles, forty sampled four-role
families, a four-role family requiring three minimal passing patterns
in its hardness construction, and four larger examples. In the tested cases with an
intersection-closed pattern family and a repeated pattern, they compare
the guard algorithm with complete three-input winner-set tables and
check explicit separators. In the hard configurations they evaluate
the reductions expanded into the original policy signature. All eleven
one-variable CNFs are checked exhaustively for the three-role cases;
four-role cases check an empty conjunction, an empty clause, and a
contradiction exhaustively; the selected four-role family checks all
eleven one-variable CNFs. The five-role example with every pair easy
and the triple hard checks all eleven one-variable CNFs on all $29791$
valuations. The five-role case with all patterns distinct uses scalar
samples and explicit SAT witnesses. General union-of-tests normalization is also
checked against native evaluation, including hard families.
The reset-circuit checks evaluate all eleven one-variable and all
$604$ two-variable CNFs on every three-role valuation, respectively
$2401$ and $117649$ per circuit equation, and the one-variable cases
on all $50625$ four-role valuations. They independently check every
valuation on the fixed three-element reset chain and the exact linear
gate count. These are finite checks of the constructions, not generic
formalizations of the complexity theorems.

The core-basis check verifies the seven dissemilattice laws on
$163484$ native winner-set triples at $K=3,4,5$, and performs
$3726$ operation comparisons for the three-state embeddings, over
every ordered triple of distinct roles. It also checks the failed
reverse distributive law. The classical generation theorem remains an
external input.

The core-obstruction check makes $200616$ quotient-map comparisons
and checks all $446$ generator sets of size at most $K$ in the
countermodels at $K=3,4,5,6$. It verifies their owner-exhaustion failures
and all $2401$ native valuations of that identity at $K=3$.
The binary classification is checked on every native valuation of
all $31176$ products in the free two-generated algebras at $K=3,4,5$,
of sizes $20,37,70$, respectively. These finite checks do not replace
the arbitrary-$K$ proofs.
