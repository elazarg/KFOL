\section{Verification details}\label{app:verification}

The paper's results are kernel-checked in Lean~4.35.0 with mathlib. \texttt{Paper.lean} restates, in the namespace \texttt{KFOL.Paper}, every theorem of the main text and the clauses of the report results that it cites: $77$ statements, listed by result in \cref{tab:status}. \texttt{Report.lean} restates $64$ further report results in the namespace \texttt{KFOL.Report} (\cref{tab:report}). Each proof is a single library theorem, and each file ends with a check that no declaration in its namespace uses an axiom beyond \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}; a separate audit covers the whole library. Nothing is admitted, and no theorem from the literature enters as a hypothesis. Where a written proof cites one, as for the reset basis and the minimum one-owner generator \cite{FreeseEtAl2002}, owned choice with union \cite{HardingRomanowska2017}, and the atomic clone \cite{Szendrei2012}, the Lean proof is direct.

The vocabulary of the statements is in \path{KFOL/Definitions/PaperAlgebra.lean}, \path{PaperGames.lean}, and \path{Complexity.lean}, which build on the other files of \path{KFOL/Definitions}. \path{notes/audit-guide.md} gives a reading order, and \path{notes/paper-definition-surface.md} and \path{notes/report-definition-surface.md} list every definition that each statement depends on.

Winner sets are nonempty sets of roles. Subgame-perfect equilibrium uses full profiles and whole-player deviations in every subtree, independently of backward induction, and agrees with the GameTheory library's Nash equilibrium in every subtree. Sealed rounds use assessments with sequential rationality at every information set and consistency as a limit of completely mixed profiles. Complexity uses ComplexityLib's deterministic multitape Turing machines, explicit encodings, and polynomial-time many-one reductions. Tree inputs have binary variable names and explicit permutation arrays; uniform inputs also encode $K$ in binary. The decoders reject malformed words. The independent algebra modules import neither GameTheory nor ComplexityLib.

The library also proves the contracts of the implementation, such as codecs, descriptors and machines (\path{KFOL/Results/Certificates.lean}). The other report results have written proofs, most with finite checks (\cref{tab:report}). The programs in \path{checks/} recompute small cases independently of Lean (\cref{app:finite-checks}). \texttt{make check paper} builds the Lean development, runs the audits and checks, and builds both manuscripts.

For \cref{app:contexts-bottom}, \path{check_structure.py} independently
computes the contextual partitions by closing membership observations
under all allowed unary translations. It checks all $248$ choices of
observer and owner set at $K=3,4,5$, including the common-unit criterion.
It checks the unit and annihilator extensions on all operands through
$K=5$. Separately, it enumerates all $9416$ three-player binary games
with at most two decision nodes and arbitrary terminal winning sets,
and compares the family recurrence with full profiles checked against
whole-player deviations in every subgame. These include the
everyone-loses counterexample; no equilibrium recurrence is used by
the full-profile checker.

For \cref{app:contexts-collective}, the same script checks the
shared-objective semilattice and union laws for every coalition through
$K=5$, comparing its formula with pointwise optimal choices. For all
$44$ partitions into at least three teams at $K=3,4,5$, it checks the
split quotient and embedding and every outcome-observer homomorphism
on all input pairs. It also enumerates all $3531$ games with three
terminal labels, at most two binary decisions, and arbitrary subsets
as controller objectives, including overlapping and repeated
objectives. Whole-controller deviations in every subgame independently
reproduce the predicted SPE winners. The delegation collapse,
common-admissibility failure, nondefinability witnesses, and the
two-team distributivity counterexample are checked as well.

\path{check_decision_rules.py} enumerates all $729$ nonempty rules on
ordered pairs of three outcomes that select only offered outcomes.
It compares the atomic and lifted commutativity and associativity
conditions, checks idempotency and union distribution, and tests the
transport criterion on $5832$ rule/map pairs, including incompatible
ones. For scalar utilities with values in $\{0,1,2\}$, it checks
pullback along every map between three-element outcome sets and
compares the lifted recurrence with independent full-controller SPE
deviations on all $39612$ games with at most two decision nodes.
It also checks all $512$ three-voter binary-payoff profiles on three
outcomes and the aggregation/lifting comparison at every quota, the
ranked-majority cycle, noninjective preferred-outcome maps and repeated
rule tags, and all $270$ strict-or-indifferent preference profiles for
three- and five-voter majority ballot games. The original-owner
proposal collapse is checked on every owner set of size at least three,
every pair of nonempty inputs, and every quota below unanimity through
$K=6$.
