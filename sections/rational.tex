\section{Owned choice}\label{sec:rational}

Throughout, $I$ is a finite set of $K\geq3$ \emph{roles}, or players; $a,b,c$ are distinct roles, $r$ is a role acting as an owner, and $k$ is a role acting as an observer. A game is a finite binary tree: a leaf $\at i$ declares that $i$ wins, and a node $G\ch r H$ is
a public choice by its \emph{owner} $r$. A role values its own win at one and every other outcome at zero, so a role that cannot win has no opinion about who does. Ties are therefore everywhere, and we keep all of them.

A \emph{full profile} prescribes an action at every node, including nodes it avoids. A deviation by $i$ may change all of $i$'s actions, and a \emph{subgame-perfect equilibrium} (SPE) is a profile that no deviation improves in any subtree. The value $\den G$ is
the set of roles that win in some SPE of $G$.

Behavior at unreached nodes is what equilibria are made of. In
\[
 D=(\at a\ch b\at c)\ch a(\at b\ch b\at c),
\]
player $b$ takes its own win on the right and is indifferent on the left. If it prescribes winner $c$ on the left, the root owner $a$ loses either way and may go right. So $b$ can win, thanks to what it would do in a subtree never entered, and $\den D=\{a,b,c\}$.

Values range over the set $\Pow$ of nonempty subsets of $I$, composed by the operation $\star_r$ of \eqref{game:eq:own}, and valuations assign values to variables. Call a singleton an \emph{atom} and a larger set \emph{ambiguous}.

\begin{proposition}[Backward induction]\label{game:thm:adequacy}
Every game has a full pure SPE profile, and
$\den{\at i}=\{i\}$, $\den{G\ch r H}=\den G\ow r\den H$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{idea}
If $r$ is the only equilibrium winner of a branch, every parent equilibrium is won by $r$, since $r$ can always enter that branch. Otherwise each branch has an equilibrium in which $r$ loses. Playing it in one branch lets $r$ rationally go to the other, whichever equilibrium is played there, so every equilibrium winner of either branch can be realized.
\end{idea}

Terms are given by
\[
 t ::= \at i\mid x_j\mid t\ch r t\mid\pi(t),
 \qquad \pi\in\Sym(I),
\]
where $\pi(t)$ renames the roles of a component, so that its value is the image under $\pi$ of the value of $t$. Repeated occurrences of a variable are independently solved copies. We write $\mathbf A^{\rm sym}$ for this full signature, $\mathbf A$ for its reduct without permutations, and $\mathbf C$ for owned choices alone.

Each $\star_r$ is a semilattice operation, so a quantifier over a finite nonempty action set is a fold and binary choice suffices. With only two roles, ground terms would be Boolean: the two owned choices are join and meet once a role is chosen as truth. With three there is genuine ambiguity, and all of it is reachable.

\begin{proposition}[Neutral union]\label{game:thm:union}
For pairwise distinct $r,s,t$,
\begin{equation}\label{game:eq:union}
 J(x,y)=(x\ow r y)\ow t(x\ow s y)=x\cup y.
\end{equation}
Consequently every member of $\Pow$ is the value of a game.
\end{proposition}

If just one inner choice collapses, its owner's atom belongs to the other result, and the outer owner is different; if both collapse, the outer choice unites them. We simply write $\cup$ from now on.

The algebra is not Boolean in any coordinates. Indifference breaks distributivity: $\den{(\at a\ch a\at b)\ch b\at c}=\{a,c\}$, whereas distributing $\ch b$ over $\ch a$ gives $D$ (\cref{cor:no-boolean-product}).
