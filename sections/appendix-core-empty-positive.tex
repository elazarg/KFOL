\paragraph{A closure criterion when all losses use one owner.}
Suppose every loss is pure at the same owner $r$. A bounded
word supplying a marked query $(i,p)$ can be replaced by an
$r$-fold on a subset of its support supplying the same query.
To do this, keep both children at $r$-gates and one child
at every foreign gate. On the marked path choose a child
carrying the mark. Elsewhere, whenever the current subword
loses the $r$-test on $p_r$, choose a child still losing it.
At an $r$-gate losing the test makes both children lose it,
and at a foreign gate such a child always exists by
conjunction. The marked child loses that test as well.
Since the root supplies the mark, every kept leaf avoids
$p_r$, and at least one is $i$.

This pruning preserves omission of every pure $r$-loss
already omitted by a subword. On such a probe no foreign
gate can absorb, so if the subword omits the mark at a
foreign gate both children do. At an $r$-gate with an
absorbing child its $r$-profile stays winning, because
pruning foreign conjunctions can only increase the
$r$-profile. If neither child absorbs, both omit the mark
and their prunings still do. This proves boundedness of
the resulting fold by induction.

Write $L(B)$ for $B$ together with the heads of all losses
whose postings are contained in $B:r$, and $C(B)$ for the
least fixed point of $L$ containing $B$. The complement of
$C(p_r)$ is the greatest safe $r$-fold support avoiding
$p_r$: each row at a used head has a posting in the support
exactly when the complement is closed under $L$. Thus the
marked query is feasible exactly when $i\notin C(p_r)$,
regardless of the foreign postings in the query.

Consequently a complete nonempty-valued candidate with
pure $r$-losses is core exactly when $L$ is idempotent.
In that case it is a named union of $r$-folds. Necessity
and sufficiency follow by comparing the specified loss
set $L(B)$ with the forced losses $C(B)$ for each pure
background $B:r$. All other backgrounds give the same
comparison at their $r$-block.
Idempotence can be tested directly from primes without
listing losses. At each head $i$ and one observer
different from $r$, take each prime in turn and use
the largest pure $r$-background $B:r$ disjoint from that
prime and leaving $i$ unposted. Check that $i\notin C(B)$
by iterating the prime-based loss test for $L$. Every
candidate positive pure background is contained in one
of these maximal backgrounds, so monotonicity makes the
checks sufficient. This is polynomial even with variable
$K$.

When the criterion holds, the distinct maximal positive
$r$-backgrounds across the heads are exactly the
meet-irreducible closed sets of $L$, with $V$ excluded.
Indeed a maximal background keeping head $i$ is closed,
since its closure still avoids $i$. It is maximal among
closed sets avoiding $i$. Conversely in a finite closure
lattice every proper meet-irreducible has a unique upper
cover. Take any input $i$ in that cover but outside the
set; all strictly larger closed sets contain $i$.
The operation therefore has a canonical irredundant union
of $r$-folds: take the fold on the complement of each such
closed set. The fold for a set $B$ alone supplies the mark
of an input $i$ witnessing its maximality on the background
$B:r$ among folds on these complements. More generally,
any bounded $r$-fold supplying that mark has a closed
complement containing $B$ and avoiding $i$, hence must
be the same fold.

With union named, the minimum occurrence count
of an individual input in a one-owner operation
is at most two. It is zero for an unused input,
two for a retained input whose posting affects
another source, and one for every other live
input. To see that foreign gates cannot improve
the count, test the operation on probes with only
$r$-postings and ambiguous marked menus. Every
foreign gate takes union there. Replacing them
by named union preserves counts and these bits,
which determine both one-owner guard families.

To prove the upper bounds, fix a live input $x$
and use the canonical union of folds. If the
support $\{x\}$ is absent, factor all supports
containing $x$ as $x\star_r H$, where $H$ is the
union of the folds on their nonempty remainders.
Distributivity of $\star_r$ over union gives
exactly the original summands, so one occurrence
suffices. The support $\{x\}$ is present exactly
when $x$ is retained: no larger fold can supply
its foreign mark when all passive inputs are
$\{r\}$. If this singleton support is present,
keep it separately and factor the other supports
as above. This uses at most two occurrences.
When the posting of a retained $x$ affects no
other source, the operation is $x+\partial_x F$
and uses it once.

For the lower bound, a sole occurrence of a
retained input cannot have an $r$-gate on its
path. At the all-$r$ passive valuation any such
gate would absorb its foreign mark. The path
therefore consists of unions, which cannot
introduce dependence of another source on the
posting of $x$. This proves the claimed minimum.

The individual minima need not be simultaneous.
On three inputs take the union of the three
$r$-folds on pairs. Every input is live and
unretained, so its individual minimum is one.
Its $r$-profile is Boolean majority. A monotone
formula with one occurrence per input would
split at the root into a singleton and a pair.
An AND would make the singleton necessary and
an OR would make it sufficient. Neither holds
for majority, so at least one input must repeat
in every realization.

In the primitive signature, a live unretained
input $x$ of a one-owner operation has a one-occurrence
realization exactly when every canonical support
contains $x$. In a one-occurrence word, every
path gate at a foreign owner would absorb on its
uniform passive diagonal, contradicting the
absence of that owner's exclusions. Thus the
path uses only $r$, and $X=\{r\}$ forces the
output $\{r\}$. For the canonical fold union,
the $r$-profile on the singleton coalition
$\{x\}$ wins exactly when every support meets
it. This proves necessity. If all supports
contain $x$ and none is singleton, the factored
term $x\star_r H$ above uses it once; all named
unions inside $H$ are passive and can be expanded.
For a retained $x$, any nonempty path would
absorb its mark on a uniform passive diagonal,
so one occurrence is possible only for the
projection.

For an unretained input failing the common-support
condition, the primitive minimum is two. Its
factored word is the union of $x\star_r H$ with a
passive term. For menus $A,B$ and three distinct
owners $r,s,t$, the identity
\[
 A\cup B=(A\star_r B)\star_t(A\star_s B)
\]
uses each argument twice: at least one inner gate
takes union, and the outer gate can absorb only
if $A=B=\{t\}$. This also gives the exact minimum
two for an independent retained input in a
nonprojection. If $x$ is retained and all supports
contain it, its nonprojection term is the union of
$x$ and $x\star_r H$. This union uses two $x$'s
primitively, by the identity
\[
 x+(x\star_r H)=x\star_r\bigl(H\star_t(x\star_s H)\bigr).
\]
Indeed the inner $t$-gate retains $H$, and when
$x\ne\{r\}$ its value cannot be $\{r\}$, so the
outer gate joins $x$ to it. When $x=\{r\}$ both
sides absorb. In the remaining retained case let
$B$ be the passive union of supports omitting $x$.
Three occurrences always suffice, by the identity
\[
 B+x+(x\star_r H)=
 \bigl(x\star_r(H+B)\bigr)\star_s
 \bigl(x\star_t B\star_t(x\star_r H)\bigr).
\]
If $x=\{r\}$, the left operand of the final gate
is $\{r\}$ and the right supplies $B$ except when
$B=\{t\}$, in which case it supplies that atom.
Their join therefore gives $B\cup\{r\}$. If
$x\ne\{r\}$, the left operand gives the full union
unless $H=B=\{r\}$, when the two operands together
give $x\cup\{r\}$. The right operand can return
$\{s\}$ only when $x,B,x\star_r H$ are all
$\{s\}$, which forces $H=\{s\}$ as well. Hence
the final $s$-gate gives the required union in
all cases. The named union inside $H+B$ is passive.

When both $B$ and $H$ are present, the exact count
is two if every input used in $H$ is used in $B$,
and three otherwise. For the two-copy construction
use
\[
 \bigl(x\star_r(H+B)\bigr)\star_s(x\star_t B).
\]
At $x=\{r\}$ it gives $B\cup\{r\}$, since one
operand supplies $r$ and the other supplies $B$
or its absorbing $t$-atom. Otherwise the left
operand supplies the full union unless
$H=B=\{r\}$, when the right operand supplies
the missing $x$. The outer $s$-gate can absorb
only when the desired union is already $\{s\}$.
For the right operand this uses
$B=\{s\}\Rightarrow H=\{s\}$: a union of
$r$-folds can give a foreign singleton only when
all inputs in its support are that singleton.
For the left operand it follows directly from
its value.

For necessity, suppose some $y$ occurs in $H$
and not in $B$, and a primitive realizer has
two $x$-occurrences. Their least common ancestor
must be the root, since any gate above it would
absorb the retained $x$ on a uniform passive
diagonal. Write $u$ for the root owner. Neither
path below the root can contain a $u$-gate: on
the all-$u$ passive diagonal that child would
absorb and the root would then suppress $x$.
Likewise both paths cannot contain an $r$-gate,
since both would lose $x$ on the all-$r$ diagonal.

If $u=r$, neither child's $y$-coefficient can
change when $x$ is posted at $r$, because neither
$x$-path contains an $r$-gate. Mark $y$ at a
foreign owner and put all other passive inputs
at a distinct foreign singleton. The target has
that second winner from $B$ with $x$ either neutral
or posted at $r$, so the root cannot absorb.
Yet the target loses the $y$-mark exactly when
$x$ is posted at $r$, a contradiction.

If $u\ne r$, both children must use $y$.
Otherwise setting every input except $y$ to
$\{u\}$ makes one child $\{u\}$ and the root
loses any different $y$. At least one child's
$x$-path has no $r$-gate. It transmits the $y$-mark
on the all-ambiguous probe and its $y$-coefficient
is independent of $x:r$. Posting $x$ at $r$
must therefore keep that mark in the child.
The target loses it and retains $r$, so the root
cannot absorb at $u$ to remove it. This proves
the lower bound, attained in particular by
$z+x+(x\star_r y)$ on independent inputs.

The same path argument gives a general obstruction
for two primitive occurrences of a retained input
$x$. At a proposed root owner $u$, let $E_u$ consist
of passive heads whose source survives when all
other inputs are posted at $u$. If a two-copy
realizer has root owner $u$, then both of the
following tests must hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item If posting $x$ at $u$ suppresses a passive
marked winner different from $u$, compared with
the same probe and $x$ neutral, the posted probe
must have output $\{u\}$.
\item For any $r\ne u$, a head in $E_u$ must
survive the probe whose only passive posting
is $x:r$.
\end{itemize}
Indeed retention forces the least common ancestor
of the two copies to be the root. Its owner $u$
is absent from both paths below it, and no other
owner can occur on both paths, by the uniform
passive diagonals. The first test follows because
only the root can then suppress the mark due to
$x:u$. For the second test each child must use
every head in $E_u$: otherwise it gives $\{u\}$
on that head's all-$u$ passive probe and the root
suppresses the head. At least one child has no
$r$-gate on its $x$-path. This child still supplies
the head when $x$ is posted at $r$ with all other
inputs neutral, by its independence of that
posting. The operation retains $r$ from $x$, so
the root cannot absorb at $u$. Both tests use only
source probes and guard evaluations. One violation
for each possible root owner therefore gives a
polynomial-size certificate that a nonprojection
with retained $x$ needs at least three primitive
occurrences of $x$, with no restriction to one
posting owner.

Both tests are decidable in polynomial time on
complete prime lists. The second uses only the
specified pure probes. For the first, choose the
head $y$ and observer $k\ne u$ of the suppressed
mark. A violation on its canonical probe must
also supply a winner $\ell\notin\{u,k\}$ from
some passive head $z\ne x,y$: the posted $x$
retains $u$, and the only possible $k$-source
was $y$. Enumerate a prime at $y$ for the neutral
setting of $x$ and a prime at $z$ on the
$\ell$-facet for the posted setting.

On the $k$-facet at $y$, keep only $x:u$ at $x$;
at $z$ allow only the $\ell$-posting or the neutral
status, so its menu can supply $\ell$. Forbid the
postings excluded by the first prime, ignoring
its exclusions at the neutral $x$. For the
second prime check its compatibility with the
fixed $x:u,y:k$ and forbid its other exclusions.
The $\ell$-postings seen by the first observer
are simply absent from this second prime, as
required by its facet. A violation exists for
these choices exactly when the allowed postings
hit every $y$-prime on the $k$-facet. By
\cref{core:thm:guard-consensus}, a minimal
transversal gives a consistent loss; adding
$x:u$ if needed preserves consistency and both
enabled primes. Conversely, every violating
probe gives such a hitting set and two enabled
primes. This test uses only alphabet restrictions
and prime scans, at any prime width.

At three owners, if a core operation has no pure
losses, a retained input with exactly two primitive
occurrences has a two-spine form. Every live
passive head survives its all-$u$ probe, for every
candidate root owner $u$. Hence both children
contain every live passive input. Their $x$-paths
are nonempty, avoid $u$, and have disjoint owner
sets. With only two owners left, each path uses
one owner throughout. By associativity the
operation has the form
\[
 (x\star_s P)\star_u(x\star_t Q),
 \qquad \{s,t,u\}=I,
\]
with passive helpers $P,Q$. Its slices at $x=\{s\}$
and $x=\{t\}$ are respectively $\{s\}\cup Q$
and $\{t\}\cup P$. In particular the guards
determine each helper's bits at every observer
outside one specified owner.

There is an exact profile condition on the helpers
in this form. Let $P,Q$ be core on the same live
passive support. The expression retains $x$ and
has no pure losses exactly when:
\begin{itemize}
\item $P$ has unanimity at $t,u$, and $Q$ has
unanimity at $s,u$;
\item every winning $s$-coalition of $P$ meets
every winning $t$-coalition of $Q$.
\end{itemize}
For retention, the sole obstruction is a passive
valuation with $P=\{s\}$ and $Q=\{t\}$. It loses
the $u$-part of an ambiguous $x$. Otherwise at
least one child retains ambiguous $x$ and the
root cannot absorb it. The singleton slices retain
$x$ directly, also at $x=\{u\}$. The obstructing
passive valuation exists exactly when the two
profiles have disjoint winners.

For no pure losses, the $x=\{s\}$ slice forces
$Q$ to retain every foreign mark on pure $s$
probes. This is unanimity of its $s$-profile:
a winning proper coalition would keep its win
at the full coalition omitting a marked input.
The $x=\{t\}$ slice similarly forces $P$'s
$t$-unanimity. On an all-$u$ passive probe with
$x=\{u\}$ and one foreign mark, losing the mark
in either helper would give $\{u\}$ there and
force root absorption. Hence both helpers have
$u$-unanimity.

Conversely, on a pure $s$-probe the $Q$ branch
retains any passive mark by $s$-unanimity, and
its $t$-gate cannot lose that mark: $x$ is never
the $t$-singleton, and absorption by $Q$ would
retain its own marked winner. The root cannot
lose the mark by a $u$-absorption, since the only
singleton owners present are $s$ and the marked
owner. The pure $t$ case is symmetric. On a pure
$u$-probe both helpers retain the foreign mark,
and each attached gate preserves it. The root
therefore keeps it as well. These checks give
the claimed sufficiency.

Two distinct retained inputs cannot both use this
two-occurrence minimum if there are at least three
live inputs. If $x,y$ both did, their two marked
paths in each child would have that child's root
owner throughout. By associativity the children
would be $s$- and $t$-folds, respectively, of
$x,y$ and a passive helper using the remaining
inputs. In particular both helpers are nonempty.
Set $x=\{s\}$, $y=\{t,u\}$, and all remaining
inputs to $\{t\}$. The first child absorbs to
$\{s\}$, and the second absorbs to $\{t\}$
through its passive helper. The root gives
$\{s,t\}$, which fails to retain $y$.

This also gives a total occurrence lower bound.
Write $n$ for the number of live inputs and $m$
for the number retained, with $n\ge3$. If no
retained input is doubled, there are at least
$n+2m$ occurrences. If one is doubled, both root
children contain every live input, and every
other retained input needs at least three copies.
The total in this case is at least $2n+m-1$.
In particular whenever $m<n$, every primitive
realizer has size at least $n+2m$.

For a fixed choice of $s,t,u$, this gives an exact
test using two greatest passive core minorants.
At each passive valuation consider all pairs of
nonempty helper menus $(P,Q)$ that give the entire
prescribed unary slice in $x$. Reject an empty
fiber. The slices at $s,t$ fix $P\setminus\{t\}$
and $Q\setminus\{s\}$, so only two membership
bits can vary. Each nonempty fiber has a greatest
pair under coordinatewise inclusion.

To see this, only incomparable pairs need checking.
Write them $(P_1,Q_0)$ and $(P_0,Q_1)$, where
$P_1=P_0\cup\{t\}$ and $Q_1=Q_0\cup\{s\}$.
At a fixed $x$, if neither pair gives an absorbing
$u$-gate, their equal outputs already contain the
union of both expanded operands. Exactly one
absorption cannot give equal outputs: a gate
without absorption cannot give the singleton $u$.
If both absorb and their join $(P_1,Q_1)$ does
not, the unchanged operands must both have been
$\{u\}$, forcing $x=P_0=Q_0=\{u\}$. But with
$x=\{s,u\}$ the first pair gives $I$ and the
second gives $\{s,u\}$, contradicting equality of
their entire slices. Hence the join remains in
the fiber.

For retained slices the greatest pair can be read
from three atomic slices and the neutral residue
$L=\partial_x F$. At a fixed passive valuation put
\[
 P_0=F(\{t\},v)\setminus\{t\},\qquad
 Q_0=F(\{s\},v)\setminus\{s\}.
\]
Use $P_0,Q_0$ as the observed parts. The optional
$t$-bit of the greatest $P$ is determined as follows.
If $Q_0$ omits $t$, use the $t$-bit of $L$.
If $Q_0$ contains $t$ and $P_0=\{u\}$, use the
$t$-bit of $F(\{u\},v)$. In all other cases with
$t\in Q_0$, include $t$ in $P$. The optional
$s$-bit of the greatest $Q$ is given by the
symmetric rule.

Indeed at $x=\{s,u\}$ the $t$-output is the
disjunction of the two helper $t$-bits, giving
the first case from the neutral residue. When
$Q$ contains $t$, its branch always carries $t$.
At $x=\{u\}$ the root then absorbs exactly when
$P=\{u\}$, giving the second case. Otherwise
adding $t$ to $P$ can contribute only $t$ itself
and, if it undoes an $s$-absorption, winners of
$x$. All are already in the prescribed output.
It cannot undo a root $u$-absorption, since a
left $u$-singleton would require $P=\{u\}$.
Thus this addition is permitted, and the join
property combines the two permitted maxima.

These rules give succinct pointwise bounds by
guard evaluation even when listing their tables
would be exponential. To check a proposed slice,
form the pair by the rules and test that it is
nonempty in both coordinates and reproduces the
slice. If a fiber exists the rules recover its
greatest pair, and the reproduction test also
establishes existence.

Take the pointwise greatest pairs as two passive
upper bounds and compute the greatest core
minorant of each, allowing zero for an empty
minorant family. The bounds admit a two-spine
realizer exactly when both resulting minorants
are nonzero and the two-spine expression on them
gives the candidate. Indeed any pair of core
helpers for a realization lies below those
minorants. The owned gates are monotone in menu
inclusion, so the minorant pair's slice lies
between the slice of that realizing pair and the
slice of the greatest pair, which agree. The
converse is direct substitution. This separates
the search for the two helpers into independent
passive problems once the root is chosen.

The passive problems can retain the full original
recognition difficulty. For $F(X,v)=X\cup C(v)$,
the greatest helper bounds are exactly $(C,C)$,
for every root choice. The pair $(C,C)$ realizes
the slice as a menu identity. The singleton slices
already fix all bits except the $t$-bit of $P$
and the $s$-bit of $Q$. At $X=\{s,u\}$ the
$t$-bit of the output is the disjunction of the
helpers' $t$-bits, since neither child can give
$\{u\}$. Thus $P$ cannot add $t$ if $C$ omits
it. The test at $X=\{t,u\}$ similarly prevents
adding $s$ to $Q$. This proves the claim.

Equivalently, $F$ has a primitive realization
with two $X$-occurrences exactly when $C$ is core:
use the binary union identity in one direction
and neutral deletion in the other. Apply this to
the width-four cube masks of
\cref{core:prop:compact-cube-masks}. At $K=3$
the resulting two-occurrence recognition problem
is PSPACE-hard even with a retained, independent
$X$ and only mixed losses. The lists simply add
the empty prime at $X$ and leave the old guards
unchanged. Thus the two-spine test requires
passive recognition even on that restricted
prime-list family.

In this class, any minimal loss containing
$x:u$ rules out root owner $u$. Such a loss also
posts a different owner $r$, since losses are
mixed, and at three owners a proper pattern
uses at most two colors. On its canonical probe
an input posted at $r$ retains that winner:
its own observer sees only $u$-postings, which
cannot give a mixed loss. Neutralizing $x$ makes
the marked head survive by minimality. Thus the
first root test fails. Consequently, if every
owner appears as an $x$-posting in some prime,
at least three primitive $x$-occurrences are
necessary. Prime appearance is equivalent to
minimal-loss appearance by transversal duality.

For a remaining root choice $u$, the helper bounds
have complete prime lists of the original width.
Since no prime contains $x:u$, posting $x$ at $u$
leaves all foreign source coefficients unchanged.
Retention of $x$ therefore gives
$F(\{u\},v)=\{u\}\cup L(v)$. On a nonempty fiber,
$t\in Q_0$ also implies $t\in L(v)$ by neutral
deletion. The optional bits in the decoder thus
reduce to the corresponding neutral bits, giving
\[
 P^+(v)=P_0\cup(L(v)\cap\{t\}),\qquad
 Q^+(v)=Q_0\cup(L(v)\cap\{s\}).
\]
At an observer $k\ne t$, the lists for $P^+$
come from those of $F$ at the passive heads:
discard primes containing $x:t$, erase the other
$x$-postings, and take inclusion minima. Indeed
the fixed input $\{t\}$ supplies no source at $k$.
Any sufficient guard on the restricted facet
extends to one on the original facet by excluding
every allowed $x$-posting other than $x:t$.
An original prime inside the extension projects
to a guard inside the given one, proving
completeness. At observer $t$, take the lists of
$L$, erasing all $x$-postings and taking minima.
The lists for $Q^+$ follow by exchanging $s,t$.
Both transformations take polynomial time in the
listed input and preserve the width bound.

The pointwise fiber test for such a root uses only
these bounds and the neutral residue. Writing
$P=P^+(v)$ and $Q=Q^+(v)$, the fiber is nonempty
exactly when
\[
 P,Q\ne\varnothing,\qquad P\cup Q=L(v),\qquad
 (P,Q)\ne(\{s\},\{t\}),\qquad
 \bigl(P=\{u\}\text{ or }Q=\{u\}\bigr)
 \ \Longrightarrow\ L(v)\subseteq\{u\}.
\]
Indeed, for a nonsingleton $x$, each observer of a
winner outside $x$ treats $x$ as neutral. Retention
then makes the prescribed output $x\cup L(v)$.
The two-spine expression gives $x\cup P\cup Q$
on these slices unless both side gates absorb,
which fails retention. In particular the slices
$x=\{s,u\}$ and $x=\{t,u\}$ determine the $t$-
and $s$-bits of $P\cup Q$. The slice $x=\{s,t\}$
determines its $u$-bit. The $s$- and $t$-singleton
slices agree by construction. For $x=\{u\}$,
once simultaneous side absorption is excluded,
the root absorbs exactly when a helper is $\{u\}$.
This gives the last condition. The decoder proves
necessity for the greatest pair of any nonempty
fiber, and these slice calculations prove sufficiency.

The resulting lists serve as separate source
upper constraints even if they disagree across
observers. By reconstruction, a helper satisfying
every constraint lies below the reconstructed
bound. Conversely, on a canonical probe for a
constraint the marked head is the only source of
its winner, so a helper below the reconstructed
bound satisfies that constraint. Thus each
remaining root choice reduces to two passive
prime-list minorant problems and a reconstruction
test. The formulas can be used even before
testing for empty fibers: a realization forces
every fiber to be nonempty, and successful
reconstruction itself certifies a realization.

For example, retain $x$ and inputs $z_r$ for
each owner $r$, and for each $r$ add the two
summands $y_r\star_r x$ and $y_r\star_{s(r)}z_r$,
where $s(r)\ne r$. Each $y_r$ has just the mixed
loss $\{x:r,z_r:s(r)\}$ and the retained inputs
have none. The criterion applies to $x$ even
though there are no pure losses at any head.

For $K=3$ this path constraint determines the
minimum total size of primitive input union. On
$n\ge3$ inputs it is $3n$ variable occurrences.
The flat union construction of
\cref{core:cor:source-box-rank} attains that size.
For the lower bound every input is retained, so
each needs at least two occurrences. If an input
$x$ occurs exactly twice, let $u$ be the root
owner. Its two paths below that root are nonempty:
on the probe with all other inputs uniformly at
$u$ a child missing a passive input would cause
root absorption and lose that input's mark.
The paths avoid $u$ and cannot share an owner,
as above. With just three owners, the children
therefore have the forms $x\star_s P$ and
$x\star_t Q$, with $s,t,u$ distinct and $P,Q$
passive words.

Set $x=\{s\}$. The root joins the $s$-atom
with the second child, whose value is $\{t\}$
if $Q=\{t\}$ and $\{s\}\cup Q$ otherwise.
Thus $Q$ must retain every passive winner other
than $s$. Symmetrically $P$ must retain every
passive winner other than $t$. Each uses every
passive input. When there are at least two
passive inputs, none can occur just once in
either helper: at a gate on that sole path,
set every other input to its owner's singleton
and mark the distinguished input at an owner
different from both that gate owner and the
one allowed omission. The helper would lose
a winner it must retain. Hence a two-copy
$x$ forces at least $2+4(n-1)>3n$ occurrences.
A size-minimizing term must therefore use each
input at least three times. For two inputs the
earlier binary union identity gives the minimum
of four.

For several owners, consider a complete nonempty-valued
candidate all of whose minimal losses are pure. There is
an exact criterion for its representation as a named union
of single-owner folds:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Each headed input has all its losses at one owner.
\item A loss may post only unheaded inputs and inputs headed
at that same owner.
\item At each owner the loss map on its headed inputs and
the unheaded inputs is idempotent.
\end{enumerate}
An empty loss makes its head unused and can be handled by
deleting that input first. If an unused input is posted by
another minimal loss, there is no such representation:
an operation omitting an input everywhere cannot depend on
its posting.

For necessity, a fold contributing a headed input must
have the owner of every pure loss at that head. A loss
using any other owner would leave its mark in that fold.
This proves the first condition. A fold contributing at
owner $r$ therefore uses no foreign headed inputs, and
the union's predicate at that head cannot depend on foreign
headed postings. This proves the second condition by
minimality. At each owner take its folds together with the
unheaded projections. They realize its one-owner budget,
giving the third condition.

Conversely, on each owner's headed inputs together with
the unheaded inputs the losses are exactly a one-owner
budget. Use its canonical folds to supply the headed
positives. Each fold is globally bounded, because it uses
no other owner's headed inputs. Add the unheaded projections
and take the union. Shared unheaded inputs need no new
compatibility condition. All three checks use the primes
directly: read the possible postings in minimal losses by
the private-prime test and use the maximal pure backgrounds
for the idempotence checks.

Pure losses alone do not force a fold union. For $r\ne s$,
the word $(x\star_r y)\star_s z$ has the pure losses
$\{y:r\}$ and $\{z:s\}$ at $x$, their counterparts at $y$,
and $\{x:s,y:s\}$ at $z$. Its headed inputs $x,y$ each
have losses at two owners, so no union of single-owner
folds gives this operation.

\paragraph{Mixed heads fed by pure-owner subsystems.}
Partition the inputs into unheaded inputs $U$, sets
$H_r$ of headed inputs for each owner $r$, and possibly
mixed heads $M$. Put $W_r=U\cup H_r$. Require every row
at a head in $H_r$ to post entirely at $r$ within $W_r$.
At a head in $M$, require each $r$-block to lie in $W_r$.
In particular no row posts an input of $M$. Native
subsystems can have cycles, and can share unheaded inputs.
Let $C_r$ be the pure closure on $W_r$ generated by the
rows at $H_r$. For a query background $p$ put
$E_r=C_r(p_r\cap W_r)$.

At a native head $i\in H_r$ the greatest bounded core
operation supplies the mark exactly when $i\notin E_r$.
At a mixed head $i\in M$ it supplies the mark exactly when
every row $t$ at $i$ has some owner $r$ with
$t_r\not\subseteq E_r$. For sufficiency, for each row
at $i$ choose a posting $u:r$ outside $E_r$. The
$r$-fold on $W_r\setminus E_r$ uses $u$, is bounded
globally, and cannot absorb at the positive query when
attached at $r$. Attach these folds to the projection
at $i$. Each row at $i$ is absorbed by one attachment,
and all the other rows are omitted by both the folds
and the projection.

For necessity take a row $t$ with $t_r\subseteq E_r$
at every owner. Each $u\in t_r$ not already posted at $r$
is a native head in $H_r$ blocked on the pure background
$(p_r\cap W_r):r$, by the one-owner closure criterion
applied to the native subbudget. Substitute these
blocks for the corresponding postings in $t$. The
resulting background is contained in $p$, leaves $i$
unposted, and never posts a substituted head: such a
head lies in its own $H_r$, outside the other owners'
domains and outside the seed set at $r$. Full-palette
loss substitution therefore blocks $i$ on the resulting
background, and hence on $p$. The native-head formula
follows by restricting its constraints to one owner for
necessity and using the native fold for sufficiency.

Thus complete prime families of this form can be
characterized by closure alone: use idempotence at the
native heads and require that each positive at a mixed
head avoid every row after the native closures have
expanded its postings. In this last test the expanded
postings are consistent: the only shared inputs of
different domains are unheaded, so their colors never
change under closure. The same test can use the primes
without listing the rows, by checking for an original
loss contained in the expanded postings.

Equivalently, after checking the native idempotence laws,
the only extra law is single-posting substitution at a
mixed head. In any minimal row $t$ there, replace a
posting $u:r$ with any minimal loss at $u\in H_r$.
If the result is consistent, require it to contain a
listed loss at the mixed head. Every such substitution
preserves properness because it introduces no new owner.
Necessity is the loss substitution rule.

For sufficiency consider a row contained in the expanded
postings of a query. Idempotence supplies a native minimal
loss contained in the query's $r$-seed for each posting
that must be replaced. Perform the replacements one at
a time. Every intermediate union is consistent: the new
postings agree with the query, and an old posting on a
head still to be replaced cannot have a conflicting
posting in any native domain. An old posting not requiring
replacement already agrees with the query. After each
step the law supplies a contained minimal row. If its
next replaced posting is absent, that row remains
contained without doing the replacement; otherwise use
the law on it. At the end a listed loss is contained
in the original query.

The local law can be checked from primes without
reconstructing minimal rows. A violation names $i$,
$u:r$, and a prime $G$ of $i$ on a facet $k$ where the
result stays positive. The row $t$ must contain $u:r$,
and all its other postings must avoid $G$ and $k$.
The native replacement must likewise avoid $G$ and $k$.
The two existence tests use transversal scans, with a
private prime to force $u:r$ in $t$. The only interaction
left between the two rows is consistency on unheaded
inputs: a replacement posting at $r$ cannot be combined
with a foreign posting on the same input. For each
potentially shared conflicting input, branch on which
side may use it, then the two tests are independent.
This is polynomial with disjoint posting domains, and
exponential only in the number of potentially shared
conflicting inputs otherwise.

For this topology the width threshold for complete
recognition is two versus three. At width two the
local law is polynomial even with variable $K$.
In the violation test, choose the original observer
facets for both rows and a private prime for $u:r$.
Use Boolean variables indicating the postings of each
row. Hitting each prime on its facet requires a clause
of width at most two. The private-prime condition
and the restrictions from $G$ and $k$ are unit
clauses. Consistency within each row and between the
two rows after removing $u:r$ forbids pairs of
postings, again giving binary clauses. A satisfying
assignment can be minimized separately on both facets.
This preserves consistency and all forbidden postings,
and the private prime keeps $u:r$. Conversely any
violating pair supplies such an assignment. The
enumeration and the resulting 2SAT tests are polynomial.

At width three recognition is coNP-complete for
each fixed $K\ge3$, with one native head $u$ at $r$
and one mixed head $i$. Use distinct owners $r,s$.
For a 3CNF formula take as unheaded inputs the two
literals of each variable. The primes at $u$ on the
facets seeing $r$ are the variable pairs and the clause
literal sets, all posted at $r$. At $i$, on each facet
seeing both owners, the primes are $\{u:r\}$ and the
variable pairs posted at $s$. Remove redundant
supersets. Facets that omit a required owner in these
lists have the empty guard instead. The native losses
are the minimal transversals of the variable pairs and
clauses at $r$. The mixed losses consist of $u:r$ and
one literal per variable posted at $s$. These lists
are canonical by transversal duality, and the
unheaded projections ensure nonempty-valued output.
The native map is idempotent, as it adds at most $u$
and its premises do not use $u$.

A consistent substitution exists exactly when some
native transversal is disjoint from a choice of one
literal per variable at $s$. Since the native
transversal meets every variable pair, this forces
it to choose exactly the opposite literal in each
pair. It then meets every clause precisely for a
satisfying assignment, which also produces such a
transversal. The replacement leaves out $u:r$, so
its consistent result contains no listed loss at
$i$. It is proper because it uses only $r,s$.
Thus the substitution law fails exactly for
satisfiable formulas. A violating row pair is
checkable in polynomial time against the primes,
giving the coNP upper bound.

\paragraph{Eliminating native subsystems below interacting mixed heads.}
Keep the pure subsystems $W_r$ and their closures $C_r$,
but now let a row at a mixed head post inputs in $M$
as well as inputs of $W_r$ at owner $r$. The mixed
heads may depend on one another. For a fixed query
$(i,p)$ with $i\in M$, keep a mixed row $t$ exactly
when all its native and unheaded postings at each $r$
lie in $E_r=C_r(p_r\cap W_r)$. Project the kept rows
to $M$. The original query is feasible exactly when
its restriction to $M$ is feasible on this reduced
budget.

For the forward direction use the pure closure criterion
to substitute away every native posting of a kept row
that is not already in $p$. As before, the replacement
backgrounds are contained in $p$ and no substituted head
is posted by another owner's seed. The mixed postings
do not participate in the substitutions. Thus each
projected row becomes a loss when the nonmixed inputs
are fixed to their statuses in $p$. Completion erasure
gives a reduced witness for all these rows and the mark.

Conversely a reduced witness ignores the nonmixed inputs
and omits every kept row. At each owner $r$ attach the
fold of $W_r\setminus E_r$, skipping empty supports.
It is bounded on the native rows and ignores every
mixed head. Each discarded row has a native or unheaded
posting in one of these fold supports, and so its
attachment absorbs that row. No attachment erases the
positive mark, because its support avoids $p_r$.

The reduced loss predicate is accessible directly from
the primes: a projected loss is contained in $d$ on
$M$ exactly when an original loss fits in $d$ together
with all the native postings $E_r:r$. This transversal
test works even when that union uses every owner.
In particular if all the projected owner blocks are
singletons or empty, exact fold saturation decides
the residual query without enumerating projected losses.
The original blocks can be arbitrarily large on the
eliminated inputs. In that class a complete candidate
can therefore be recognized in coNP from the primes:
a missed positive query is a polynomially checkable
certificate, with native heads tested by their own
closure criterion.

This elimination gives a prime description in terms of
closed native sets. Fix a mixed head and observer facet.
For each tuple $D_r$ of native closed sets agreeing in
the sense that their postings on shared unheaded inputs
are consistent, form the reduced mixed budget by retaining
the rows whose nonmixed postings lie in $D_r$ at each
owner. For every canonical prime $G$ of its greatest
bounded mixed operation at the chosen head and facet,
form the lifted guard
\[
 G\ \cup\ \bigcup_r\{u:r\mid u\in W_r\setminus D_r\},
\]
omitting forbidden observer postings. The lifted guards
give exactly the full mixed source predicate. Indeed their
nonmixed part ensures $p_r\cap W_r\subseteq D_r$, equivalently
$C_r(p_r\cap W_r)\subseteq D_r$, and hence the residual
witness is bounded for the actual query. Conversely, for
a feasible query choose its actual closure tuple and a
residual prime enabled there. Taking consensus closure
and inclusion minima gives its full canonical primes.

If every unheaded input is relevant to at most one owner,
in the sense that its postings in all rows use at most
one owner, no further consensus steps are needed. Restrict
each $W_r$ to its headed inputs and relevant unheaded
inputs in the construction. Competing exclusions in two
lifted guards can then occur only on mixed inputs. For
the two native tuples $D,D'$, use the closed tuple
$D_r\cap D'_r$. Its residual budget keeps only rows kept
by both tuples, so both residual guards are sufficient
there. Their resolvent contains a residual prime there,
and the native exclusions of the intersection tuple are
exactly the union of the two native exclusion sets.
Thus every resolvent of lifted guards already contains
a lifted guard. The inclusion minima alone are the
canonical primes.

With shared unheaded inputs there is instead a direct
combination formula. Let $R$ consist of the unheaded
inputs relevant to more than one owner. Fix one observer
facet throughout. For any consistent posting pattern
$\sigma$ on $R$, compute the branch primes on the
remaining inputs by the same tuple construction, now
requiring each $D_r$ to contain the fixed shared seed
$\sigma_r\cap W_r$. Omit the shared exclusions from
the lifted guards. The private domains are disjoint
across owners, so the preceding intersection argument
shows that their inclusion minima are exactly the branch
primes with $\sigma$ fixed.

For each shared exclusion set $S$, let $\mathcal A(S)$
be the maximal consistent postings on $R$ avoiding $S$.
Choose one branch prime $G_\sigma$ for each
$\sigma\in\mathcal A(S)$ and form
\[
                 S\ \cup\ \bigcup_{\sigma\in\mathcal A(S)}G_\sigma .
\]
The inclusion minima of these guards, over all $S$ and
all choices, are the full canonical primes. Every
allowed shared pattern extends to one of the maximal
patterns, so the guard is sufficient by antitonicity.
Conversely a sufficient guard with shared part $S$
must supply every maximal branch, on which its private
part contains a branch prime. The resulting union is
contained in the given guard. Thus only the shared inputs
require the simultaneous combination of branch primes;
all other exclusions come from the closed native sets
and the mixed budgets.

Even one shared input can require this combination.
On a facet seeing distinct owners $a,b$, take unheaded
$x,z$ and mixed heads $i,j$ with the sole row
$\{z:a\}$ at $i$ and the two rows $\{x:a\},\{x:b\}$
at $j$. There are no native heads. The canonical prime
at $i$ is $\{z:a\}$. Every lifted guard from a consistent
native tuple excludes at least one of $x:a,x:b$, because
that tuple cannot allow both. Combining the two shared
branches removes the extra restriction at $i$.

There is a static cut criterion when the mixed posting
graph of the kept rows has no monochromatic cycle.
A marked query fails
exactly when one can choose a set $J\subseteq M$ containing
the mark and one original row per head in $J$, with
every nonmixed posting in $E_r$ at its owner $r$ and
every posting on $M\setminus J$ agreeing with the query.
The sufficiency of this obstruction holds whenever the
selected internal arrows have no monochromatic cycle,
even if other rows do have such cycles. Indeed the
elimination above keeps the selected rows. Fixing the
exterior mixed inputs to the query and erasing them
then gives the ranked survivor contradiction on $J$.

For necessity, on the reduced budget repeatedly make
an input available when each of its rows has an
available posting that disagrees with the query.
Start with the unheaded inputs. The projections at
available inputs synthesize these deletions in order,
so an unavailable mark must remain if the query fails.
Let $J$ be all the remaining heads and choose at each
one a row that cannot be deleted. Its exterior mixed
postings agree with the query, and any original row
giving this projection meets the native condition.
The selected arrows inherit the absence of monochromatic
cycles. This is the stated cut. The availability
test is a transversal scan excluding postings at
available inputs that disagree with the query, and
allowing the nonmixed postings precisely in $E_r:r$.

Such a cut needs at most one original loss per headed
input, even counting the native evidence. Record one
pure row for each newly added native head in the
derivation of $E_r$ from $p_r\cap W_r$. These rows still
force every native membership used by the cut. Together
with the selected mixed rows they already block the
query by the same substitution and ranked argument.

\paragraph{Foreign postings of a single pure subsystem.}
There is an exact elimination when a single pure subsystem
is posted at arbitrary owners by the mixed heads. Let $V_0$
be the subsystem, with no row there posting a mixed input.
Its losses all use owner $r$ and give the closure $c$ on
$V_0$. Assume $c(\varnothing)=\varnothing$. The greatest
bounded native operation is the one-owner trace $C$ of $c$.
Every globally bounded word is bounded by $C$ on the native
sources, by the pure criterion, and adjoining $C$ by union
preserves all mixed constraints and positive marks. Thus
the block-extension theorem applies with baseline $C$.

Write a mixed row as $(b,t)$ for its native and mixed
postings, and let $p$ be the native part of a marked query.
Put $H=c(p_r)$. By
\cref{core:thm:one-owner-activation-order,core:lem:block-source-patterns}
the reduced mixed budget consists of the projections $t$
of exactly those rows satisfying
\[
 b_r\subseteq H,\qquad
 b_s\setminus H\subseteq p_s\setminus H,\qquad
 c(H\setminus b_s)=H \quad(s\ne r).
\]
The mixed query is feasible exactly when its projection
is feasible on this budget. This accounts for foreign
postings on the same native heads, including cross-owner
dependencies excluded from the preceding elimination.

There is a prime test for a projected loss to fit in a
prescribed set of mixed postings. For each $s\ne r$ choose
a minimal spanning set $T_s\subseteq H$ with $c(T_s)=H$.
In a single transversal scan allow the $r$-postings on
$H$ and the $s$-postings on
$(H\setminus T_s)\cup(p_s\setminus H)$, together with the
prescribed mixed postings. A projected loss fits if some
choice of spanning sets gives a loss in this scan. Indeed
$H\setminus b_s$ spans $H$ exactly when it contains such
a $T_s$. If every closed set has a unique minimal spanning
set, then $T_s$ is that same set for all foreign owners.
It can be found by greedily discarding redundant inputs,
and each projected loss test requires just one scan.

For a single terminal head, comparing a fixed original
loss $b$ to a proposed guard $G$ can be done by descending
closure, without enumerating possible query backgrounds.
Fix the observer $k$ of $G$. Let $R$ consist of the inputs
allowed by $G$ at $r$, taking $R=\varnothing$ if $k=r$.
Let $D$ consist of $b_r$ and every foreign posting input
of $b$ whose posting is forbidden by $G$ or uses $k$.
Starting with $H=V_0$, repeatedly replace it by
\[
           c(H\cap R)\ \cap\!
           \bigcap_{s\ne r} c(H\setminus b_s).
\]
Each update stays closed and decreases $H$. Its final
set is the greatest closed set satisfying all the
equalities $c(H\cap R)=H$ and $c(H\setminus b_s)=H$,
because the update is monotone and preserves every
common fixed set below it.

There is an enabled query above $b$ in the activation
preorder exactly when the final $H$ contains $D$.
Indeed the closure of the $r$-seed of any such query
is one of those common fixed sets; it must include
$b_r$ and any foreign posting that cannot remain
outside it. Conversely take the final $H$, post
$H\cap R$ at $r$ and $b_s\setminus H$ at each foreign
owner $s$. These postings are consistent because the
foreign parts come from the original row and avoid
the native seed. They omit $k$, avoid $G$, and meet
the activation comparison. This explicitly gives the
violating query.

Thus if the terminal minimal losses are supplied
explicitly, complete recognition is polynomial in
those rows and the listed primes, for any pure native
closure and arbitrary mixtures of terminal colors.
One comparison per row and prime suffices, with at
most $|V_0|$ strict closure updates per comparison.
In particular this decides complete recognition from
primes alone when the terminal prime width is one,
since each facet contributes at most one minimal row.

With one mixed head, marked feasibility in this topology
is coNP-complete at prime width two, even when the pure
subsystem is a disjoint union of folds on at most three
inputs each. For hardness, take a 3CNF formula with
nonempty clauses and give each literal occurrence a
separate input. The native operation is the union of
the $r$-folds on the clause groups of occurrences.
Its closure $c$ adds an entire group whenever a seed
meets it. Post one occurrence per group at $r$ in the
query, so $H$ is the whole native input set.

At the sole mixed head $i$, for an owner $s\ne r$, use
the pairs of contradictory literal occurrences posted
at $s$ as its primes on facets seeing $s$. On the
facet omitting $s$ use the empty prime. The canonical
lists describe the minimal transversals of those
pairs, all pure at $s$. A projected loss exists exactly
when a transversal $b_s$ has a complement meeting
every clause group: this is precisely $c(H\setminus
b_s)=H$. That complement contains no contradictory
pair, so choosing an occurrence per group gives a
satisfying assignment. Conversely such a choice for
a satisfying assignment has a transversal as its
complement, and minimizing the transversal preserves
the condition. The projected loss is empty on the
single mixed input. Thus the mark fails exactly for
satisfiable formulas. A native fold of size three
has a prime excluding the other two inputs at its
owner, so the native and mixed primes have width
at most two.

For membership in coNP, a failed query has one
accepted original loss as a certificate, since every
projected loss on the sole mixed input is empty.
The activation conditions and membership in the
original loss family are checkable directly from the
primes. At width one each facet instead supplies at
most one minimal loss, so testing all those rows is
polynomial, including with variable $K$.

Complete recognition has a simpler criterion when the
sole mixed head has only pure losses at one foreign
owner $s$. Write $\psi_s(A)$ for its proposed loss bit
on $A:s$. After checking the native closure law, the
terminal predicate is realizable exactly when each
minimal loss $A$ has $c$-closed complement. An empty
terminal loss simply deletes that head. Otherwise the
foreign subset law of
\cref{core:thm:one-owner-source-interval} becomes
\[
 \psi_s(A)=\psi_s\bigl(A\setminus c(E)\bigr)
 \qquad(E\cap A=\varnothing).
\]
For a minimal loss take $E=V_0\setminus A$ to force a
closed complement. Conversely, if $A$ contains a minimal
loss with closed complement, the closure of any disjoint
$E$ lies in that complement. Removing it preserves that
loss. This proves the law for all $A,E$.

In the disjoint-fold case the closed sets are the unions
of whole native groups. Complete recognition is then
polynomial on the primes at any width. On a facet seeing
$s$, require that each terminal prime uses at most one
input per group and that replacing a used input with any
other member of its group gives another prime. Whole-group
minimal losses have exactly this symmetry among their
minimal transversals. Conversely, if a hitting set does
not include an entire group, its partial selection in
that group can be removed: a prime hit only there could
have its sole member of that group exchanged for an
input outside the hitting set. Thus minimal hitting sets
are unions of whole groups. The test only needs the
listed primes and their within-group exchanges, even
though individual marked feasibility for a general
upper budget in this class can be coNP-hard.

The terminal criterion extends to several pure loss
owners at the same head. First check the native terminal
factor $\psi_r$ for invariance under $c$. For each
foreign owner $s$, each minimal $s$-loss $A$, and each
prime $G$ for retaining the head on pure $r$-backgrounds
(viewed as a set of inputs), require
\[
             c\bigl(V_0\setminus(A\cup G)\bigr)\cap A=\varnothing.
\]
This is exactly the foreign subset law: an $r$-background
disjoint from $A$ and retaining the terminal head is
contained in one of these maximal allowed sets. Removing
its closure must preserve each minimal foreign loss.
Conversely every foreign loss contains a minimal one,
and the stated tests keep that minimal loss intact.
The pure primes of a factor are obtained by deleting
other-owner exclusions on a facet seeing that factor
and taking inclusion minima.

Once the native loss map is idempotent, the condition
has a local obstruction. A violation consists of a
foreign minimal loss $A$ containing a native head $u$,
an $r$-loss $T$ at $u$, and a pure $r$-prime $G$ at the
terminal head with $T\cap(A\cup G)=\varnothing$.
Indeed idempotence makes capture by
$c(V_0\setminus(A\cup G))$ a one-step test at each input
of $A$. The native terminal invariance itself is checked
by requiring that each maximal retaining pure background,
the complement of one of its primes, be closed.

This local test is polynomial when the native primes
and the pure foreign factors have width at most two.
Choose $u,G$, and a private prime witnessing $u\in A$.
The two minimal transversals $T$ and $A$ can be sought
by 2SAT: their primes give clauses of width at most two,
the private prime forces $u$ and excludes its other
members from $A$, and the remaining conditions forbid
$T$ on $G$ and forbid a common input of $T$ and $A$.
Minimize both hitting sets after a successful test.
The private prime preserves $u$ and every disjointness
condition persists. The native terminal factor can
have arbitrary width, since its primes are only scanned
individually.

Width three is already coNP-hard for this foreign-only
terminal class. Use the two-headed formula construction
above with native head $u$ at $r$, but post $u$ at $s$
in every terminal loss. The terminal pure $s$-primes
are $\{u:s\}$ and the literal pairs at $s$; the native
primes at $u$ are the literal pairs and clause sets at
$r$. There is no native terminal loss, so $G$ is empty.
The obstruction is exactly a native transversal $T$
disjoint from a terminal loss $A$, which exists precisely
for a satisfying assignment. The local obstruction is
polynomially checkable, so the width-three case is
coNP-complete for every fixed $K\geq3$.

For disjoint native folds, invariance of $\psi_r$ says
that every pure $r$-prime is a union of whole native
groups: maximal retaining backgrounds are closed, and
every closed background is a union of groups. Call a
group protected if every pure $r$-prime excludes it.
Equivalently touching that group alone at $r$ loses
the terminal mark. A minimal foreign loss can meet an
unprotected group only by containing all its inputs.
Indeed a partial group can be captured from its
complement using an $r$-prime that leaves that group
available. A group wholly contained in the loss cannot
be captured by a disjoint seed, and a protected group
cannot be touched by a retaining seed. Thus for each
pure foreign factor apply the within-group prime
exchange test and the at-most-one rule only on the
unprotected groups. Protected groups impose no
additional restrictions. This gives a polynomial
complete-recognition test at arbitrary prime width.

For arbitrary mixed losses at a single terminal head
above disjoint native $r$-folds there is a local
replacement law. In a minimal row $b$, a native group
can be replaced if no foreign owner posts the entire
group in $b$. Replace all its postings by one $r$-posting
at any member. Require the result to contain a listed
loss whenever it is proper. There is one additional
test: if $b$ uses all $K-1$ foreign colors and omits $r$,
choose a foreign color $k$ and simultaneously replace
every group containing a $k$-posting of $b$, provided
all these groups can be replaced. Again require a
listed loss in the result.

Both rules are necessary by the one-owner activation
formula. In a group touched at $r$ by the new pattern,
each foreign block of $b$ leaves a spanning input just
when it does not post that whole group. In every other
group the old postings are unchanged.

For sufficiency, compare a minimal loss $b$ with any
proper $p$ above it in the activation preorder.
Every group touched at $r$ by $p$ can be replaced, and
the postings of $b$ outside these groups already
appear in $p$. Replace the touched groups using the
representatives posted at $r$ in $p$. If this would
introduce the only missing color of $b$, first choose
an owner $k\ne r$ omitted by $p$ and apply the batch
rule to all groups using $k$. They are touched at $r$
by $p$, so the batch is allowed and leaves $k$ absent.
All other replacements can be single steps, since
they introduce no further color. At each step choose
a listed loss contained in the result and continue on
that smaller row. Shrinking the current row only
preserves replaceability and decreases the next result.
The last row is contained in $p$, proving loss closure
in the activation preorder and hence exact recognition.

The batch rule is essential on proper colorings. Take
two native groups of size $K-1$ and one terminal row
posting every foreign owner once in each group.
Replacing just one group uses every owner, so there is
no proper single replacement to test. Replacing both
gives a pure $r$-background which must lose, but does
not contain the only listed terminal row.

The single-group tests can be done directly from primes.
Choose the group, its new $r$-posting, the original
observer facet, and a prime $G$ on the resulting
facet in which the new posting is allowed and absent
from $G$. Forbid all original postings outside the group
that would meet $G$ or use the resulting missing owner.
Inside the group require at least one missing posting
at each foreign owner, so that replacement is allowed.
For fixed $K$ enumerate these missing members and use
a transversal scan to find a loss. For groups of size
at most two and prime width at most two, these tests
are 2SAT even with variable $K$: the missing-member
condition forbids the pair of postings at that owner.

At that same width and group size, the batch tests
also reduce to 2SAT. Choose the final missing owner
$k\ne r$ and a final prime $G$ not containing that
owner's postings. Use variables for the original
postings on facet $r$, with its prime hitting clauses
and pairwise consistency constraints. A group switches
exactly when it contains a $k$-posting. For each group,
forbid it from being entirely posted at $k$, and
forbid any $k$-posting there if $G$ excludes all its
possible $r$-representatives. No other foreign owner
can fill a group that contains a $k$-posting. Finally,
an original posting in $G$ can appear only in a switched
group. If it appears, a different member of that group
must be posted at $k$, since $G$ omits $k$. For a
two-input group this is a binary implication, and for
a singleton it forbids that posting.

Every failed batch provides a satisfying assignment.
Conversely, if the formula is satisfiable, minimize
the original hitting set to a listed loss. Keep the
set of groups switched by the assignment, even if
the minimization removes some of their $k$-postings.
They remain replaceable. First switch the groups
still posting $k$, then any additional groups in the
set. If the first step would use every owner one at
a time, it is precisely a batch of the required type;
otherwise single replacements suffice. The resulting
pattern avoids $G$ and omits $k$. If all local laws
held, it would contain a listed loss by successive
replacement and minimization. Hence satisfiability
already witnesses failure of complete recognition,
even if a nonminimal hitting set was found first.

For native groups of size two the width bound is
sharp. Complete recognition is coNP-complete at
width three for every fixed $K\ge3$, with hardness
entirely in the batch rule. Let $r$ be native and
choose a foreign owner $a$. Give each variable a
two-input native group for its two literals. At the
terminal head, on facet $r$, include the variable
pairs and clause literal sets of a 3CNF formula as
primes, all posted at $a$. Use additional two-input
groups and singleton primes to force the following
postings in every minimal terminal loss:
\begin{itemize}
\item at $a$, one member of each of two separate
dummy groups;
\item for every other foreign owner, both members
of one dummy group and one member of a second group.
\end{itemize}
All dummy groups are distinct, and their unforced
members occur in no prime. Remove redundant
supersets. The other facets have just the empty
prime, since every terminal loss uses all foreign
owners. At native heads the primes are those of
the disjoint $r$-folds. This gives complete canonical
lists of width at most three and nonempty outputs.

No single replacement is proper: every foreign
color occurs in at least two groups and a replacement
introduces $r$. A batch for a missing foreign
color other than $a$ is blocked by a whole group
at that color. A batch for $a$ is allowed exactly
when the loss posts at most one literal per variable
group at $a$. It already meets every variable pair
and clause, so such a loss exists exactly for a
satisfying assignment. The batch loses color $a$
and therefore contains no listed terminal loss.
Conversely an unsatisfiable formula leaves no
allowed batch, so the local rules hold and the
candidate is core. A violating replacement and its
original minimal loss give a polynomially checkable
certificate.

Conversely, groups of size three already make
width two coNP-complete. In the same padding
construction replace the variable groups by the
clause groups of literal occurrences. On facet $r$
use the pairs of contradictory occurrences at
$a$ as primes, keeping the singleton padding
primes unchanged. A batch for $a$ is allowed
exactly when no clause group is fully posted at
$a$. The unposted occurrences then meet every
clause group and contain no contradiction, since
the posted occurrences hit all opposite pairs.
This is equivalent to satisfiability: for the
reverse direction choose one true occurrence per
clause, post their complement at $a$, and minimize
the resulting hitting set together with the padding.
Minimization preserves the omitted occurrence
in each group. The same padding excludes all
single replacements and batches for other missing
colors. Thus the polynomial width-two test for
pairs cannot extend to three-input groups unless
polynomial time equals NP.

More generally, take several pure subsystems with
disjoint headed inputs and possibly shared unheaded
inputs. Each subsystem's own rows are pure at its owner
and use only its own inputs. The mixed heads may post
every subsystem at arbitrary owners. For a fixed query,
first fix all shared unheaded inputs to their statuses
in the query. Retain the rows whose postings there agree
with the fixing and project away the shared inputs.
This preserves feasibility by unmarked-background
elimination. Equivalently, reverse the reduction by
attaching unheaded projections to absorb each discarded
row at one of its disagreeing postings.

The retained native budgets are now disjoint. If a
native closure has $c(\varnothing)\ne\varnothing$, remove
those inputs and erase their postings from all remaining
rows. They cannot occur in a bounded word: they lose
their marks even on the empty background, where every
occurring input survives. Conversely a word on the
remaining inputs already ignores their original heads
and postings. The surviving native closure has empty
closure of the empty set, since a row with all premises
removed would have captured its head in the original
$c(\varnothing)$.

On this reduced budget take the sum of the disjoint
native traces as baseline, including any remaining
unheaded inputs outside the blocks as projections. By
\cref{core:thm:disjoint-activation-product}, the projected
mixed budget keeps a row precisely when its native part
passes the one-owner activation test in every block.
This is an exact elimination of all native heads,
including foreign postings and cycles in the mixed
graph. For the prime implementation, use the spanning
set choices independently in each block. In the
transversal scan on the original primes, also allow
the fixed shared and passive projection postings and arbitrary postings on
the removed unused inputs. The scan thereby implements
both earlier projections without constructing either
intermediate loss list.

For a single terminal head this also extends the
descending-closure recognition test. Check the native
predicates first by their one-owner idempotence laws.
On disjoint native blocks, fix a terminal prime $G$
and one original terminal loss $b$. The activation
product says that an enabled query above $b$ exists
exactly when the descending-closure comparison succeeds
in every block, using the restriction of $G$ and the
same missing observer throughout. The comparisons
construct compatible witnesses because the blocks
are disjoint. For passive projection inputs simply
check that the required postings avoid $G$ and the
missing observer.

When there are shared unheaded inputs, branch for
each terminal prime over the maximal consistent
postings there avoiding that prime and its observer.
This suffices because any failed positive remains
failed under extension to one of those patterns.
For a branch, filter each original terminal row by
compatibility with the shared fixing, and project it
through the shared inputs and the unused native
inputs as above. Use the component comparisons on
these projected rows, with the remaining exclusions
of the prime. Unused inputs may be left unposted
when lifting a counterexample. No branch loss
catalogue needs to be constructed: every projected
loss has an original representative, and the test
also works on nonminimal projections. Thus with
explicit terminal losses the test is polynomial
times at most $(K-1)^m$ branches per prime, where
$m$ is the number of shared unheaded inputs. Prime
width one again gives the terminal rows directly.

The same comparison works for a family of mixed rows,
even when their native postings disagree. Fix a prime
$G$ on facet $k$ and a family of native parts $b^j$ of
selected rows. In each disjoint pure block, use its own
native owner $r$ and let $R$ be the set of allowed native
postings, as in the single-row comparison. Descend from
the full closed set by the update
\[
            H\longmapsto c(H\cap R)
             \ \cap\!\!\bigcap_{j,\ s\ne r}c(H\setminus b^j_s).
\]
The final $H$ is the greatest common fixed set.
Require it to contain every input posted at $r$ in
the rows, every foreign posting input forbidden by
$G$ or $k$, and each input posted at two distinct
foreign owners across the selected rows. The first
two requirements are those of the single-row test.
The third is needed because conflicting foreign
postings can coexist in the comparisons only inside
the captured set $H$. These tests are sufficient:
post $H\cap R$ at $r$ and the union of the required
foreign postings outside $H$. The result is
consistent, enables the prime on this block, and
lies above every selected row. Necessity follows by
taking $H$ to be the closure of an existing common
query's native seed, which is contained in the
greatest fixed set.

Hence there is a structural recognition test for
the full mixed part when its posting graph has no
monochromatic cycle. Seek a set $J$ of mixed heads
containing the queried head, one row per head of
$J$, and a prime $G$ at the queried head. The union
of the rows' mixed postings outside $J$ must be
consistent, omit the observer, and avoid $G$.
In each native block require the common comparison
just given. Shared unheaded inputs can first be
fixed in a branch avoiding $G$, using the preceding
elimination. These conditions construct a positive
query with a ranked cut in the retained mixed budget,
so it is impossible. Conversely, from any missing
positive take the ranked cut after the native
reduction and lift its rows to original rows.
They meet these conditions on the branch of the
original query. Thus a failed recognition test
needs only the prime, the shared fixing, and one
row per selected mixed head; the remaining parts
of the query are synthesized by closure. Row
membership and all checks use the canonical primes.
Passive projections can be treated as singleton
blocks with identity closure.

For a bounded number $m$ of mixed inputs, the same
comparison gives a finite obstruction test even with
monochromatic cycles. On a chosen observer facet,
choose a mixed query and a collection of projected
losses on the $m$ inputs which already forbid it.
Whether the collection forbids that query depends
only on $K,m$ and can be tabulated once in the finite
core clone on $m$ inputs. A collection has at most
$m(K+1)^{m-1}$ distinct rows.

To detect nonrecognition, choose a prime at the
queried head and require its mixed part to allow
the mixed query. Choose a shared fixing avoiding
the prime, and one original representative per
projected loss in the collection, compatible with
that fixing and projected through the unused
inputs. Use the simultaneous native comparison
for the representatives against the nonmixed
part of the prime. If this succeeds, the assembled
query enables the prime but its reduced budget
contains the forbidden collection. Conversely,
at a missed positive, the full projected budget
is one of the forbidden collections. Choose one
representative per distinct projected loss. An
existing query meets all their native comparisons,
so the descending tests recover a counterexample.
No condition on cycles is needed in this argument.

With explicitly listed original rows this gives a
polynomial recognition test at fixed $K,m$ and a
fixed bound on shared inputs, by enumerating the
bounded number of representatives. From primes
alone it gives a coNP certificate at fixed $K,m$,
requiring only the listed prime, shared fixing and
mixed coloring, and that bounded number of
original rows. Their membership and all native
comparison tests are polynomial in the primes.

\paragraph{The greatest union of single-owner folds.}
For an arbitrary loss budget let $C_r$ be the closure
generated by the implications $t_r\to j$ for every
row $t$ at head $j$, including empty premises. A fold
at $r$ is bounded exactly when its support is the
complement of a $C_r$-closed set: each row at a used
head must have an $r$-posting in the support. Hence the
greatest bounded union of single-owner folds supplies
$(i,p)$ exactly when
\[
                   i\notin C_r(p_r)\quad\text{for some }r.
\]
The complement of $C_r(p_r)$ is a witnessing support
for a successful owner. This tests a marked query by
ordinary closure scans of the primes, since finding a
rule at $j$ with premise contained in a current set only
requires excluding the other $r$-postings from a loss
existence test.

For a complete nonempty-valued candidate, a direct
prime criterion for this representation is the following.
For each prime $G$ at $i$ on facet $k$, there must be no
pairwise disjoint choice of sets $B_r$ with
$B_k=\varnothing$, all excluding $i$, such that
$B_r:r$ avoids $G$ and $i\in C_r(B_r)$ for every owner.
Such a choice is precisely a candidate positive absent
from the greatest fold union. It suffices to consider
inclusion-minimal forcing sets at each owner. This gives
a coNP test from prime lists.

There is a polynomial test when the relevant input
domains for the owner closures are pairwise disjoint at
each head. For owner $r$, take the posting ancestors of
$i$ in the graph of the implications for $C_r$ and exclude
$i$ itself. Membership of $i$ in the closure of a set
avoiding $i$ depends only on that set's intersection with
these ancestors: only rules in this backward closure can
contribute to a derivation. For a given prime and facet,
take each $B_r$ to be all these ancestors allowed by the
prime at $r$, or empty at the missing owner. The choices
are disjoint by hypothesis. If they all force $i$, they
witness failure. If one does not, no smaller choice at
that owner forces $i$, so the prime passes.
More generally, enumerate the postings of the inputs
shared by two or more relevant domains, then take all
allowed private inputs at each owner. Monotonicity makes
these maximal choices sufficient on each branch. The
test takes at most $(K+1)^m$ closure computations per
owner and prime, where $m$ bounds the number of shared
inputs in the domains of any head.

When every owner block is empty or a singleton, the
compatibility test is polynomial even with unrestricted
overlap and variable $K$. In each $C_r$, closure is
reachability from the initial inputs and the conclusions
of empty-premise rules. For $i$ and a proposed guard,
ignore owners already forcing $i$ from the empty set.
For each remaining owner list the allowed inputs other
than $i$ which alone force $i$. At the missing observer
this list is empty. A compatible forcing tuple exists
exactly when these owners can be matched to distinct
inputs on their lists. Indeed each nonempty forcing
coalition contains such a singleton by reachability,
and a matching provides disjoint coalitions.

This gives an explicit formula for the canonical primes
of the fold union in the singleton-block case. At a head
$i$ and observer facet, form the bipartite graph above
before imposing any guard. For each nonempty set $X$ of
its remaining owners and each set $Y$ of inputs of size
at most $|X|-1$, take the guard excluding all postings
corresponding to edges from $X$ to inputs outside $Y$.
The inclusion minima of these guards are exactly the
canonical primes. Each such guard violates Hall's
condition in the allowed graph. Conversely, if a guard
prevents all matchings of the owners, Hall's condition
gives $X$ with at most $|X|-1$ allowed neighbors. Using
those neighbors for $Y$ gives one of the displayed guards
contained in the original guard.

At a headed input there are at most $K-1$ remaining owners,
since a proper row has an empty premise for at least one
owner. An unheaded input has the empty prime. Thus for
fixed $K$ the formula lists at most $O_K(n^{K-2})$
candidate guards per head and facet, where $n=|V|$.
The exponent is attained. At a single head $i$ use $m$
rows on $m\ge K-1$ other inputs, each row posting
$K-1$ consecutive inputs in cyclic order at the respective
$K-1$ owners different from a fixed observer. Every
remaining owner is adjacent to every input. Take $X$
to be all $K-1$ owners and $Y$ any $K-2$ inputs. After
excluding the postings to the complement of $Y$, the
allowed graph is complete on these owners and $Y$.
Restoring any one excluded edge matches its owner to
the restored input and the others to $Y$. Thus all
$\binom{m}{K-2}$ guards obtained this way are prime.

The matching description also tests whether this fold union
is the greatest bounded core operation, without being given
that operation. Every loss of the fold union contains an
inclusion-minimal loss consisting of at most one posting
per remaining owner, namely a matching. At a headed input
this uses at most $K-1$ postings. Enumerate those matchings
on each observer facet and test their marks against the
original budget by singleton-block fold saturation.
If a tested mark passes, a bounded word supplies a mark
missing from the fold union. Conversely any discrepancy
has one on a contained matching: the fold union still
loses there, and the core envelope retains the mark by
antitonicity. Thus equality is decidable in polynomial time
at fixed $K$, directly from the original primes.

The bound of $K-1$ postings for detecting a gap is sharp.
In the cyclic-row example, choose a matching that is not
one of the listed cyclic rows, taking $m>K-1$.
The fold union loses there, but the full budget permits
the mark: all other inputs are unheaded, so each row at
$i$ can be removed by attaching the projection of an
input whose posting disagrees with the query. On fewer
than $K-1$ postings no matching exists, so there is no
gap at $i$, and all other inputs have their projections.

The Hall formula extends to owner closures with quota
conditions at the queried head. Suppose that for each
owner $r$ there are laminar input sets $D_{r,\ell}$ and
nonnegative integer quotas $q_{r,\ell}$ such that, for all $B$ excluding
$i$, $i\in C_r(B)$ exactly when
$|B\cap D_{r,\ell}|\ge q_{r,\ell}$ for every $\ell$.
The sets for different owners may overlap. Place each
owner's groups in an inclusion forest. At a group $D$
with quota $q_D$, compute
\[
 d_D=\max\bigl(q_D,\sum_{E\text{ child of }D}d_E\bigr),
 \qquad b_D=d_D-\sum_{E\text{ child of }D}d_E .
\]
Make $b_D$ copies of a left vertex whose neighbors are
the eligible inputs of $D$, with an edge meaning a
posting at that group's owner. Every input has capacity
one across all copies and owners. A matching of the
copies gives $d_D$ distinct chosen inputs in each
group $D$ from the copies in its subtree, meeting
its quota. Conversely a set satisfying the quotas
contains at least $d_D$ inputs in each group, by induction
and disjointness of the children. Assign inputs to the
copies from the leaves up, using any still unused inputs
in a group for its own $b_D$ copies. This fills all demands.
For disjoint groups the demands are the original quotas.
If some $d_D>|D|$, the tuple is impossible and the empty
guard suffices. Otherwise the matching graph has polynomial
size in the group lists and the input set.

In particular the canonical primes of the fold union are
the inclusion minima of guards obtained as follows. Choose
a nonempty set $X$ of groups and a set $Y$ of inputs with
$|Y|<\sum_{D\in X}b_D$. Exclude every posting
on an edge from $X$ to the complement of $Y$. Hall's
condition on the copies proves sufficiency. Conversely
a deficient set of copies can be enlarged to include
every copy of each of its groups without changing its
neighborhood, so it yields one of these guards inside
any sufficient guard. The singleton case has one group
of quota one per remaining owner.
Every coloring losing at the fold union contains a
subcoloring with exactly $\sum_D b_D$ postings
that still loses there, by keeping the edges of an
assignment filling the demands. Thus any gap from the
full bounded core envelope also occurs with at most
that many postings.

With unrestricted overlap, checking one proposed guard
for the greatest bounded fold union is coNP-complete,
even on acyclic budgets of canonical prime width one
with at most two postings per owner block.
For hardness let $a,b,c$ be distinct owners and use facet
$c$. For a Boolean formula in conjunctive normal form,
give each variable two unheaded inputs for its two
literals. At $a$ encode a monotone circuit on these
inputs checking that at least one literal of each
variable is present and that each clause has a present
literal. At $b$ encode just the variable-pair checks
on the same inputs. Use distinct auxiliary heads for
the two circuits. A binary OR gate at owner $r$ has one
singleton loss per predecessor at $r$; a binary AND
gate has one loss posting both predecessors at $r$.
Let $u,v$ be the respective output heads. At a fresh
marked head $i$ use the row $\{u:a,v:b\}$.

The proposed guard forbids postings at $a$ and $b$
on all auxiliary circuit inputs. Thus on an enabled
coloring every native circuit head enters its native
closure exactly by its gate rule, and $i$ enters
$C_a(p_a)$ and $C_b(p_b)$ exactly when both circuits
accept. At every other owner its row has empty premise,
so $i$ lies in those closures automatically. The
two accepting circuits require each variable pair to
have an $a$-posting and a $b$-posting. Consistency
makes these opposite literals. The $a$-posts therefore
encode a satisfying assignment, and every satisfying
assignment conversely gives such a coloring. The guard
fails for the fold union exactly when the formula is
satisfiable.

To achieve width one, pad the two OR rows at each
gate of owner $r$ to omit two distinct owners other
than $r$. Use one unheaded input per padding color
and put one posting at each color other than $r$ and
the intended missing owner. The native $r$-premise
is unchanged; the other owner's circuit uses separate
heads and is unaffected. Every OR row is now visible
on just one observer facet, and at most one of the
two is visible on any given facet. Each AND gate
and the target already have only one row. On every
facet the primes are therefore either the singleton
postings of its one row or the empty guard when
there is no row. Padding adds no dependency cycle
and never enlarges an owner block beyond size two.
The construction has polynomial size for every fixed
$K\ge3$. Membership follows by guessing the coloring
and computing the owner closures.

\paragraph{A polynomial test when the positive and foreign colors are disjoint.}
Fix an owner $a$. The negative queries may use $a$ arbitrarily often,
but each uses every other color at most once. For a positive $(i,p)$
put $B=p_a$. Suppose no vertex of $B$ receives a foreign color in
any negative query. In particular $B$ may be empty. There is no
inclusion condition on the repeated $a$-coalitions.

For a set $U$ of negative queries and $s\ne a$, draw $j\to u$ for
$(j,t)\in U$ with $t_s=\{u\}$, and $j\to\dagger$ if $t_s$ is empty.
Let $C_s^U(v)$ be the set reachable from $v$, including $v$.
If it contains $\dagger$, regard it as contained in no input coalition.
Otherwise it is the least support containing $v$ whose $s$-fold
omits every mark in $U$. Indeed, whenever its support contains a
head $j$, the fold must absorb on that row, forcing its $s$-colored
vertex into the support.

For a given budget $S$ start with $U_0=\varnothing$ and put
\[
 U_{m+1}=\bigl\{(j,t)\in S:
   C_s^{U_m}(v)\not\subseteq t_a
   \text{ for every }s\ne a,\ v\notin B\bigr\}.
\]
These sets increase. Write $U$ for their eventual value.

\begin{theorem}[Removing all attainable $a$-singletons]
\label{core:thm:disjoint-foreign-guards}
For every $A\subseteq V$ some $S$-bounded word wins the $a$-test on
$A$ and loses it on $B$ if and only if $C_s^U(v)\subseteq A$ for
some $s\ne a$ and $v\notin B$. If there is any such word, a single
$a$-fold of at most $(K-1)|V|$ foreign folds attains all these positive
tests simultaneously while losing on $B$.

To decide the positive source query, form $U$ for the current budget.
A projection supplying $(i,p)$ and omitting the marks of $U$ succeeds.
Otherwise apply any foreign fold from the reachability test of
\cref{core:thm:injective-blockers} that deletes a nonempty set of
rows from $U$, and repeat on the retained rows. If there is no such
fold, the query fails. The algorithm takes polynomial time on an
explicit sample. Every impossible query is already impossible with
at most $(K-1)|V|(|V|+1)+1$ of its original negative rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We claim at each stage that no word bounded on $U_m$ and losing the
$a$-test on $B$ wins that test on the $a$-tail of any row of $U_m$.
This starts vacuously. Under that claim, suppose a word bounded on
$U_m$ wins on $A$ and loses on $B$. If it is a projection, its input
$v$ lies in $A\setminus B$ and is absent from the heads, so its closure is $\{v\}$.
At an $a$-root no row of $U_m$ absorbs by the claim. Both children
therefore fit the rows, and one retains both profile tests.
At a foreign $s$-root without absorption on $U_m$ both children
again fit the rows and one retains both tests. If instead this root
absorbs on a row with $t_s=\{v\}$, the whole word wins the $s$-test
on $\{v\}$ and $v\notin B$ by hypothesis. All its winning singleton
inputs for $s$ lie in $A$ by the common-source property. They are
closed under the displayed edges and avoid $\dagger$: by
\cref{core:lem:singleton-profile-absorption} the word can omit a
winning input's mark only by returning $\{s\}$ there. Thus
$C_s^{U_m}(v)\subseteq A$. Descent proves the asserted necessity.
Its converse is the $s$-fold of this closure. In particular no
$U_m$-bounded word losing on $B$ can win on any $a$-tail in
$U_{m+1}$. Since $U_m\subseteq U_{m+1}$, this proves the induction
claim at the next stage.

At the fixed point take the $a$-fold of all the foreign folds on the
closures avoiding $\dagger$ with $v\notin B$. Each omits the marks of
$U$ and loses the $a$-test on $B$. Every row of $S\setminus U$ is
absorbed by at least one factor. This gives the simultaneous witness;
if there are no factors, the proved necessity excludes every bounded
word losing on $B$, since every word wins on $V$.

A marked witness on $U$ loses the $a$-test on $B$ and therefore
provides a nonempty family of factors. Attaching the simultaneous
witness at owner $a$ preserves the mark and enforces $S\setminus U$.
It remains to recognize the mark on $U$. A foreign fold that passes
the reachability test is safe there and preserves the mark on attachment.
If none passes, at a foreign root both children fit every row of $U$
by the reachability argument. At an $a$-root both fit by the induction
claim, since the marked word loses on $B$. Descent must reach the
positive projection. Computing the closures and strictly enlarging or
restricting finite row sets takes polynomial time.

For the size bound take the terminal failing budget $U$. All its rows
enter the increasing process starting at the empty set even when
started with $U$ alone: deleting rows outside its limit does not change
any stage. Keep the first row introducing each edge $j\to u$ or
$j\to\dagger$ in each foreign graph, and one row with head $i$.
In the original order of entry, each retained row still enters: all
edges available before its entry have an equally early retained witness.
The final graphs are therefore unchanged. The retained rows still
exclude the projection and every $a$-absorption. They also exclude
every foreign escape: reversing the singleton edges and using
the empty-tail heads as seeds recovers the same reachable vertices,
so each retained tail is reached. There are at most
$(K-1)|V|(|V|+1)$ edges.
\end{proof}
