\paragraph{All observers at one source.}
There is a closed criterion for prescribing the complete coefficient
family of one source, together with a greatest core completion.
Fix a source $x$ and a nonempty finite set $W$ of other inputs.
Write
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal D_W&=\{(E_r)_{r\in I}:E_r\subseteq W\text{ pairwise disjoint},
                    \ E_k=\varnothing\text{ for some }k\},\\
 \mathcal D_k&=\{E\in\mathcal D_W:E_k=\varnothing\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Order these partial colorings componentwise by inclusion. A coefficient
at observer $k$ is a predicate $c_k$ on $\mathcal D_k$: its canonical
probe puts $x=\{k\}$, the color-$r$ inputs at $\{r\}$, and all remaining
inputs at $I\setminus\{k\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Single-source compatibility and greatest completion]
\label{core:thm:source-completion}
For finite $K\geq3$, a family $(c_k)_k$ is the complete coefficient
family of one source in a core word if and only if each $c_k$ is
antitone and
\[
 c_k(E)=c_\ell(E)\qquad(E\in\mathcal D_k\cap\mathcal D_\ell).
\]
The predicates glue to a unique antitone $P:\mathcal D_W\to\{0,1\}$.
Extend it by zero to partial colorings outside $\mathcal D_W$.
Put $U(w)=\bigcup_{i\in W}w_i$ and $E_r(w)=\{i:w_i=\{r\}\}$.
The unique greatest core operation with that source family is
\[
 F_P(X,w)=
 \begin{cases}
 U(w)\cup X,&P(E(w))=1,\\
 U(w),&P(E(w))=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Consequently these are exactly the core operations retaining every
input in $W$. The distinguished input is unused precisely when $P=0$;
otherwise $P(\varnothing)=1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For an occurrence $o$ of $x$, collect its path filters by owner:
\[
 B_{o,r}(E)=\bigwedge_{\substack{\text{$r$-ancestors of }o\\
                          \text{with sibling }s}}
       \neg\Phi_r(s)(E),\qquad E\subseteq W.
\]
Here all $x$-occurrences have false $r$-atomic flag. Each factor is
antitone and $B_{o,r}(\varnothing)=1$. At observer $k$, the occurrence
contributes $\bigwedge_{r\ne k}B_{o,r}(E_r)$. On $\mathcal D_k$ its
missing factor is $B_{o,k}(\varnothing)=1$, so this is the full product.
The disjunction of these products proves antitonicity and agreement on
overlaps. Conversely, agreement defines $P$. If $E\leq E'$, choose an
owner missing from $E'$; both colorings lie in its facet, proving that
$P$ is antitone. Its value at the empty coloring is zero exactly when
it is identically zero.

For synthesis, for every positive coloring $E$ start a spine at $x$.
For each $r$ with $W\setminus E_r\ne\varnothing$, attach by $\star_r$
the $r$-fold of the variables in $W\setminus E_r$. Its owner-$r$ path
factor at another coloring $E'$ is exactly $[E'_r\subseteq E_r]$.
Take the union of all these spines with $U$. Every contribution from
a passive input lies in $U$. If $k\notin U$, then $E'_k=\varnothing$,
so the ignored own-$k$ factor is automatically true. A spine supplies
$k$ from $X$ exactly when $E'\leq E$. Antitonicity proves soundness,
and the spine indexed by $E'$ supplies every required winner outside
$U$. If $U=I$, retention already determines the output, including when
the coloring is outside $\mathcal D_W$. If $P=0$, use $U$ alone.

To prove greatestness, any winner of another core completion lying
outside $U$ must come from $x$. Its source coefficient is exactly
$P(E(w))$, because the other inputs omit that queried winner. Thus the
completion is below $F_P$. Finally any core operation retaining $W$
must already contain $U$; the same argument forces it to equal $F_P$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Meet-irreducibles of the full core.}
Fix at least two inputs and order the core operations, with formal
zero adjoined, by pointwise inclusion. This is a finite lattice since
it is closed under union. For a head $x$ and a proper coloring
$E\in\mathcal D_W$ of the other inputs, write $M_{x,E}$ for the
single-source completion with predicate $[E\nleq E']$ at $E'$.
It is the greatest core operation omitting the mark at $(x,E)$:
antitonicity forces every extension of $E$ to be a loss, and
all other heads are retained by the completion.
It has the explicit term
$U+\sum_{r\in I}\sum_{j\in E_r}(x\star_r x_j)$,
omitting the sum for the empty coloring. A winner outside $U$
survives exactly when at least one posting of $E$ fails.

These are exactly the meet-irreducibles. To see that $M_{x,E}$
has a unique upper cover, replace the predicate by the indicator
of the complement of the strict upper cone of $E$.
This is again an antitone predicate, and the new completion
has restored exactly the coefficient at $E$. Every strict
upper bound of $M_{x,E}$ retains the other heads and must
restore that coefficient, by antitonicity. Here pointwise order
is equivalent to coefficientwise order: probes give one
implication and source reconstruction gives the converse.
Distinct pairs $(x,E)$ have distinct loss cones.

Conversely, every nonzero core operation $F$ is the meet of
the $M_{x,E}$ indexed by its minimal losses, taking minima at
each head on the glued coloring poset. All these factors lie
above $F$, and a common minorant loses every extension of every
listed loss, so source reconstruction places it below $F$.
The empty meet is the full input union. Thus any nonzero
meet-irreducible must be one of the single-loss completions.
Formal zero is the meet of two distinct projections, since
a tuple with different singleton values at their inputs
leaves no nonempty common minorant. With one input the lattice
has only the projection and formal zero, so zero is its
only meet-irreducible.

\paragraph{When a pointwise intersection admits source reconstruction.}
Fix a nonempty family of core operations, allowing formal zero.
At a proper coloring $E$ of the inputs, let $S_F(E)$ be the set of
unposted heads with positive coefficient in factor $F$.
The pointwise intersection admits source reconstruction exactly when,
for every $E$, one factor has
\[
 S_F(E)=\bigcap_G S_G(E).
\]
In particular, for two factors their sets of available heads must
be nested at each coloring. Pairwise nesting suffices for any finite
family, since a finite chain has a least member.

Indeed choose an owner $k$ missing from $E$. The inputs containing
$k$ may be any subset $H$ of the unposted heads: give its elements
ambiguous menus containing $k$ and the others $I\setminus\{k\}$.
The factors' values all contain $k$ exactly when $H$ hits every
$S_F(E)$. On the canonical probes a single head supplies $k$, so
the intersection has coefficient set $S=\bigcap_F S_F(E)$ among the
unposted heads. Reconstruction would require $H\cap S\ne\varnothing$.
These tests agree for all $H$ if some factor has set $S$.
Otherwise the complement of $S$ among the unposted heads hits every
factor and misses $S$. Every ordinary valuation at a queried
winner has this form at the level of foreign postings and inputs
containing the winner, so the tests give the claimed equivalence.

For single-loss factors $M_{x,E}$, the available set is the entire
unposted set with at most one head removed. The least-set condition
therefore fails exactly when two listed losses $(x,E),(y,D)$ satisfy
all of the following: $x\ne y$, neither loss posts the other head,
the postings agree wherever both post, and their union omits an
owner. Such a pair can be tested on the union coloring, and every
failure at a coloring gives a pair by choosing two removed heads.
On probes the factor intersection has precisely the prescribed losses,
including their extensions. For a realizable family this test decides
whether its single-loss meet is pointwise. In general, passing the test
establishes agreement with the reconstructed operation, whose core
realizability is a further condition.

There is a stronger pair test for the pointwise intersection itself
to be core. Use the minimal losses in the prescribed lists. For each
pair at different heads $x,y$, erase the postings of $x,y$ from
both losses. Whenever the union of what remains is a consistent
proper coloring, the original losses must post each other's heads
at the same owner. This is necessary in any arity and sufficient
when at most two heads have losses.

For necessity, suppose the pointwise intersection is core and
adjoin every projection other than $x,y$ by union. This retains the
other heads and preserves the coefficients at $x,y$.
An unmatched pair would then force both heads to be lost at the
background union with $x,y$ unposted, by
\cref{core:cor:two-conditional-guard-complexity}. But at that
coloring each original factor can lose only one of the two heads.
Their common coefficient set cannot be the set of any factor,
contradicting source reconstruction.

Conversely the stronger test excludes the pairs that obstruct
source reconstruction. With two headed inputs it also makes the
two-source compatibility rule vacuous: every consistent proper
background union has matching reciprocal postings. There is
a positive bit, since two empty losses would violate the test
and any unheaded input is retained. The two-source rule therefore
realizes the reconstructed operation, which equals the pointwise
intersection. With one headed input use
\cref{core:thm:source-completion}, and with none use the full union.

The pair test can miss an implication that requires every head.
Take $3\le n\le K$ inputs and choose a palette of $n-1$ owners.
At each head prescribe one loss posting all other inputs, once at
each selected owner. Require also that each input is posted at
distinct owners in its appearances across the other heads' rows.
Any two rows then disagree at a third input, so the stronger pair
test holds. At most one row can match any tuple, so the pointwise
intersection has nonempty values and agrees with reconstruction.

For each selected owner the posting edges form a permutation of
the inputs, oriented from the posted input to the row head.
If every such permutation is a single cycle, the core meet
adds every bijective posting of the passive inputs at the selected
owners as a loss at each head. If some permutation has multiple
cycles, the pointwise intersection is core as it stands.

Indeed apply \cref{core:thm:injective-blockers} to a query at a head.
A one-row subset can only force its stated loss. For a proper
subset of at least two rows, an omitted head is a tail at different
owners in those rows. No edge in the subset reaches that head,
and it cannot be seeded by the query at both owners. Thus the
subset forces no new loss. In the full subset there are no
missing-color seeds at the selected owners, and reachability
requires a query seed in each cycle at each selected owner.
There are only $n-1$ passive inputs and hence at most one seed
per owner if all selected owners have seeds. This proves both
cases and shows that each new loss requires the entire list.
Neutral marks survive, so the meet is nonzero.

For example, number the inputs modulo $n$ and at head $i$ post
$i+d$ at owner $a_d$ for $1\le d<n$. The permutation at owner $a_d$
is a single cycle exactly when $d$ is relatively prime to $n$.
Thus for prime $n$ the $n$ rows force all $(n-1)!$ permutations
at each head, while for composite $n$ the original rows are
already complete. At $n=3$ this gives the two oppositely
oriented triangles and adds exactly the reversed posting at
each head, for every $K\ge3$.

On a facet whose observer is outside the selected palette, this
closure changes $m=n-1$ one-literal primes at each head into
$\binom{2m}{m+1}$ primes. After closure a sufficient guard must
exclude all perfect matchings between the passive inputs and
the selected owners. Hall's condition says its exclusions must
contain a rectangle $A\times B$ with $|A|+|B|=m+1$.
Each such rectangle is minimal: restoring any of its edges
allows a matching using that edge, matching the remaining
inputs of $A$ outside $B$ and the remaining owners of $B$
outside $A$. Thus the rectangles are exactly the primes,
and their count follows by choosing $m+1$ elements from the
two disjoint sides.

The number of rows needed for one loss implication is unbounded
even with a fixed palette. On $n\ge3$ inputs indexed cyclically,
give head $i$ the sole loss posting $i-1$ at $a$ and $i+1$ at $b$.
The core meet has precisely the losses that post both $a$ and $b$
somewhere off the queried head.
Indeed the full row set has an owner cycle in each direction,
so \cref{core:thm:injective-blockers} forces every such loss.
Conversely the union of the full $a$-fold and the full $b$-fold
obeys the rows and supplies every mark on a coloring missing
either of these colors.

For a specific new loss at $i$, reverse its two postings.
If a subset of rows blocks this mark by the reachability test,
it contains the row of $i$. Any predecessor of a head in the
subset must also be a head in it unless that predecessor is
the single $a$-seed $i+1$: a vertex outside the subset has
no incoming edges and can only be reached as a seed.
Starting at $i$ this includes every head except possibly $i+1$.
The $b$-tail at $i$ is $i+1$, which is not the $b$-seed, so
its row must be included as well. Thus all $n$ input rows
are necessary for the reversed loss. On an observer facet
seeing both colors, each head initially has two one-literal
primes. After closure each of the two primes excludes every
posting at one of the colors, and has width $n-1$.

The reachability test has a direct graph form for any budget
with at most one row at each head and at most one posting per
owner in each row. For a query $(i,p)$, draw one arrow from each
head to each posted input, labeled by its owner. Delete an arrow
of owner $r$ if its target lies in $p_r$.
The greatest bounded operation loses the query exactly when
every vertex reachable from $i$ is a head and the reachable
subgraph has no monochromatic directed cycle.

Indeed, for a subset of rows to block the query in
\cref{core:thm:injective-blockers}, it must contain the row at $i$.
Every surviving arrow out of one of its heads must lead to another
of its heads: an outside tail is reachable in the theorem's reversed
graph only if it is seeded. Thus the subset must contain the entire
reachable set. Moreover a reachable monochromatic cycle would have
no seed on that cycle. In the reversed graph each of its vertices
has exactly one incoming edge at that owner, coming from inside the
cycle. Hence its tails cannot be reached, and no subset blocks the query.

Conversely, if the stated condition holds, use the rows of the
reachable set. At each owner, follow the posting arrows from a head.
The path stays in the set until it meets a deleted arrow or ends
at a head with no posting at that owner, since an infinite path
would give a monochromatic cycle. The target of the deleted arrow
is a query seed, and a head without a posting is a seed by the
missing-color rule. Reversing the path witnesses reachability
of the original tail from a seed, including when its own arrow
was deleted immediately. Thus every row meets the reachability
condition for an obstruction. The reachable set is consequently
the unique least subset that forces the loss, whenever the query
is lost.

On an observer facet the graph test gives a disjunction of guards.
Use one guard for each simple path from the queried head to an
input without a row, and one for each simple path to a monochromatic
cycle together with that cycle. A guard excludes the posting at
the target and owner of each edge used. Exclusions at the queried
head or at the observer are automatic and can be dropped.
The graph test says exactly that some such guard holds.
If all incoming arrows at each input have a common owner,
the inclusion-minimal guards in this presentation are precisely
the canonical primes. Indeed, to test whether any set of
exclusions is sufficient, post every passive input at its incoming
owner unless that posting is forbidden by the facet or the guard.
This is a consistent coloring that disables as many edges as
possible. It supplies the mark only if a path or cycle witness
uses exclusively automatic or explicitly excluded postings.

In the common-incoming-owner case this also recognizes exact
guard families directly. For each head $i$ and observer $k$,
retain the arrows entering $i$ and all arrows labeled $k$.
For an unheaded input or a row posting at $k$, its only prime
on that facet is empty: test for a path to an unheaded input
or to a monochromatic cycle using just those retained arrows.
For any other row, test each of its singleton primes in turn
by also retaining the arrows entering the input excluded by that
prime. The family is realized, allowing formal zero, exactly
when every test succeeds.

Indeed the arrows used in each test are precisely those that
survive the maximal posting of passive inputs at their incoming
owners subject to the prime. The prime is supplied everywhere
on its facet exactly when it is supplied there, by antitonicity
in those postings. All prescribed losses already hold in the
core meet, so testing the primes checks the opposite inclusion.
This takes a graph search and monochromatic cycle check per
listed prime, without constructing the primes of the meet.
For ordinary core realizability also require a nonempty prime
list at some head and observer.

For arbitrary incoming owners, a closure and a matching test
decide whether a guard admits a losing coloring. Fix the queried
head $i$, observer $k$, and guard $G$. A posting is permitted
if its input is not $i$, its owner is not $k$, and $G$ does
not exclude it. Start a set $S$ with $i$ and close it under
these implications:
\begin{itemize}
\item If an arrow starts in $S$ and its target posting is
forbidden, put its target in $S$.
\item If arrows of different owners start in $S$ and have
the same target, put that target in $S$.
\end{itemize}
There is a losing coloring satisfying $G$ exactly when every
vertex in the least such $S$ has a row and its internal
monochromatic cycles can be matched to distinct vertices.
A cycle may be matched only to one of its vertices where
its owner is a permitted posting.

To prove this, consider any row subset witnessing a loss by
\cref{core:thm:injective-blockers}. A tail outside the subset
must be seeded at every owner required by an arrow from the
subset. This forces both closure implications, so the subset
contains $S$. Each monochromatic cycle inside $S$ must receive
a seed at one of its own vertices: at that owner each cycle
vertex has just one incoming edge in the theorem's reversed
graph, from its predecessor on the cycle. Distinct cycles
of one owner are disjoint since the posting arrows at that
owner are functional. Cycles of different owners cannot use
the same seed since a vertex has at most one posting. This
gives the necessary matching.

Conversely, post the outside targets of arrows from $S$ at
their required owners. Closure ensures their required postings
are permitted and agree. Post the matched vertices inside $S$
at their matched owners, leaving every other input unposted.
In each owner graph, following posting arrows from a head of
$S$ either reaches a seeded outside target, ends at a head
without that owner, or reaches an internal cycle with a seed.
Reversing the paths gives reachability of all tails from the
seeds required by the theorem. Thus the rows of $S$ force
the queried loss on this coloring. In particular, polynomial
recognition from the original prime lists extends to arbitrary
incoming owners by applying this test to each listed prime:
the family is exact precisely when none admits a losing coloring.

The same guard test extends to several injective rows per head
with a cost exponential only in the number of excess rows.
For each head with multiple rows, guess exactly which of its
rows belong to the obstruction, allowing the empty choice.
Start $S$ with the queried head and all the heads with nonempty
guesses. Apply the same two closure implications to the chosen
rows and to the unique row at each singly headed input in $S$.
Reject the guess if a forced input has no selected or selectable
row. Every obstruction with this guess contains the resulting
$S$, and uses exactly the same rows there.

For each owner now draw the reversed posting graph on $S$,
with arrows pointing from posted inputs to heads. Seed a head
if one of its rows omits that owner or if it has an incoming
arrow from outside $S$. Every such outside tail can be posted
as closure prescribes. Match each source strongly connected
component without a seed to a distinct permitted vertex of
that component at its owner. This replaces the cycle matching
when several rows give several incoming edges to a head.
At one owner the components are disjoint, and between owners
the matching enforces consistency. In any larger obstruction
with the same guess, none of these unseeded source components
can gain an incoming edge or a missing-owner seed: both depend
only on the rows at their own heads. Consequently the matching
is necessary. It is sufficient because it makes every vertex
reachable from a seed at every owner, so all internal tails
are reached and all outside tails are seeded by their postings.
The selected rows on $S$ therefore witness a loss.

If the budget has $t$ rows beyond one per headed input,
the multiply headed inputs have at most $2t$ rows altogether.
Testing all guesses takes at most $4^t$ polynomial-time
closure and matching tests per guard. This applies to checking
exactness directly from the original primes when the loss
budget is also given.

Alternatively, one can bound the search in terms of the number
$b$ of heads with multiple rows. The reachability certificate
argument following \cref{core:thm:injective-blockers} keeps the
same head set and uses at most $K$ rows at each head: keep one
row and at most one additional row witnessing first arrival
for each owner posted by that row. Guess only subsets of size
at most $K$ at the multiply headed inputs. With $M$ explicit
loss rows the test then takes $(M+1)^{Kb}$ polynomial-time
closure and matching tests. Thus at fixed $K$ and $b$ the
number of rows at each of those heads may be unbounded.

The closure and matching test also permits arbitrary repetitions
at retained inputs. More precisely, every row may have several
postings per owner provided at most one of them is on an input
that has a row anywhere in the budget. Keep all postings in
the two closure implications, rejecting any guess that forces
an input without a row. In the matching test use only the
arrows between inputs that have rows. The outside postings
from the selected rows still have to be made simultaneously.

Indeed, fix the postings on inputs without rows. The
unmarked-background reduction
(\cref{core:lem:unmarked-background}) keeps just the rows whose
background postings hold, then erases those postings.
The remaining rows are injective. For any obstruction on this
reduced budget, the original background postings of its rows
are therefore required, in addition to the postings at outside
targets that still have rows. Conflicts or forbidden postings
force the target into $S$ by the closure rules, which is
impossible for a retained input. Conversely the construction
from a successful matching posts all the required outside
targets, including retained inputs, and seeds the chosen
components. This enables all chosen rows on the fixed
background, where the injective obstruction applies.
The same bounds in the excess or number of multiply headed
inputs hold when the rows are explicit, since the certificate
bound of $K$ rows per head applies after erasing background
postings. In particular a single row per head still gives
polynomial guard implication for unbounded background blocks.

The loss budget can also be recovered from the canonical primes
with $K$ counted as a parameter. On a facet, a sufficient guard
must hit each visible minimal loss with an exclusion: otherwise
that loss itself satisfies the guard. Conversely a guard hitting
all visible minimal losses excludes their upward closure.
Thus if the excess is at most $t$, every prime has width at
most $t+1$, since there are at most $t+1$ losses at a head and
a minimal hitting set uses at most one witness per loss.
Reject wider primes when testing for membership in this class.

At each head and observer start from the empty coloring. If the
current coloring satisfies a prime, branch on the postings excluded
by that prime, adding one consistent posting at an unused owner.
A zero needs to hit this prime, so every injective minimal loss
extending the current coloring extends some branch.
Stop at a zero and record it if all its immediate subcolorings
are positive. Every injective minimal loss is found: along a
branch contained in it, no proper subcoloring can be zero.
The tree has depth at most $K-1$ and branching at most $t+1$.
Unite the recovered rows across facets and check the total excess.

To certify that the recovered rows describe the whole family,
reconstruct their canonical prime lists and compare with the input.
On each facet choose one exclusion from each row omitting its
observer, and minimize the resulting sets. With no visible row
this gives the empty guard, and a visible empty row gives no
guard. There are at most $(K-1)^{t+1}$ choices per list.
Equality certifies completeness of the recovered rows and
agreement across observers. A discrepancy rules out a complete
injective budget of the specified excess. Together with closure
and matching, this gives recognition from prime lists alone in
time $f(K,t)$ times a polynomial in the input length and arity.

Unbounded background blocks require a different recovery step.
With excess at most $t$, on each facet maintain a list of the
minimal losses already found. To find a new one, choose one
posting to exclude from each known loss. If the remaining
ground set hits every prime, shrink it to a minimal transversal.
Canonicity makes this a consistent loss, and it is new since it
omits a posting from every known loss. Conversely any new
minimal loss omits some posting from each known one and hence
is contained in the remaining ground set for some choice.
An empty known loss precludes any new one. Stop when no choice
succeeds or the count exceeds $t+1$. Unite the facet results
and check the total excess. Facet canonicity and agreement
can be checked separately by consensus and restriction.
This takes $N^{t+O(1)}$ time for input length $N$ even when $K$
varies, and gives a polynomial recognition test from primes
for fixed excess and arbitrary background blocks, using the
closure and matching test above.

Injectivity of the minimal losses can itself be checked in
polynomial time directly from canonical primes, even with no
excess bound and with $K$ varying. On one facet, choose two
postings at the same owner on distinct inputs. A minimal
transversal containing both exists exactly when they have
private guards that can coexist. Specifically, guess two guards
whose intersections with the chosen pair are its two distinct
singletons. Remove
all other postings in these two guards from the ground set.
If the remaining set hits every guard, shrink it to a minimal
transversal. The private guards force both chosen postings
to survive. Canonicity makes that transversal consistent
by \cref{core:thm:guard-consensus}. Conversely, any minimal
loss containing both postings has such private guards and
is contained in the remaining ground set for that choice.
Trying all pairs of postings and private guards detects
every repeated owner. More generally, for any fixed $d$,
trying $d+1$ postings at one owner and their private guards
checks the bound of $d$ postings per owner.

It follows that complete-family recognition within the
injective class is in coNP even for varying $K$. The class
check above and facet canonicity are polynomial. To check
observer agreement, compare the lists for each observer
pair after deleting the newly hidden postings and discarding
supersets. For a valid family with a positive bit, a
nonrealizability certificate consists of a positive coloring
and a blocking subset of at most $nK$ injective rows, using
the certificate bound after \cref{core:thm:injective-blockers}.
Each selected row is checked against the prime lists, and
the certificate is verified by graph reachability. This
certificate needs no enumeration of the minimal losses.
The private-guard check also detects repeats restricted to headed
inputs: a head is retained exactly when its list on every facet
contains the empty guard. Thus the same coNP upper bound applies
when only the headed part of each loss is injective. Verify the
guessed original losses, check their background postings against
the queried coloring, and apply reachability after erasing those
postings. The reduced obstruction bound again gives at most
$nK$ original rows in the certificate.

Allowing a fixed positive number $d$ of repeated classes
on headed inputs already gives coNP-completeness for
complete-family recognition. The coNP bound even permits
the $d$ classes to vary with the background fixing. More
precisely, for each proper coloring of the retained inputs,
keep only the minimal losses whose retained postings it
extends. Erase the retained postings and count distinct
owner--block pairs of size at least two in these rows.
Require this count to be at most $d$ at every fixing.

Violation of the count has a certificate consisting of the
fixing and $d+1$ original minimal losses exhibiting different
repeated pairs. Minimality can be checked against the prime
lists by deleting each posting in turn. If the count holds,
guess a positive query that cannot be supplied and restrict
to its background by \cref{core:lem:unmarked-background}.
The obstruction bound following
\cref{core:thm:repeated-blocker-budgets} gives a polynomial
number of reduced rows for fixed $d$, each lifted to one
original minimal loss that is enabled on that background.
The same repeated-blocker test verifies failure on the
selected rows in polynomial time for fixed $d$. Thus a
failed realization or a failed class check has a polynomial
certificate, with the palette allowed to vary.

For hardness with $d=1$, use the depth-two reduction in
\cref{core:thm:complete-acyclic-guard-complexity}.
The clause heads post only retained flags and truth inputs.
The final head's single row posts all the clause heads at
one owner, giving the only repeated class on headed inputs.
The reduction supplies complete prime lists and works for
every fixed $K\ge3$. Hence the upper bound is sharp already
for one repeated class in the whole family.

For budgets with at most one row per head and
injective postings on the headed inputs, guard
implication for the core meet is polynomial,
including in the presence of cycles. Postings on
inputs that never head a row can repeat at the
same owner. For a fixed probe, keep just the rows
whose postings at these unheaded inputs agree with
the probe, then erase those postings
(\cref{core:lem:unmarked-background}). The remaining
rows are injective, so the blocker theorem applies.
In the search over probes every chosen row must
therefore require its original unheaded postings,
in addition to its reduced blocker constraints.
Fix the query head $i$, observer, and guard. A posting
is allowed if it can occur on that facet with the guard
enabled, so in particular no posting at $i$ is allowed.
Introduce a Boolean variable $e_j$ for choosing the
unique row at head $j$ in a blocking subfamily. Force
$e_i$ and forbid $e_u$ at inputs with no row. For each
posting $u{:}r$ in the row at $j$, impose
$e_j\Rightarrow e_u$ if the posting is not allowed.
For each pair of rows at $j,j'$ posting the same $u$
at different owners, impose
$e_j\land e_{j'}\Rightarrow e_u$.

Every blocking subfamily on an enabled probe must
satisfy the Horn system: it contains the row at $i$,
and each reachable tail is either headed in the
subfamily or posted at its required owner on the
probe. The latter postings must be allowed and
consistent. If the system is satisfiable, let $H$
be the heads in its least solution. Post their
required tails outside $H$ at the required owners.
The implications make this coloring consistent and
allowed.

For each owner $r$, draw the $r$-edges of the selected
rows after erasing their original unheaded postings.
Those erased postings have already been enforced
by the Horn system and the external coloring.
Every head has at most one incoming $r$-edge.
Outside the directed cycles, reachability follows
by tracing predecessors to a selected empty-tail
row, an externally posted tail, or a cycle.
Each directed cycle still needs a seed: one of its
vertices must be posted at $r$, since no edge enters
it from outside. Use bipartite matching to assign
distinct vertices to the cycles across all owners,
allowing a vertex only when its posting at that
cycle's owner is allowed. These seeds lie in $H$
and cannot conflict with the external postings.
If the matching covers all cycles, every head is
reachable at every owner, so the chosen rows form
a blocker by \cref{core:thm:injective-blockers}.

If no such matching exists, enlarging $H$ cannot
help. Any blocking subfamily contains the least
Horn solution, and every existing cycle remains a
cycle with no external incoming edge because each
head still uses its unique row. Its seeds would
give a matching for the original cycles. Thus the
least solution and matching give an exact search
for an enabled probe with no bounded witness.
For acyclic budgets the matching is vacuous.

Consequently one can check every prime for this
class in polynomial time, giving complete-family
recognition as well. From canonical primes the
single-row lists are recovered directly from the
singleton clauses on each visible facet, including
the cases of no loss or an empty loss. The count
of rows in this result is per head, across all
facets.

The same test extends to a fixed choice of rows at all
multiply headed inputs. Guess a subset of the rows at
each such head, possibly empty, and fix its Boolean
variable accordingly. Run the Horn construction using
only the guessed rows at these heads and every unique
row at the other heads. In the least solution, replace
directed cycles in the matching step by source strongly
connected components of each owner's reduced graph
restricted to the selected heads. A head is already
seeded if one of its selected rows has an empty
reduced tail at that owner
or has a tail posted externally at that owner.
Every source component without such a head needs an allowed
posting on one of its selected heads. As before, a
matching supplies these postings, and all other
components are reached from source components.
No larger solution within the guess can remove one
of these obligations: all incoming edges and all
empty-tail rows at its previously selected heads
are already fixed. An unseeded source component
therefore cannot gain an incoming edge or an
empty-tail seed.

Only guesses of at most $K$ rows per multiply headed
input are needed. In a blocker every head is reachable
at every owner. At each owner take shortest paths
from the seeds, and at each head not already posted
there retain a row witnessing its empty-tail seed
or its last path edge in the reduced graph. These
choices preserve all
the reachabilities by induction on distance. Every
head is unposted at some owner, so at least one of its
rows is retained. Because the head set stays the same,
each tail of every retained row is still reachable
at its owner. Thus these choices again give a blocker.

With $h$ multiply headed inputs and $M$ explicit loss
rows, guard implication and complete recognition take
$M^{O(Kh)}$ times a polynomial in the input size.
More precisely the branching factor is the product,
over the multiply headed inputs, of the counts of
subsets of size at most $K$ of their row lists. This
gives fixed-parameter time in the total number of
rows at those inputs. For the latter parameter one
can also start from canonical primes: the incremental
loss recovery in \emph{Recovering small packed budgets
from primes} applies to each list with the same
parameter bound, and the singleton lists require
only a polynomial scan.

The guard implication problem for the core meet is coNP-complete
without the excess bound, even at fixed $K\ge3$, for acyclic
injective rows and a queried empty guard. For membership, guess
a losing coloring on the facet and run the polynomial fixed-query
test in \cref{core:thm:injective-blockers}. To prove hardness,
take a $3$CNF with at least two nonempty clauses. Use a head $h_j$
per clause and a retained input per Boolean variable. At $h_j$
give one row per literal of clause $j$. It posts that literal's
variable at $a$ for a positive literal or $b$ for a negative one.
Unless $j$ is the last clause, the same row posts $h_{j+1}$ at
$c$, where $a,b,c$ are distinct. Query the empty guard at $h_1$
on the facet omitting $c$.

Any obstruction to that guard contains a row at $h_1$. Its
$c$-tail cannot be posted at $c$ in the query, so there must be a
row at $h_2$, and inductively at every clause. The variable
tails of those rows have no rows themselves, so they must be
posted at the corresponding owners. These consistent postings
confirm a literal in every clause. Conversely, given a model,
post its truth values at $a,b$ and select a confirmed row per
clause. The $a,b$ tails are all query seeds. At $c$ the last
row seeds its head, and the reversed edges reach every earlier
head, so these rows force the mark to be lost. Thus the queried
guard holds in the meet exactly for unsatisfiable formulas.
The input also has polynomial-size canonical prime lists: on
each facet the visible rows at a clause share the same optional
$c$-posting, and their other postings are single literals.
Their minimal hitting sets are the common posting alone, if
present, or the set of all the visible literal postings.
The queried empty guard is itself a prime of the prescribed
family, since all rows at $h_1$ use $c$.

The polynomial bound of one row per head is sharp:
hardness persists with at most two rows per head. For a
three-literal clause, keep its first literal row and replace
the other two by a row posting a fresh auxiliary head at $c$.
Give the auxiliary those two literal rows, with the same
optional $c$-posting of the next clause as before. On the
queried facet the auxiliary is forced whenever the new row
is selected. An obstruction still follows a chain through
every clause, directly or via its auxiliary, and confirms one
literal per clause. A model chooses such a chain, which is
seeded at its end at $c$ and at the confirmed truth inputs
at $a,b$, as before. All rows remain injective and acyclic.
Because each head now has at most two rows, its canonical
prime guards have width at most two.

The number of multiply headed inputs alone does not
give a fixed-parameter bound for guard implication
unless $\mathrm{FPT}=\mathrm{W[1]}$. Existence of a
counterexample to the empty guard is $\mathrm{W[1]}$-hard
in this parameter, even for acyclic injective rows with
at most two postings in every row. Reduce from Clique
parameterized by its requested size $h$
\cite[Corollary~3.2]{DowneyFellows1995Clique}.

Make $h$ ordered choice heads, with one possible row
per vertex of the graph at each choice head. Its row
posts at $c$ the start of a private chain for that
vertex at that position. Every chain leads to the
next choice head, or terminates at the final position.
For each incompatible pair of vertex choices at two
different positions, introduce one retained input
shared by those two chains. Incompatibility means
equality of the vertices or absence of their edge.
The chain at the earlier position requires this
input at $a$, and the chain at the later position
requires it at $b$. Here $a,b,c$ are distinct.
Give each chain head a single row requiring its
next head at $c$ if there is one, and at most
one such retained posting. Use enough chain
heads to require every incident incompatible pair.
Only the $h$ choice heads can have multiple rows.

Query the empty guard at the first choice head
on the facet omitting $c$. Any blocker must
select a row there, and because $c$ cannot be
posted on the probe, it must include that row's
entire chain and a choice at the next position.
Every required retained input must be posted
on the probe at the specified owner. Thus any
one selected vertex per position forms a clique,
since incompatible selections require competing
postings. Conversely a clique gives compatible
postings and selects a chain at every position.
These rows form a blocker by reachability in
source order, as for the clause-chain reduction.
The construction has polynomial size, also in
canonical primes: the rows at a choice head
are singleton losses at $c$, and every other
head has at most one row. This lower bound
applies to guard implication, including with
the budget supplied as canonical primes.

This yields exponential prime growth under taking the core meet
even with two posting owners and one row per head. Start with a
head $i$ and then $d$ layers of two inputs each, labeled $a,b$.
Each head before the last layer has a row posting both inputs
of the next layer at their labels. The final layer has no rows.
On a facet seeing both owners each headed input originally has
two singleton primes, one for each next-layer input.
In the core meet a path from $i$ to an input without a row
chooses exactly one input per layer. Every such choice gives a
canonical prime of width $d$, so there are exactly $2^d$ primes
at $i$ on that facet.

Neutral restriction to a subset of inputs is left adjoint to adjoining
the union of the remaining inputs. Indeed, for disjoint sets $V,W$,
let $\rho$ make the inputs in $W$ neutral, using
\cref{core:thm:neutral-elimination}, and put $e(G)=G+\sum_{w\in W}w$
for an operation $G$ on $V$. Allow empty sums and the formal zero.
If $F\subseteq e(G)$ then $\rho F\subseteq G$, since the neutral
menus omit the queried winner. Conversely, for a source in $V$, its
coefficient in $F$ can only increase when the postings on $W$ are
erased. It is then the coefficient of $\rho F$, so $\rho F\subseteq G$
implies $F\subseteq e(G)$ by source reconstruction. As $\rho e(G)=G$,
adjoining the background union embeds the lattice on $V$ into that on
$V\cup W$, preserving meets and all nonempty joins.

In particular, let $X_a$ denote the greatest operation omitting $x$
when $y$ is posted at owner $a$, with the other inputs neutral in that
test. Define $Y_b$ by interchanging $x$ and $y$. Their meet is the
background union plus $x\star_a y$ if $a=b$, and the background
union alone if $a\ne b$. On the two inputs this follows from the
binary normal forms (\cref{core:prop:binary}): only the owned choice
at a common owner can obey both reciprocal losses. Adjoining the
background union gives the asserted meet in arbitrary arity.
In the distinct-owner case the two nonempty minimal losses force
both heads to have empty losses. This gives two distinct irredundant
meet decompositions of the background union, one using the two
empty losses and the other using the two reciprocal losses.
In both decompositions each factor alone retains at least one of
the two heads.

The full core lattice fails both semidistributive laws already on
two inputs. Take three distinct owners $a,b,c$.
Then $X_a\wedge Y_b=X_a\wedge Y_c=0$, whereas $Y_b\vee Y_c$
is top, so $X_a\wedge(Y_b\vee Y_c)=X_a$.
Dually $X_a\vee Y_b=X_a\vee X_c$ is top, whereas $Y_b\wedge X_c=0$,
so $X_a\vee(Y_b\wedge X_c)=X_a$. The joins are top because the
two loss cones in each case are disjoint as sets of source
coefficients. The same failures hold with any background inputs
by the lattice embedding.

\begin{corollary}[Colored cofactor dependence]
\label{core:cor:colored-cofactor-dependence}
Let $F$ be any core operation with the source predicate $P$ above.
Fix the inputs in $E_r$ to $\{r\}$, for $E\in\mathcal D_W$, leaving
the other inputs arbitrary. The resulting cofactor is independent of
$x$ if and only if $P(E)=0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose an unused owner $k$. If $P(E)=1$, the canonical probe with
$x=\{k\}$ contains $k$, whereas replacing $x$ by $I\setminus\{k\}$
makes every input omit $k$. This witnesses dependence.

If $P(E)=0$, every $x$-occurrence is blocked on that probe by some
foreign-owned ancestor with an absorbing sibling. That sibling's
$r$-profile is true using only the fixed $E_r$ inputs; every unfixed
input, including $x$, has false $r$-atomic flag on the probe. Changing
unfixed inputs can only increase those flags, so this sibling stays
$\{r\}$ and its parent stays $\{r\}$. Choose inclusion-maximal such
constant subwords. They cover every $x$-occurrence, and replacing them
by their fixed atoms leaves a word without $x$ for the entire cofactor.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Checking independence on a fixed face.}
For a candidate given by canonical prime guards of width at
most two, one can test in polynomial time whether fixing a
specified proper coloring makes the output independent of one
remaining input. In particular, all instances of the colored
cofactor law can be tested in polynomial time at width one,
since there is at most one minimal loss per facet.

Fix the winner $k$ whose output bit is compared. The bit is a
disjunction over heads and their facet primes. Each term demands
membership of $k$ at its head and absence of every posting of
that prime. Enumerate an ordered pair of $k$-views for the changed
input and a term witnessing presence of $k$ at the first view.
A view records membership of $k$ and the foreign singleton
flags, so there are only $|I|+1$ views per input.
Fix that term's membership bit and posting exclusions. At
other unfixed inputs we may set membership of $k$ to false
unless it is required by the face or the chosen term.
This leaves the other atomic flags unchanged: a menu with
$k$ and no foreign singleton flag can be replaced by the
ambiguous menu $I\setminus\{k\}$. This does not spoil the
chosen term and cannot make the bit true at the second menu.

Use one Boolean variable per foreign singleton flag, with
pairwise exclusions for flags of the same input. An input
required to supply $k$ has none of these flags. Fix the
flags specified by the face and the two chosen menus, with
the changed input interpreted separately on each side.
To make the second output bit zero, every prime at every
head supplying $k$ must be hit by a posting. Each such clause
has size at most two. Together with the exclusions from the
chosen term these give a 2SAT instance. Conversely any
satisfying flag assignment is realized by menus: use the
specified singleton when a flag is present, and otherwise
use $\{k\}$ or $I\setminus\{k\}$ as required. A successful
test witnesses dependence. Testing only minimal loss fixings
suffices for the full cofactor law, as independence persists
under further fixing.

At width two the same test checks all fixings on the
reported winner's own facet without enumerating them.
To ask whether $x$ can be captured while that winner's
bit changes, require the common foreign singleton flags
of the two valuations to hit every prime at $x$ on that
facet. These are additional clauses of size at most two.
The common flags form a proper fixing with the reported
winner absent as a singleton color. Conversely any such
fixing witnessing capture is contained in the common
flags and hence satisfies the added clauses.

The whole cofactor law is polynomial at prime width two
when the number of postings of any one owner in a minimal
loss is bounded. To test a failure at observer $k$, guess the
input $x$ whose menu changes, a facet witnessing its capture,
and the set $S$ of inputs posted at $k$ in a minimal loss
fixing on that facet. The bound makes this last enumeration
polynomial. Use the fixed-face 2SAT construction with $S$
fixed at $k$, but leave the other common flags free. Add
the clauses saying that every prime at $x$ on the capture
facet is hit by either an allowed posting of $S$ or one of
the common foreign flags away from $x$ visible on that facet.
Each added clause still has width at most two.

A satisfying assignment supplies a proper loss fixing
on the chosen facet, since the flags there are consistent,
and witnesses dependence at $k$. Conversely a failure of
the law occurs under a minimal loss fixing, which supplies
one of the guessed sets $S$. Keep those inputs fixed and
keep the $k$ membership of the chosen positive head.
At other unchanged inputs remove any $k$ membership
while keeping every foreign singleton flag. This preserves
the first positive witness and cannot spoil absence in the
second output. It also preserves the capture, whose required
$k$-postings were all in $S$. Thus the flags satisfy the
added clauses. The posting bound itself can be checked by
looking for a minimal transversal containing a set of
one more than the allowed number of postings at a fixed
owner, using one private prime for each required posting.

There is a direct Boolean test without the posting bound.
Keep the guesses of $k$, the capture facet, the two views of
$x$, and one prime witnessing the first positive bit at a
head $h$. At unchanged inputs use one variable $y_u$ for
posting $u$ at $k$, in addition to the foreign singleton
flags. Enforce at most one singleton flag per input. Realize
the common flags by those singleton menus when present.
At unchanged inputs with no flag, use $I\setminus\{k\}$,
except that the positive head $h$ must carry $k$ and can
use $I$ there. Require its membership at $k$ and the
absence of the postings in the chosen positive prime.
Capture uses the common singleton flags visible on the
guessed facet, hitting every prime at $x$ there.

For the second output to omit $k$, the primes of $h$ when
$h\ne x$ and of $x$ when its second view contains $k$ must
all be hit. At every other unchanged input $u$, hitting
each of its primes is conditional on $y_u$. For a prime $G$
this gives a clause
\[
       \neg y_u\ \lor\ \bigvee_{a\in G}x_a ,
\]
where the $x_a$ are the corresponding foreign flags, using
the second view at $x$. These clauses are both necessary and
sufficient. Any witness can be normalized to the stated
menus by removing extra $k$ membership only at inputs that
are not $k$ singletons or the positive head. This preserves
the capture and the first witness and cannot restore $k$ in
the second output.

At width two, the only possible clauses of width three
are the conditional clauses at unchanged heads having
a binary prime on the output facet. Thus one can instead
parameterize the global test by the number of those heads
per facet: assign their $y_u$ bits and solve the remaining
2SAT instances. This allows arbitrarily many $k$-postings
in a minimal loss as long as few can activate binary guards
in the absence test.

There is another bound on the Boolean test that does
not require small prime width. After fixing the guessed
views and positive witness, discard singleton flags
forbidden by that witness or by its head membership
requirement, and simplify the clauses. At each common
input keep only flags occurring positively in a remaining
clause. Call an input shared if two or more of its
singleton colors remain. Enumerate just the singleton
statuses at shared inputs. An unposted shared input can
always be changed to one of its available foreign colors:
the assignment has no other flag there, the change helps
positive literals, and only the $k$-flag can activate a
conditional absence clause. Flags with no positive
occurrence can always be set false, since that preserves
every remaining clause.

After these choices, set every remaining foreign flag
true. This is safe: a foreign flag occurs negatively only
in one-input mutual exclusions or in the requirements
already fixed by the positive witness. No other available
flag shares its input. The remaining common flags are
$k$-flags. Each occurs negatively only as the antecedent
of a conditional absence clause, whose other flags are
now fixed. Set it false if any such clause requires it
false, and true otherwise. These last choices maximize
the flags available to the capture clauses subject to
the absence constraints. Consequently a residual test
succeeds exactly when this assignment satisfies its
remaining clauses. Membership at the positive head is
handled as in the original test, even if all its flags
are false.

For a guess with $b$ shared inputs the test therefore
takes $K^b$ times polynomial work in the prime
lists. It allows arbitrary width and arbitrarily many
$k$-postings. The parameter counts only conflicts among
colors that can supply a necessary positive flag at the
same input across the capture and absence tests.

The enumeration splits further when the shared choices
interact only in small components. Before branching,
set the available foreign flags at unshared inputs true.
For each remaining unshared $k$-flag, substitute the
conjunction of the posting tests that must hold for its
head to lose at the second view. This is its greatest
permitted value once the shared statuses are assigned.
The conjuncts are positive foreign-posting clauses, so
they read only the shared statuses after the unshared
foreign flags have been set. The substitution preserves
satisfiability, since the flag is otherwise used only
positively in the capture clauses. Keep each substituted
expression as a conjunction of clauses.

For every remaining constraint, record the shared
inputs it reads, directly or through these substituted
expressions. Join any two inputs read by the same
constraint. Each constraint belongs to just one component,
apart from constant constraints. Thus one can test each
component separately and assemble the assignments before
restoring the unshared $k$-flags. With component sizes
$b_1,\ldots,b_m$, this costs
$1+\sum_{i=1}^m K^{b_i}$ times polynomial work.
Many independent color conflicts therefore need only
the cost of their largest connected group in the exponent.

The width threshold for a specified fixing is sharp.
For any fixed $K\ge3$, reduce 3CNF satisfiability to dependence.
Choose distinct owners $k,a,b$. Use one unconditionally retained
input per Boolean variable, one head per clause, and a queried
input $x$. A clause head loses when any posting satisfying one
of its literals is present, using $a$ for true and $b$ for false.
Thus its list on each facet consists of the single guard of
visible satisfying postings, of width at most three.
Choose one clause head $h_0$ and let $x$ lose precisely when
$h_0$ is posted at $k$, on facets seeing $k$. All these lists
are canonical and agree on overlaps. Retained variable inputs
ensure a nonempty output.

Fix every clause head at $k$. No observing winner other than
$k$ can depend on $x$: its own source is suppressed by $h_0$,
and no other source reads it. At observer $k$, varying $x$
can change the output only if every fixed clause head loses
and no variable input supplies $k$. If all clause heads lose,
the visible variable postings satisfy every clause and extend
to a Boolean assignment, since no variable has two postings.
Conversely a satisfying assignment lets us post every variable
at $a$ or $b$, so all clause heads lose and no variable supplies
$k$. The source at $x$ is then the only possible source of $k$,
and changing its menu changes that bit. Thus independence on
the specified face is coNP-hard at width three. A pair of
valuations differing only at $x$ certifies dependence, so
independence is coNP-complete at that width.

At width two the whole colored-cofactor law is
coNP-complete, as is checking all fixings of a
specified input.
Use the same three owners and retained variable inputs,
but split each clause into two parts of sizes one and two.
Add a retained input $d_s$ for every owner $s\ne k$.
Use two heads for each clause. For a literal with
satisfying posting $v:r$, the loss at its part head
requires that posting together with $d_s:s$ for
every $s\notin\{k,r\}$. Give $x$ one binary
prime per clause on facets seeing $k$, excluding the
$k$-postings of its two heads. On the facet hiding $k$,
give $x$ the empty prime. The lists are canonical:
the loss patterns at each part head are consistent
and on the facet hiding $k$ there are at most two of
them. The prime guards choose one posting from each
pattern and minimize. Every other facet hides a
required owner, so has the empty guard there.
The minimal losses at $x$ choose one head per clause
at $k$. All lists agree across observers.

At a minimal loss fixing of $x$, the chosen heads
are fixed at $k$. The other winners are independent
of $x$ because its source is captured and no other
head reads it. At observer $k$, a dependence witness
must suppress every fixed head. The corresponding
variable postings then satisfy one of the two parts of
every clause and hence the formula. Conversely, choose
one satisfied part per clause under a satisfying
assignment, and fix its head at $k$. Post the variable
assignment and each $d_s$ at $s$, and choose menus
omitting $k$ at every other unchanged input.
Then all fixed heads lose, so
varying $x$ changes the $k$ bit. Proper extensions of
minimal fixings introduce no new dependence if all
minimal fixings pass. This proves the reduction, and
a loss fixing and two valuations certify failure.

The law holds at every other input regardless of
satisfiability. Retained inputs have no loss fixing.
A loss fixing of a clause head supplies every owner
other than $k$ from retained inputs, at every extension.
The only bit it could change is $k$. At that observer
the clause head is suppressed, the source at $x$ ignores
its $k$ flag, and no other head reads it. Thus these
fixings never violate the law. This proves hardness of
the full test as well as the test at $x$.

\paragraph{Two layers with complementary posting colors.}
Consider three groups of live inputs. The first group is
always retained. The middle group has losses posting only
first-group inputs, and each such loss uses every owner other
than $k$ and omits $k$. The terminal group has only pure-$k$
losses posting first-group or middle inputs. There are no
postings of terminal inputs in any minimal loss. For a
consistent coloring $d$ of the first group omitting $k$, let
$M(d)$ be the set of middle heads lost on $d$.
Then the candidate is core exactly when no terminal loss
premise is contained in any $M(d)$. Equivalently, its
terminal guards must all be positive on the pure-$k$
posting of each $M(d)$.

If a premise is contained in $M(d)$, fix it at $k$ and
use $d$ on the first group. Give all other unchanged
inputs menus omitting $k$. Every fixed source is lost at
observer $k$, so the terminal input can change that bit
under its loss fixing. This violates cofactor independence.

For sufficiency the prefix of first and middle inputs
is core, by union of the single-source completions.
At a background coloring $e$ its middle coefficient is
union on the heads retained there. By
\cref{core:thm:block-source-extension}, a terminal premise
activates at a proper probe precisely when its first-group
postings are present and each of its middle inputs is
either posted at $k$ or ignored by that coefficient.
With no ignored middle inputs, the activated premises are
exactly the prescribed ones. With an ignored input, all
owners except $k$ occur in the first group, so the probe
has no $k$ posting. An activated premise would then have
to lie in $M(d)$, which the test rules out. Thus the
activated losses give exactly the prescribed terminal
coefficients, using union on the retained terminal heads,
and \cref{core:lem:block-source-patterns} realizes them.

A failing test is witnessed by $d$ and a terminal head,
and both the set $M(d)$ and the head's bit are evaluable
directly from the complete primes. This gives a coNP
upper bound in the class. The width-two construction above
is coNP-hard within it: some $M(d)$ contains a terminal
premise exactly when at least one part per clause is
satisfied. In particular, the cofactor law is exact for
this class, even with arbitrarily many terminal heads.

\paragraph{Retained postings that separate cyclic dependencies.}
Cycles are allowed under a stronger condition on the retained
inputs. Give each headed input $j$ a designated owner $r_j$,
and suppose every loss at $j$ omits $r_j$ and posts every
other owner on at least one retained input. As above, require
that any loss posting a headed input $i$ posts it at $r_i$.
Then the candidate is always core, with no acyclicity condition.

To see this, take the union of all retained inputs as the
prefix in \cref{core:lem:block-source-patterns}. Its activation
preorder is subcoloring. At a proper background coloring
$e$ of those inputs, a row activates exactly when its
retained postings agree with $e$. If any row activates,
its retained postings already use all owners except one,
so every active row has the same missing owner $k$.
All its headed controls have designated owners different
from $k$, since the row omits $k$. These controls are
unconditionally retained in the coefficient: all activated
rows are headed at inputs designated $k$. Thus the
coefficient is a union of single-source completions on a
common retained set, together with the other retained
inputs. Rows with an empty internal premise simply make
their head unused. The coefficient is core or zero and
has exactly the required internal bits. These are exactly
the candidate's bits at every proper joint probe, so the
block extension realizes the candidate.

Already at three owners $a,b,c$ one can take retained
inputs $u_a,u_b,u_c$ and two heads $i,j$ designated $a,b$.
At $i$ use the loss posting $j,u_b$ at $b$ and $u_c$ at
$c$. At $j$ use the loss posting $i,u_a$ at $a$ and
$u_c$ at $c$. The posting graph has a reciprocal pair
across owners, and each proper retained background can
activate at most one direction.

More generally, no assignment of designated owners is
needed if the following test passes. For every loss $A$
posting a headed input $j$, and every loss $B$ at $j$,
their restrictions to the retained inputs cannot be
subcolorings of one proper background. Then at every
proper retained background the activated rows post only
inputs with no activated loss of their own. The same
conditional union of single-source completions realizes
the family. This test is sufficient even when individual
rows use only a few owners or have no retained postings.

At prime width two the test is polynomial. To look for
a violating pair, choose its two heads and observer facets,
the posting of $j$ in $A$, and one owner missing from the
joint retained background. Use separate posting variables
for $A$ and $B$, with hitting clauses for the primes at
their heads. Require the posting of $j$ in $A$ and
enumerate its private prime, forbidding that prime's other
postings in $A$. Forbid competing postings within each
row and across the two rows on retained inputs. Also forbid
the missing owner on the retained inputs in both rows.
These are 2SAT instances. Their solutions can be minimized
to loss pairs: compatibility and the missing-owner condition
survive minimization, and the private prime preserves the
posting of $j$. Conversely every violating pair supplies
these choices.

The sufficient test itself is coNP-complete at width three.
Use two heads $i,j$ and retained positive and negative
literal inputs per variable. At $i$ use as primes the
variable pairs, the formula clauses on literal inputs, and
the singleton $\{j\}$, all at one owner. At $j$ use the
variable pairs at a different owner. Minimize the lists
on visible facets and use the empty guard on hidden ones.
The monochromatic transversals are consistent and give
canonical lists. A violating pair must hit both copies of
the variable pairs without assigning different owners to
one literal input. Each row thus chooses exactly one
literal per variable, with opposite choices in the two
rows. The row at $i$ satisfies all clauses. Conversely
a satisfying assignment and the opposite choices give
such a pair, and its retained union is proper because it
uses only two owners. The singleton prime keeps $j$
in the row at $i$. This proves hardness of checking the
sufficient condition.

\paragraph{One internal posting owner at each retained background.}
Fix the always-retained inputs as the initial block. Suppose
that at each proper coloring $e$ of this block, the internal
parts of all activated loss rows post at most one common
owner $r$; empty internal parts are allowed. The choice of
$r$ may depend on $e$. Then colored-cofactor independence
is necessary and sufficient for realization, even when the
posting graph has cycles.

Indeed the retained union activates exactly the rows whose
retained parts are subcolorings of $e$. The one-owner case of
\cref{core:thm:conditional-component-capture} computes the
greatest bounded coefficient by the Horn closure of their
internal rules. An original loss already fires in this
coefficient. To prove the reverse implication on a proper
joint probe, start the closure with the $r$-postings of its
internal coloring. Follow the derivation until it captures
the queried head, leaving that head's menu free. Whenever
an earlier rule derives an input, the current fixing extends
its original loss row: the retained part still agrees with
$e$, and its internal premise is posted at $r$. Cofactor
independence permits changing the derived input to the $r$
atom without changing the entire cofactor of the queried
head. This change preserves all $r$-postings already used
by the derivation. It may erase foreign postings or lead
to a coloring using every owner, but the original loss
fixing still guarantees independence under those extensions.
At the final rule the queried head is itself independent.
It was therefore already independent at the original probe.
Since it was the sole possible source of the observing
winner there, its bit must be zero. This proves the required
coefficient equality, and the block extension realizes the
family.

The condition on activated rows can be tested from primes
without computing the coefficients. A violation consists of
two minimal losses whose retained parts are jointly consistent
and omit a common owner, with one specified internal posting
in each at distinct owners. As in the preceding test, choose
their facets and private primes for the required postings.
Use two hitting-set instances with the compatibility
exclusions on retained postings. At prime width two this
is again 2SAT, and minimization preserves both specified
postings. Two postings in a single row are covered by allowing
the same row in the two choices.

\paragraph{One internal posting per activated row.}
There is also an exact cofactor test with multiple internal
owners when every activated internal premise has size at most
one. At each retained background delete the heads of empty
premises and all their descendants. Realization is equivalent
to cofactor independence together with the requirement that
no remaining strongly connected component has two internal
edge colors, at any proper retained background.

By \cref{core:thm:conditional-component-capture}, the
greatest coefficient removes the deleted heads and completes
the surviving graph by first-color paths. Both changes are
forced by cofactor independence in the original candidate.
An empty activated premise fixes its head independently of
the internal menus. To propagate this along an edge labeled
$s$, set the captured input to $s$: the edge's original
premise is now fixed, and the target is captured as well.
Each change preserves the cofactor of the queried head
until the path first reaches it. The same argument applies
to a first-color path whose initial input already has the
first edge's atom. Thus every forced loss of the coefficient
is an original loss on the proper joint probe, where the
queried input is the sole possible source of the observing
winner. This proves the coefficient equalities.

The restriction on mixed components cannot simply be dropped.
At three owners $r,s,k$, retain inputs $u,v$, and give two
other inputs $i,j$ one loss each. Both losses require
$u:r,v:s$. The loss at $i$ further requires $j:r$, and
the loss at $j$ further requires $i:s$. Under either loss
fixing, the retained inputs supply $r,s$ independently of
the captured head. The only other bit is $k$: the captured
head loses it, and changing that head can affect the other
head only when the other head is already fixed at a foreign
atom. Hence the law holds. But at background $u:r,v:s$ the
internal graph is a mixed reciprocal pair. Its bounded
coefficient is zero, whereas the candidate retains both
heads when neither is posted. The block test excludes
realization.

In general the same obstruction proves necessity of the graph
condition. A vertex in a surviving mixed component has no
empty activated premise, so the candidate retains it when
all internal inputs are unposted. The additive minorant removes
the entire component, violating the coefficient equality at
that proper probe.

For a given $e$, the graph is directly computable from primes.
The empty premises are detected by querying $e$ alone. At a
head without such a premise, an edge $u:r$ exists exactly when
the posting set consisting of $e$ and $u:r$ contains an original
loss. Check this set on each facet after erasing its invisible
owner, since the full set can use every owner. Any loss found
must contain $u:r$ because $e$ alone has none. Incoming edges at
heads already having an empty premise are immaterial to their
deletion or the test on survivors. Thus a background and mixed
component give a polynomial failure certificate. Together with
the two-valuation certificates for cofactor failure, this yields
a coNP recognition bound from the complete primes in the
singleton-internal class.

Detecting surviving mixed components is coNP-hard even
when cofactor independence is already guaranteed. Use
retained inputs $u,v$ and the positive and negative literal
inputs of a 3CNF formula. At two additional heads $i,j$
require $u:r,v:s$ in every loss, for distinct owners $r,s$.
A loss at $i$ also posts $j:r$ and a set of literal inputs
at $r$ meeting every variable pair and every formula clause.
A loss at $j$ also posts $i:s$ and a set meeting every variable
pair at $s$. The prime lists consist of the displayed
variable and clause sets at their respective owners and
the mandatory singleton postings. On facets hiding either
owner the lists have the empty guard. Minimization gives
the complete canonical lists of width at most three:
the minimal transversals of the displayed lists are
consistent, since the two colors within a row post
disjoint sets of inputs.

The retained inputs supply $r,s$ under every loss fixing.
At any other observer, changing a captured head cannot
affect a mark from the other head, which is fixed at a
foreign atom. Thus the cofactor law holds. An activated
mixed component can only be the reciprocal pair $i,j$.
It occurs exactly when the two literal sets can be posted
at different colors on one proper retained background.
They must then pick opposite literals for every variable,
and the set at $i$ gives a satisfying assignment.
Conversely such an assignment gives both activated edges.
There are no empty internal premises. The exact criterion
therefore gives coNP-complete recognition at width three
in the singleton-internal class, even with two headed
inputs and cofactor independence as a promise.

For two headed inputs recognition is polynomial at width two,
making the width-three lower bound sharp. Cofactor independence
can be checked by branching on the observing-owner posting bits
of at most two heads in the Boolean test above: always-retained
inputs have only an empty guard. A surviving mixed component
must be a reciprocal
pair with distinct edge colors, with neither head disabled
by an empty premise. To find one, choose the two internal
postings, their observer facets and private primes, and one
owner missing from the common retained background. Also
choose one positive prime per head on the facet of that
missing owner, certifying that neither head is disabled
on the retained background alone.

Use separate hitting-set variables for the two edge rows.
Require their specified internal postings to be the only
hits of their private primes, prohibit any other internal
postings, and forbid competing retained postings within
and between the rows. Forbid the missing owner on both
retained parts and forbid the retained exclusions of both
chosen positive primes on their union. At width two all
clauses are binary or unit. A solution minimizes to loss
rows retaining their required internal postings. Their
retained parts then give a proper background activating
both edges and neither empty premise. Conversely, from
any surviving mixed pair choose rows justifying its edges
and restrict the background to their retained union.
This cannot activate an empty premise, so both positive
primes can be chosen there. Thus the polynomial list of
2SAT tests finds precisely the mixed obstructions.

With the complete minimal losses supplied instead,
two-headed recognition is polynomial even with a
variable palette and no width bound. For the mixed
obstruction enumerate a row at each head having a
posting of the other head, with distinct posting
owners. Take the union of their retained parts.
Check that it is a consistent proper background
and that neither head has an empty activated
premise there. This finds every surviving mixed
pair: shrinking its background to the union of
the two witnesses leaves both edges and cannot
activate any empty premise.

For the cofactor law, choose the captured head, its
minimal loss fixing, the observed owner and the
second-side supplier as in the bounded-owner-block
test. Every retained input fixed at the observed
owner already supplies that winner, so reject such
a choice. Outside the fixing only the selected
second-side supplier needs to carry that owner.
Hence at most two inputs can need a first-side
loss and all such inputs must be headed. Enumerate
their loss rows and impose those postings and the
menu restrictions. At the second-side supplier,
evaluate positivity on the least allowed foreign
posting pattern, as in the explicit-loss cofactor
test. This decides whether the selected rows give
a dependence witness. The enumeration and the
mixed-pair check use polynomially many scans of
the supplied rows. Thus the width-three hardness
for complete primes at two heads depends on the
succinct representation of their losses.

More generally, for $h$ headed inputs and $K$ owners,
an obstruction using explicit loss rows needs at most
$h(K+1)^{h-1}$ rows and a retained background equal
to the union of their retained parts. To see this,
use the block test and choose a positive probe
$(i,e,d)$ missed by the coefficient at $e$. For every
distinct internal row activated by $e$, choose one
original row witnessing activation. There are at
most $h(K+1)^{h-1}$ internal rows because every
passive internal input is either unposted or has
one owner. The union $e'$ of the retained parts of
the chosen rows is contained in $e$. All chosen
rows are still activated there. Also $(i,e',d)$
is proper and positive by antitonicity. The same
internal obstruction therefore certifies failure
at $e'$.

Conversely, a bundle of listed rows whose retained
union $e'$ is consistent obstructs exact recognition
if its internal rows block a proper query $(i,d)$
that is prescribed positive at $(i,e',d)$. This is
the same block test, since adding the other rows
activated at $e'$ cannot make the query feasible.
Enumerate bundles up to the bound and internal
queries, and test prescribed positivity by scanning
the explicit losses. The internal feasibility test
depends only on $h,K$. This is polynomial for fixed
$h,K$, without a bound on loss sizes or on the
number of retained inputs. If there are no retained
inputs only the finite internal test is needed.

If the internal part of every row is injective, the
cut bound of \cref{core:cor:input-guard-cuts} reduces
the number of rows needed to $h\min(K,h)$ for
$h\ge3$. The internal test is then polynomial by
reachability. Thus explicit-loss recognition in this
class is polynomial for fixed $h$ even when the
palette varies. Only the internal postings must
be injective; the retained parts may repeat an
owner arbitrarily often.

\paragraph{A bounded number of headed inputs at width two.}
At prime width two, recognition is fixed-parameter tractable
in $K$ and the number $h$ of inputs with any loss, with no
restriction on the internal premises. Keep the always-retained
inputs as the initial union block. On the $h$ remaining inputs,
enumerate a positive internal probe and a set of negative
internal probes that no core operation can satisfy together.
This finite enumeration depends only on $K,h$: compute the
term operations on the $h$ inputs by closing their projections
under the owned gates. If there is no retained block, this
finite test already decides recognition.

For each enumerated obstruction choose a facet for the positive
probe and a candidate prime certifying that positive. The internal
part of the probe must avoid the prime. Use one variable per
permitted retained posting, with binary exclusions for competing
postings at the same input. Forbid the observer's postings and
the retained postings in the chosen positive prime. Discard
postings absent from all prime lists, since they occur in none
of these constraints.

For each negative internal probe $(j,t)$ choose a facet on which
an original loss at $j$ is to activate. On that facet, the
background $e$ together with $t$, erasing invisible postings,
must hit every candidate prime at $j$. Drop the clauses already
hit by visible internal postings of $t$. The remaining clauses
require at least one permissible retained posting from each
remaining prime. All negatives use the same retained background
variables. At width two this is 2SAT.

These constraints are exact. The queried $e$ combined with $t$
on the chosen facet is already a consistent coloring. If it hits
all the primes, it contains a minimal loss whose retained part
agrees with $e$ and whose internal part is contained in $t$.
That loss activates the required negative. Conversely every
activating row is contained in such a coloring on one of its
facets. Thus the Boolean tests find precisely the obstructed
positives from the block criterion. Only the choice of positive
prime and the size of each 2SAT instance depend on the size of
the given lists.

If the internal parts of the minimal losses are
injective, the same prime-only test is polynomial
for fixed $h$ with a variable palette. Enumerate
the internal query and at most $h^2$ internal
negative rows, using the cut bound above (for
$h\le2$ one may use the bound $h\min(K,h)$ directly
from the discovery-order proof). Keep only lists
that block the internal query by reachability.
The number of such choices, including one
observer per row, is $(K+1)^{O(h^3)}$ because
each row assigns statuses to at most $h-1$
inputs. For each choice use the same width-two
background $2$SAT test. Each row accepted by the
test contains the internal part of some activated
loss, so the actual coefficient still misses the
query. Conversely, any failed positive has a
blocking list of this size made up of activated
minimal losses. In particular the algorithm does
not expand the complete loss lists or enumerate
local term operations. The injectivity hypothesis
is checkable from the primes by testing for two
same-owner postings on distinct internal inputs
with private primes in a minimal loss.

The same bound decides sufficiency of a proposed
guard for the greatest bounded operation. Instead
of choosing a prescribed positive prime, forbid
the postings of the proposed guard in the query
on its observer facet. Enumerate the internal
status and the small blocking list exactly as
above. Each background $2$SAT instance then asks
whether an enabled query is lost. The guard
suffices exactly when all these tests fail.
Testing every single-exclusion deletion decides
primality with the same polynomial bound for
fixed $h$, including a variable palette.

At two heads the obstruction has a simpler form.
To lose the $x$-mark, first activate a row at $x$.
If its internal part is empty, this row suffices.
Otherwise its sole posting $y{:}r$ rules out every
local supplier except the $r$-fold. That fold is
excluded by the query posting $y{:}r$, or by one
activated row at either head whose internal part
does not post the other head at $r$. These cases
exhaust binary local terms. Thus at most two
activated rows suffice. With one facet and one
internal status per activated row, the number
of $2$CNF tests for a specified output facet is
$O((K+1)^4)$: there are $O((K+1)^2)$ choices
per row. Keep the query's own internal posting
as a Boolean variable, using unit clauses only
when a case requires it. This gives a direct
width-two counterpart of the width-three
coNP and $\mathrm{DP}$ lower bounds below.

With three headed inputs the injectivity condition
can be dropped, still with a variable palette.
After projecting to the three heads, a repeated
owner block posts both passive inputs at that
owner. Its omission requires exactly the positive
singleton demand on this pair: on the sole-source
probe, the only possible output without the mark
is the posted singleton. Conversely the demand
forces that output. Move all such rows to the
demand list and keep the injective negatives.
The marked, rainbow and gap tests of
\cref{core:thm:repeated-blocker-budgets} now use
only injective negatives, so their fold-deletion
tests are polynomial. As in
\cref{core:cor:ternary-guard-recognition}, at most
four demands need be tried together in a rainbow.

Moreover a failed sample already fails with at
most ten negatives. Apply the certificate
argument of \cref{core:cor:ternary-uniform-interpolation}
to these demands and the remaining injective
negatives. Each retained demand is one original
repeated row, so no extra rows are needed to
encode it. The proof uses only the marked, rainbow
and gap tests on the sample; it does not require
complete local predicates. Enumerating at most
ten internal rows and their facets is therefore
polynomial in $K$, and the same background
$2$SAT tests decide exact recognition and
output guard sufficiency and primality from
width-two primes. Enumeration of original
row bundles instead gives polynomial exact
recognition from explicit losses at arbitrary
prime width.

For more heads, call an internal repeated block
spanning when it contains every passive internal
input of its row. Such a row is again equivalent
to a singleton demand, by the same sole-source
probe argument. Move all spanning blocks to the
demand list. Suppose there are at most $b$ distinct
remaining repeated classes $(r,C)$ among the
projected minimal losses, over all backgrounds.
There is no bound on the number of spanning
classes or on the number of owners.

A failed internal sample again fails one of the
marked, rainbow or gap tests. A failed rainbow
requires at most $2h$ demands: if their intersection
is empty, retain one excluding demand per input.
Otherwise retain up to two distinct-owner demands
excluding each input outside the intersection.
This preserves the intersection and every
foreign-owner seed set of the rainbow test, as
in \cref{core:cor:ternary-guard-recognition}.
A gap requires just one demand. For any of these
primitive tests the repeated-class certificate
bound in the reachability appendix retains at
most
\[
 h^2\sum_{j=0}^b\frac{b!}{(b-j)!}
\]
of the other rows. Counting the demand rows gives
a bound on the whole obstruction depending only
on $h,b$. Enumerating such bundles and testing
their backgrounds by $2$SAT therefore gives
polynomial width-two recognition and output
guard testing for fixed $h,b$, even with a
variable palette. With explicit losses, use the
same cardinality bound and test the union
background directly at any prime width.

The obstruction bound also applies when repeated
blocks are removed by several activated rows
acting together. For a fixed retained background,
call the pure posting of $C$ at $r$ forcing if
every head outside $C$ has an activated row whose
internal postings are contained in this pure
posting. Every local word respecting these rows
must win the $r$ singleton demand on $C$:
put the inputs of $C$ at $r$ and the other inputs
at the full menu. Antitonicity removes each
outside source's foreign marks, leaving only $r$.
Each forcing demand thus has a certificate of
at most $h$ activated rows.

Add all such demands and drop every internal
negative having a block that forces its owner.
This preserves the local constraints, since a
word meeting those demands omits every dropped
mark. A repeated spanning block is a special
case with one certifying row. If at every
background at most $b$ distinct repeated classes
remain after this deletion, the same failed
primitive-test argument gives at most
$h^2\sum_{j=0}^b b!/(b-j)!$ retained negatives and
at most $2h$ demands. Replace each selected
demand by its at most $h$ certifying rows. The
original rows activating this combined sample
are a bounded obstruction, even if the other
forcing demands are discarded. This gives the
same polynomial recognition and guard tests
for fixed $h,b$ under the more general
background-dependent hypothesis.

For fixed $b$, this certificate also gives a coNP
bound directly from arbitrary-width prime lists
with no bound on $h$ or $K$. Guess a proper
positive probe and the original rows for the
retained negatives and certifying demands.
Check each guessed original row on an observer
facet: it need only be a loss, since enlarging a
minimal loss preserves omission. Check that its
retained postings agree with the probe. For a
certifying family at $(r,C)$, verify that each
head outside $C$ has one guessed row with
internal part contained in the pure posting.
Finally check the specified failed primitive
test on the other projected rows. Require at
most $b$ distinct repeated classes there; the
repeated-blocker algorithm then verifies failure
in polynomial time. The original omissions imply
all selected demands, so this verifier is sound
even without testing the background-dependent
hypothesis. That hypothesis gives completeness
and a polynomial certificate size.

The same coNP bound holds if the remaining
repeated classes at each background instead
form one inclusion chain at one owner together
with a fixed number of other classes. Use the maximal-block
certificate following
\cref{core:thm:repeated-blocker-budgets} for
the failed primitive test. Such a chain
has length at most $h$, so the retained rows
and the verification tree have polynomial
size. The selected forcing demands still
cost only their original certifying rows.

The coNP bound is sharp already at $b=0$ and
$h=2$. In the two-head width-three construction
above the only internal dependency at either
head is one posting of the other head, so no
projected row can repeat an owner.

The palette parameter in the finite internal test
can instead count only owners occurring in postings
of headed inputs. Let $A$ be that set, read from
the prime lists, and put $q=|A|$. At width two,
recognition is fixed-parameter tractable in $h,q$,
with no injectivity assumption and no bound on
the palette used by the retained inputs.

All activated internal loss rows use only $A$.
Replace gates outside $A$ by derived union.
On their loss probes the replaced gates cannot
absorb, so this preserves all omissions. For
a positive query it can only increase output:
the tests of owners in $A$ are unchanged, and
union keeps both outputs at a replaced gate.
The resulting word ignores non-$A$ postings in
its source predicates. Consequently feasibility
at an arbitrary internal coloring is equivalent
to feasibility after dropping its non-$A$
postings. If a prescribed positive fails, the
smaller coloring is still prescribed positive
and still fails. To tabulate these tests use
$A$ and, if available, one outside observer,
padding to at least three owners. Every row
uses only $A$, and its source bit in a word
using only $A$ and union is the same on both
palettes. Enumerate the finite obstructions
over this smaller palette with gate owners
restricted to $A$ and union.

In the background $2$SAT test one still uses
the original lists. Enumerate the original
observer of the positive and one extra owner
outside $A$ to forbid in the retained
background, or no such extra owner. For each
negative internal row choose its observer
from $A$ or from this one extra owner. This
is complete: if an actual background omits
any owner outside $A$, that same owner is
absent from every internal row and can test
every activated original loss. If it omits
none, all activating losses have observers
in $A$. Soundness follows from the same
facet tests with the extra owner's postings
forbidden. Only the two outer observer choices
and the size of the $2$SAT instances depend
polynomially on the original palette.

The same palette reduction gives succinct source
circuits for the greatest bounded operation, with
no restriction on prime width. On a query split
into retained postings $e$ and internal postings
$d$, enumerate the local term operations on the
reduced palette with gate owners in $A$ and union.
For each such operation, test that it supplies the
internal mark at $d$ and respects every activated
loss. For the second test, for each of its positive
internal rows $(j,t)$ and each original observer
facet, allow the retained postings of $e$ and
the internal postings of $t$, excluding the
observer's color. The operation respects the
budget precisely when every such ground fails
to hit some prime. If a ground hits them all,
it contains a minimal original loss whose
internal part survives in that operation by
antitonicity. Conversely a forbidden mark at
an activated loss gives one of these positive
rows and grounds.

Each ground test is monotone in the query's
retained exclusions, since a prime misses the
ground when all its available postings are
excluded. The internal mark test is likewise
monotone in the internal exclusions and can
be tabulated on the reduced palette. Disjoin
over the local terms. By the block test this
computes each coefficient of the greatest
bounded operation in circuit size and
construction time $f(h,q)N^{O(1)}$, where $N$
is the original list length. No enumeration
of retained backgrounds or original losses
is required.

For two headed inputs the formula is uniform
even without bounding $q$. At head $x$ try
projection $x$ and the folds $x\star_r y$,
where $y$ is the other head. Union need not
be tried, because it supplies an $x$-mark
only when the projection already respects
all losses at $x$. For the projection, forbid
any activated loss at $x$. For the $r$-fold,
forbid any activated loss at either head not
posting the other at $r$, and require that
$d$ does not post $y{:}r$. The ground test
checks each prohibition in one scan of the
original primes: allow all internal postings
for the projection, or all except the other
head at $r$ for the fold. This gives a
monotone circuit of size $O((K+1)N)$ for
each headed source.

The circuits can be exponentially smaller than
the completed canonical lists even at three owners,
two headed inputs and input prime width three.
Use distinct owners $a,r,k$ and headed inputs
$x,y$. Given a $3$CNF, introduce two retained
literal ports per variable. Let $B$ mean that the
ports posted at $a$ fail to meet some variable
pair or some clause. Let $A$ mean that the ports
posted at $r$ fail to meet some variable pair.
Prescribe $B$ or absence of $y{:}a$ at $x$, and
$A$ at $y$. At $x$ the proposed guards exclude
all the $a$-postings of a pair or clause, or
exclude $y{:}a$. At $y$ each guard excludes
both $r$-postings of a pair. These lists become
canonical after inclusion minimization since
the postings at each head use only one owner.
A facet hiding that owner has the empty guard.

On the observer-$k$ facet at $x$, leave $y$
unposted. When $B$ holds, projection $x$
supplies the mark. Otherwise its activated
internal loss is $y{:}a$, so only the $a$-fold
can supply it. The fold fits exactly when $A$
holds, since otherwise $y$ has an activated
empty loss. Thus the completed bit is $B\lor A$.
A losing coloring must post exactly one port
of every pair at $a$ and the other at $r$.
Its $a$-ports then encode exactly a model of
the formula.

Choose as the formula a linear-size $3$CNF
encoding a fan-in-two Boolean circuit for
$\bigvee_{j=1}^m (u_j\land v_j)$, with a variable
per gate, clauses for its truth table, and an
asserted output. For each $j$ choose one of
$u_j,v_j$ and exclude the $a$-posting of its
positive literal port. Each resulting guard
suffices on the section because these exclusions
make the formula unsatisfiable. Deleting any
exclusion permits both inputs of that pair to
be true, extending to a satisfying assignment
and hence a losing coloring. This gives $2^m$
distinct restricted primes. Each is the
restriction of a full prime: extend it by
excluding all foreign postings of $y$ and
take a minimal sufficient subguard. The
restricted guard's own minimality ensures
that its retained exclusions survive.
Thus linear-size width-three input lists can
produce at least $2^m$ canonical primes.

This construction also characterizes the complexity
of recognizing an individual output prime from
complete width-three input lists. On the full
observer-$k$ facet the completed $x$-bit is
$B\lor(A\land[y{:}a\text{ absent}])$.
Consequently the guard excluding $y{:}a$ suffices
exactly when the formula is unsatisfiable.
Sufficiency is coNP-complete, since a counterexample
coloring can be checked by the two-head circuit.

Primality is $\mathrm{DP}$-complete already for a
proposed guard of size two. For hardness start with
formulas $F,G$ with disjoint variables and use a new
selector $e$. Encode the circuit
$(\neg e\land F)\lor(e\land G)$ with gate truth-table
clauses and a positive output unit. Use a separate
output variable, so every clause has at least one
literal other than positive $e$. Test the guard
excluding $y{:}a$ and the $a$-posting of the positive
port of $e$. It suffices exactly when $F$ is
unsatisfiable, since the enabled losing colorings
encode models with $e$ false. Dropping the exclusion
on $e$ allows a losing coloring exactly when $F$ or
$G$ has a model. Dropping the exclusion on $y{:}a$
always allows one: post $y$ and every retained port
except the forbidden port at $a$. This hits the
variable pairs and all clauses, so $B$ is false.
The guard is therefore prime exactly when $F$ is
unsatisfiable and $G$ is satisfiable. For membership,
sufficiency gives the coNP test, and a losing
coloring for each single-exclusion deletion
certifies minimality in NP.

The width-two recognition test also permits arbitrary-width primes if each prime
contains at most two retained postings at inputs that occur at
multiple owners in the lists. Set every allowed posting at
a retained input with only one available owner: this cannot
conflict, cannot disable the positive prime, and only helps the
negative constraints. Delete the clauses these postings satisfy.
Every remaining clause uses only the competing inputs and
therefore has width at most two. This extends the same
fixed-parameter bound to the stated arbitrary-width class.
The two-head hardness example above uses at most three
such retained postings per prime, so allowing three
already gives coNP-hardness.

At arbitrary prime width one can instead bound the number
$b$ of retained inputs that occur at two or more owners
in the given prime lists. Recognition is fixed-parameter
tractable in $K,h,b$. To see this, fix a head, a proper
internal positive probe, its observer, and one prime
certifying positivity. On retained inputs discard postings
that never occur in any prime: they cannot belong to a
minimal loss, which has a private prime for each posting.
Also discard the observing owner's postings and the
postings forbidden by the chosen positive prime.

It suffices to check maximal consistent backgrounds among
the allowed retained postings. Any smaller background
certifying this same positive can be enlarged to one of
them, keeping the prime enabled. Enlargement only adds
activated losses on the internal inputs, so a coefficient
that misses the positive before enlargement still misses
it afterward. Outside the $b$ inputs there is at most
one allowed posting per input, and the maximal background
takes it whenever it exists. At the remaining inputs
enumerate the possible permitted owners, leaving only an
unavailable input unposted. There are at most $K^b$
backgrounds per choice of positive prime and probe.

For a selected background, test each internal pattern
for an activated loss by joining it to the retained
postings and checking the candidate on all facets of
that set. A facet loss is exactly the presence of a
proper loss contained in the union, even if the union
itself uses every owner. The finite test on the $h$
internal inputs now decides whether their bounded
coefficient supplies the chosen positive. This yields
the stated bound without generating any minimal losses.

The bound on $h$ can be omitted in the two conditional
classes above. Enumerate a head, observer, positive prime
and maximal retained background exactly as before.
For singleton internal premises, construct the greatest
coefficient by the graph test. A missed positive needs
only a removed head or a single internal posting that
captures it in the completed graph. Check whether such
a posting is permitted by the observer and the positive
prime. For one internal owner $r$, use the seed consisting
of all internal $r$-postings allowed by that prime and
observer, excluding the queried head. By monotonicity
this is the seed most likely to suppress the positive.
Compute its activated Horn closure one head at a time:
to add a head, test whether the combined background
and current seed contains a loss at that head on some
facet. This tests whether an activated rule now fires.
Iteration is polynomial and never lists the rules.
The active owner, if any, can also be read from the
lists by a private-prime test for an internal posting in
a loss whose retained part is contained in the background.
Thus in either class recognition takes a polynomial
number of prime-list operations per choice of background,
giving a bound of $K^b$ times a polynomial in the input size.

\paragraph{Prime lists for the greatest bounded operation.}
For fixed $K,h,b$, the same split gives a polynomial-time
construction of the complete prime lists of the greatest
core operation below the candidate. First fix an observer
facet and all statuses of the $h$ headed inputs and the
$b$ retained inputs with competing postings. The remaining
retained postings that occur in the prime lists have at
most one owner per input, so they are independent Boolean
flags.

For a head's positive probe with these fixed statuses, try
each core operation $T$ on the $h$ internal inputs that
supplies its internal mark. The coefficient can use $T$
exactly when none of its positive internal probes contains
an activated loss. For every such internal probe $(j,t)$
and facet, require a candidate prime at $j$ to be enabled
on the retained coloring together with $t$, with the
invisible postings erased. This is a disjunction of guards
on the remaining flags: discard primes contradicted by
fixed postings and delete exclusions of postings that
cannot occur. Conjoin these tests for $T$ and disjoin over
the eligible operations. The numbers of tests in a
conjunction and of operations depend only on $K,h$.
Expanding the conjunctions by taking unions of exclusion
sets gives polynomially many guards for this restricted
predicate. On the independent flags, inclusion
minimization gives its canonical primes.

To recover primes on the original facet, enumerate a set
of exclusions on the $h+b$ fixed coordinates. For every
consistent status on those coordinates avoiding the
exclusions, take the restricted list just computed.
A guard with the chosen exclusions is sufficient exactly
when its remaining exclusions suffice on every one of
these restricted predicates. Take products of the
restricted lists, adjoining the chosen exclusions, and
minimize the resulting union of lists. Every sufficient
guard contains one of these products, by sufficiency on
the independent flags at each fixed status. Hence the
output is canonical. There are only boundedly many fixed
statuses and tests per product. The retained sources
have empty guards throughout. Any core minorant can
be unioned with the retained inputs and stay below the
candidate, so \cref{core:thm:block-source-extension}
gives greatestness among all core minorants.

At prime width two, no bound on the number of
competing retained inputs is needed for polynomial
prime-list construction at fixed $K,h$. Fix an
observer facet, a queried head and one internal
coloring $d$. List the local term operations that
supply the mark at $d$. The mark fails in the
greatest bounded operation exactly when each of
these operations violates some activated loss.
For each operation choose one positive internal
row and one original facet witnessing such a
violation. There are only a bounded number of
joint choices, depending on $K,h$. For each
choice conjoin the corresponding prime hits
on the retained background. After treating the
visible internal postings as constants these are
clauses of width at most two. Add the binary
consistency clauses on retained postings and
forbid the queried observer's postings. Thus the
losing backgrounds are a union of solution sets
of a bounded number of $2$CNFs.

For one such $2$CNF, every sufficient set of
exclusions contains at most two exclusions already
making it unsatisfiable. Indeed, eliminate the
variables outside the set of exclusions by
resolution, which preserves width two and gives
the existential projection. The all-zero
assignment violates a clause of at most two
variables, and excluding just those variables
already forces failure. This includes an empty
clause when the original formula is unsatisfiable.
Enumerate these small exclusion sets by $2$SAT and
take products across the bounded number of
formulas. Minimization gives the canonical
retained guards at $d$.

Finally, enumerate exclusions on the internal
inputs. For each such choice take products of
the retained lists over all internal colorings
that enable those exclusions, then adjoin the
internal exclusions and minimize. This is exact
because a guard must suffice on every remaining
internal section. Both products have a number
of factors bounded in $K,h$, so the construction
is polynomial and gives a bound on output
prime width depending only on $K,h$.

If explicit primes are unnecessary, a circuit representation
works with no bound on $b$. On each observer facet use a
Boolean input for absence of each posting. For each core
operation $T$ on the $h$ internal inputs, compute a gate
depending only on the retained inputs that says $T$ is
admissible there. For each positive internal probe $(j,t)$
of $T$ and each facet, take the disjunction of the candidate
primes at $j$ restricted to the coloring $t$ and to the
visible retained postings. Conjoin these disjunctions.
The resulting gate is exactly admissibility: it excludes
every activated loss that $T$ would fail to respect.

At an internal head, conjoin this gate with $T$'s own
coefficient at the queried internal coloring, and disjoin
over $T$. This gives the greatest minorant's source bit by
the block criterion. The circuit has constant depth with
unbounded fan-in and size at most a function of $K,h$ times
the size of the input lists. It can be constructed within
the same bound by sharing admissibility gates across heads.
All gates are monotone in the absence flags; competing
postings need no special treatment because the circuit is
evaluated on proper colorings. Thus the exponential output
below concerns expansion into canonical primes.

The output size in this construction cannot in general
be bounded by a fixed polynomial exponent independent
of $h$. Fix one owner $r$ and a binary tree with $d$
leaves. Use its vertices as headed inputs, with a disjoint
group of $m$ always-retained controls at each leaf.
A leaf loses exactly when all its controls are posted at
$r$. An internal vertex loses exactly when either child
is posted at $r$. On a facet seeing $r$, the leaf's
primes are the $m$ singleton exclusions of its controls,
and an internal vertex has one prime excluding the two
children's postings. These are canonical and have width
at most two. On the facet hiding $r$ all sources have
the empty prime. There are no competing retained postings.

The greatest core minorant's root bit follows directly
from the acyclic marked-source test
(\cref{core:thm:acyclic-marked-source-test}). A leaf
is active exactly when some control is not posted at
$r$. A parent is active exactly when both children are
active and neither is posted at $r$. Thus at a facet
seeing $r$, each root prime must exclude the $r$-posting
of every nonroot tree vertex and choose one control
exclusion at each leaf. Every such choice is sufficient
and minimal, so the root has $m^d$ primes.
The input size is polynomial in $dm$, with $h=2d-1$,
and the output is exponential in $d$ already at $m=2$.
Varying $m$ for each fixed $d$ also rules out a bound
of the form $f(K,h,b)$ times a fixed polynomial in the
input size for explicit canonical output.

\paragraph{A direct cofactor test with one loss per head.}
Suppose the live family has at most one minimal loss
$t_i$ at each head. There is a test using just consistency
of singleton postings, including for mixed loss rows.
At a fixing of $t_i$ we must decide whether changing
the free menu at $i$ can change a winner. Test the
following two kinds of proposed witness, for each
observed winner $k$.

\begin{enumerate}
\item If $t_i$ posts owner $k$, fix $t_i$ and
give $i$ the ambiguous menu omitting $k$. Ask
whether $k$ can be absent. If it can, changing
$i$ to the full menu adds $k$: the sole loss at
$i$ is invisible to observer $k$, and no singleton
flags change.
\item Choose $j\ne i$ with a loss $t_j$ that
posts $i$ at an owner $s$ and omits owner $k$.
Fix all of $t_i$ and $t_j$. Require $j$ to carry
$k$, using its fixed menu if it has one, and
the full menu otherwise. Ask whether $k$ can
be absent despite this. If it can, changing
$i=\{s\}$ to the ambiguous menu omitting $k$
removes the required posting from $t_j$, and
$j$ supplies $k$.
\end{enumerate}

Each absence test is elementary. The mandatory
sources of $k$ are the fixed $k$-singletons and
the designated head in the second test. Every
one of them must have a loss omitting $k$.
Adjoin all postings of those losses and check
that the combined fixing is consistent with
the already fixed menus. A designated nonsingleton
must remain nonsingleton. None of these new
postings is at $k$, so they introduce no more
mandatory sources. Assign every other free input
the ambiguous menu omitting $k$. This completes
the absence witness whenever the check passes.
Conversely any actual absence must satisfy these
same postings, because every mandatory source
has only one possible loss.

The tests are exhaustive. Compare each menu at
the captured input with the ambiguous menu omitting
$k$. A change of membership there can only enable
its own contribution, and that contribution survives
at the fixing only if $k$ was posted in $t_i$.
A change of singleton flag at any other owner can
only suppress other contributions: some supplying
head $j$ must then have lost its mark at a row
posting $i$ at that owner. This is precisely the
second test. A foreign singleton has no
$k$-membership, and a menu carrying $k$ adds no
posting in the $k$-view. Thus there is always a
change of one of the two tested kinds. Independence
at the minimal fixing implies independence at all
its extensions, so checking these cases decides
the full colored-cofactor law.

\paragraph{Checking all loss fixings at width two.}
For every fixed $K\ge3$, checking the colored-cofactor law
is coNP-complete at prime width two. Hardness holds even
with acyclic dependence of depth two and when only one
specified input can violate the law. A certificate of
failure gives a proper loss fixing and two valuations
differing only at the captured input.

For a clause $\ell_1\lor\ell_2\lor\ell_3$ of a 3CNF
introduce a fresh variable $y$ and replace the clause by
$y\lor\ell_2\lor\ell_3$ and a link $\neg y\lor\ell_1$.
The resulting formula is equisatisfiable. All the variables
are retained inputs, with truth and falsity represented by
$b$ and $a$ respectively for distinct owners $a,b,c$.
Also retain a pad input for each owner other than $c$.

Give each three-literal clause a head $h$, and each link
a head $h'$. At $h$ put the width-two prime for the
satisfying postings of $\ell_2\lor\ell_3$, and at $h'$
the prime for the satisfying postings of the link.
Also put at each such head one singleton prime for
each pad's posting at its own owner. These losses require
all pads and a satisfying posting in the corresponding
two-literal test. Thus they are visible on the $c$-facet
only. Their primes use a fixed color per input at each
head, taking three distinct variables per original clause.

The last head $x$ has a prime $\{y:b,h:c\}$ for each
three-literal clause and the singleton prime $\{h':c\}$
for each link. These lists also have fixed colors per
input. Use facet restriction and subsumption throughout
to obtain the canonical lists. In particular $x$ has
the empty guard on the $c$-facet, since there is at least
one link. The retained inputs guarantee nonempty outputs.

A loss fixing at a clause or link head fixes every pad.
Its own $c$-contribution is suppressed, and every other
winner is already supplied by a pad. Changing its menu
can affect no other head except $x$, whose $c$-coefficient
is unconditional and whose other contributions are
covered by the pads. Thus independence holds at all
these heads.

Under a fixing capturing $x$, its own contribution
can affect only the $c$-bit. The only other possibly
hidden color in a loss of $x$ is $b$, and if a required
$y:b$ posting is used, that retained input already
supplies $b$. No other source predicate depends on $x$.
Therefore a violation at $x$ requires a valuation
extending a loss fixing in which no other input supplies
$c$. For each three-literal clause the fixing either
posts $y$ at $b$ or posts $h$ at $c$. The first posting
satisfies the first literal. In the second case lack of
$c$ requires a satisfying posting for $\ell_2\lor\ell_3$.
Every link head is posted at $c$, so each link also
requires a satisfying posting. These consistent singleton
flags extend to a satisfying Boolean assignment.

Conversely, given a satisfying assignment, post each
variable at its Boolean color and each pad at its owner.
Post all link heads at $c$ and post a three-literal
clause head at $c$ exactly when its $y$ is false, using
$a$ otherwise. The selected $y:b$ and head $c$ postings
give a proper loss fixing for $x$. Every other source
omits $c$, so changing just $x$ between $I\setminus\{c\}$
and $\{c\}$ exhibits dependence. This proves hardness
for both the whole law and the test at one input.

By \cref{core:thm:acyclic-source-guards}, exact recognition
on acyclic data is consequently coNP-complete at width
two. At width one it is polynomial by the fixed-face
test above. If nonempty outputs are not promised, reject
the all-zero family first. Once the cofactor law passes,
unused inputs are irrelevant. Neutralize them and take the
first live source in the acyclic order. It has no earlier
live input, so it retains its nonempty menu at every valuation.

For a specified fixing the width-two bound is also sharp:
cofactor independence is coNP-complete at width three.
Use one retained input for each SAT variable, with the
two Boolean values represented by singleton postings at
$a,b$. Give each clause a head $h$ with one prime,
consisting of its satisfying postings on the $c$-facet.
On the other facets drop the hidden postings. A single
guard has consistent minimal transversals, and all these
facets agree by restriction of the same singleton losses.
Give a fresh input $x$ a single loss when one designated
clause head is posted at $c$. Fix every clause head at
$c$, so this captures $x$. The variable inputs remain
unfixed and retained, ensuring nonempty output.

Only $x$'s contribution can change when its menu changes.
Under this fixing that contribution can affect only $c$.
The clause heads all offer $c$, and suppress it exactly
when each clause has a satisfying posting. Such postings
give a consistent partial assignment and can be extended
to a satisfying assignment. Conversely a satisfying
assignment supplies only $a,b$ at the variable inputs
and suppresses $c$ at every clause head. Then changing
the menu of $x$ changes the $c$-bit. This proves the
hardness for a single specified face.

\paragraph{Prescribing source guards with the singleton profiles fixed.}
Let $V=H\mathbin{\dot\cup}W$, with both parts nonempty, and fix an
admissible family $\phi_r:2^V\to\{0,1\}$ of singleton profiles.
For each $h\in H$ prescribe its source predicate $P_h$ on $\mathcal D_W$.
Thus inputs in $W$ may be posted at any owners, and all other heads in
$H$ are uncolored. The profiles, in contrast, are prescribed on
\emph{all} coalitions in $V$. Write $Z_h$ for the minimal zeros of
$P_h$, and put $Z_v=\varnothing$ for $v\in W$.

\begin{theorem}[Source guards on uncolored heads at fixed profiles]
\label{core:thm:uncolored-head-profiles}
A core word has profiles $\phi$ and the given source predicates on
$\mathcal D_W$ if and only if the $P_h$ are antitone and the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $P_h(t)=1$, then $\phi_r(t_r)=0$ for every $r$.
\item For each choice of winning coalitions $A_r$ at all owners,
some $v\in\bigcap_r A_r$ satisfies
\[
  \forall t\in Z_v\quad \exists q\in I:\quad
             t_q\cap\bigcap_{r\ne q}A_r\ne\varnothing .
\]
\item For each owner $r$ and coalitions $A,B$ with
$\phi_r(A)=1$ and $\phi_r(B)=0$, some $v\in A\setminus B$ satisfies
\[
 \forall t\in Z_v\quad
 t_r\setminus B\ne\varnothing
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \exists q\ne r:\ t_q\cap A\ne\varnothing .
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At a probe for $(h,t)$ choose an owner $k$ missing from $t$, give $h$
the menu $I$, give posted inputs their atoms and all other inputs
$I\setminus\{k\}$. These menus have exactly the singleton coalitions
$t_r$, and only $h$ carries $k$. The probe reads the source bit since
its foreign singleton tests and its input memberships of $k$ are
unchanged from the canonical probe. A prescribed singleton therefore
excludes the mark, proving the first condition.

Call a word safe if it omits the mark at every listed zero. Consider
one rainbow demand $(A_r)_r$ in (2) or one pair $(r,A,B)$ in (3).
An eligible $v$ belongs to the displayed intersection or difference.
If $v$ passes its test, start with $x_v$. For each $t\in Z_v$, attach
a posted input $x_u$ by choice at its posting owner $q$, using the
witness in the test. For (3) take $q=r$ and $u\in t_r\setminus B$
in the first alternative. At that gate the posting absorbs the mark
on its row. All attachments use inputs from $W$, so later gates
cannot reintroduce it, and no other head occurs. In a rainbow the
helper belongs to every foreign winning coalition. For a pair, an
own-owner helper lies outside $B$ and a foreign helper belongs to
$A$. Thus every gate preserves the demands of its predecessor.

Conversely, if every eligible $v$ fails, select one offending row
there. A term satisfying the demand cannot be singleton at any of
these selected probes. Indeed a $q$-singleton wins on $t_q$, so in
a rainbow common source gives an input in
$t_q\cap\bigcap_{r\ne q} A_r$. For a pair at owner $r$, a
$q$-singleton with $q\ne r$ similarly gives an input in $t_q\cap A$,
and an $r$-singleton forces $t_r\setminus B\ne\varnothing$ by
monotonicity. A safe term satisfying the demand would therefore take
a union at its root on every selected row. Both children omit those
marks, and the profile rules select one child retaining the demand.
Descending on the selected rows ends at an eligible projection,
contradicting its own selected loss. This proves the equivalence for
each demand separately.

The profile tables of the safe words form a relation closed under
every owned gate. By \cref{core:thm:profile-relations} the two tests
place $\phi$ in this relation: a partial rainbow extends with top
coalitions at the missing owners, and a forced bit is tested by
pairing it with its owner's top coalition. Let $s$ be a safe word
with profiles $\phi$. For every $h\in H$, the greatest single-source
completion from \cref{core:thm:source-completion} on $W\cup\{h\}$
realizes $P_h$ and uses no other head. Take the union $u$ of these
completions. Its bit at $(h,t)$ is exactly $P_h(t)$, since only its
$h$-summand can carry the mark. Now $u\del s$ has exactly the
profiles of $s$. At a desired positive, (1) makes $s$ ambiguous and
reset adds the mark from $u$. At a zero both words omit it, by
antitonicity and safety. This realizes the whole family.
\end{proof}

In fact the construction gives the greatest completion on all menus.
Write $C_\phi$ for the greatest profile completion and $F_{P_h}$ for
the single-source completion on $W\cup\{h\}$. Whenever the tests pass,
the greatest operation with the specified profiles and source predicates is
\begin{equation}\label{core:eq:fixed-profile-block-maximum}
                 C_\phi\ \cap\ \bigcup_{h\in H} F_{P_h}.
\end{equation}
The intersection is pointwise. Indeed at any full coloring $p$ away
from $h$, a supplying $h$ must already supply at $p|_W$, by
antitonicity. Source reconstruction therefore bounds every realizer by
$u=\bigcup_hF_{P_h}$, which retains all passive inputs. In particular
$s$ from the proof is also bounded by $u$: its zeros on $\mathcal D_W$
include all the specified zeros. At a prescribed singleton $u$ contains
that winner, and elsewhere $C_\phi$ returns the whole input union.
Thus $u\del s$ realizes exactly the displayed intersection.

\begin{corollary}[Complete guards after accounting for prescribed singletons]
\label{core:cor:profile-residual-guards}
Given a complete antitone, observer-agreeing guard family, reconstruct
its candidate $F$ by \cref{core:thm:source-guards}. Read $\phi_r(A)$
by testing $F=\{r\}$ with $A$ at $\{r\}$ and the remaining inputs at
$I$. Write $U_j(p)=[\forall r,\ \phi_r(p_r)=0]$ on proper colorings
away from $j$. Require $\phi$ to be admissible and $P_j\le U_j$ for
all $j$. Let $H$ be the heads $j$ with $P_j\ne U_j$, and
$W=V\setminus H$. Suppose every minimal zero $p$ with $U_j(p)=1$
posts only inputs in $W$. Then the complete family is realizable
if and only if conditions (2) and (3) above hold for the predicates
restricted to colorings on $W$ at the heads in $H$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A realizer must have the profiles read at the stated valuations.
For $h\in H$ the hypothesis gives, on every proper coloring,
\[
                 P_h(p)=U_h(p)\land P_h(p|_W).
\]
Indeed, if $U_h(p)=1$ then it is also one at every subcoloring. Any
loss below $p$ thus contains a minimal loss supported on $W$.
Formula \eqref{core:eq:fixed-profile-block-maximum} has exactly these
bits, as its marked probe is either a competing singleton or the union
of the indicated completions. The other heads have bits $U_j$.
When $H$ is empty use $C_\phi$. If $H=V$, each head has a minimal
loss with $U_j=1$, necessarily at the empty coloring, so every head
is unused and (2) fails on the top coalitions.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The residual tests on canonical primes.}
At fixed $K$ the residual posting graph can be computed directly
from complete prime lists. For each potential edge from $u$ to
$h$, test for a minimal zero at $h$ containing $u:r$ for some $r$
and with no spanning block. Use the nonspanning witnesses and a
private prime for $u:r$ as in the test for repeated postings
outside singleton outcomes. Taking no required posting instead
finds the heads in $H$. In particular the hypotheses of
\cref{core:cor:profile-residual-guards} and the bounded-height
hypothesis below can be tested without expanding the losses.

When all residual losses post at a single owner $a$,
there is a closure criterion. For $E\subseteq V$ put
\[
 c(E)=E\cup\{j:\text{some residual loss at $j$
                            has its block contained in $E$}\}.
\]
Assume $P_j\leq U_j$ at every head. The candidate is
core exactly when the following conditions hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item Its singleton profiles satisfy the common-source
      condition and the full and empty coalition tests.
\item Every maximal losing $a$-coalition is fixed by
      $c$, as is the complement of every minimal winning
      coalition of a foreign profile.
\item For every losing $a$-coalition $E$,
      $c(c(E))=c(E)$.
\end{itemize}

The complement of a set is a safe $a$-fold support
exactly when the set is fixed by $c$, allowing the
empty support in this correspondence. The complements
in the second test therefore give exactly the individual
profile conditions of the one-owner fold criterion.
Its joint condition says that each intersection of
foreign minimal winners contains an active CNF clause,
or equivalently meets every winning $a$-coalition.
This is precisely the common-source condition.

At a proper probe where all profiles lose, write $E$
for its $a$-block. The surviving sources lie outside
$c(E)$, since all remaining loss tests use only $a$.
The residual core minorant is the union of safe folds,
whose possible sources on this probe lie outside the
iterated closure of $E$ under $c$. Equality of these
source sets at every such probe is equivalent to the
last condition. In the reverse direction one can test
any losing $E$ at a pure $a$-posting, probing each
head outside $E$ on a foreign facet. All the other
profiles then lose on the empty block. Thus the
last condition is necessary as well as sufficient.
With the profile mask imposed, these three conditions
supply exactly the candidate.

The source part of this criterion also splits across
blocks with different posting owners. Allow blocks
to overlap at inputs with no residual losses, but
require each residual head to belong to one block.
Every residual loss must stay in its head's block
and use only that block's chosen owner $a_B$. Let $c_B$ be the
same capture map on the block $B$. The greatest
residual core minorant is the union of the block
minorants. To prove this, take a marked query at
$i\in B$ and keep only the residual losses headed in
$B$. All their outside postings are empty.
\Cref{core:lem:unmarked-background} reduces feasibility
of the mark under these constraints to a bounded word
on $B$. Such a word automatically omits the losses of
the other blocks, since it uses none of their heads.
Thus every global mark belongs to a block minorant,
and their union is bounded. For an input in several
blocks its projection alone is bounded.

For a disjoint partition there is also a least
block-independent upper bound of any complete source
candidate. At a head in $B$, erase outside postings
from each observer prime and minimize. Equivalently,
evaluate that head's predicate with all outside inputs
unposted. This allows every original positive by
antitonicity. Any block-independent upper bound must
allow the same mark at the coloring with empty
outside statuses, and hence at every coloring with
that restriction to $B$. Thus the resulting bound
is least, and is the union of the candidate's
neutral residues on the blocks. If the candidate
is core, this upper bound is core by neutral
elimination, omitting zero residues.

The construction composes under refinement. Applying
a finer partition after a coarser one erases exactly
the postings outside each finer block. Minimization
commutes with this erasure because inclusion is
preserved. In particular block-independent source
bounds form a closure system under this operation:
a candidate is fixed precisely when its canonical
primes at every head use only the head's block.

The greatest block-independent lower source bound
is obtained by projecting all losses to the block
of their head. A block-independent predicate omits
an original loss exactly when it omits that projected
loss. Its greatest core minorant is the union of the
scoped core minorants. Indeed a block-independent core
word splits as the union of its neutral residues,
and each residue must lie below the original candidate
because it is contained in that word. Conversely any
union of bounded words on individual blocks is both
bounded and block-independent. Therefore this core
lower approximation is the same whether computed
below the candidate or below its greatest core
minorant, allowing zero when no bounded word exists.

The projected lower lists also have a direct formula,
with one factor per original observer regardless of
the number of omitted inputs. At local observer $k$
and head $i\in B$, start with the original list at
each observer $\ell$. Keep only primes supported
inside $B$, and delete their exclusions at $k$.
Take the minimal unions of one resulting guard per
$\ell$. To verify the formula, a local guard $G$
must hit every projected loss visible at $k$.
This is equivalent to hitting every original loss
after adjoining to $G$ all $k$-postings inside $B$.
Indeed the added exclusions hit the original rows
whose projections are invisible, while the other
rows must be hit by $G$. On each original facet
the resulting exclusions suffice exactly when they
contain an original prime. That prime cannot use
inputs outside $B$, and its $k$-exclusions are
already satisfied. Thus the unions give exactly
the local sufficient guards and minimization makes
them canonical. This takes polynomial time at fixed
$K$ without enumerating extensions of the scope.

All original observers are required. A loss posting
only an outside input at $k$ is invisible on the
original $k$-facet, but its projection is empty and
therefore forbids the local mark even on that facet.

Both the product bound and the possible $K$-fold
increase in prime width are sharp. Fix an output
observer $k$. For each original observer $\ell$,
choose an owner $r_\ell$ different from $k$ and
$\ell$. Give the one conditional head $d$ losses
for $\ell$, each posting a separate group of
$m$ retained controls at $r_\ell$. Use disjoint
groups across all losses. In addition every loss
for $\ell$ posts the same outside padding, with
one fresh control at each owner other than $\ell$.
Thus that loss is visible on the original
$\ell$-facet alone. Its facet primes are the
singleton padding exclusions and the
$m^d$ choices of one control exclusion from
each of its $d$ groups. The one-source completion
with retained controls realizes the family.

Project away the padding. On the output $k$-facet
there are now $Kd$ disjoint projected losses.
The primes choose one exclusion from each of
these groups, giving width $Kd$ and
$m^{Kd}$ primes. Before projection the width
was $d$ and each observer list had
$m^d+K-1$ primes. In particular the product
growth already occurs at original width one.

The upper approximation gives only an inequality:
the least block-independent bound above the core
minorant is itself core and lies below the core
minorant of the least block-independent bound.
The inequality can be strict for singleton blocks.
At $x$ put the sole loss $y:a$ and at $y$ the sole
loss $x:b$, with $a\ne b$. Retain a fresh input $z$
to make the candidate everywhere nonempty. A cut on
$\{x,y\}$ has empty boundary and no monochromatic
cycle, so neither head can be supplied at the empty
query by a bounded word. Antitonicity excludes them
at every probe. The greatest core minorant is thus
$z$. But the candidate's least bound on singleton
blocks is full union, already core. This strictness
occurs at prime width one.

The minorant split itself needs no restriction on
the owners within a block. The proof only uses
containment of each loss in its head's block.
It also applies after restricting to a scope $A$:
project each loss at a head in $A$ to that scope
and minimize. Consequently the live-support
operator $L(A)$ for the whole residual bound is
the union of the live supports on the scopes
$A\cap B$, computed under the corresponding
projected block bounds. For any prescribed
owner absent from all residual postings, this
gives the exact complete-profile tests by scoped
unanimity and intersection of minimal winners.
This use of the split allows mixed losses inside
each block. The additional one-owner hypothesis
is needed for the closure and safe-fold
calculations below.

It follows that, assuming $P_j\leq U_j$ and joint
attainability of the prescribed profiles under the
residual bound, the exact source test is blockwise
idempotence. For every block $B$ and every
$E\subseteq B$ losing at $a_B$, require
$c_B(c_B(E))=c_B(E)$. Indeed the mark in the
residual minorant depends only on the postings at
$a_B$ within $B$, and the profile mask deletes the
marks at forced singleton outputs. A global
nonspanning probe restricts to a losing $E$ by
monotonicity. Conversely every losing $E$ can be
probed alone, with the other owner blocks empty.
This separates profile feasibility from source
compatibility even when different blocks contain
cycles at different owners.

The profile tests for these pure blocks also reduce
to their safe folds. For a rainbow of positive tests
$r:A_r$, a block of owner $a$ can provide a witness
exactly when its greatest safe support inside
$\bigcap_{r\ne a}A_r$ meets $A_a$, taking an absent
demand to have coalition $V$. For a signed test
$r:A$ winning and $r:D$ losing, use the greatest
safe support in $B\setminus D$ and ask whether it
meets $A$ if $r=a$. If $r\ne a$, use the greatest
safe support in $B\cap A$ and ask whether it meets
$B\setminus D$. In either case it suffices that
one block passes.

For necessity, start with any global witness.
Follow a path to a leaf by keeping all query bits:
for a positive rainbow select a child passing
the root owner's demand, if any, while the other
tests pass to both. For a signed pair at $r$ select
a winning child at an $r$-gate and a losing child
at a foreign gate; the opposite bit passes to both.
The leaf lies in the intersection of the requested
coalitions for the rainbow, or in $A\setminus D$
for the signed pair. A leaf with no residual loss
already gives a bounded projection satisfying the
query. Otherwise let $B$ be its unique block, of
owner $a$.

Now prune the global word to an $a$-fold containing
that leaf. Keep both children at $a$-gates and one
at all other gates, preserving the chosen leaf
path. Off the path preserve the foreign positive
tests for a rainbow. For a signed pair preserve
the negative if $r=a$, and the positive otherwise.
Each of these retained tests passes to both
children at $a$-gates and can be passed to a
suitable single child at every pruned gate.
On loss probes headed in $B$, only $a$ occurs as
a singleton input, so each pruned gate acts by
union. Pruning therefore preserves all these
omissions. Restrict the resulting fold to its
leaves in $B$. This support is still safe, since
every loss at a used head within $B$ posts only
that block. The chosen leaf supplies the required
intersection, and the retained tests impose the
stated support restrictions. Conversely the safe
fold found in any block is globally bounded and
passes the query.

For an individual profile query a mixed block
can use the same localization when its losses
satisfy coalition containment. For a rainbow,
put $J=\bigcap_r A_r$ over the demanded owners.
Require that at each head in $J$ in this block,
the $r$-block of every loss be contained in $A_r$
for each demanded owner $r$. For a signed pair,
use the same condition with $J=A$ and just its
positive demand at $r$. This includes blocks
that avoid the queried owners altogether.

Again choose a leaf on a path preserving the
query bits. If its block is pure, use the fold
argument above. In a mixed block prune at a
demanded owner's gate only when just one child
passes that owner's positive test, retaining
that child. When both pass, keep both. For a
rainbow all other tests pass to both children,
so the pruned word lies on $J$ and retains the
chosen path. At a relevant loss probe, a
discarded child fails its owner's test on the
positive coalition, so by containment it also
fails on the corresponding loss block. It
cannot absorb at that gate. Thus the pruned
word still omits the chosen block's losses:
pruning only shrinks the value at the loss
probes for heads in $J$, and no other head
can supply a mark. The leaf supplies an
empty-query mark there. Localize that mark to
the block, using only inputs in the common
intersection. The resulting bounded word on
that scope passes the rainbow.

For a rainbow, the containment test can also use the
other positive coalitions. At a demanded owner's
gate, the discarded child still passes all the
foreign demands, since those tests conjoin.
It cannot be singleton at $r$ on a loss whose
$r$-block misses the intersection of those foreign
coalitions, by common source. Thus for every such
loss and owner it is enough that the $r$-block
either lie inside $A_r$ or miss that foreign
intersection. In either case the discarded child
cannot absorb that loss probe, so the same
localization applies.

A violation requires a loss with an $r$-posting
outside $A_r$ and an $r$-posting inside the foreign
intersection. These can be the same posting.
For a specified rainbow, scan these pairs of
postings in the prime lists. Require private primes
for the selected postings, and impose the
nonspanning restrictions as in the residual graph
test. A minimal loss in the resulting alphabet
must use the selected postings. Conversely any
violating minimal loss gives the private primes
and nonspanning restrictions. At fixed $K$ this
checks the weaker hypothesis without listing losses.

For a signed pair at $r$, use the same pruning at
$r$-gates, retaining the positive test on all surviving
branches and the signed path to the chosen leaf
$i\in A\setminus D$. The resulting word uses
only $A$. Its $r$-profile can only decrease at
each pruned node. On the path, every surviving
node still loses the $r$-test on $D$. On the
probe posting $D$ at $r$ and giving the other
inputs ambiguous menus, no foreign gate can
absorb, and no $r$-gate on that path absorbs.
The leaf therefore supplies its mark at this
probe. Localize the mark to the chosen block
and the scope $A$. It witnesses the negative
test, while containment in $A$ ensures the
positive. Thus the mixed block can again be
tested locally: by a scoped empty mark for
a rainbow, or a scoped foreign mark on $D$
for a signed pair.

By \cref{core:thm:profile-relations}, the rainbow
and signed tests decide complete profile
attainability. For signed checks, it suffices to
use minimal winning and maximal losing coalitions
by monotonicity. The full coalition must win at
every prescribed owner. With both kinds of
extremal coalition listed, these tests use
polynomially many block prunings for fixed $K$.
Together with blockwise idempotence they give
an exact criterion for pure residual blocks with
all profiles prescribed.

At fixed $K$ this criterion is polynomial given the
candidate's profiles in mixed presentation: monotone
CNF at $a$ and minimal winning coalitions at the other
owners. It does not require listing the residual
losses or their primes. First check $P_j\leq U_j$. For a prime at
$j$ on any facet and a visible owner $r$, the
largest permitted pure $r$-block consists of all
passive inputs whose $r$-postings the prime does
not exclude. If the profile wins there, the prime
allows a mark on a forced-singleton probe. If none
of these tests wins, every positive satisfies
$U_j$ by monotonicity. At the observer itself
the atomic block is empty.

Next use the one-owner fold and mixed-profile tests
on the residual budget. Their only loss queries
ask whether a head has a residual loss avoiding
specified $a$-postings. Let $D$ be the allowed
passive inputs for those postings. A residual loss
exists inside $D$ exactly when, for some CNF clause
$C$ of the $a$-profile, the pure posting of
$D\setminus C$ is a loss in the original list.
Indeed any residual loss misses a clause, and
conversely a loss at this enlarged block contains
a minimal original loss that still misses the
clause. The other profiles lose on empty blocks.
This test provides every safe-support pruning
step using just the input lists.

Finally check that the unrestricted greatest
residual minorant supplies all the candidate's
positive marks. For a prime at a head on a foreign
facet, it suffices to test the largest pure
$a$-block permitted by that prime. The residual
minorant is a union of $a$-folds, so its mark
depends only on this block and is antitone in it.
On the $a$-facet test the empty block. If the mixed
profiles are feasible and these mark checks pass,
the greatest-operation mask realizes exactly the
candidate. The residual budget enforces all
unexplained losses and the mask enforces the rest.
Conversely any realizing word passes the tests.
Thus this class permits cycles of arbitrary size
in the residual posting graph.

The profile CNFs can themselves be read from the input
primes. On a facet seeing $r$, use a clause
$\{i\}\cup E_r$ for each prime at $i$, where $E_r$
contains its $r$-exclusions, then remove redundant
clauses. A pure $r$-coalition hits every clause
exactly when no unposted source can give a foreign
winner. Nonemptiness then makes the output $\{r\}$.
If each foreign CNF has a bounded number of
nonsingleton clauses, expand it into minimal winning
coalitions in polynomial time: include every
singleton clause and choose one input from each
remaining clause, then minimize. In particular this
gives the polynomial recognition test from the source
primes alone when the foreign profiles are principal.

There is also a polynomial test with one nonprincipal
foreign profile of CNF width at most two, provided
the source prime width and the active CNF width are
at most two and the other foreign profiles are
principal. Let $J_0$ be the intersection of their
winning supports. The individual foreign test above
can use an implicit residual loss. Fix its head $j$
and guess a private clause $Q$ for $j$ in the foreign
CNF. Use Boolean variables for the pure $a$-postings
of the proposed residual loss, with $j$ unposted.
Require it to hit every original prime on a facet
seeing $a$, and guess one active clause it misses.
The latter restriction gives absence units. For
each foreign clause $D$, require some input of
$D\setminus(Q\setminus\{j\})$ to be unposted.
These negative clauses say that a foreign
transversal can avoid the proposed loss while
using $j$ as the sole hit on $Q$.

All the clauses have width at most two. If the
instance is satisfiable, shrink the proposed
postings to a minimal original loss. It remains
residual and the foreign transversal can still
avoid it. Conversely a violating minimal foreign
winner and a disjoint residual loss at one of
its members supply the chosen clauses and a
satisfying assignment. This checks safety of
every minimal foreign winner without listing
either winners or losses. Check the principal
supports individually by fold pruning.

For the joint test, let $A$ be a minimal winner of
the remaining foreign profile. The intersection
$A\cap J_0$ must contain an active clause. Since
the clause itself is safe, no additional condition
on the intersection is needed. Apply the same
$2$SAT test, retaining only the active clauses
contained in $J_0$ as negative clauses on the
counterexample coalition.

More generally, for several nonprincipal foreign
profiles, use one membership bit per owner and
input. Require each coalition to hit every clause
of its own profile. For every active clause inside
$J_0$, require at least one of its inputs to be
omitted by a nonprincipal owner. This is exactly
the search for a tuple whose intersection contains
no active clause, since it is enough to search
among all winning coalitions. After substituting
the memberships forced by singleton foreign
clauses, the joint test is again $2$SAT whenever
each active clause has at most two remaining
owner--input pairs that can omit it. The
individual safety tests are unchanged.

From canonical primes alone, exact recognition for
these pure residual blocks is coNP-complete at fixed
$K$. Delete inputs without residual losses from the
residual dependency graph and take weakly connected
components on the remaining heads. Each component
must have a common posting owner for all losses at
its heads, counting even postings to the deleted
inputs. Attach the deleted inputs to every block.
This finds the required cover, or shows that none
exists, by scanning for residual postings. If no
head remains, use a single block with any owner.

The profile CNFs, the $P_j\le U_j$ check and each
block pruning step take polynomial time. In a
block of owner $a$, run the pure-posting loss test
above with the allowed inputs restricted to that
block. To check all candidate marks, at each head
and prime use the largest permitted block of its
local owner on that facet, or the empty block if
the facet hides the owner. This is the worst case
for survival in the block minorant. Hence the
source part needs no guessed valuation and no
enumeration of losses.

A profile failure is certified by its full or
empty coalition test, a positive rainbow, or a
same-owner winning and losing pair. Evaluate the
guessed coalitions using the profile CNFs and
check that no block's greatest safe support
witnesses the query. The previous fold argument
and the closed profile relation theorem make
these certificates complete. The lower bound is
the empty-residual construction in
\cref{core:cor:profile-residual-height} below.

A retained input shows why the pure-block condition
cannot be replaced by unrestricted mixed blocks while
keeping the same fold criterion. More generally, let
$F$ be any nonzero source candidate and add a fresh
retained input $w$, giving the candidate $F+w$.
The old source lists are unchanged, and $w$ has
the empty guard on every facet. Every singleton
profile of the enlarged candidate requires $w$ in
its atomic coalition. No old minimal loss posts
$w$. Thus all old losses are residual. Core
realizability is preserved in both directions:
adjoin a projection for the forward implication
and take the neutral residue at $w$ for the reverse.
The enlargement preserves the old prime width.

For example, use
$F=(x\star_a y)\star_b z$ with $a\ne b$.
At $x$ the two losses post $y:a$ or $z:b$;
at $y$ they post $x:a$ or $z:b$.
The loss at $z$ posts both $x,y$ at $b$.
In $F+w$ these are all residual. On the scope
$\{x,y,z\}$ no safe single-owner fold can use
$x$ or $y$, since each has losses at two
different owners. A fold using only $z$
misses its loss. Nevertheless the bounded
body $F$ wins every owner's singleton test
on the entire scope. Thus mixed blocks may
need a genuine composition even to supply a
single profile test.

For mixed blocks, a sufficient uniform containment
condition can be checked directly on the profile CNFs.
For each residual posting $u:r$ at a head $j$ in a
mixed block, require every minimal winner of the
$r$-profile that contains $j$ to contain $u$.
This implies the containment needed for all the
minimal-winner rainbows and signed pairs. To test it,
seek a profile clause $Q$ containing $j$ whose other
members can be excluded along with $u$ while still
hitting every profile clause. If such a clause exists,
a minimal transversal in the remaining alphabet
must contain $j$ and miss $u$. Conversely a violating
minimal winner has such a private clause. Thus no
enumeration of minimal winners is needed to check
the uniform condition.

With this condition, if the mixed blocks have
bounded arity, exact recognition is again in coNP
from the canonical prime lists at fixed $K$.
On a mixed block enumerate its proper colorings
to recover the local residual losses. The pure
blocks can be treated by the preceding compact
tests. For each scoped query on a mixed block,
close its projections under the owned gates on
the finite set of value tables, and check the
localized mark. All this work is bounded in the
block arity. For the source check, test each
local proper coloring enabled by a candidate
prime after restriction to that block. Such a
coloring extends by empty outside statuses, so
these local tests are equivalent to all the
required source marks. A profile obstruction
still only needs a rainbow or a signed pair,
with each local witness test now exact under
the uniform condition.

The residual operation itself can have exponentially more primes
than the input, even for a core one-owner trace. Take $m\ge2$
disjoint pairs partitioning the passive set $W$ and add a head
$x$. At owner $r$, call $E\subseteq W$ spanning if it meets
the complement of each pair. On $V=W\cup\{x\}$ define a
closure by sending $E$ to $V$ when $E\cap W$ spans, and otherwise
adding just $x$ if $|E\cap W|\ge2$. This is a loopless closure:
adding $x$ does not change either test. Its one-owner trace is
core by the construction of \cref{core:eq:one-owner-trace-value}.
The spanning profile is the stated one at $r$, and unanimity
at every other owner.

On a facet seeing $r$, the head $x$ loses exactly when at
least two passive inputs are posted at $r$, since spanning
already requires two. Its prime guards are the $2m$ exclusions
of all but one passive input. At a passive head the primes
come from the $m$ complement clauses, with its own posting
removed and redundant clauses discarded. Facets hiding $r$
have only empty guards. The full input lists therefore have
polynomial size.

Every loss at a passive head is explained by the spanning
profile. The minimal losses at $x$ are all unordered pairs,
but a two-element set fails to span exactly when it is one
of the original disjoint pairs. Hence deleting the singleton
outcomes leaves exactly those $m$ residual losses at $x$.
The primes of the residual operation must choose one posting
from each pair, yielding $2^m$ distinct guards at $x$ on
each facet seeing $r$. This is why the residual graph and
quotient tests work with the original lists instead of
materializing residual prime lists.

Removing the singleton-explained losses can also destroy
realizability. This can be decided explicitly for a one-owner
trace with closure $c$. Write $c'(E)$ for $E$ together with the
heads having a residual premise contained in $E$. The residual
operation is core if and only if $c'$ is idempotent on the
spanning coalitions of $c$. Indeed $c'$ is extensive, monotone
and loopless. On a nonspanning coalition it agrees with $c$,
since every subcoalition is nonspanning. Moreover $c(E)$ is
itself nonspanning there, so idempotence already holds at $E$.
On the remaining coalitions idempotence is necessary for the
pure-owner closure of a core term and sufficient by the
one-owner trace construction. A failure certificate is a
spanning coalition $E$ with $c'(c'(E))\ne c'(E)$.
Both applications of $c'$ can be checked from the original
lists by the nonspanning loss test, without generating the
residual primes.

For example, on $\{a,b,j,x\}$ let $c$ be generated by the
implications $a\to j$ and $\{j,b\}\to x$. Its minimal
loss premises at $x$ are $\{j,b\}$ and $\{a,b\}$.
The latter spans and is removed, whereas $\{j,b\}$ and the
premise $\{a\}$ at $j$ do not span. Thus
$c'(\{a,b\})=\{a,b,j\}$, and the second application adds $x$.
The original trace is core, but the residual operation is
not. The residual constraints should therefore be used with
their prescribed profiles, rather than requiring the residual
operation to have an independent realizer.

Conversely, fix arbitrary prospective residual premises at one
owner on a nonempty live set $V$. Require nonempty premises
missing their head and an inclusion antichain at each head.
Write $\delta(E)$ for one simultaneous application of the
rules to $E$, retaining $E$ itself, and $d$ for their generated
closure. The possible spanning profiles of one-owner traces
with exactly these residual premises form an interval of
upward families on the ordered set of $d$-closed subsets.
Its lower endpoint is the upward family generated by $V$
and all $d(E)$ with $\delta(E)\ne d(E)$. Its upper endpoint
consists of the closed sets contained in none of the $d(A)$
for listed premises $A$, excluding the empty set.
If the lower endpoint is not contained in the upper one, no such trace
exists.

For necessity let $c$ be a realizing closure. It obeys all
the residual rules, so $d(E)\subseteq c(E)$. If $E$ is
nonspanning then $c(E)=\delta(E)$, since all applicable
minimal premises are nonspanning. Consequently
$d(E)=\delta(E)=c(E)$ there. The spanning predicate is
constant on $d$-closure classes, since a nonspanning $E$
has nonspanning $d(E)$ and spanning is upward closed.
It therefore gives an upward family of closed sets, must
include each of the lower generators, and must exclude
each $d(A)$ and the empty set.

Conversely for an upward family in the interval put
$c(E)=V$ if $d(E)$ is in the family, and $c(E)=d(E)$
otherwise. This is an extensive, monotone, loopless closure.
Its spanning predicate is exactly the chosen one, since
$V$ belongs to the family. On nonspanning coalitions
$c(E)=\delta(E)$ by the lower endpoint. Every listed
premise remains nonspanning by the upper endpoint and
remains minimal by the antichain condition, since every
proper subcoalition of it is also nonspanning. Conversely
every nonspanning loss contains a listed premise. The
one-owner trace of $c$ therefore has exactly the prescribed
residual premises.

The lower endpoint has a polynomial-size generating list
when the premises are explicit. For each rule $U\to i$ and
rule $T\to j$ with $j\in U$, put
$R=(U\setminus\{j\})\cup T$. Keep $d(R)$ when $R$
contains neither $i$ nor a premise at $i$. Such an $R$
derives $i$ in two rounds and misses it in one, so its
closure is one of the lower generators.
Conversely, if $\delta(E)\ne d(E)$, choose $i$ added in
the second round and absent after the first. Start from a
premise at $i$ contained in $\delta(E)$. Substitute the
first-round premises for its members outside $E$, one at a
time. A successful substitution gives a contained premise
with fewer members outside $E$. If all succeed, there is
a premise contained in $E$, a contradiction. Thus some
substitution fails and its seed $R$ is contained in
$\delta(E)$, so $d(R)\subseteq d(E)$. This shows that the
closures of failing pairs, together with $V$, generate
the whole lower endpoint.

The upper endpoint is specified by forbidden downsets
generated by the $d(A)$ and by the empty set. Consequently
nonemptiness of the interval is just a test that no lower
generator is contained in a forbidden upper set. One can
also impose any explicit finite list of extra profile bits:
add the closure of each required winning coalition to the
lower generators and the closure of each required losing
coalition to the forbidden upper sets. The same containment
test is exact and polynomial.

\paragraph{Joint necessary tests on monochromatic residuals.}
For any core candidate, the pure-$r$ closure has as its
nonspanning minimal premises exactly the residual rows
posted wholly at $r$. A pure-$r$ probe cannot contain a
mixed row, and no other owner has a nonempty posting block
on it. Thus the interval construction applies separately at
every owner, even when the complete candidate has mixed rows.

One can compute the least tuple in these intervals satisfying
feedback elimination, or certify that none exists. Check
that every interval is nonempty and start with the lower
endpoints, writing $d_r$ for the residual
closure at owner $r$. Apply the premises of
\eqref{core:eq:feedback-elimination} to the current closures.
For each conclusion requiring $i\in c_r(E)$, where
$E=A\setminus\bigcup_j X_j$, the requirement is already
met if $i\in d_r(E)$ or $E$ is spanning. Otherwise promote
$d_r(E)$ to the spanning family at $r$, together with all
larger closed sets. This is the only way to supply the required
capture in that interval. Reject if a promotion reaches a
forbidden upper set.

Every tuple in the intervals satisfying feedback must contain
each promotion. Indeed the closure memberships in the premises
of a feedback instance persist under enlargement, and the
coalition cover is independent of the closures. A missing
conclusion therefore forces the same promotion in every
such tuple. There are finitely many closed sets. If saturation
stops without rejection, all feedback conclusions hold and
the result is the least feasible tuple. The same proof allows
extra forced winning and losing profile bits in the intervals.
Finally, common-source compatibility holds for at least one
feedback-compatible tuple exactly when it holds for this least
tuple: enlarging winning families can only add rainbow demands.
This tests joint necessary conditions on the monochromatic
residuals and profiles, whose scope does not include all the
mixed-source requirements.

\paragraph{Identifying equal roles in the residual losses.}
The signature quotient by block unions also applies to the
remaining losses alone. Compare the signatures of two inputs
as follows. To distinguish their posted roles, look for a
nonspanning minimal loss containing one input's $r$-posting
while forbidding the other's. This uses one private prime.
If their posted roles agree, a loss headed at either leaves
both unposted. On that restricted ground, compare their head
roles by testing for a nonspanning probe negative at one
input and positive at the other, in both directions. For
the positive bit choose an enabled prime and forbid all its
postings, in addition to the prohibitions supplied by the
nonspanning witnesses. The remaining ground need only hit
every prime at the negative head.

The head comparison is exact even though it searches probes
rather than pairs of minimal losses. Minimize the negative
probe to a loss; positivity at the other head and absence of
spanning blocks survive the deletion of postings. Conversely,
if the two families of nonspanning minimal losses differ,
some row in one is positive at the other: otherwise each
row in either family contains a row of the other, and
minimality forces equality. The subrow in this argument
remains nonspanning by monotonicity of the profiles.

For fixed $K$ these tests compute all signature classes in
polynomial time directly from the complete primes. The
operation obtained by keeping just the residual losses can
be recovered from its quotient on the classes by substituting
block unions, by the signature argument in the guard-consensus
appendix. Its quotient table can be evaluated without listing
the losses: a loss in a queried coloring exists exactly when
its posting ground contains a nonspanning transversal, using
the same nonspanning witnesses. This quotient compresses the
residual constraints; the candidate's prescribed singleton
profiles still have to be imposed on the original inputs.

There is a direct test for whether those profiles also descend
through the quotient. On a facet seeing $r$, let $G_r$ denote
the input indices of the $r$-postings in a prime $G$ at head $i$.
The pure-$r$ spanning predicate has the monotone CNF with clauses
$\{i\}\cup G_r$, one for every head and prime on that facet.
Indeed a coalition spans exactly when it contains each head or
hits all that head's primes by $r$-postings. This gives a compact
representation even when the minimal winning coalitions are large
in number.

A profile descends through block unions exactly when each of its
minimal winning coalitions is a union of whole blocks. At owner
$r$ a block union is atomic exactly when every member is posted at
$r$, so only whole blocks may affect the profile. To test the
condition, for each pair $u,v$ in a block compare the three CNFs
obtained by fixing their bits to $(0,0),(0,1),(1,0)$.
Minimize each restricted clause list by inclusion. They agree
exactly when no minimal winning coalition contains just one of
$u,v$: a restricted winner that differs from the $(0,0)$ case
would contain a minimal winner using the one present input, and
each such minimal winner witnesses a difference. Comparison of
the minimized lists is exact for monotone CNFs. Checking every
pair and owner is polynomial in the complete primes.

For the residual signature partition, if these comparisons pass,
the whole candidate factors through its quotient. At a source
probe with a spanning block the singleton determines the mark.
At any other probe the only relevant losses are the residual
ones, since every subcoloring of a nonspanning coloring is
nonspanning. Thus the profiles and the residual constraints
together determine all source bits, and both descend through the
block unions. Equivalently, identifying the inputs of each block
gives a quotient whose substitution by block unions recovers the
candidate. Conversely any block-union factorization must preserve
the profiles under the same block test. In particular, on
candidates satisfying the profile test, recognition is
fixed-parameter tractable in $K$ and the number of residual
signature classes, by testing the finite quotient. The profile
condition matters for this bound: the vertex-cover construction
has a single residual signature class, and its recognition
problem is coNP-complete.

\begin{corollary}[Recognition after deleting losses forced by singletons]
\label{core:cor:profile-residual-height}
Draw arrows from posted inputs to heads using only
minimal losses $p$ with $U_j(p)=1$. At fixed $K$, recognition from
complete canonical prime lists is in coNP when these arrows form a DAG
of bounded height. It is coNP-complete even with no arrows and
PSPACE-complete at unrestricted acyclic height, even with the generators
of principal singleton profiles supplied in the hardness examples.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The bit $\phi_r(A)$ is read by a single evaluation of the reconstruction.
A violation of admissibility or of $P_j\le U_j$ has a polynomial
certificate. Otherwise omit only the indicated minimal losses.
Every other minimal loss is automatic at profiles $\phi$. By
\cref{core:thm:profile-relations} a safe word with profiles $\phi$
exists exactly when every winning rainbow and every same-owner
winning/losing pair is feasible under these omissions. To realize the
full table it remains to supply each positive under the same omissions:
reset the union of the positive witnesses by the safe profile word.
It is ambiguous on each positive probe by $P_j\le U_j$.
Thus a failure specifies one rainbow, pair or positive.
\Cref{core:thm:bounded-height-guard-certificate} gives a polynomial
certificate for that failure. Each cited loss can be checked from
the primes by testing the coloring and its single-posting deletions,
and by computing $U_j(p)$. The vertex-cover profile construction
has no residual losses and gives the coNP lower bound. At arbitrary
acyclic height the same primitive tests
use polynomial space by \cref{core:thm:bounded-height-guard-certificate}:
enumerate the coalitions and colorings, checking membership in the
remaining minimal losses from $P_j$ and $U_j$. The reduction in
\cref{core:thm:complete-guard-pspace} has precisely the acyclicity
required here and supplies its canonical primes in logarithmic space.
\end{proof}

\Cref{core:thm:uncolored-head-profiles} also allows a finite list of requested positives $(h,p)$ in
which other heads may be posted, while all prescribed zeros still
post only $W$. Replace (1) by the two conditions
$\phi_r(p_r)=0$ for every $r$ and
$t\nsubseteq p|_W$ for every $t\in Z_h$. Both are necessary.
For sufficiency use in the reset construction at $h$ the downset
generated by the restrictions $p|_W$ of its requested positives.
The resulting summand reads only $h$ and $W$, so posting other heads
at a positive does not affect its contribution.

For the complete predicates on $\mathcal D_W$, (2) and (3) can
also be read directly from their canonical positive prime lists.
For each demand write $R_q$ for the passive inputs that qualify
as helpers at $q$ in its displayed test. At observer $k$ form the
guard forbidding every posting $(u,q)$ with $u\in R_q$ and
$q\ne k$. It enables exactly the colorings with no qualifying helper.
An eligible head $v$ works for this demand if and only if at each
observer the guard contains a listed prime for $v$. An eligible
passive input works unconditionally. It suffices to use minimal
winners for the $A_r,A$ and maximal losers for $B$, since enlarging
a winning coalition or shrinking a losing one only adds witnesses.
To check (1), test the pure coloring $A:r$ for each minimal winning
$A\subseteq W$ and each head: positivity at any coloring with an
$r$-singleton would imply positivity there. These tests take polynomial
time at fixed $K$ given the primes on $\mathcal D_W$ and the extremal
coalitions of the admissible profiles.

Individual feasibility at different heads need not imply joint
feasibility. Take $H=\{x,y\}$, $W=\{u,v\}$ and three profiles, written
as monotone Boolean formulas,
\[
 \phi_0=xu(y\lor v),\qquad \phi_1=yu(x\lor v),\qquad \phi_2=xyv .
\]
Every minimal winning rainbow has a common input: if owner $0$ takes
$xyu$ then $y$ is common, if owner $1$ takes $xyu$ then $x$ is common,
and otherwise $v$ is common. Require $x$ to lose when both passive
inputs are posted at $1$, and $y$ to lose when both are posted at $0$.
For the first loss, a gap at owner $0$ or $2$ has a passive input in
its winning coalition. For a gap at owner $1$ with $A\setminus B=\{x\}$,
$y,u\in B$ and $v\notin B$ since $B$ loses, so $v$ is a helper. Every
rainbow has either a common input other than $x$ or a helper: if the
minimal $0$-coalition is $xyu$ then $y$ is common, and otherwise the
$0$- and $2$-coalitions share $v$. Thus $x$ can lose as required.
Swapping $x,y$ and $0,1$ gives the other omission. Together the losses
fail (2) on $(xyu,xyu,xyv)$: the only common inputs are $x,y$, and for
either loss the two foreign coalitions meet only in $\{x,y\}$.

For one head $h$, this gives a distributive lattice with explicit
endpoints. For $t\in\mathcal D_W$ put $U(t)=[\forall r,\ \phi_r(t_r)=0]$.
Put $L(t)=1$ if (2) or (3) fails with the single loss $Z_h=\{t\}$.
These definitions use only coalitions and the given profiles.

\begin{corollary}[The range of one source at fixed profiles]
\label{core:cor:fixed-profile-source}
The predicates at $h$ realized by words with profiles $\phi$ are
exactly the antitone $P$ with $L\le P\le U$. Equivalently they are
in bijection, under restriction, with the downsets of
$\{t:L(t)=0,\ U(t)=1\}$ in the induced order.
For unanimity profiles $\phi_r(A)=[A=V]$, they are precisely the
antitone predicates containing every coloring that uses at most one owner.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The proof of the theorem identifies $L(t)=1$ with failure to omit
$t$ at profiles $\phi$, so $L$ is the intersection of the attained
predicates. Their maximum is $U$ by \cref{core:prop:profile-maximum}.
For each demand in (2) or (3), an eligible input in $W$ works for
every row. If there is no such input, the only eligible input is $h$,
by admissibility. It works on a list exactly when it works on each
row individually. Thus the tests on all zeros reduce to $L\le P$.
The inverse to restriction adjoins the downset $\{t:L(t)=1\}$.

For unanimity, $U=1$ and every rainbow has a passive common input.
The only gap without a passive witness has $A=V$, $B=W$. At owner
$r$ its test reads $t_q\ne\varnothing$ for some $q\ne r$. Hence a
loss is allowed exactly when at least two owners are posted.
\end{proof}

The intersection also has a term construction. For two words $s,u$
with profiles $\phi$, replace every $h$-leaf of $s$ by $u$.
At a coalition $A$ this changes its $h$-bit to $\phi_r(A)$.
Changing one input of a monotone Boolean function to its current
output preserves that output, so the profiles stay $\phi$. At a
marked probe the new bit is the conjunction of the two old bits:
if $u$ loses the mark no input supplies it, and if $u$ supplies it
then $u$ is ambiguous and has the same foreign singleton tests as
the old $h$-input. Iterating gives a word $s$ attaining $L$. For
any unrestricted single-source predicate $P$ realized by $u$, reset
$u\del s$ then attains $L\lor(P\land U)$ at the prescribed profiles.

The same substitution limits how often a head must be tested in a
general obstruction, even when the other heads depend cyclically on
one another.

\begin{corollary}[One loss per unposted head]
\label{core:cor:unposted-head-omissions}
Fix the singleton profiles and any list of positive source queries.
In an inclusion-minimal infeasible list of additional losses, a head
with two losses must itself be posted at a loss of another head where
the prescribed output is ambiguous.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $h$ is never so posted. If each of its losses separately fits
all the other heads' losses and the positives, combine the witnesses
by successive substitution at $h$. Its own bits intersect. At a
different head $j$, read the probe with $j$ carrying the ambiguous
menu $I$. If the fixed profiles prescribe a singleton, every combined
word has that singleton. Otherwise the substituted word is ambiguous.
On a loss the old $h$-menu was also ambiguous, so no singleton test
changes, and neither word supplies the mark. On a positive the only
input carrying the mark before substitution was $j$. Replacing $h$
by an ambiguous menu can only erase a foreign posting at $h$; by
antitonicity $j$ still supplies the mark. Thus every common requirement
is preserved. Two or more losses at $h$ would each fit the other heads
by minimality, contradicting infeasibility.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Occurrences of one source.}
An \emph{owner box} on $\mathcal D_W$ is a predicate
$\bigwedge_r B_r(E_r)$, where each $B_r$ is antitone on $2^W$ and
$B_r(\varnothing)=1$. Define $b(P)$ as the least number of owner boxes
whose disjunction is $P$ on $\mathcal D_W$, allowing $b(0)=0$.
Principal boxes $[E'\leq E]$ show this number is finite.

\begin{corollary}[Exact source multiplicity]\label{core:cor:source-box-rank}
With union named, the least number of $x$-occurrences among all core
words realizing the source family of $P$ is exactly $b(P)$.
The greatest completion $F_P$ attains this minimum. A primitive core
realization uses at most $K b(P)$ occurrences of $x$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Each occurrence in the preceding proof supplies one owner box, giving
the lower bound for every realizer. Conversely, for a nontrivial factor
$B_r$ choose an $x$-free core word with $r$-profile $\neg B_r$, using
\cref{core:thm:profiles} with unanimity at the other owners. This tuple
is admissible because every positive $r$-coalition is nonempty. Attach
that word by $\star_r$ to a single $x$-leaf; omit factors identically
one. The resulting spine has exactly the prescribed box. Its passive
contributions lie in $U$, so the union with $U$ and the other box spines
realizes $F_P$ with exactly $b(P)$ source occurrences.

For the primitive bound, a union of any nonempty family of menus has a
core realization using each argument $K$ times. Fold all arguments once
at each owner $r$, obtaining $A_r$. If their union $S$ is ambiguous,
each $A_r$ is either $\{r\}$ or $S$. Combine the $K$ distinct labeled
leaves $A_r$ by a read-once tree whose node owner differs from the labels
of its leaf children. There are at most two such forbidden labels, so
$K\geq3$ suffices. Every nontrivial subtree is ambiguous and each gate
is union. The result is $S$: either some $A_r=S$, or all owners already
occur among the arguments. Singleton $S$ follows by idempotence.
Apply this construction to $U$ and the box spines.
\end{proof}

The common box rank has a coloring description on the canonical prime
lists, given in \cref{app:core-ordered-guards}.

For $b(P)=1$ and $W\ne\varnothing$, the primitive
occurrence minimum for the greatest completion is
either one or two. Recover the box factors by testing
$P$ on monochromatic probes. The minimum is one if
at least two factors are nontrivial, and two otherwise.

For necessity, in a primitive word with a single
$x$-occurrence every ancestor gate at owner $r$
forces a loss at the full $r$-coalition: all its
sibling inputs are passive and idempotence makes
the sibling $\{r\}$. Thus every owner on the path
has a nontrivial factor. With no such owner the term
would be $x$ alone, which fails passive retention.
With only one such owner $r$, the input $X=\{r\}$
absorbs at every path gate. The output is $\{r\}$
even if all passive inputs are $\{s\}$ for $s\ne r$.

For sufficiency when two factors are nontrivial,
restore a single box spine at those owners using
\eqref{core:eq:two-owner-restoration}. The added
filters exclude only the full coalitions already
lost by the box. For a single nontrivial factor at
$r$, take two copies of its spine and
restore one at $s,r$ and one at $t,r$, with $r,s,t$
distinct. Their extra filters at $s$ and $t$ cannot
both fail at the same valuation. Each restored
term contains $U$ and returns $\{r\}$ exactly when
$U=\{r\}$, so their $\star_r$ combines them by
pointwise union, recovering the original box.
Finally, for the unrestricted box use
$(x\star_r U)\star_t(x\star_s U)$ at three distinct
owners. The two inner terms cannot both absorb,
since their absorbing singletons differ. At least
one therefore gives $x\cup U$. The outer gate can
absorb only if an inner term gives $\{t\}$, which
requires $x=U=\{t\}$ and already gives their union.
This realizes the unrestricted greatest completion
with two occurrences.

For arbitrary positive box rank $b=b(P)$, the primitive
greatest completion needs at most $b+K+2$ occurrences.
If $P$ loses on the full coalition at some owner $t$,
at most $b+1$ suffice. To see this, start with a minimum
cover. Unless the unrestricted box suffices, every box
has a nontrivial factor. There is at most one box
restricted only at each given owner, since two such
boxes merge by disjoining that owner's factors. Realize
every individual greatest completion by the one-box
construction above. This takes at most $b+K$ copies
altogether, with at most one extra per owner.

If $P$ loses on the full $t$-coalition, every box is
restricted at $t$, so only the single box restricted
just at $t$ can take an extra copy. The $\star_t$-fold
of the completed boxes takes their pointwise union:
when $U=\{t\}$ all boxes lose and give $\{t\}$, and
otherwise every argument contains $U$ and so cannot
give $\{t\}$. This proves the $b+1$ bound.

In the remaining case every full monochromatic
coalition is positive. Choose distinct owners $s,t$
and let $T$ be the $\star_t$-fold of the completed
boxes. It gives the desired union whenever
$U\ne\{t\}$. To repair the other valuations, take
the principal boxes below the full $s$- and full
$t$-coalitions. They are bounded by $P$ and each
restricts $K-1\ge2$ owners, so their greatest
completions use one occurrence each. Fold these two
at a third owner to obtain their pointwise union
$R$: both lose on the full coalition of that third
owner. Now $T\star_s R$ gives the greatest completion.
When $U=\{t\}$, $R$ supplies the required $x$-menu;
when $U=\{s\}$ both terms already give the desired
union. Elsewhere the last gate takes union, with
$T$ exact and $R$ bounded by the desired value.
The repair adds just two occurrences.

Individual box covers do not yield a simultaneous linear bound on word size.

\begin{proposition}[Quadratic size with unit source ranks]
\label{core:prop:unit-ranks-quadratic}
For every fixed $K\geq3$ and $d\geq1$, put
$n=Kd+1$ and $t=(K-1)d+1$. The operation on $n$ inputs
\[
 U_d(w)=\begin{cases}
 \{r\},&\text{at least $t$ inputs equal }\{r\},\\
 \bigcup_iw_i,&\text{otherwise}
 \end{cases}
\]
is core and every source has box rank one. Nevertheless every
choice/union word realizing it has at least
\[
 t(n-t+1)=((K-1)d+1)(d+1)
\]
leaves. Thus no constant depending only on $K$ bounds the word size
by the sum of the individual source box ranks.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The profiles $\Phi_r(E)=[|E|\geq t]$ are admissible: each positive
coalition omits at most $d$ positions, so $K$ such coalitions have
nonempty intersection. Their greatest completion is $U_d$ by
\cref{core:prop:profile-maximum}. Two different quorums cannot coexist,
since $2t>n$. At a canonical source probe the only way to lose its
winner is a foreign quorum. Hence its coefficient is
\[
 c_{k,j}(E)=\bigwedge_{r\ne k}[|E_r|<t],
\]
the restriction of one normalized owner box. It is nonzero, so its
rank is one.

An owner's singleton profile of a word with $L$ leaves is a Boolean
AND/OR formula with $L$ leaves; named union gives AND. We show that
a formula for the threshold $t$ has $L\geq t(n-t+1)$.
Let $A,B$ be the Boolean layers of weights $t-1,t$, respectively,
and let $\mathcal E$ consist of pairs $(a,b)$ where $b$ adds one bit
to $a$. Traverse the formula on a pair in $A\times B$: at OR select
a child true at $b$, and at AND a child false at $a$, breaking ties
in a fixed way. A leaf labeled $i$ has $a_i=0,b_i=1$.
The choices partition $A\times B$ into at most $L$ rectangles
$A_\ell\times B_\ell$. The edges of $\mathcal E$ in each rectangle
change only its leaf's coordinate and form a matching. Therefore
\[
 |\mathcal E|\leq\sum_\ell\sqrt{|A_\ell||B_\ell|}
 \leq\sqrt{L\sum_\ell|A_\ell||B_\ell|}
 =\sqrt{L|A||B|}.
\]
Since $|\mathcal E|=|A|(n-t+1)=|B|t$, the bound follows.
For fixed $K$ its value grows quadratically in $n$, whereas the sum
of the source ranks is $n$.
\end{proof}
