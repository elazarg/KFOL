\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:verification}\label{sec:development}

\paragraph{Two players.}
We have assumed $K\geq3$, and much of the structure depends on it. With two players the values form the chain $\{b\}<\{a,b\}<\{a\}$, on which $\star_a$ and $\star_b$ are maximum and minimum and the role swap is negation: this is Kleene's strong three-valued logic \cite{Kleene1952}, which has a classical finite equational basis \cite{Kalman1958}. Games themselves reach only the two atoms, so their values are classical truth values; the third value $\{a,b\}$ occurs only as an input. A value says only who can force a win, so the algebra sits inside Olde Loohuis and Venema's for two players. Neutral union disappears, reset and $H_a$ become projections, owned choice itself preserves atoms, and idempotent operations need not come from $\mathbf C$. \Cref{app:two-players} has the details.

\paragraph{Hidden moves.}
On public trees, mixed strategies add no possible winner (\cref{se:cor:public}). A hidden move changes this: if one player picks a bit and another guesses it unseen, there is no pure equilibrium, so values must keep probabilities. The report treats rounds in which every player commits privately and all actions are then revealed. Their values compose on sets of winner distributions (\cref{se:thm:round}), but with three players no compositional semantics with finitely many values tracks even who might win: whether a third player can win in suitable contexts determines the exact winning probabilities of a two-player component (\cref{se:thm:infinite,se:prop:pair-observation}). With two players the winner-set algebra extends to these rounds, and what it misses is a scalar value, which belongs with the equilibrium semantics of independence-friendly logic \cite{SevensterSandu2010} (\cref{app:two-players}). Open games offer another compositional approach, deterministic \cite{GhaniEtAl2018} and probabilistic \cite{GhaniEtAl2020}. The unit and associativity laws of the probabilistic version have finite counterexamples \cite{GameTheoryOpenGameObstructions2026}, which we reproduce in \cref{se:sec:open-game-obstruction}; the round recursion is proved directly.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Olde Loohuis and Venema's algebras observe winning strategies, and they prove equivalence coNP-complete \cite[Theorem~5.50]{OldeLoohuisVenema2010} by reduction from the word problem for distributive lattices, which Bloniarz, Hunt, and Rosenkrantz showed coNP-hard \cite{BloniarzHuntRosenkrantz1984}. Individual forcing does not define possible SPE winners, and owned choice is not distributive, so that reduction does not apply here. Xi and Wu's multirole logic has multiparty cut elimination and is the closest proof-theoretic precursor to the binder
\cite{XiWu2023}. The dependent reading of the binder is related to dependent session typing \cite{ToninhoYoshida2017,WuXi2018,ToninhoCairesPfenning2011}, and full contingent strategies have established treatments \cite{Rubinstein1991,EscardoOliva2023}. We rely on established mathematics: the clone of functions preserving triples with a repeat \cite{Szendrei2012}, contextual congruences \cite{BlokPigozzi1989,Font1993}, and the order algebra, finite basis, and path normal forms of Freese et al.\ \cite{FreeseEtAl2002}. The report also uses the factorpower of the symmetric group \cite{GanyushkinMazorchuk1995,Mazorchuk2009}, closure operators \cite{Crapo1967}, and the residual largeness of the order algebra \cite{FreeseEtAl2002}.

\paragraph{Formalization.}
Every theorem of the main text is stated and proved in Lean~4 with mathlib, together with the report results it cites, with no admitted proofs and no added axioms. The Lean proofs assume no theorem from the literature, and the complexity results are proved for Turing machines on encoded terms. \Cref{app:verification} lists the statements and the evidence for the remaining report results.

\paragraph{Term operations.}
The core's term operations have canonical normal forms at every arity,
and realizability reduces to repeated tests of singleton outputs
(\cref{core:thm:source-guards,core:cor:guard-peeling}). They are not
finitely related: no finite family of relations closed under
coordinatewise owned choice determines them, since for every $m$ some
$(m+3)$-ary operation agrees with a core term on any $m$ valuations
without being one (\cref{core:thm:not-finitely-related}).

\paragraph{Open problems.}
A short structural condition on these normal forms that characterizes
the term operations is open. The binder $\Q_r(x:A).G$ is dependent: later types may depend on earlier choices, whereas the winner algebra records only who wins. \Cref{app:dependent} develops a richer interface that keeps dependent witnesses, full strategies, and what each player has seen, for a fixed order of moves; orders that depend on the values chosen remain open.
