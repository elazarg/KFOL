\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:verification}\label{sec:development}

\paragraph{Related work.}
Olde Loohuis and Venema's algebras observe winning strategies \cite{OldeLoohuisVenema2010}; individual forcing does not define possible SPE winners. Xi and Wu's multirole logic has multiparty cut elimination and is the closest proof-theoretic precursor to the binder
\cite{XiWu2023}. The dependent reading of the binder is related to dependent session typing \cite{ToninhoYoshida2017,WuXi2018,ToninhoCairesPfenning2011}, and full contingent strategies have established treatments \cite{Rubinstein1991,EscardoOliva2023}. We rely on established mathematics
throughout: the clone preserving triples with a repeat \cite{Szendrei2012}; the factorpower of the symmetric group for ambiguity actions \cite{GanyushkinMazorchuk1995,Mazorchuk2009}; contextual congruences and closure operators \cite{BlokPigozzi1989,Font1993,Crapo1967}; and the order algebra, finite
basis, path normal forms, and residual largeness of Freese et al.\ \cite{FreeseEtAl2002}. Deterministic open games give a related compositional approach \cite{GhaniEtAl2018}. The probabilistic extension of Ghani et al.\ \cite{GhaniEtAl2020} has finite counterexamples to its unit and associativity laws
\cite{GameTheoryOpenGameObstructions2026}, which we reproduce in \cref{se:sec:open-game-obstruction}; our round recursion is proved directly.

\paragraph{Formalization.}
The Lean development uses only \texttt{Std} and has no admitted proofs or added axioms. It covers the SPE recurrence, player views, the pair theorem and minimum test domain, idempotent elimination, the common-source theorem, reset's selector semantics and three-element completeness, the semantic core of
the hardness reductions, and one-owner completeness with its four-element generator. \Cref{app:verification} records the evidence for each result, including what is imported from the literature.

\paragraph{Open problems.}
The main question is a presentation. Reset has a finite equational basis. Is there one for $\{\del,H_a\}$, whose four-element generator makes it the natural next target? And for the core, or the full algebra? \Cref{app:basis} sets out a conditional route through Willard's theorem and the
obstacles to it. The full clone also remains undetermined, since pair tests control inputs and not which functions on them are realizable. Blocking policies give further tractable families (\cref{app:selective}). Chains are polynomial, and two policies have a complete complexity classification: for overlapping incomparable policies, duplicating an atomic pattern changes hardness to polynomiality. Empty common intersection yields hardness even for pairwise-overlapping families. Three or more policies remain to be classified; full equality can be easy even when one player's observational equality is hard.

Finally, the binder $\Q_r(x:A).G$ is a dependent one, in which later types may depend on earlier choices, and we have studied only who wins. \Cref{app:dependent} develops the dependent interface: each endpoint sees a dependent type, execution is dependent substitution, full strategies differ from executable plans, and a
committed value stays hidden until the first release of it or of anything depending on it. How connection should preserve witnesses, full strategies, and permitted observations together is the type-theoretic question that remains.
