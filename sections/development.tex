\subsection{Open problems in the algebra}

The most pressing question is a presentation. Reset has a finite equational basis. A finite basis for full owned choice or its intrinsic core is unresolved here, and the
core's coNP-completeness rules out extending reset's polynomial equality algorithm to the whole core unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$. It does not prevent a finite equational basis.

A smaller target is the one-owner fragment. A finite basis for the minimum four-element expansion is not supplied by the polynomial guard algorithm, and its abstract guard tables lack a
realizability criterion analogous to reset's: canonical comparison of tables extracted from terms does not characterize which tables can occur. Since the generator is conservative, it may
yield to the methods that classify the conservative models of reset. Our polynomial bounds also concern trees, and shared subexpressions require a separate size analysis.

The full clone remains undetermined. Pair tests control inputs, not all-arity realizability of tables on those inputs. The unary ambiguity quotient is exact, but does not determine arbitrary
singleton fibers or mixed multi-input operations.

There is a conditional route to a finite basis. Each owned choice is a semilattice operation, and for fixed $K$ the full finite-signature variety is congruence meet-semidistributive. Concretely,
the semilattice witness terms displayed just before Willard's Theorem~2.1 satisfy his condition $C_2(T)$ when their product is interpreted as any fixed $\star_r$ \cite{Willard2000}; the
characterization then supplies meet-semidistributivity, and the expansion by other operations preserves those term identities. Willard's finite-basis theorem applies if the variety has a finite
residual bound \cite{Willard2000}. But classifying quotients of the generator does not bound irreducibles throughout the variety, and reset's residual largeness does not transfer automatically to
an expansion by other operations.

Two specific obstacles stand in the way of a residual bound. The pair test domain is not a subalgebra, and testing all identities on that domain does not bound the irreducible models of those identities.
Likewise, the $K+1$-state player quotients describe the generator's reduct, not every subalgebra and quotient of arbitrary powers. In the opposite direction, the reset combs would prove residual largeness
for full owned choice if each could be expanded to a member of the full generated variety with the prescribed reset term operation. Such an expansion would remain subdirectly irreducible: every nonidentity
congruence of the expansion is a reset congruence and therefore contains the comb's nontrivial monolith. The missing step is constructing expansions satisfying all full-signature identities. We have neither
those expansions nor a residual bound here.

The tractable fragments are also constrained by symmetry. With role switching, one owned choice defines every other:
\[
 x\star_b y=\pi(\pi^{-1}x\star_a\pi^{-1}y),\qquad\pi(a)=b.
\]
So a role-symmetric fragment containing unrestricted owned choice already contains the hard core, and a tractable role-symmetric fragment needs something weaker.

\subsection{From winners to dependent interaction}

We began with a binder $\Q_r(x:A).G$ in which later types may depend on earlier choices, and we have so far studied only who wins. \Cref{app:dependent} develops the interface that the winner algebra
abstracts away, and it is worth saying what this interface is.

A script gives each endpoint a dependent type: the owner of a binder sees a $\sum$, every other player sees a $\prod$. Execution is dependent substitution, and all endpoints continue with the same value. This
is an instance of dependent multiparty session typing \cite{ToninhoYoshida2017,WuXi2018,ToninhoCairesPfenning2011}, and the operations are definable in an existing dependent calculus. Two points deserve emphasis.
Full strategies and executable plans differ: reducing a full strategy to a plan preserves execution but can lose subgame perfection, so equilibria do not factor through execution. And hidden values need stable
binding: a committed cell keeps its name and value across publication, a dependent cell can be published only after its index is visible, and a value remains hidden until the first release of it \emph{or of
anything depending on it}, a qualification that the copy-and-publish example shows to be necessary.

A general connection rule would have to preserve dependent contexts and the sealing certificate, provide one supplier for each witness, and relate open components to full strategies as well as execution. The
complete-revelation recursion of \cref{sec:sealed-rounds} gives an exact recursion at public boundaries, and proves that its qualitative contextual quotient is already infinite, but it does not permit hidden cells
to cross those boundaries. Neither that recursion nor a winner-set equation proves general hidden composition, and no such cut theorem or stronger CIC-style theory is claimed. The stable-witness interface currently
consists of the written judgments $\Gamma_{E_m}\vdash t_m:A$, $\Gamma_{E_m}\vdash A:\U$, and the recipient-prefix premise $\Gamma_i^{<c}(\Xi)\vdash A:\U$ of \cref{sec:disclosure}. A Lean interface for staged visibility
would need a formal data calculus, well-formed dependent context projections, and an insertion/substitution theorem; the existing Lean modules formalize the public game algebra only.

Finally, a warning against a tempting identification. Antimatroid prerequisites record when an input contributes under ambiguity statuses. They are not automatically the information dependencies of commit--reveal, and
connecting the two would require specified judgments and a preservation theorem. Adding control also requires an account of stable dependent values and conversion; the known two-role degeneracy constraints already apply
before adding players \cite{Herbelin2005,Miquey2017}.
