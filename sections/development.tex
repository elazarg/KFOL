\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:verification}\label{sec:development}

\paragraph{Two players.}
We have assumed $K\geq3$, and much of the structure depends on it. With two players the values form the chain $\{b\}<\{a,b\}<\{a\}$, on which $\star_a$ and $\star_b$ are maximum and minimum and the role switch is negation: this is Kleene's strong three-valued logic \cite{Kleene1952}, which has a classical finite equational basis \cite{Kalman1958}. Public games reach only the two atoms. A value says only who can force a win, so the algebra sits inside Olde Loohuis and Venema's for two players. Neutral union disappears, reset and $H_a$ become projections, owned choice itself preserves atoms, and idempotent operations need not come from $\mathbf C$. \Cref{app:two-players} has the details.

\paragraph{Hidden moves.}
On public trees, mixed strategies add no possible winner (\cref{se:cor:public}). A hidden move changes this: if one player picks a bit and another guesses it unseen, there is no pure equilibrium, so values must keep probabilities. The report treats rounds in which every player commits privately and all actions are then revealed. Their values compose on sets of winner distributions (\cref{se:thm:round}), but with three players no compositional semantics with finitely many values tracks even who might win (\cref{se:thm:infinite}). With two players the winner-set algebra extends to these rounds, and what it misses is a scalar value, which belongs with the equilibrium semantics of independence-friendly logic \cite{SevensterSandu2010} (\cref{app:two-players}). The probabilistic open games of Ghani et al.\ \cite{GhaniEtAl2020} offer another composition, but their unit and associativity laws have finite counterexamples \cite{GameTheoryOpenGameObstructions2026}, which we reproduce in \cref{se:sec:open-game-obstruction}; the round recursion is proved directly.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Olde Loohuis and Venema's algebras observe winning strategies, and they prove equivalence coNP-complete by reduction from distributive lattices \cite[Theorem~5.50]{OldeLoohuisVenema2010}. Individual forcing does not define possible SPE winners, and owned choice is not distributive, so that reduction does not apply here. Xi and Wu's multirole logic has multiparty cut elimination and is the closest proof-theoretic precursor to the binder
\cite{XiWu2023}. The dependent reading of the binder is related to dependent session typing \cite{ToninhoYoshida2017,WuXi2018,ToninhoCairesPfenning2011}, and full contingent strategies have established treatments \cite{Rubinstein1991,EscardoOliva2023}. We rely on established mathematics
throughout: the clone preserving triples with a repeat \cite{Szendrei2012}; the factorpower of the symmetric group for ambiguity actions \cite{GanyushkinMazorchuk1995,Mazorchuk2009}; contextual congruences and closure operators \cite{BlokPigozzi1989,Font1993,Crapo1967}; and the order algebra, finite
basis, path normal forms, and residual largeness of Freese et al.\ \cite{FreeseEtAl2002}. Deterministic open games give a related compositional approach \cite{GhaniEtAl2018}.

\paragraph{Formalization.}
Every theorem of the main text is stated and proved in Lean~4 with mathlib, together with the report results it cites, with no admitted proofs and no added axioms. The Lean proofs assume no theorem from the literature, and the complexity results are proved for Turing machines on encoded terms. \Cref{app:verification} lists the statements and the evidence for the remaining report results.

\paragraph{Open problems.}
For one owner, a basis needs at least five variables and six suffice. Do five suffice? \Cref{app:basis} gives the bounds. Which functions the core's terms realize beyond arity two is also open, since pair tests control the inputs and not the functions on them.

Finally, the binder $\Q_r(x:A).G$ is dependent: later types may depend on earlier choices, whereas the winner algebra records only who wins. \Cref{app:dependent} develops a richer interface that keeps dependent witnesses, full strategies, and what each player has seen, for a fixed order of moves; orders that depend on the values chosen remain open.
