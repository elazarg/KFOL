The full pair theorem, idempotent elimination and optimal diagonal tests,
complete congruence classifications, external $S_2$ step, unary fibers,
antimatroid representation and synthesis, forest membership in the reset
variety, and full complexity reduction have written proofs here. Finite
checks do not make them generic Lean theorems. Reset basis completeness
is imported mathematically from Freese et al., not as a Lean axiom.
Dependent script metatheory and hiding also have written proofs.
For sealed rounds, the SE correspondence, consistency and public-boundary
pasting, semialgebraic closure, scalar game realization and contextual
full abstraction have written proofs in \cref{sec:sealed-rounds}.
\texttt{SealedRounds.lean} proves the stage and arithmetic certificates;
it does not define a general SE assessment or connect the full round
syntax to the threshold probes internally.
For the smaller hard fragments, \texttt{Fragments.lean} checks the
semantic certificates under the stated baseline-containment hypotheses,
including both absorption and fresh-marker separation. Assembly of
the syntactic reductions, their polynomial tree sizes, and the final
complexity statements are written proofs in \cref{sec:complexity}.
The colouring proof has a generic Lean pigeonhole/guard certificate;
the complete identity family and its role-set embeddings are proved
mathematically there.

Run \texttt{make check paper}. Independent finite checks in \texttt{checks/}
enumerate small full profiles and deviations, synthesize the $131$
reduct and $751$ full three-role unary functions, and compare congruences,
tests, matching criteria, and fibers with independent constructions.
They check the Boolean reduction on $4369$ small CNFs and $28$ actual
constant-free equation pairs on all $7^4$ mixed valuations. Reset checks
compare antimatroid enumeration with term closure through arity four,
all $140^2$ products, and $20^4$ candidate root-fixed five-input tables,
and compare conservative models with forest enumeration. Separate
bit-parallel checks evaluate the new native fragment equations on every
winner-set valuation: $11$ one-variable and $604$ two-variable CNFs at
$K=3$, with $823543$ valuations per two-variable reset/union equation
and $2401$ per two-variable $H$ equation. These include empty clauses,
contradictions, and tautological clauses. At $K=4$ the one-variable
reset/union equations are checked on all $759375$ valuations. The
complete-graph identities are checked on all $16807$ valuations at
$K=3$ and $11390625$ at $K=4$, with explicit next-role counterexamples.
Scalar evaluation independently checks witnesses and samples from the
bit-parallel tables. Separate
models execute dependent phase and sealing rules. These computations
check their finite implementations, not the conversion or normalization
metatheory of the dependent data calculus.
Sealed-round checks compare complete rational Nash support rectangles
with direct unilateral deviations on a grid for $256$ two-by-two
continuation tables. They check $2209$ diagonal games, rational synthesis
through denominator $100$, $24$ reciprocal games and $576$ threshold
contexts, as well as role transport, commitment exchange, and the
nonconvex distribution-set witness. These are local stage and arithmetic
checks, not a solver for beliefs in general imperfect-information games.

\subsection{Algebraic boundaries}

Reset has a finite equational presentation. A finite basis for full
owned choice or its intrinsic core is unresolved here. Pair tests
control inputs, not all-arity realizability of tables on those inputs.
The unary ambiguity quotient is exact, but does not determine arbitrary
singleton fibers or mixed multi-input operations. The core's
coNP-completeness prevents extending reset's polynomial equality
algorithm to the whole core unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$; it does not
prevent a finite equational basis.

Each owned choice is a semilattice operation. For fixed $K$, the
full finite-signature variety is congruence meet-semidistributive.
Concretely, the semilattice witness terms displayed just before
Willard's Theorem~2.1 satisfy his condition $C_2(T)$ when their product
is interpreted as any fixed $\star_r$ \cite{Willard2000}. The
characterization then supplies meet-semidistributivity; the expansion
by other operations preserves those term identities.
Willard's finite-basis theorem applies if it has a finite residual bound
\cite{Willard2000}. Classifying quotients of the generator does not bound
irreducibles throughout the variety. Reset's residual largeness does
not transfer automatically to an expansion by other operations.

There are two specific obstacles to a residual-bound argument. The pair
test domain is not a subalgebra, and testing all identities on that
domain does not bound the irreducible models of those identities.
Likewise, the $K+1$-state player quotients describe the generator's
reduct, not every subalgebra and quotient of arbitrary powers.
In the opposite direction, the reset combs would prove residual
largeness for full owned choice if each could be expanded to a member
of the full generated variety with the prescribed reset term operation.
Such an expansion would remain subdirectly irreducible: every
nonidentity congruence of the expansion is a reset congruence and
therefore contains the comb's nontrivial monolith. The missing step is
constructing expansions satisfying all full-signature identities, not
preserving irreducibility after an expansion has been obtained. We
have neither those expansions nor a residual bound here.

With role switching, one owned choice defines every other:
\[
 x\star_b y=\pi(\pi^{-1}x\star_a\pi^{-1}y),\qquad\pi(a)=b.
\]
A role-symmetric fragment containing unrestricted owned choice already
contains the hard core. The lower boundary is sharper:
\cref{thm:owner-free-hardness,thm:H-hardness} show coNP-completeness
for reset with union, which has no owners, and for two owners'
idempotent atom-preserving $H$ operations. Neither removing owners nor
requiring atom preservation alone ensures tractability. The fragment
generated by reset and one fixed $H_a$ remains unresolved here.

\subsection{Dependent and hidden interaction}

The dependent interface in \cref{app:dependent} retains shared witnesses,
full alternatives, and audience-derived observations. A general
connection rule must preserve dependent contexts and the sealing
certificate, provide one supplier for each witness, and relate open
components to full strategies as well as execution. The complete-revelation
fragment in \cref{sec:sealed-rounds} supplies an exact SE recursion at
public boundaries, and proves that its qualitative contextual quotient
is already infinite. It does not permit hidden cells to cross those
boundaries. Neither that recursion nor a winner-set equation proves
general hidden composition. No such general cut theorem or stronger
CIC-style theory is claimed.

The stable-witness interface currently consists of the written judgments
$\Gamma_{E_m}\vdash t_m:A$, $\Gamma_{E_m}\vdash A:\U$, and the
recipient-prefix premise $\Gamma_i^{<c}(\Xi)\vdash A:\U$ for publication
in \cref{sec:disclosure}. A Lean interface for staged visibility would
need a formal data calculus, well-formed dependent context projections,
and an insertion/substitution theorem before it could certify those
judgments or their preservation. The existing Lean modules formalize
the public game algebra; they do not yet provide this dependent interface.

Antimatroid prerequisites record when an input contributes under
ambiguity statuses. They are not automatically the information
dependencies of commit--reveal. Connecting the structures requires
specified judgments and a preservation theorem. Adding control also
requires an account of stable dependent values and conversion; the
known two-role degeneracy constraints already apply before adding
players \cite{Herbelin2005,Miquey2017}.
