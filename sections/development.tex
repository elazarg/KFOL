\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:verification}\label{sec:development}

\paragraph{Two players.}
We have assumed $K\geq3$, and much of the structure depends on it. With two players the values form the chain $\{b\}<\{a,b\}<\{a\}$, on which $\star_a$ and $\star_b$ are maximum and minimum and the role switch is negation: this is Kleene's strong three-valued logic \cite{Kleene1952}, which has a classical finite equational basis \cite{Kalman1958}. Public games reach only the two atoms. A value says only who can force a win, so the algebra sits inside Olde Loohuis and Venema's for two players. Neutral union disappears, reset and $H_a$ become projections, owned choice itself preserves atoms, and idempotent operations need not come from $\mathbf C$. Sealed rounds behave better than for $K\geq3$: the possible winners of a round are the union of those of the two public games in which one player commits first, so the winner-set algebra extends to rounds, and hiding adds only the value $\{a,b\}$. What escapes forcing is the scalar value, which belongs with the equilibrium semantics of independence-friendly logic \cite{SevensterSandu2010}. \Cref{app:two-players} has the details.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Olde Loohuis and Venema's algebras observe winning strategies, and they prove equivalence coNP-complete by reduction from distributive lattices \cite[Theorem~5.50]{OldeLoohuisVenema2010}. Individual forcing does not define possible SPE winners, and owned choice is not distributive, so that reduction does not apply here. Xi and Wu's multirole logic has multiparty cut elimination and is the closest proof-theoretic precursor to the binder
\cite{XiWu2023}. The dependent reading of the binder is related to dependent session typing \cite{ToninhoYoshida2017,WuXi2018,ToninhoCairesPfenning2011}, and full contingent strategies have established treatments \cite{Rubinstein1991,EscardoOliva2023}. We rely on established mathematics
throughout: the clone preserving triples with a repeat \cite{Szendrei2012}; the factorpower of the symmetric group for ambiguity actions \cite{GanyushkinMazorchuk1995,Mazorchuk2009}; contextual congruences and closure operators \cite{BlokPigozzi1989,Font1993,Crapo1967}; and the order algebra, finite
basis, path normal forms, and residual largeness of Freese et al.\ \cite{FreeseEtAl2002}. Deterministic open games give a related compositional approach \cite{GhaniEtAl2018}. The probabilistic extension of Ghani et al.\ \cite{GhaniEtAl2020} has finite counterexamples to its unit and associativity laws
\cite{GameTheoryOpenGameObstructions2026}, which we reproduce in \cref{se:sec:open-game-obstruction}; our round recursion is proved directly.

\paragraph{Formalization.}
The Lean development uses only \texttt{Std} and has no admitted proofs or added axioms. It covers the SPE recurrence, player views, the pair theorem and minimum test domain, idempotent elimination, the common-source theorem, reset's selector semantics and three-element completeness, the semantic core of
the hardness reductions, and one-owner completeness with its four-element generator. \Cref{app:verification} records the evidence for each result, including what is imported from the literature.

\paragraph{Open problems.}
Reset and its one-owner expansion both have finite equational bases.
For one owner, forty identities using at most six variables suffice
over arbitrary algebras (\cref{owner:thm:finite-basis}). The minimum
variable bound lies between four and six: a four-element countermodel
satisfies every three-variable identity
(\cref{owner:prop:four-variables}). Can the upper bound be reduced to
four? Within conservative algebras,
thirteen equations suffice, with an optimal bound of four variables
(\cref{owner:thm:conservative-basis}). Any countermodel to the entire
four-variable theory has at least six elements
(\cref{owner:cor:small-four-variable}).
That relative presentation fails on an explicit nonconservative model,
and even the intended variety has nonconservative irreducible members
(\cref{owner:prop:relative-not-full,owner:prop:nonconservative-si}).
Finite basability of the core and full algebra remains open.
For the $K$-owner core, every basis must contain an equation using
at least $K+1$ variables: a $(K+2)$-element algebra satisfies its
entire $K$-variable theory but fails owner exhaustion
(\cref{core:thm:basis-arity}). Its binary term functions are determined:
the free two-generated core has $2^{K+1}+K+1$ elements
(\cref{core:prop:binary}).
The core has no finite residual bound: it contains expansions of all
meet-distributive bisemilattices that preserve their congruences
(\cref{core:prop:dissemilattice-lift}). The residual-bound route
through Willard's theorem \cite{Willard2000} therefore remains a possibility only for
the richer signatures. Raw owned choice with union has the classical
seven-equation dissemilattice basis
(\cref{core:prop:choice-union-basis}). One-owner table realizability and its clone
are determined by \cref{owner:thm:synthesis}. The full owned-choice
clone remains undetermined, since pair tests control inputs and not
which functions on them are realizable. The blocking-policy complexity
question is settled for every fixed finite family by
\cref{selective:thm:families}. The distinction between trees and shared
circuits is also settled: reset alone becomes coNP-complete with
sharing (\cref{reset:thm:circuits}).

Finally, the binder $\Q_r(x:A).G$ is dependent: later types may depend
on earlier choices, whereas the winner algebra records only who wins.
\Cref{app:dependent} develops the richer interface. Private-store
connection preserves dependent witnesses, full strategies, and audience
views together, and composes sealing certificates
(\cref{dep:thm:connection}). This resolves connection for the finite
phase language stated there. Extending it to data-dependent schedules,
asynchronous channels, and observable failure requires additional
interfaces and proof rules.
