\section{Dependent execution, full strategies, and disclosure}
\label{app:dependent}

The winner-set algebra observes finite public games. This appendix develops the
dependent execution and disclosure interfaces that retain what that observation
discards: witnesses shared by all endpoints, full alternatives, and
audience-derived observations. Multiparty coherence \cite{CarboneEtAl2016} and dependent
command/response interaction \cite{HancockSetzer2000} are further foundations for it.

Consider the role-owned script
\begin{equation}
 \Q_A(n:\Nat_{>0}).\ \Q_B(v:\mathsf{Vec}(\Bool,n)).\
 \Q_C(j:\mathsf{Fin}(n)).\ G(n,v,j).
 \label{dep:eq:1}
\end{equation}
There is one supplied value at each binder, shared by all endpoints.
The owner-as-supplier and receivers-as-consumers interpretations are
related to dependent session typing
\cite{ToninhoYoshida2017,WuXi2018,ToninhoCairesPfenning2011}.

\subsection{Toward a connection rule}

A general connection rule must preserve dependent contexts and the sealing
certificate, provide one supplier for each witness, and relate open components
to full strategies as well as execution. The stable-witness interface currently
consists of the judgments $\Gamma_{E_m}\vdash t_m:A$, $\Gamma_{E_m}\vdash A:\U$,
and the recipient-prefix premise $\Gamma_i^{<c}(\Xi)\vdash A:\U$ of
\cref{sec:disclosure}. A Lean interface for staged visibility would need a formal
data calculus, well-formed dependent context projections, and an
insertion/substitution theorem. Adding control also requires an account of stable
dependent values and conversion; the known two-role degeneracy constraints already
apply before adding players \cite{Herbelin2005,Miquey2017}.
