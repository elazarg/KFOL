\section{Introduction}

Suppose Alice chooses a positive number $n$, Bob supplies a vector of length
$n$, and Carol selects a valid position. Alice's choice changes the types of
Bob's and Carol's choices. Each participant has a different interface to the
same event. Alice supplies $n$; Bob and Carol receive it. At the next event,
Bob becomes the supplier. No participant is permanently the verifier or
the environment.

A role-owned quantifier records this structure directly:
\[
 \Q_A(n:\Nat_{>0}).\
 \Q_B(v:\mathsf{Vec}(\Bool,n)).\
 \Q_C(j:\mathsf{Fin}(n)).\ G(n,v,j).
 \tag{1}
\]
An owned choice has the local shape of a dependent sum: supply a witness
and continue at its instance. A received choice has the shape of a
dependent product: accept the witness and continue at that instance.
Globally, there is one binder and one chosen value, shared by all the
continuations.

Player-indexed choices have a well-developed semantic foundation.
Abramsky gives choices, quantifiers, role permutations, and information
constraints an intrinsic game interpretation \cite{Abramsky2007}.
Multirole logic supplies role-indexed proof rules and multiparty cut
\cite{XiWu2023}. Dependent session types make communicated values
available in continuation types \cite{ToninhoCairesPfenning2011};
Wu and Xi's multiparty system includes endpoint-specific quantification
\cite{WuXi2018}. Toninho and Yoshida project value-dependent global types
even to endpoints with partial views \cite{ToninhoYoshida2017}.
We use these results as the foundation for explicit interfaces between
binding, strategic behavior, and disclosure.

The first computational requirement is shared dependent substitution.
When Alice supplies a number $a$, every endpoint advances to the
continuation for that same $a$. The substitution changes types as well as
programs. We isolate this familiar mechanism in a small public calculus
over a pure dependent data language. Its elementary execution laws provide
the background for two harder questions.

\paragraph{Which behaviors does an interaction retain?}
A participant's full strategy specifies what to do after every history,
including histories excluded by its own earlier decisions. An executable
plan can omit those counterfactual continuations. That reduction preserves
complete execution, but loses information needed for subgame perfection.
Even Nash equilibrium cannot be determined from an actual execution alone:
it compares the profile with unilateral deviations.

The interaction object therefore contains a script, full strategy spaces,
and evaluation. An objective is a predicate or utility on executions.
A solution concept is a property of the strategies and their alternatives.
The same interaction can be analyzed under different goals or rationality
conditions without changing who owns its choices.

For a concrete logical target, consider finite perfect-information games
with one winner at each leaf. Evaluate a game by the set of winners obtained
by full pure subgame-perfect profiles. Ordinary backward induction gives
explicit role-indexed operations on these sets. At two roles, one-winner
values recover Boolean truth. At three roles, indifference supports extra
values and refutes distributivity. This yields an equational research
problem that retains role-owned choice without defining its meaning by
adversarial guarantees.

\paragraph{When does a chosen value become usable?}
In a sealed-bid interaction, Alice fixes a value before Bob acts and
discloses it afterward. If Alice could choose at the later moment, her
choice could depend on Bob's intervening action. Commitment must therefore
preserve the earlier binding across a change of audience.

Visibility is also a typing issue. Alice may privately construct
$v:\mathsf{Vec}(\Bool,n)$ while $n$ is still hidden from Bob.
Bob cannot use that declared type until its index is available or a
specified package abstracts it. Publication should enlarge a local context
without replacing $n$ by a new unrelated witness.

We define a finite phase calculus for this purpose. Its local views are
computed from audience annotations, not supplied independently.
A two-execution theorem proves hiding for prefixes with no release of the
protected cell or its dependencies. The dependency condition is essential:
publishing a copy of a hidden payload can disclose it before its own
opening command. The theorem makes that indirect channel explicit.

\paragraph{What is established, and what remains a contribution question?}
The broadcast projection, dependent path construction, and elementary
execution metatheory are established constructions used as background.
The phase calculus and finite rational algebra specify two concrete
research targets: dependent connection preserving stable binding and
observation permissions, and a proof theory for rational role-owned choice.
The statements below establish their indicated finite laws and give
counterexamples to stronger claims. Originality of this assembly, or of a
new logic or type theory, has not been established.

Definability in an existing dependent kernel explains an implementation
route. It is neither a novelty claim nor a reason to dismiss all conservative
calculi. A new proof calculus still needs its own judgments, identity,
connectives, connection rules, and metatheory. We specify those obligations
after presenting the semantic interfaces.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The public core has finite dependent scripts and complete connections.
Its conversation composition publishes endpoint returns together, thereby
adding a simultaneous decision stage when those returns admit choices.
The disclosure language has a fixed public schedule and successful
publication; its theorem does not cover arbitrary timing or failure
channels. The rational algebra concerns perfect-information trees with
unit terminal returns. These are different declared interfaces for the
same research program, rather than interchangeable notions of composition
or observation.
