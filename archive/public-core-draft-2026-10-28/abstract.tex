\begin{abstract}
An interactive choice has an owner. Existential and universal quantification
describe that choice from different sides; a role-indexed binder records the
owner directly. We develop this perspective as a small dependent calculus of
multiparty interaction. A global script specifies role-owned choices and their
dependent continuations. Endpoint terms supply witnesses at their own choices
and accept inputs at the others. Connecting endpoints performs one shared
dependent substitution at each step.

The public core has an explicit interpretation by dependent sums and products.
We prove substitution compatibility, preservation of types under interaction,
termination and uniqueness of complete execution, and equivariance under role
renaming. Public return boundaries support a concrete associative sequencing
operation. A separate information semantics splits the fixing of a value from
its disclosure: commitment introduces one stable private binding, and
publication changes its audience without choosing the value again. We prove
binding preservation, observation uniformity, and a sufficient condition for
public outcomes to be common knowledge.

The construction brings together player-indexed game syntax, multirole logic,
and dependent session typing. Its meaning retains the participants' behaviors
and the complete execution; objectives and solution concepts are observations
of that object. The resulting core isolates the binding and observation laws
that a dependent multiparty cut calculus must preserve.
\end{abstract}
