\noindent This report holds the appendices of the paper \emph{Rational Owned Choice: Finite Algebras of Multiplayer Games} \cite{GershuniOwnedChoice2026}, which gives the definitions, the notation, and the main statements. Lettered references, such as \cref{app:proofs}, are to this report. Numbered ones, such as \cref{sec:rational} and \cref{sym:thm:pairs}, are to the paper.

\paragraph{Further results.}
Several results concern structures and observations beyond the main paper's
equational classifications:
\begin{itemize}
\item Arbitrarily branching well-founded public games have finite binary
representatives, with the sharp local support bound $K-1$
(\cref{game:prop:choice-support}).
\item Linear-size core circuits can require exponentially large trees
even after discarding winners (\cref{core:thm:robust-tree-size}).
The reset-circuit expansion bound also survives arbitrary core choices
(\cref{core:cor:reset-ambient-tree-bound}).
\item Boolean winner observations recover the complete equilibrium value
and order of the two-player fragment (\cref{se:prop:pair-observation}).
\item A projection's singleton-profile fiber contains a copy of the entire
lower-arity core (\cref{core:thm:projection-fiber}), whereas read-once trees
have a closed profile formula for their full values and are least in their
profile fibers (\cref{core:thm:read-once-minimum}). At three inputs a
seven-leaf operation requires every variable repeatedly, and seven
leaves are optimal (\cref{core:prop:seven-leaf-branching}).
\item Collapsing all ambiguity gives an explicitly finitely based subvariety
with least near-unanimity arity $K+1$ (\cref{core:thm:atomic-basis}).
\item Finite powerset multiplications have a general faithful relational
representation (\cref{query:lem:powerset}); canonical quadratic bichain
extensions are definable exactly for cographs
(\cref{core:thm:cograph-extension}).
\item Exponential local interpolation can fail globally even for prime
operations whose proper identification minors and neutral residuals are
all core (\cref{core:thm:exponential-locality,core:cor:prime-obstructions}).
\item Source-separable upper intervals above greatest profile completions
have source-linear normal forms (\cref{core:thm:capture-trigger-normal-form}).
Complete-head circuits have a closed realizability classification;
even realizable source-separable operations can lack greatest full
occurrence types while attaining their greatest old-observer coefficients
(\cref{core:thm:head-circuits,core:thm:head-types}).
\item Two foreign owners admit exact feedback compression. Even feedback
after every unused-owner extension does not characterize pure traces:
a nine-variable tuple passes it but violates a forced singleton profile
(\cref{core:thm:two-color-feedback,core:thm:identity-stable-feedback}).
Conditional traces have an exact description by full-support core
minorants and need not themselves be jointly realizable
(\cref{core:thm:conditional-trace-minorants,core:prop:conditional-trace-nonrealizable}).
A four-variable term already has a pure port that cannot be retained
by any subordinate word using its source once
(\cref{core:prop:pure-source-repetition}); five variables suffice
even for a source-separable core operation
(\cref{core:prop:separable-source-repetition}).
\item Additive owner closures have a closed graph criterion and explicit
component-spine realizers. Mixed-component deletion also gives the
greatest core minorant of every additive candidate, when one exists
(\cref{core:thm:additive-closures,core:thm:additive-greatest-minorant}).
\end{itemize}
Verification status is recorded separately in \cref{app:verification}.
